# Read-aloud script — "Dusty the Donkey and the Bremen Band"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story by a crackling fire at bedtime, your voice getting softer as the four friends settle down for the night.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the night-time half of the story drift — the friends are heading toward sleep, and so is the listener.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Dusty: kind and a little wistful at first, then bright with hope.
- Bruno: deep, friendly, a little creaky.
- Cleo: soft and silvery, shy at first, then warm.
- Rory: proud and bright, never shrill.
- The robbers: gruff and startled, playful rather than scary.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (the mill valley): paint the picture slowly — "turning mill wheel", "slow and tired", then the loving lift on "he loved music above all things".
- Paragraph 2 (the decision): golden and hopeful — "I will go to Bremen", "sing in the town band". A happy little bounce on "humming as he went".
- Paragraph 3 (Bruno): warm and creaky — "fine deep voice like yours". Let Bruno's tail-wag land with a smile.
- Paragraph 4 (Cleo): soft and silvery — "soft, silvery voice like yours". Cleo's purr grows a little louder; let the listener hear it.
- Paragraph 5 (Rory): bright and proud — "bright morning voice like yours". A cheerful flap of wings.
- Paragraph 6 (the walk): long and dreamy — hills, meadows, sunset, then the forest darkening and the warm light glowing. Lower the voice to a whisper on "Let us rest there".
- Paragraph 7 (the cottage): hushed and conspiratorial — peeping through the window, the gruff robbers, then Dusty's bright idea: "We will sing, all together, as loud and as grand as we can."
- Paragraph 8 (the tower): build the excitement step by step — bottom, back, back, top — then a big, joyful burst on the four voices together: brayed, barked, yowled, crowed.
- Paragraph 9 (the fright): a quick gasp — "A monster is singing at the door!" — then giggles as the robbers run off and the friends tiptoe inside.
- Paragraph 10 (the ending): the softest, warmest part — cosy supper, one soft song, the morning smile. Let "a home, and friends to sing with" land gently, shown by the story, not preached.
- Final line: "Sleep now, little Dusty." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
