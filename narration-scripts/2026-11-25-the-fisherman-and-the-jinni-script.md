# Read-Aloud Script — The Fisherman and the Jinni

Warm, slow bedtime voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. The jinni is big and booming but never scary — more grumpy than frightening. Let the clever trick land; never rush the pond.

**Paragraph 1 — Opening (warm, sunny):**
Slow and soft, like settling beside the sea at morning. Emphasize "wide blue sea" and "little house with a red roof." The fisherman's white beard, blue cap and striped shirt are cozy details — say them with a smile.

**Paragraph 2 — Home (gentle, homely):**
Soft and safe. "A wife and three children at home" is warm; "fish for their supper" is content and unhurried.

**Paragraph 3 — One, two, three casts (playful, rhythmic):**
A little bounce, counting along — once, twice, three times. Seaweed, old shoe, tangle of rope — each one a small, funny disappointment. Keep it light.

**Paragraph 4 — The fourth cast (surprise, wonder):**
"— and oh!" is a real gasp of surprise. "The net was heavy" slows down. Pulling the brass jar up is an effort — "pulled and pulled" with weight. "Sealed tight with a lump of lead" is mysterious and hushed.

**Paragraph 5 — A jar! (curious, bright):**
The fisherman is delighted and curious. "I wonder what is inside" is a wondering whisper.

**Paragraph 6 — Pop! (magical, hushed):**
"— pop! —" is a small, crisp pop, then the smoke pours out — slow and swirling. "Rose and rose and twisted" builds gently. The jinni appears tall and booming — a deeper voice, but warm, not frightening.

**Paragraph 7 — At last! (big, grumpy):**
The jinni roars, big and grumpy — "a hundred years" is huge and dramatic. "I shall gobble you up!" is loud and blustering, like a grumpy giant, never truly scary.

**Paragraph 8 — Wobbly knees (gentle worry, then calm):**
"The fisherman's knees wobbled" is small and trembly. Then the deep breath — slow, steady. His "kindest voice" is soft and brave.

**Paragraph 9 — The clever question (curious, sly):**
"Wait, great jinni" is gentle and polite. "Tell me one thing" is curious. "How did someone as big as you ever fit inside a jar as small as that?" is the clever heart of the story — say it slowly, with a twinkle.

**Paragraph 10 — Ha! Easy! (proud, puffed-up):**
The jinni puffs out his chest — proud and silly. "Ha! Easy! Watch!" is quick and boastful.

**Paragraph 11 — Whoosh (quick, swirly):**
"A great whoosh" is fast and swirly. "Swirl, swirl, swirl" spins down, slower each time, right back into the jar.

**Paragraph 12 — Quick as a wink (quick, cheeky):**
Quick and triumphant — the stopper pops back on with a satisfying click. A small, clever victory.

**Paragraph 13 — Let me out! (small, muffled):**
The jinni's voice is suddenly small and muffled, coming from inside the jar. "I promise to be good!" is squeaky and eager.

**Paragraph 14 — Three times (warm, firm):**
The fisherman smiles — warm and sure. "Promise me three times" is gentle but firm. "Never gobble anyone" is a kind rule, not a scolding.

**Paragraph 15 — I promise! (eager, three times):**
"I promise, I promise, I promise!" is quick and eager, three little promises tumbling out.

**Paragraph 16 — Thank you (grateful, bowing):**
The jinni floats out and bows low — grateful and polite now. "I know a secret pond where the fish shine like jewels" is a whispered wonder.

**Paragraph 17 — The jewel pond (glowing, wondrous):**
Slow and sparkling. Red and white and blue and gold — each color gets its own small pause. "Flashing in the sun like little lamps" glows.

**Paragraph 18 — The shining catch (happy, full):**
The net, the market, the smiling people — warm and busy. "The prettiest fish they had ever seen" is proud and happy. The warm supper is cozy; "cheeks were round and rosy" is soft and full.

**Paragraph 19 — Goodbye, jinni (gentle, pink evening):**
The jinni waves goodbye — a soft farewell. "Into the pink evening sky" drifts away slowly.

**Paragraph 20 — Goodnight (sleepy, soft):**
Very slow now, sleepy and safe. The little wooden boat, the moon "as round as a pearl," warm and safe and full of supper. "Sleep now, little fisherman" is a whisper, barely above a breath. Then "The end" — quiet, final, and kind.
