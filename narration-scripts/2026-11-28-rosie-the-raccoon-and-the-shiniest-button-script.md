# Read-Aloud Script — Rosie the Raccoon and the Shiniest Button

Warm, slow bedtime voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Rosie is gentle and thoughtful; Saffy is small and sad at first, then bright with joy. Let the quiet moments land; never rush the giving scene.

**Paragraph 1 — Opening (warm, peaceful):**
Slow and soft, like settling beside a quiet pond at dusk. Emphasize "hollow oak tree" and "quiet pond." Rosie's name is said with a smile.

**Paragraph 2 — Rosie's look (cozy, fond):**
The soft grey fur, stripy tail and neat black mask are dear details — say them warmly. "Always ready for a nighttime adventure" is playful but hushed. "Loved shiny things" is a happy little secret.

**Paragraph 3 — The treasures (gentle, sparkling):**
Each treasure gets its own small sparkle — the silver thimble, the blue marble, the round blue button. "Brightest of all" glows a little.

**Paragraph 4 — Polishing (soft, nightly ritual):**
"Every night, before bed" is a cozy routine. "My shiniest treasure" is whispered, like a secret. "Tucked it safely away" is tender and careful.

**Paragraph 5 — The wind rises (a little cooler, a little quicker):**
The leaves rustle and the pond ripples — the evening turns. "A soft sound from the branches above" is curious, a little wondering.

**Paragraph 6 — Meet Saffy (sad, small):**
A new friend arrives, and she is crying. Say "little sparrow named Saffy" gently, and let "she was crying" be soft and sad.

**Paragraph 7 — Saffy's story (trembling, heartfelt):**
Saffy sniffs — small and shaky. The lost button matters: it came from her grandmother and kept the cold wind out. "My baby brother shivers" is the saddest part — say it very softly.

**Paragraph 8 — Rosie goes still (hushed, realizing):**
"Rosie's paws went very still" is a held breath. "A little blue button" is slow, dawning. Her shiniest treasure is named again — the listener should feel the connection click.

**Paragraph 9 — The heavy heart (slow, torn):**
Rosie looks up, then at her shelf. "Her heart felt heavy as a stone" is the weight of a hard choice — unhurried and honest.

**Paragraph 10 — But it is mine (quiet, small voice):**
This is Rosie's thought, not spoken aloud — a little voice inside. "I found it" and "I polished it" are small and stubborn. "The shiniest thing I own" is wistful.

**Paragraph 11 — The wind blows colder (chilly, worried):**
The cold comes back. Saffy shivers and wraps her wings around herself — a small, cold picture. Keep it tender, not scary.

**Paragraph 12 — Remembering (slow, dawning):**
Rosie closes her eyes. The memory of finding the button is bright and happy — then it turns. "The button had a home" is the turning point: warm, sure, and quiet.

**Paragraph 13 — The climb (gentle, brave):**
Rosie climbs to her shelf and takes the button down. Each small action is steady and sure. No rush — this is the brave moment.

**Paragraph 14 — Here (soft, giving):**
"Here" is the warmest word in the story — soft and open. "Waiting to come home to you" is gentle and kind, like a gift wrapped in words.

**Paragraph 15 — Joy (bright, delighted):**
Saffy's eyes go wide and bright. "My button!" is a happy gasp. "Oh, Rosie, thank you!" sparkles with joy.

**Paragraph 16 — Home again (snug, safe):**
Saffy sews the button on with spider silk — a small, clever detail. The door shuts snug, the shivering stops, and the baby brother falls fast asleep. Everything settles, safe and warm.

**Paragraph 17 — The thank-you song (sweet, floating):**
Saffy sings back to Rosie — the sweetest song. It floats "like moonlight on water" — slow, glowing, and lovely.

**Paragraph 18 — Warmer than shiny things (warm, glowing):**
Rosie listens, and her chest feels warm. "Warmer than any shiny thing ever had" is the heart of the story — say it like a small, happy discovery.

**Paragraph 19 — Night falls (quiet, still):**
Rosie curls up in her hollow oak. The moon peeks through the leaves; the pond goes quiet and still. Everything is calm and safe.

**Paragraph 20 — No need to polish (soft, content):**
"She did not polish anything tonight." — slow, satisfied, a little smile in the voice. "Her heart was full, and her eyes were heavy with sleep" drifts off, sleepy and warm.

**Goodnight — Sleep now, little Rosie (very soft, a whisper):**
The gentlest line of all. Barely above a whisper, like tucking a child in. Let it linger, then rest.
