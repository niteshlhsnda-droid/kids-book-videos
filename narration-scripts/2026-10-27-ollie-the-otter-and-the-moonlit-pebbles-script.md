# Read-aloud script — "Ollie the Otter and the Moonlit Pebbles"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story beside a quiet river at bedtime, your voice getting softer as Ollie gets sleepier.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the last third of the story drift — Ollie is heading home to bed, and so is the listener.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Ollie the otter: bright and eager at first, then small and tired, then warm with gratitude.
- The frog: kind and chuckling, never teasing — a friendly, wise little voice.
- The dragonfly: sleepy and soft, barely above a whisper.
- The friends (final goodnight): hushed and tender together.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (river introduction): paint the picture slowly — "water whispered over smooth stones", "peach and gold". Calm and cozy.
- Paragraph 2 (the pebble path): fond and dreamy — Ollie loves his pebbles; the path matters to him.
- Paragraph 3 (carrying them all): eager and confident — "I will carry them all at once!" Then a proud little list of pebbles.
- Paragraph 4 (pebbles slip away): playful sound effects — "plop!" and "plip-plop!" light and splashy, then Ollie's huffing, a little breathless.
- Paragraph 5 (the frog offers help): the frog chuckles kindly; Ollie's "I can do it myself" is brave but wobbly.
- Paragraph 6 (the tumble): the big spill — "plop, plip, splash!" tumbling down — then slow right down for the white pebble "glowing like a tiny moon".
- Paragraph 7 (sitting on the bank): the quietest, saddest moment. Ollie's sigh is real. The frog's line is soft wisdom: "Even rivers ask the banks for help."
- Paragraph 8 (gathering together): gentle lift of hope — "Yes, please," whispered. One careful pebble at a time, unhurried.
- Paragraph 9 (the finished path): warm pride — the white pebble "glowing like a tiny lantern". Ollie's smile in your voice.
- Paragraph 10 (thank you): tender and grateful — "The path is lovelier than I ever could have made alone."
- Paragraph 11 (home to bed): barely above a whisper — the river whispering outside.
- Final line: "Sleep now, little Ollie." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
