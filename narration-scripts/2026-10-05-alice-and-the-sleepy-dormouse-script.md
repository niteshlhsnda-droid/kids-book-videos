# Narration Script: Alice and the Sleepy Dormouse

*Companion to `stories/2026-10-05-alice-and-the-sleepy-dormouse.md`. Written for a calm, warm read-aloud voice. Total runtime: about 4 minutes.*

**How to use this script:** Read the **bold narrator lines** aloud. Follow the *[bracketed cues]* for pacing, tone, and pauses. The *(parentheses)* are acting notes — don't read those.

---

*[SCENE 1 — Music soft. Warm, curious tone, like the start of a secret.]*

**One warm afternoon, Alice followed a white rabbit with pink eyes down, down, down into a dark tunnel, and landed with a soft thump on a pile of soft leaves.**

*(Let "down, down, down" tumble gently downward in pitch.)*

**She was in a funny green garden where the flowers were as big as umbrellas and the birds sang backward.**

*[SCENE 2 — Tone turns playful.]*

**Past the flowers she heard clinking — clink, clink, clink. It was a long table under a tall tree, set with dozens of teacups. At the table sat the Mad Hatter with his great tall hat, and the March Hare with his long whiskers. And between them, squished very tight, was the littlest one of all: the Dormouse. Fast asleep.**

*(Say "Fast asleep" like a cozy whisper.)*

**"Shhh," said the Hatter, putting a finger to his lips. "He falls asleep in the middle of everything."**

*[Pause 1 second.]*

**The March Hare poured tea for Alice. "He naps at breakfast, he naps at lunch, he naps at tea," he said. "We've tried everything to wake him. We tried shouting! We tried tickling! Nothing works."**

*(Make the Hare sound bouncy and a little dramatic — he's the comedy.)*

*[SCENE 3 — Tone softens. This is the heart of the story. Slow down.]*

**Alice looked at the tiny Dormouse. His whiskers twitched in his sleep, and he smiled a small, cozy smile, as if he were dreaming something very sweet. "You don't need to shout at someone who's sleepy," Alice said softly. "Maybe he just needs the world to be a little quieter."**

*[Pause 2 seconds.]*

**So Alice pulled a teacup close and spoke in the gentlest voice she had. She told the Dormouse about her garden at home, about the daisies that opened in the morning and closed their petals at night, just like sleepy eyes.**

*(Soft, lilting — like telling a story to someone drifting off.)*

**The Hatter poured more tea, very carefully, without a single clink. The March Hare folded a napkin into a tiny pillow and tucked it under the Dormouse's cheek.**

*[SCENE 4 — Slowest, sleepiest tone.]*

**The Dormouse sighed a happy little sigh in his sleep. His tiny paw reached out and held Alice's finger, and he did not wake up — not even a little. And that was quite all right.**

*[Pause 2 seconds.]*

**The garden grew golden, then pink, then purple. Fireflies lit their small lamps. The teacups were all empty now, and the moon climbed up over the tree, round and kind. The Hatter took off his great tall hat and laid it over the Dormouse like a soft blanket.**

**"Sleepy people are not broken," Alice whispered to the Hare. "They are just resting. We can wait."**

*[SCENE 5 — Barely above a whisper.]*

**And as the stars came out, Alice thought the whole garden felt like a bedtime story itself — warm tea, soft pillows, and one very sleepy, very happy little mouse.**

*[Pause 3 seconds.]*

**Sleep now, little Alice.**

*[Pause 2 seconds.]*

**The end.** *(read warmly, with a soft smile in the voice)*
