# Script — Poppy the Porcupine and the Patchwork Quilt

**Voice:** warm, slow bedtime narrator. Soft and calm throughout, like a parent telling a story at lights-out.

**Pacing:** about 120–130 words per minute. Pause 2 seconds between paragraphs; 1 second between sentences. Never rush the ending.

## Tone cues

- "At the foot of a mossy hill" — slow, dreamy, hushed, like opening a picture book.
- Poppy's grumbling — a little pouty and comic, but always gentle; lean into "Nothing fits. Nothing is right."
- The quilt descriptions — warm and tactile; make the listener feel the frayed edges and the favorite red square.
- The cold wind — low and whispery, a touch of drama, then settle.
- The wren — tiny, bright, cheerful chirp. The old field mouse — small, creaky, kind.
- "Every patch was a gift" — slow down; this is the heart of the story. Let the realization land.
- "Thank you for every single patch." — tender, sincere, barely above a whisper.
- "Sleep now, little Poppy." — a whispered sigh, the last words before sleep.
- "The end. 🌙" — barely above a whisper.

## Emphasis

- Contrast Poppy's grumpy hurried middle with the soft, warm ending.
- Lean into the sensory images: "warm as fresh bread," "wrapped in a hundred little loves."
- Never moralize — let the quilt do the teaching. End warm and drowsy.
