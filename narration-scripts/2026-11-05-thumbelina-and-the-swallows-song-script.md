# Read-aloud script — "Thumbelina and the Swallow's Song"

Voice: warm, gentle bedtime narrator. Slow, unhurried pace throughout. Pause briefly at each paragraph break.

- **Opening (soft, wistful):** "In a sunny garden…" — gentle and quiet, like a wish being made. Linger on "tiny child."
- **The tulip (wonder, hushed):** A soft gasp of delight on "soft pop." Say "no taller than her thumb" with warm amazement.
- **Walnut shell and boat (playful, light):** Breezy and happy. "She sang sweet songs" — lilting, as if singing.
- **The toad (a little serious, never scary):** Lower the voice slightly for the toad's croak: "What a pretty little thing." Keep it gruff, not frightening.
- **The fishes (kind, hopeful):** Brighten on "they did not want her to be sad." Gentle triumph as the leaf boat floats free.
- **Autumn and the mouse (cozy, warm):** Slow down; "warm soup" and "stay all winter" should feel like a blanket. "She thanked her with songs each evening" — soft and grateful.
- **The mole (hushed, a little sad):** Darker, quieter tone for the tunnel. Pause before "her heart sank" — let the sadness land, then lift on "the sky, the flowers."
- **The swallow found (tender, careful):** Very soft. "Still and cold" whispered; "sang softly" slower still, until "he fluttered his wing" — a small, happy lift.
- **Spring (joyful, warm):** The swallow's words are sung, bright and grateful: "Little friend, you saved me."
- **The flight (wonder, rising):** "Up, up, up they flew" — rising gently, each "up" a little higher. Slow and golden on "white flowers and golden light."
- **The flower land (glowing, content):** Warm and satisfied. "Her small, kind heart could shine" — the heart of the story, said softly.
- **Goodnight (softest of all):** Barely above a whisper. "Sleep now, little Thumbelina." Then a pause, and "The end. 🌙" — let the last words fade like a lullaby.
