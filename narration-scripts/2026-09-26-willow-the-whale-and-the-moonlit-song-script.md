# Read-Aloud Script — "Willow the Whale and the Moonlit Song"

**Overall tone:** Slow, dreamy, oceanic. Imagine the narrator is floating. Speak at a lullaby pace — unhurried, warm, a little hushed by the end.

## Direction by paragraph

**Title — "Willow the Whale and the Moonlit Song"**
Say softly, like the beginning of a secret. Pause 2 beats after.

**Para 1 — "In the deepest, bluest part of the sea..."**
Slow and shimmery. Emphasize *deepest, bluest*, then *silver honey* with a smile in your voice. This is the scene-setting: paint the picture gently.

**Para 2–3 — "Willow was the smallest whale..." / "My song is too small," Willow sighed...**
A little sadder, smaller. Drop your volume on *no bigger than a hum*. For Willow's sigh line: breathe it out, like a real sigh, gentle and wistful.

**Para 4 — "That night, the moon was round and white as a pearl..."**
Brighten — *round and white as a pearl* with wonder. *Calm as a blanket* smooth and soothing. Pause after "...humming her tiny song to herself."

**Para 5–7 — Taro's sniffle and his worry**
A tiny sniffle sound is okay (gentle, not startling). Taro's voice: small, wobbly, a little faster — a child who is trying not to cry. Pause after "I cannot see the way."

**Para 8 — "Willow's heart thumped like a soft drum..."**
Warm and brave. *Hold my fin* — tender and sure. On *"but I can sing it"* — a small lift of hope, like a tiny light turning on.

**Para 9 — "So Willow began her little song... Hmmmm... hmmmm..."**
The hum: actually hum, low and soft, for about 3 seconds. Let it fade.

**Para 10 — "And as she sang, something wonderful happened."**
*Wonderful* in hushed awe. Slow down for *twinkled like a trail of stars through the water* — sparkle on the word *twinkled*.

**Para 11 — "Follow the twinkles! ... step by splash..."**
Playful energy: *Follow the twinkles!* bright and encouraging. *Step by splash* with a tiny rhythmic lilt, like gentle paddling.

**Para 12–14 — The reunion**
Warm flood of relief on "There you are!" — but keep it soft (bedtime, not a shout). Taro's grin: proud and happy. Emphasize *led the whole sea* — that's the heart of the story.

**Final para — "That night, the great whales gathered around Willow..."**
The great whales' voices: deep, slow, kind rumbles. Then the whole closing should melt downward — each line softer, until the last line is barely above a whisper. End with a 2-second silence.
