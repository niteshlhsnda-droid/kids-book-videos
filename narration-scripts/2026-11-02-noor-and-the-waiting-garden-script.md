# Read-aloud script — "Noor and the Waiting Garden"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story under a blanket at bedtime, your voice getting softer as the evening sky turns pink and gold.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the garden feel sunny and safe, and let the waiting days drift by peacefully, never hurried.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Noor: a small, curious, friendly girl's voice — eager at first, then patient and proud.
- Noor's mother: warm and calm, a gentle grown-up voice.
- Shelly the snail: no spoken lines — a soft, amused pause whenever she slides by.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (the cottage and garden): paint the picture slowly — "little cottage at the end of a lane", "yellow dress covered in tiny white daisies", "one sunny patch of soil waited empty, just for her".
- Paragraph 2 (planting): tender and careful — "single striped seed", "tucked the seed inside", "patted the soil soft", "slow, silver stream". A wondering pause on "water and time".
- Paragraph 3 (morning disappointment): a little wistful — "Grow tall by tomorrow" whispered hopefully, then "exactly the same", "No green shoot. No leaf." Let the quiet land gently.
- Paragraph 4 (impatience): playful and honest — "waited all afternoon", "Maybe I planted it upside down", "her fingers itched to dig" with a small, mischievous lift.
- Paragraph 5 (Shelly): light and giggly — "Hello, Shelly", "You are slower than my seed!" with a giggle, then slow and steady on "steady and unhurried".
- Paragraph 6 (the new rhythm): calm and content — "did not dig", "water, a song, and play", "without peeking under the soil". A peaceful, repeating rhythm.
- Paragraph 7 (the shoot): bright with delight — "one dewy morning", "You came!" clapped and joyful, "no bigger than her thumbnail".
- Paragraph 8 (growing tall): growing wonder — "grew and grew", "leaves as big as Noor's hands", "taller than Noor herself", "great golden sunflower" in full bloom.
- Paragraph 9 (evening under the flower): golden and sleepy — "sky turned pink and gold", "Bees hummed", "Shelly rested on a leaf in the shade".
- Paragraph 10 (goodnight to the flower): the softest storytelling beat — "first stars blinked on", "Goodnight, sunflower", "in your own good time" whispered warmly.
- Final line: "Sleep now, little Noor." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
