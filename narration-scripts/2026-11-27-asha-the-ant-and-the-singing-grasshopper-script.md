# Read-Aloud Script — Asha the Ant and the Singing Grasshopper

Warm, slow bedtime voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Asha is steady and kind; Gus is bright and bouncy but never loud. Let the winter quiet land; never rush the fire scene.

**Paragraph 1 — Opening (warm, sunny):**
Slow and soft, like settling into a sunny meadow. Emphasize "sunny meadow" and "tall daisies." Asha's name is said with a smile.

**Paragraph 2 — Asha's scarf (cozy, homely):**
The tiny yellow scarf is a cozy detail — say it warmly. "Every morning" is steady and unhurried, like a little march.

**Paragraph 3 — Gathering seeds (gentle, rhythmic):**
"One by one, crumb by crumb" is slow and careful, each seed a small treasure. The pantry under the old oak root is safe and snug.

**Paragraph 4 — A little every day (soft, sing-song):**
Asha hums her little song — light and content. "Soon there will be plenty" is hopeful, not worried.

**Paragraph 5 — Meet Gus (bright, friendly):**
A new friend arrives. "Nearby, in the same sunny meadow" is welcoming. Gus's name is cheerful.

**Paragraph 6 — The fiddle (playful, musical):**
"Long springy legs" bounces a little. The little brown fiddle is dear to him — "from morning until night" is a happy sigh.

**Paragraph 7 — Tra-la-la! (singing, joyful):**
Gus sings! "Tra-la-la!" is bright and lilting. "Why work when you can sing?" is teasing and light, never mean.

**Paragraph 8 — Come and sing (inviting, sunny):**
Gus hops over, full of sunshine. "The daisies are dancing!" is delighted.

**Paragraph 9 — Three more seeds (gentle, firm):**
Asha smiles — she is not cross, just steady. "Three more seeds" is small and sure. "But you may sing while I work" is kind.

**Paragraph 10 — Step, step, step (rhythmic, companionable):**
Gus plays, Asha marches. The steps are a soft little beat — work and music together.

**Paragraph 11 — The meadow turns gold (slow, golden):**
The season changes. "Softer" and "cooler" slow down. "Turned gold" is beautiful and a little wistful.

**Paragraph 12 — Frost (quiet, chilly):**
The daisies droop gently. Frost on the little yellow scarf is a small, cold surprise — say it softly.

**Paragraph 13 — Winter is coming (calm, ready):**
Asha is not afraid. "Winter is coming" is matter-of-fact and calm. The pantry door shutting is a cozy, safe sound.

**Paragraph 14 — Snow (hushed, soft):**
The wind is cold but the snow is soft — "like a blanket" is gentle. Everything goes quiet.

**Paragraph 15 — Gus shivers (small, cold):**
Gus is cold and hungry. His fiddle is quiet now — a little sad, but not scary. "Not one crumb" is very small.

**Paragraph 16 — Hop, hop, hop (hopeful, brave):**
Through the snow he hops — each hop a little brave. The knock is "very softly" — shy and polite.

**Paragraph 17 — Please (small, honest):**
Gus asks plainly. "My log is so cold" is honest and small. Let the listener feel how much he needs help.

**Paragraph 18 — Come in, Gus (warm, wide-open):**
Asha opens the door wide — this is the warm heart of the story. "Room by the fire, and soup enough for two" is generous and glowing.

**Paragraph 19 — The fireside tune (warm, sleepy):**
Seed soup, warm fire, a soft sleepy tune "just for her" — everything is cozy now. Slow down; let it glow.

**Paragraph 20 — Drifting off (slowest, sleepiest):**
The snow falls quiet outside. The yellow scarf around them both is the last cozy picture. "Warm and full and safe" is the sleepiest line of all — barely above a whisper.

**Goodnight — "Sleep now, little Asha." (tender, closing):**
The softest line. A kiss-goodnight for Asha — and for the listener too.

**"The end." (quiet, final):**
Gentle and complete. Then silence.
