# Read-aloud script — "Eli the Little Elephant and the Sleepy River"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story beside a quiet river at bedtime, your voice getting softer as Eli gets sleepier.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the last third of the story drift — Eli is heading toward sleep, and so is the listener.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Eli: soft and a little worried at first, then proud and calm.
- Mina the mouse: small, kind, encouraging.
- Bree the bluebird: bright and cheerful, never loud.
- Old Tomo the tortoise: slow, deep, and wise.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (the valley): paint the picture slowly — "curled like a silver ribbon", "wiggled whenever he was happy". Calm and cozy.
- Paragraph 2 (Eli's worry): small and sighing — "thump, thump, thump", then the sad whisper, "too big and too clumsy".
- Paragraph 3 (the picnic): warm and golden — peach and gold sky, berries, seeds, soft round buns, then the problem: wet, slippery stones.
- Paragraph 4 (Eli's doubt): hesitant — "what if I splash? What if I slip?" Let the worry hang for a beat.
- Paragraph 5 (Tomo's advice): slow and steady — "Slow feet make a steady elephant." This is the heart of the story; give it room.
- Paragraph 6 (the crossing): careful and quiet — soft steps, one by one, the basket held high and steady.
- Paragraph 7 (the wobble): a quick gasp — "Plop!" — then delight: "Well caught!" Keep it playful, never scary.
- Paragraph 8 (the spill): the quietest, saddest moment — seeds like tiny stars, drooping ears, the whispered "Too big. Too clumsy."
- Paragraph 9 (friends help): the warmest small moment — Mina's pat, "You did not drop the berries", and Bree's soft, slow song.
- Paragraph 10 (the far bank): gentle pride — "without a splash", moon rising over the sleepy river.
- Paragraph 11 (Tomo's lesson): slow and wise — "Being big is not the trouble, Eli. Hurrying is." Let it land, shown by the story, not preached.
- Paragraph 12 (the moonlight): dreamy and warm — shimmering water, crickets singing, Eli's happy wiggling trunk.
- Paragraph 13 (settling down): barely above a whisper — water whispering past.
- Final line: "Sleep now, little Eli." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
