# Read-aloud script — "The Little Grey Duckling and the Moonlit Lake"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story beside a quiet pond at bedtime, your voice getting softer as the duckling gets sleepier.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the last third of the story drift — the duckling is heading toward sleep, and so is the listener.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Mother Duck: soft and motherly.
- The yellow ducklings and the old hen: curious and chirpy, never cruel — more puzzled than mean.
- The frog: kind and sleepy, a low little song of a voice.
- The wild ducks: cheerful and welcoming.
- The swans: graceful and tender, like a lullaby.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (farmyard morning): paint the picture slowly — "sparkled like a mirror", "waited and waited". Calm and cozy.
- Paragraph 2 (the eggs hatch): playful pops — "pop, pop, pop" — then wonder at the big grey duckling.
- Paragraph 3 (the teasing): small and sad — the duckling hangs his head. Never mocking; the listener should feel for him.
- Paragraph 4 (slipping away): quiet and moonlit — a tiptoe feeling as he follows the winding stream.
- Paragraph 5 (the frog): the warmest small moment — "There is room for one more." Let the duckling's first smile land.
- Paragraph 6 (the wild ducks): a lift of joy — splashing, diving, "You swim beautifully!"
- Paragraph 7 (autumn and ice): slow right down — gold leaves, cold wind, the frozen pond. The loneliest, quietest moment.
- Paragraph 8 (spring morning): bright and rising — the wings feel "strong and wide", higher and higher.
- Paragraph 9 (the moonlit lake): hushed awe — the beautiful white swans, the whispered "Please don't chase me away."
- Paragraph 10 (the reflection): the wonder moment — "It was him!" with gentle delight, then soft pride: "in his own good time."
- Paragraph 11 (settling down): barely above a whisper — the water rocking "like a cradle".
- Final line: "Sleep now, little grey duckling." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
