# Read-aloud script — "Clover the Chipmunk and the Quiet Leaf Blanket"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story under a blanket at bedtime, your voice getting softer as Clover settles under her leaf blanket.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the autumn wood feel calm and golden, and let the night-time ending drift toward sleep.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Clover: kind, small and bright, a little wistful when her basket is empty.
- Squeak: tiny, sleepy, trembling at first, then soft with thanks.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (the oak tree): paint the picture slowly — "tall oak tree", "soft russet fur", "cheeks that puffed up like round acorns". A smile on "gathering leaves for winter".
- Paragraph 2 (the plan): hopeful and cosy — "softest, warmest leaves", "thick leaf blanket", "cold nights would feel cosy". Morning light lifts on "turned the leaves to gold".
- Paragraph 3 (gathering): gentle and touchable — "soft as velvet", "rustled like paper", "still warm from the sun". Whisper "This will be the middle".
- Paragraph 4 (Squeak): hushed and tender — "a tiny shiver", "curled up tight", "whiskers trembling". Let the listener feel how small and cold he is.
- Paragraph 5 (the gift): the warmest beat — "did not stop to think", "like a little blanket", "sleepy squeak of thanks". Let "slipped away quietly" land softly; Clover asks for nothing.
- Paragraph 6 (the wind): a little wilder, then sad — "wild and whistling", leaves tumbling "over the brook and into the brambles". Slow down on "her blanket was gone".
- Paragraph 7 (the empty basket): the quietest, saddest part — "cheeks drooped", "hugged her knees close". Pause on "she should have kept them after all" — no answer, just the feeling.
- Paragraph 8 (the return): brighten gently, one friend at a time — a rustle, then another; Squeak, Juniper, Briar. Let "soft and high" feel like a small happy surprise.
- Paragraph 9 (together): warm and settled — "layer upon layer", "curled beneath it", "wind sang softly outside". Let "all was warm" land gently, shown by the story, not preached.
- Final line: "Sleep now, little Clover." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
