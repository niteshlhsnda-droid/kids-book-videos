# Read-Aloud Script — Doctor Dolittle and the Talking Parrot

Warm, slow bedtime voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Let the wonder land; never rush the midnight rescue.

**Paragraph 1 — Opening (warm, cozy):**
Slow and soft, like settling into a little house with a big garden. Emphasize "Puddleby-on-the-Marsh" and "loved animals more than anything." A gentle smile in the voice on "tall black hat."

**Paragraph 2 — The animal household (cheerful, bustling):**
Light and happy, a little quicker as the animals are named — Dab-Dab, Jip, Gub-Gub, Too-Too, Polynesia. A fond lift on "nearly two hundred years old and very wise." The last line — "he could not understand a word they said back" — is soft and a little wistful.

**Paragraph 3 — Once a people's doctor (gentle, a touch sad):**
Slower. A small sigh in the voice on "one by one they stopped coming." Warm on "he liked his animals better than money." Then drop quieter for "But one thing made him sad" — let it hang a beat.

**Paragraph 4 — Dab-Dab's sore wing (worried, tender):**
Dab-Dab's quacks are small and worried — "Quack, quack, quack" in a soft, anxious voice. The doctor's care is gentle. The last line — "He wished with all his heart that he knew what she was trying to say" — is slow and longing.

**Paragraph 5 — Polynesia offers to teach (wise, kind):**
Polynesia is calm and wise, an old friend's voice. "I can teach you" is warm and sure. Emphasize "listen with your whole heart" — slow, golden, safe.

**Paragraph 6 — The evening lessons (patient, magical):**
A soft, lilting rhythm, like a lesson after supper. Each animal sound is playful — a little quack, a little bark, a little squeak. "Little by little, the sounds turned into words" — wonder rising, then settling.

**Paragraph 7 — Tap-tap-tap at the window (hushed, urgent):**
Hushed and moonlit. The tiny knocking — "tap-tap-tap" — is barely above a whisper. Nibbles is small and shivering, her squeak quick and urgent. "Old Prince, the farm horse, has a sore tooth" — worried, but hopeful.

**Paragraph 8 — The midnight rescue (brave, gentle):**
A quiet hurry — coat, hat, lantern, frosty night. Old Prince is low and tired. The doctor's hands are soft and careful. "There it was — the sore tooth" is a small discovery. The happy whinny at the end is warm and relieved.

**Paragraph 9 — Word travels fast (bright, joyful):**
Brighter and quicker — animals coming from all around! A cat, a rabbit, each with a little trouble. The doctor listens, helps, and every animal goes home smiling. Let the joy sparkle, then settle.

**Paragraph 10 — Thank-you gifts (warm, content):**
Cozy and grateful. Fresh eggs, honey, sweet blackberries — each gift is a small delight. "The little house was full of friends" is warm and full. "Never lonely again" is soft and sure.

**Paragraph 11 — Moonrise (sleepy, soft):**
Slow, drowsy, moonlit. The animals curl up, Polynesia tucks her head beneath her wing. Let the voice drift down like a yawn.

**Goodnight line:**
Very soft, almost a lullaby. A long pause before it. "Sleep now, Doctor Dolittle."
