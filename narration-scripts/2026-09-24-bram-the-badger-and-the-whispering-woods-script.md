# Read-aloud script — "Bram the Badger and the Whispering Woods"

**Overall tone:** Warm, hushed bedtime voice. Slow pace throughout — imagine the listener is already under the blanket, eyes getting heavy. Let the scary-sounding parts be gentle, never truly frightening.

**Opening (title + first paragraph):**
Speak softly, like the beginning of a secret. Pause ~1 second after the title.

**"Creeeak... Shhh-shhh-shhh..." (the woods sounds):**
Make these whispery and playful, not scary — like the trees are singing a lullaby. Stretch the sounds slightly.

**Bram's line — "What's out there?" (small whisper):**
Voice drops to a trembling whisper. Let the listener feel Bram's wobble.

**Hattie the hedgehog:**
Brighter and lighter than the narrator — cheerful, like a little friend popping up. Keep her warm, never bossy.

**The fear-shrinking lines ("I'm scared of the dark woods"):**
- First time: very small, shaky. Pause after. Then say "Pop!" with a soft, happy little pop sound — like a bubble bursting.
- Second time: a little louder, a little braver. Another soft "pop!"
- Third time ("The dark feels scary, but I'm brave enough..."): steady and proud. Hold the word "brave" a beat longer.

**"Creeeak... shhhh... creeeak... shhhh" (the lullaby walk):**
Slow, rocking rhythm — like swaying side to side. This is the sleepiest part; let the pace drift.

**The owl — "Hoo... hoo...":**
Deep, soft, slow. Two gentle hoots, then a tiny smile in the voice on "which sounded exactly like, 'Brave... you...'"

**Ending ("That night, Bram curled up..."):**
Slowest of all. Voice grows drowsier with each line. Let the final italic line — "Sweet dreams, little one..." — be barely above a whisper, fading out.

**Moral:**
Read it clearly but softly, like tucking the lesson in with the blanket.

**Pacing notes:**
- Pause ~0.8 s between paragraphs.
- Pause ~1.2 s before "That night..." (scene change to the burrow).
- Total read time should land around 3:00–3:30 at bedtime pace.
