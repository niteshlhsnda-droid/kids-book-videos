# Narration script — "Dara the Fawn and the Moon-Moth Dance"

Read-aloud directions for the warm narrator voice. Overall pace: slow and
lullaby-soft throughout; never rushed. A gentle smile should be audible in the
voice.

## Directions

- **Opening:** warm and bright, like opening a picture book — a little energy
  on "run — run, run, run!" but keep it cozy, not loud.
- **Moon moths arrive:** hushed wonder. Slow down for "wings like moonlit
  paper" and "slow, swirling circles." The whispered wish is the tiniest
  voice in the story.
- **The dash:** quick, playful rhythm for the chase; then drop the voice for
  "drifting away like smoke." A small, disappointed sigh on "They did not."
- **Tiptoe attempt:** tiptoe-quiet voice, careful and small. Let the pause
  before "thump-swish" hang, then a soft, sad droop on "His ears drooped
  down to his toes."
- **Marsh the toad:** a deeper, slower, warmer voice for Marsh — old and
  unhurried. Her line "They come to quiet" lands like a secret; hold it a
  beat.
- **The beetle:** comic timing — stillness builds, then a bright, springy
  "sproing!" followed by a groaning, flop-down "I can't be still."
- **Breathing beat:** the calmest section of the whole story. Very slow.
  Breathe audibly with Dara's "long, sleepy sigh." Each sound in the list —
  crickets, pond, wind — gets its own soft little pause. "And just… was."
  should feel like floating.
- **The moths return:** awe, unhurried. "First one. Then another." — each
  its own moment. Let the wonder bloom on "The most beautiful dance in the
  whole wide world."
- **Resolution:** melting, drowsy warmth. Slowest of all. Let the final
  insight land gently, then fade.
- **Goodnight line:** barely above a whisper — "Sleep now, little Dara." —
  then a soft, full stop for "The end."
