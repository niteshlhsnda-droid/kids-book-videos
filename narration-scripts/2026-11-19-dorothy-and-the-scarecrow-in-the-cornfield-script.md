# Narration Script — Dorothy and the Scarecrow in the Cornfield

**Voice:** warm, slow bedtime narrator (avocado_v2:MAI_01). Gentle pace throughout, like telling a story to a sleepy child. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Never rush the goodnight line.

## Directions

- **Title** — read warmly, with a little smile in the voice: "Dorothy and the Scarecrow in the Cornfield."
- **Opening (the yellow brick road)** — bright and sunny. "Tap, tap, tap" — light and quick, like little footsteps.
- **The cornfield** — curious and wondering. "That scarecrow is waving hello!" — delighted, like a child spotting a friend.
- **The talking scarecrow** — surprised and soft. "Oh, please!" — small and rustly. "A talking scarecrow!" — hushed with wonder.
- **Lifting him down** — warm and kind. "Thank you" — gentle and grateful. "Will you take me with you to see the Wizard?" — hopeful, a little shy.
- **Friends walk together** — bright and cheerful. "Friends walk together" — steady, like a promise. "Now you look quite splendid" — proud and pleased.
- **The road splits** — a little worried, a little sad. "Oh dear" — a soft sigh. "If only I had brains" — drooping and downhearted.
- **Listening for the river** — slow and thoughtful. "Wait" — a small pause, then dawning wonder. "You found the way all by yourself!" — delighted. "Perhaps my straw head is cleverer than I thought" — a proud little grin.
- **The little stream** — gentle and careful. "Toto did not like the splashing water one bit" — small and worried. "Safe and dry on the other side" — relieved and warm.
- **The haystack** — sleepy and golden. "Painting the sky pink and gold" — slow and dreamy. "And tomorrow we will walk to the Emerald City together" — soft, like a lullaby promise.
- **Goodnight in the hay** — the sleepiest part. "Humming a soft, rustly song" — almost a whisper. "Goodnight, dear Dorothy" — warm and rustly. "Sweet dreams" — barely above a whisper.
- **Goodnight line** — "Sleep now, little Dorothy." — the softest line of all, almost a whisper, like tucking a child in.
- **"The end."** — quiet, final, warm. Then silence.
