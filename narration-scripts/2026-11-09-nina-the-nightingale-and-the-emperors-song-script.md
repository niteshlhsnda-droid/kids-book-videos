# Narration Script — Nina the Nightingale and the Emperor's Song

Read slowly and warmly, like a bedtime story. Soft, gentle voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Let the nightingale's song feel tender; let the Emperor's sadness feel quiet, never frightening.

## Directions

**Paragraph 1 — The palace by the sea**
Warm, wonder-filled tone. Linger on "pink blossom trees" and "floating lotus flowers". A gentle, picture-painting pace.

**Paragraph 2 — Nina in the forest**
Softer now, intimate. "Small brown nightingale named Nina" — say her name fondly, like an old friend. Slow down on "sat on a low branch and sang."

**Paragraph 3 — Her song**
Dreamy and hushed. Pause after "like water over smooth stones." Let "Even the moon seemed to pause and listen" float — the quietest line so far.

**Paragraph 4 — The travellers tell the Emperor**
A little brighter, storyteller energy for the travellers' words. The Emperor's line — "A bird? In my own forest? Bring her to me at once." — curious and delighted, not angry.

**Paragraph 5 — The courtiers find Nina**
Playful, searching pace. Slow for "There was Nina, perched on a branch, singing." The courtier bows — polite, formal. Nina's reply is simple and brave: "Then I will come."

**Paragraph 6 — Nina sings in the hall**
Warm and glowing. The Emperor's line is full of wonder: "That was the most beautiful thing I have ever heard." Gentle smile in the voice.

**Paragraph 7 — The golden bird arrives**
Bright, tinkling excitement for the courtiers — quick and shiny. Then a small, quiet drop in tone on "everyone forgot all about Nina."

**Paragraph 8 — Nina flies away**
Sad but calm. Slow down. "No one came to listen" — soft, a little ache. "The music stopped" — a clear pause after it. The Emperor "could not sleep" — tired and heavy.

**Paragraph 9 — Nina returns**
The softest, most tender paragraph. Start almost in a whisper: "One still night..." Let the song drift in. The Emperor's whisper — "You came back, even though I forgot you" — is quiet and grateful. Nina's reply is gentle and sure.

**Paragraph 10 — Peaceful sleep**
Warm, settled, slow. The last line — "And every evening the Emperor opened his window wide" — is a soft, happy ending.

**Closing**
"Sleep now, little Nina." — very soft, like a lullaby. Then a gentle pause. "The end." — warm and final, with a smile.
