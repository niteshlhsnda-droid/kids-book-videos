# Read-aloud script — "Goldie and the Three Bears"

**Overall tone:** warm, gentle, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story under a blanket at bedtime, your voice getting softer as Goldie curls up in the tiny wee bed.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the green wood feel sunny and safe, and let the evening ending drift toward sleep.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- Papa Bear: a great big, slow, kind growl — never scary.
- Mama Bear: warm and middle-sized, motherly and calm.
- Baby Bear: tiny, wee, high and sweet, a little squeaky.
- Goldie: a small, curious, friendly girl's voice — sorry and sincere when she apologises.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (the cottage): paint the picture slowly — "green wood", "cottage with a red door", "great big bowl, a middle-sized bowl, and a tiny wee bowl". Let the three sizes roll like a little song.
- Paragraph 2 (the walk): easy and breezy — "too hot to eat", "walk in the wood while it cools". The cottage left "quiet" should feel peaceful, not empty.
- Paragraph 3 (Goldie arrives): curious and gentle — "golden curls", "tummy rumbled", "knocked twice". A wondering pause on "nobody answered".
- Paragraph 4 (the porridge): playful, with a lift on each tasting — "too hot", "too cold", "just right". Let "ate it all up" land with a small, satisfied smile.
- Paragraph 5 (the chairs): the same rising pattern — "far too high", "far too soft", "just right" — then a sharp little "crack!" and a soft gasp as the chair breaks.
- Paragraph 6 (the beds): sleepier and sleepier — "too hard", "too lumpy", "just right". Slow right down on "curled up and fell fast asleep".
- Paragraph 7 (the porridge discovery): three voices, big to tiny — Papa's growl, Mama's middle voice, Baby's squeak on "eaten it all up!" Keep it funny, not frightening.
- Paragraph 8 (the chairs discovery): the same three-voice pattern, ending hushed — "tiptoed" whispered, "fast asleep" barely breathed.
- Paragraph 9 (the apology): the warmest beat — Goldie's sorry words slow and sincere, Baby Bear's smile bright. Let "if you ask first" land gently, shown by the story, not preached. "Shared fresh porridge" warm and cosy.
- Paragraph 10 (going home): golden evening light — "evening stars", "waved from the red door", "Come in, Goldie!" joyful and welcoming.
- Final line: "Sleep now, little Goldie." — the softest line of all. Then a breath of silence before "The end."
