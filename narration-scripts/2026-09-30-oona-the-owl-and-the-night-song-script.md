# Read-Aloud Script — "Oona the Owl and the Night Song"

**Voice:** warm, soft, slow bedtime pace. **Mood:** quiet wonder.
**General pace:** unhurried; let each scene breathe. Pause a beat at line breaks.

---

**[Opening — soft, like settling in]**
"In the middle of a mossy old oak… lived a little owl named Oona."
*Pause. Let the picture of the old oak settle.*

**[Explaining who Oona is — gentle, tender]**
"Oona was new to the big oak, and she was very small, and she had never hooted a real owl hoot before."
"When the sun went down and the moon came up, the other owls along the branches puffed their chests and hooted —"
*Use a deep, round voice:* **"Hoo! Hoo!"**
*Then back to the narrator voice, smiling:* "Their voices were round and deep, like pebbles rolling down a hill."

**[Oona's first try — small, shy, endearing]**
"Oona opened her beak and tried. — *in a tiny squeak:* 'Huh… hoo…' — It came out all squeaky and small."
*Pause.* "She shut her beak quickly… and tucked her head under her wing."

**[The listening begins — slower, dreamier]**
"That night, as she sat quietly on her branch, she began to listen. Really listen."

**[The night sounds — layer them softly, a little playful]**
"First she heard the river, far below, humming its low, slow song over the stones."
"Then the leaves began to whisper to each other — *soft:* shh-shh, shh-shh — as the night breeze combed through them."
"A frog by the pond sang a wobbly little song — *in a wobbly little voice:* brrrp! brrrp! — and laughed at his own voice."

**[Curious, gentle]**
"Oona leaned out of her hollow and listened harder."
"There was the mouse, tip-tap-tipping between the roots."
"There was the moth, soft as a sigh, brushing past her ear."
"And somewhere high above, the wind sang through the very top branches like a flute."

**[A small realization — warm and quiet]**
"'Nobody is telling them to sing,' Oona thought. 'They just… do. In their own way.'"

**[Her brave try — tender, a little hopeful]**
"She opened her beak again — very gently this time — and let out the tiniest sound: — *in a small soft hoot:* 'hoo.' — It was small, and it was soft, and it floated down through the leaves like a dandelion puff."

**[The night answers — warm]**
"The frog stopped his brrrp for a moment and blinked up at her. The mouse paused his tip-tapping."
"Then the river hummed a little louder, as if it liked her sound, and the leaves whispered back — *soft:* shh-shh."

**[The night song — full and happy, but still gentle]**
"So Oona tried once more: — *a little stronger:* 'hoo… hoo.' — A little stronger. A little braver."
"The night breeze wrapped around her like a warm wing, and her soft hoots joined the river's hum and the leaves' whisper and the frog's wobbly song, and together they made the most beautiful night song the old oak had ever heard."

**[Closing — very soft, almost a whisper]**
"When the moon had climbed to the very top of the sky, Oona curled her soft feathers around herself and listened to the night song one last time."
"Every voice was different… and every voice belonged."

*Pause, then the goodnight — the softest line of all:*
"Sleep now, little Oona."
"The end. 🌙"
