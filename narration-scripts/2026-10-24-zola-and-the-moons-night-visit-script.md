# Read-aloud script — "Zola and the Moon's Night Visit"

**Overall tone:** hushed, warm, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story under a blanket with a flashlight. Smile gently while reading.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Never rush the moon's lines — they should feel like they float.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Let sentences melt into each other.
- The grandmother: older and tender, a gentle whisper.
- The moon: slow, silvery, and kind — like cool water over stones. Pause before each of her lines.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1: paint the picture slowly — "still blue lake", "color of a ripe mango". Lingering, dreamy.
- Paragraph 2 ("Little Zola..."): soften on "small and shy", "like a seed in a pod" — say it as if tucking the words in.
- Paragraph 3 (grandmother's line): whisper it. A tiny pause before "so sing her something true."
- Paragraph 4: build a little wonder — "something wonderful happened" with a rising glow; "slow as a falling feather" very slow; pause after "The whole village gasped."
- Paragraph 5 (moon's greeting): pause before speaking. Speak the moon's words gently, evenly spaced.
- Paragraph 6: brighter, like a festival — "danced", "happy hearts", "shine like a mirror". Then settle.
- Paragraph 7: quieter again — "at the very edge", "her voice was still hiding". Lonely but safe.
- Paragraph 8: the heart of the story. "Closer than anyone had ever seen her" — awe. The moon's two lines: warmest part of the whole read. Pause between them.
- Paragraph 9: start very quietly on the hum; grow gradually — "drums picked up the tune", "children clapped" — to a soft joyful swell, then land on "the happiest song the lake had ever heard" with a smile.
- Paragraph 10: slow down again — "learning the song by heart" tenderly; "rose slowly back into the sky" drifting upward; "everyone waved goodbye" soft and sleepy.
- Paragraph 11: the calmest of all. "Hummed it back, silver and soft" — nearly a whisper. "A gift big enough to light up the sky" — let it glow, then fade.
- Closing line "Sleep now, little Zola.": the softest line in the story — a true tuck-in. Pause. Then "The end." barely above a breath.

**Breathing:** leave two full beats of silence at the start and three at the end so the music can carry the listener in and out.
