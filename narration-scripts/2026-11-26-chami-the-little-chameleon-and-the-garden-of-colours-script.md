# Read-Aloud Script — Chami the Little Chameleon and the Garden of Colours

Warm, slow bedtime voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Let the colours change slowly in the listener's mind; never rush the singing.

**Paragraph 1 — Opening (warm, sunny):**
Slow and soft, like settling into a sunny garden. Emphasize "soft grass and tall flowers" and "a little chameleon named Chami." A gentle smile in the voice on "big round eyes" and "tiny curled tail."

**Paragraph 2 — Colours of feeling (wonder-filled, gentle):**
Each colour gets its own small pause — leafy green for calm, soft pink for happy, warm gold for wonderful. Let the listener picture each one.

**Paragraph 3 — Garden Singing Day (bright, then fluttery):**
"Garden Singing Day" is bright and exciting. Then slow down on "her tummy felt fluttery" — a soft little wobble in the voice. "Worried grey" is quiet and small.

**Paragraph 4 — A small, squeaky worry (soft, tender):**
Chami whispers. "My voice is too small" is shy and small; "And squeaky" is even smaller. "What if everyone laughs?" is a real little worry — tender, never scary.

**Paragraph 5 — Hiding (hushed, peeking):**
Slow right down. "Hid behind a big green leaf" is hushed. "Peeked out with one round eye" is shy and sweet — a tiny pause before it.

**Paragraph 6 — Bella arrives (warm, kind):**
Bella is soft blue and gentle. "There you are" is warm and glad. "I saved you a dewdrop" is kind and generous; "the sweetest one" is a small smile.

**Paragraph 7 — A dewdrop, unhurried (calm, sweet):**
Cool and sweet, slow and easy. "Bella did not ask her to hurry" is warm and steady. The humming is slow and gentle. Let the grey soften — a quiet little change.

**Paragraph 8 — The singing begins (playful, musical):**
The bee's note is low and buzzy; the ladybird's beat is tiny and neat. Then the sparrow — his wobbly note wobbles "all the way up and all the way down again," playful and wobbly.

**Paragraph 9 — Everyone smiles (warm, delighted):**
"Everyone smiled" is warm and easy. The sparrow smiles too. The bee buzzing along is a happy little joining-in.

**Paragraph 10 — A small, brave feeling (brave, breathy):**
Pause on "a small, brave feeling, right in the middle." "She took a deep breath" is a real breath — slow. "Stepped out from behind her leaf" is brave and bright.

**Paragraph 11 — Chami sings (glowing, joyful):**
Her voice is small and a little bit squeaky — and it is hers; say that with pride. Her colours bloom one by one — pink for happy, gold for brave, green for calm — "like a tiny rainbow sitting on a leaf" glows.

**Paragraph 12 — The garden sings together (full, warm):**
Full and warm, like an evening filling up. Each sound fits — the wobbly notes, the buzzy hum, the fluttering wings. "Like the colours of a rainbow" is soft and glowing.

**Paragraph 13 — Goodnight (slow, sleepy):**
Very slow now, sleepy and soft. The moon rises; Chami curls up. Her colours soften "one by one" — let each one drift away. "Sleep now, little Chami" is the softest line of all. "The end" is a gentle close, like tucking in a blanket.
