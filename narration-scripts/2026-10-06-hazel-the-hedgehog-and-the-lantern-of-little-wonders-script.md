# Read-aloud script: Hazel the Hedgehog and the Lantern of Little Wonders

Tone: soft, warm, and wonder-filled. Imagine telling this to a sleepy child with the lights low. Slow down a little more with each paragraph.

---

**Opening (burrow under the hazel bush):** Gentle and cozy, like tucking someone in. Smile in your voice on "loved to look at everything twice."

**Lantern walk (paras 2–3):** Light, playful curiosity. On Hazel's whispered line — *"Let's see what we can see"* — drop to a real whisper.

**Dewdrop & leaf-boat (paras 4–5):** Full of quiet marvel. Pause before each "twice" reveal — *"A tiny moon"* … *"A leaf-boat"* — let each land like a small gift.

**Brin arrives (paras 6–7):** Brin is gruff but not scary — a low, tired grumble. Hazel stays bright and patient. Let Brin's smile land gently: "His grumble softened into a small smile."

**The candle goes out (paras 8–9):** *Poof!* is a small, surprised puff — not a scare. Then the dark: slower, lower, almost a lullaby.

**Velvet blanket (paras 10–11):** The heart of the story. Warm, wide-eyed wonder. Pause after "It's a great velvet blanket" so the picture can settle. *"Every single one"* — tender certainty.

**Walk home (paras 12–13):** Happy and sleepy at once. Brin's laugh is a warm, rumbly chuckle — keep it soft, bedtime-safe.

**Bedtime close (paras 14–16):** Slow right down. Nearly a whisper by *"waiting to be looked at twice."* Then *"Sleep now, little Hazel."* — the sleepiest line in the whole story, soft as a breath. A gentle beat of silence. *"The end."* — warm and final.
