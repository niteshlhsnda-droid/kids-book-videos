# Read-aloud script — "Gigi the Giraffe and the Starlit Waterhole"

Voice: warm, gentle bedtime narrator. Slow, unhurried pace throughout. Pause briefly at each paragraph break.

- **Opening (soft, cozy):** "On the wide, golden savanna…" — gentle and warm, like tucking a blanket in. Linger on "small, fuzzy horns that wiggled when she laughed."
- **Evening and the stars (dreamy, hushed):** Slow down on "twinkled high above her" and "with all her heart." A small, wistful sigh on "If only I were taller."
- **The shortest giraffe (gentle, sympathetic):** "Her mother reached the top leaves" — a little lift, then softer on "the stars stayed far, far away."
- **The rock (playful, then a soft sigh):** Brighten on "She climbed onto a smooth, flat rock" and stretch "up, up, up" a little higher each time. "Still too far" — a small, sleepy sigh.
- **Zuri the oxpecker (bright, friendly):** Lively and quick for Zuri fluttering down. "What are you reaching for?" — a cheerful chirp. "Ask him. He knows everything about the sky" — warm and encouraging.
- **Grandpa Giraffe (deep, warm, wise):** Slow and deep for Grandpa's chuckle. "The stars are far away for everyone — even for me" — kind and steady. "They come closer than you think" — a gentle secret, said with a smile.
- **The waterhole (wonder, hushed):** Slow right down. "The water lay still as glass" — soft and still. A hushed gasp on "The whole night sky was in the waterhole!"
- **They came down to me (joy, quiet):** "Oh!" — a breath of delight. "They came down to me!" — warm wonder, not loud.
- **The whole herd (warm, content):** "Soon the whole herd came" — cozy and glad. "Close and bright and beautiful" — said slowly, like watching the light.
- **Goodnight (sleepy, very soft):** Slow right down. "I didn't need to be taller" — soft and sure. "Dreaming of a whole sky in a waterhole" — barely above a breath. Then the closing line, "Sleep now, little Gigi," softer still, like a lullaby.
