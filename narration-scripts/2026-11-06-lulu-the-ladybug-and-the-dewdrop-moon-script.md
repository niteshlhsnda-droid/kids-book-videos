# Read-aloud script — "Lulu the Ladybug and the Dewdrop Moon"

Voice: warm, gentle bedtime narrator. Slow, unhurried pace throughout. Pause briefly at each paragraph break.

- **Opening (soft, cozy):** "In a quiet garden…" — gentle and warm, like tucking a blanket in. Linger on "seven round black spots."
- **Evening and the moon (dreamy, hushed):** Slow down on "soft and blue" and "round and gold." Say "to say hello" with a small smile in the voice.
- **The dewdrop moon (wonder, hushed):** A soft gasp of delight on "silver rain." "No bigger than a berry" — warm amazement, said slowly.
- **"My moon" (quiet, a little possessive):** "My moon," whispered — small and secretive, not greedy, just a child holding something precious.
- **Annie the ant (bright, friendly):** Lively and quick for Annie hurrying along. "Oh!" — a surprised little lift. "It's the prettiest thing I've ever seen" — full of wonder.
- **The wobbling drop (a little worried, never scary):** Quicken just slightly as the leaf trembles. "What if it rolled away?" — soft worry. Then quieter on "the little moon grew dim."
- **Benny the beetle (gentle discovery):** "Why is it going dark?" — small and curious. Pause, then a warm lift as the light returns, "brighter than before."
- **Come and see (warm, opening up):** "Come and see," said Lulu — generous and glad. Let the light "spill" slowly across the leaf, over the clover, all the way to the wall.
- **The whole garden glows (glowing, content):** Warm and satisfied. "It was bigger, because everyone could see it" — the heart of the story, said softly and slowly.
- **Goodnight (sleepy, very soft):** Slow right down. "Goodnight, little moon" — a whisper. "See you tomorrow" — barely above a breath. Then the closing line, "Sleep now, little Lulu," softer still, like a lullaby.
