# Narration Script — Willie Winkie and the Sleepy Town

**Voice:** warm, hushed bedtime narrator. Slow, soft, and steady throughout — this is a story that should make listeners drowsy.

## Scene-by-scene directions

**Title + opening rhyme** — Light, playful, sing-song lilt for the rhyme itself; smile in the voice. Then a beat of silence before "Well —".

**Paragraphs 3–5 (can't sleep / nightgown / tiptoeing out)** — Lower the voice; whisper-quiet on dialogue. Long pause after "creep, creep, creep" — let the tiptoeing land.

**Paragraphs 6–7 (dreaming town / baker's window)** — Dreamy, drifting tone. "Breathing slow and deep" — slow down markedly; a satisfied, warm "Good."

**Paragraphs 8–10 (ducklings)** — Tender and gentle; a tiny gasp of delight on "rustled." The lantern swing — hushed wonder.

**Paragraphs 11–13 (lantern goes out)** — Quickening slightly with the gust, a soft gasp on "Puff!" and "Oh!" Then stillness. Long pause before "Then he looked up."

**Paragraphs 14–16 (moon and stars)** — Awe and relief; brighter warmth. "Thank you" — sincere, quiet gratitude.

**Paragraphs 17–19 (hilltop song)** — The night-watching song: half-sung, very soft, like a lullaby. Pause between the two lines of the song.

**Paragraphs 20–22 (home / mother / falling asleep)** — Growing drowsier; each sentence a little slower than the last. The mother's line — a kiss in the voice. Final bedtime sentence: barely above a whisper.

**Closing** — "Sleep now, little Willie." — the warmest, slowest line of all. One full beat of silence. "The end." — soft, final, content.

**Do not:** rush the quiet moments, sharpen any consonant into a startle, or add a moral — the story's lesson lives in the hush, not in words.
