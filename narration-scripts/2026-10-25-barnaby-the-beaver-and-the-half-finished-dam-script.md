# Read-aloud script — "Barnaby the Beaver and the Half-Finished Dam"

**Overall tone:** warm, drowsy, lullaby-slow. Imagine telling this story beside a quiet river at bedtime, your voice getting softer as the story goes on.

**Pacing:** slow and even, with a soft pause at every paragraph break. Let the last third of the story drift — Barnaby is getting sleepy, and so is the listener.

**Voice:**
- Narration: warm, low, soothing. Gentle and unhurried throughout.
- The firefly: tiny, twinkly, a soft whisper.
- Olive the otter: kind and easy, like a friend sliding up beside you.
- Barnaby: small and earnest at first, then sleepier and sleepier.

**Emphasis & pauses:**
- Paragraph 1 (riverbank introduction): paint the picture slowly — "slow, silver river", "pat-pat-pat on the mud". Playful but calm.
- Paragraph 2 (the dam): steady, industrious energy — "one after another", "patting each one snug".
- Paragraph 3 (evening colours): slow down — "golden, and then pink, and then a soft, sleepy blue". Let the colours melt.
- Paragraph 4 (firefly): whisper it, twinkly and light. A tiny pause before "The stars are coming out."
- Paragraph 5 (one more stick): Barnaby's voice, a big sleepy yawn on "Just one more stick."
- Paragraph 6 (splop!): a small splash of surprise — "splop!" bright, then quiet again. "spinning slowly like a little boat" very slow.
- Paragraph 7 (Olive arrives): warm and kind. "You look sleepy, Barnaby" — gentle, no teasing.
- Paragraph 8 (the river isn't in a hurry): the heart of the story. Slow right down. "slow and steady" — say it like the river itself.
- Paragraph 9 (watching the water): softest part so far — "shhhh" hushed, "silver path on the water" dreamy, "tiny lanterns" twinkling.
- Paragraph 10 (paws telling him): a small, happy realisation. Warm, not preachy.
- Paragraph 11 (tucking in): very sleepy now. "leaf blanket up to his chin" — tuck the words in.
- Paragraph 12 (the dam waits): quiet and peaceful. "half-finished and perfectly fine" — gentle and content.
- Goodnight line ("Sleep now, little Barnaby."): the softest line of all. Barely above a whisper. Then a beat of silence.
- "The end.": whispered, final, like blowing out a candle.
