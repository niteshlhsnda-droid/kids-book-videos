# Narration Script — Wisp the Little Cloud and the Thirsty Garden

Read slowly and warmly, like a bedtime story. Soft, gentle voice throughout. Pause briefly between paragraphs. Let Wisp's fear feel small and tender, never frightening; let the rain feel soft and kind.

## Directions

**Paragraph 1 — Wisp above the valley**
Warm, dreamy, picture-painting pace. Linger on "curled like a silver ribbon" and "light as a feather". Wisp's name — say it fondly, like an old friend.

**Paragraph 2 — The cottage garden**
Bright and cheerful, a little garden in full colour. Slow down on "roses glowed the colour of sunset" and "tall sunflowers turned their faces to the sun". Ana sings — light and happy.

**Paragraph 3 — The long dry summer**
The tone dries out a little. Shorter, quieter sentences: "The river grew thin. The well ran low." Slow and sad on "their leaves hanging down like tired hands."

**Paragraph 4 — Ana's last water**
Very soft and worried. Ana's whisper — "Please hold on, little flowers" — is tender, almost a secret. "Rain will come soon, I hope" — hopeful, not certain.

**Paragraph 5 — Wisp is afraid**
Hushed and small. Wisp's worries come one at a time, each a little quieter: "What if I rain too hard?" … "What if the garden does not want my rain?" Let the fear feel real but gentle.

**Paragraph 6 — The great grey cloud**
Bigger, louder energy for the storm cloud — rumbly and splashy. Then a quick drop in tone: Wisp trembles. "I will keep my rain inside" — small and determined.

**Paragraph 7 — The sparrow's song**
Sad at first — Ana's sad eyes, the drooping flowers — then the sparrow's song lifts the mood, sweet and hopeful. "Braver than fear" — warm, with a little smile in the voice.

**Paragraph 8 — The old oak whispers**
Slow, wise, rustling. The oak's words are the heart of the story — say them like a kind secret: "Rain softly, a little at a time, and that will be enough." Pause after "enough".

**Paragraph 9 — The gentle rain**
Soft and magical. "Drip, drip, drip" — slow, each drop its own tiny sound. "Light as a lullaby" — the softest line so far.

**Paragraph 10 — The garden drinks**
Joyful and relieved. Ana laughs — let the laugh be real. The flowers lift, open, stand tall — a little brighter with each one. "Light as a feather once more" — light and happy.

**Paragraph 11 — Rainbow and rest**
Warm and glowing. The rainbow is pink and gold and soft blue — paint it slowly. Ana's wave and "Thank you, little cloud!" are bright and grateful. Wisp drifts up to the moon — slow, sleepy, peaceful.

**Closing**
"Sleep now, little Wisp." — very soft, like a lullaby. Then a gentle pause. "The end." — warm and final, with a smile.
