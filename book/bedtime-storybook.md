## Story 1: Willa the Bunny and the Moonflower

*A bedtime story for ages 4–7 · about 2 minutes to read aloud*

Willa was a small brown bunny with long, wiggly ears, and she lived in a cozy burrow under the old apple tree. In her garden grew one tiny moonflower bud — her very favorite thing in the whole world. Every morning she whispered, "Please open today?" And every morning the bud stayed shut, snug as a button. Willa huffed, Willa puffed, and her ears went all sideways with impatience.

"Be patient, little one," said Mama Rabbit, pouring Willa a cup of warm chamomile tea. "Some wonderful things take their own sweet time." So Willa tried very hard to wait. She counted every snail in the garden (twelve!), sang a song about carrots, and taught a ladybug to do somersaults.

That evening the sky turned the color of blackberry jam, and the first stars peeked out one by one. Willa tiptoed to the flower bed in her polka-dot pajamas, letting out the biggest yawn. And there it was — the moonflower was opening, petal by petal, glowing soft and silvery in the dark!

"Ohhh," whispered Willa, too amazed to feel sleepy anymore. The flower smelled like honey and moonbeams, and it seemed to wink right at her. She curled up beside it and smiled the sleepiest smile. Mama was right — waiting had made the surprise even sweeter.

---

*💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: patience makes sweet surprises even sweeter.*

## Story 2: A Blanket of Stars

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Lolo the lamb left her blanket at Grandma's house. It was her favorite blanket. Soft, yellow, smelling of lavender. Without it, her bed felt cold. She felt sad and chilly.

Her mother tucked her in with a spare blanket. It was warm enough. But it was not the same. Lolo tossed and turned. She missed her blanket.

She looked out the window. The night sky was full of stars. They twinkled and shimmered. They looked soft, like tiny lights on velvet.

"I wish I had a blanket made of stars," whispered Lolo. She felt wistful.

Just then, something magical happened. One star twinkled brighter. Then another. And another. They began to drift down, slow as snowflakes. They landed softly on her bed.

Lolo felt amazed. The stars gathered around her. They wove themselves together. They made a blanket of pure starlight. It glowed warm and golden.

"Warm and bright, through the night, stars will hold you till morning light," sang the stars.

Lolo pulled the star blanket up to her chin. It was the coziest blanket ever. It was warm like sunshine. It smelled like bedtime. Lolo felt happy and safe.

"Warm and bright, through the night, stars will hold you till morning light," sang the stars again.

Her eyelids grew heavy. The starlight glowed softly. It was like sleeping inside a gentle dream. Lolo's breathing grew slow.

"Warm and bright, through the night, stars will hold you till morning light," sang the stars one last time.

Lolo fell fast asleep. She dreamed she was floating among the stars. They tucked her in with clouds. They sang her their twinkling song.

In the morning, the star blanket was gone. Sunlight streamed through the window. But Lolo felt warm and rested. On her pillow lay a note: "Love, the Stars."

And guess what? Grandma brought her yellow blanket back that very day. But Lolo would never forget her blanket of stars.

Sleep now, little lamb. The stars are watching over you. They will keep you warm until morning.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Comfort can be found in unexpected places when you wish with an open heart.*

## Story 3: Andy the Ant and the Fallen Crumbs

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Andy was a tiny red ant.
He lived in a hill under the oak tree.
Every day, Andy carried crumbs home.
Big crumbs.
Small crumbs.
Sweet cake crumbs were the best.
One afternoon, Andy found a giant cookie crumb.
It was the biggest crumb ever!
He lifted it onto his back.
He marched toward home.
Step step step.
Then a gust of wind blew.
Whoosh!
The crumb tumbled off his back.
It broke into ten tiny pieces.
They scattered everywhere.
Andy felt dismayed.
His shoulders drooped.
"My big crumb is ruined," he said.
He felt sad.
All that work for nothing.

His friend Bella the beetle crawled over.
She saw the scattered pieces.
She saw Andy's sad face.
"What happened, Andy?" asked Bella.
"The wind broke my crumb," said Andy.
"I cannot carry ten pieces alone."
Bella smiled her beetle smile.
"You do not have to," she said.
"Many small hands make light work."
"Many small hands make light work."
Andy repeated it softly.
"Many small hands make light work."
It became their teamwork chant.
"Many small hands make light work."

Bella called her beetle brothers.
Andy called his ant sisters.
Soon ten friends stood in a line.
Each friend picked up one piece.
Lift!
They marched together.
Step step step.
"Many small hands make light work!" they chanted.
The line moved like a parade.
Left, right, left, right.
They reached the ant hill.
They piled the pieces inside.
The cookie crumb was saved!
Andy felt grateful.
He felt happy.
His friends had turned a mishap into fun.

Can you march like an ant?
Pick up an pretend crumb: lift!
March in place: step step step!
Now put it down gently.
That evening, the ants held a crumb feast.
Everyone got a piece of cookie.
Crunch crunch crunch.
Bella got the biggest piece.
"For the best helper," said Andy.
They told stories.
They laughed.
The moon rose over the oak tree.
Andy felt warm inside.
Helping friends was the sweetest crumb of all.

At bedtime, Andy curled in his cozy tunnel.
Bella rested in the moss nearby.
"Goodnight, crumb saver," whispered Bella.
"Goodnight," yawned Andy.
He dreamed of cookie crumb parades.
Step step step.
The earth was soft and dark.
The hill hummed a quiet song.
And Andy slept, snug and happy.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Friends can turn a spill into a celebration.*

His soft blanket.

"Papa and I are here," she said.

He hung the picture on the wall.

Slowly, it felt a little like home.

That afternoon, Benny explored.

He found a tunnel to the meadow.

He found a neighbor mole.

"I am new too."

Benny felt happy.

He felt brave.

At bedtime, Benny snuggled in his nook.

His blanket smelled like home.

## Story 5: Billy's Bug Garden

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Billy had a small garden behind his house.

One morning he found tiny holes in his lettuce leaves.

"Oh no!" he said. "Something is eating my garden!"

He felt upset.

He looked closer.

A green caterpillar was munching away.

Munch, munch, munch.

Billy felt angry at first.

Then he felt curious.

"Why are you eating my lettuce, little caterpillar?" he asked.

The caterpillar just kept munching.

Billy looked under another leaf.

A ladybug was there, red with black spots.

"Hello, ladybug," said Billy.

The ladybug crawled onto his finger.

Tickle, tickle, tickle.

Billy giggled.

He felt his anger melt away.

Then he saw an ant carrying a crumb.

Then a bee humming over the flowers.

Then a worm wiggling in the soil.

"My garden is full of bugs," said Billy.

"Full, full, full," he said.

That was three times he said it.

He felt surprised.

He ran to ask his big sister.

"Are bugs bad for gardens?" he asked.

His sister laughed kindly.

"Some bugs munch leaves," she said.

"But ladybugs eat the munchers."

"Bees help flowers grow."

"Worms make the soil soft."

Billy felt thoughtful.

Maybe the bugs were not enemies.

Maybe they were neighbors.

He had an idea.

He planted extra lettuce at the edge of the garden.

"This row is for the caterpillars," he said.

"And this row is for me."

The caterpillar seemed to nod.

Munch, munch, went the caterpillar, happily now.

Billy watched the ladybug fly to a leaf.

He watched the bee visit every flower.

He watched the worm dive into the dark soil.

"Goodnight, bugs," whispered Billy as the sun set.

"Goodnight, goodnight, goodnight," he whispered.

That was three times again.

He felt peaceful.

The garden hummed softly in the evening air.

Sleep now, little gardener.

Dream of ladybugs with polka-dot wings.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing a little can make room for everyone.*

## Story 7: Bobby's Bubble Bay

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Bobby the beaver did not want a bath. "Baths are boring," he said. He felt stubborn. He crossed his arms and pouted.

His father smiled. "Then let us not take a bath," he said. "Let us sail to Bubble Bay instead."

"Bubble Bay?" asked Bobby. He felt curious.

Father filled the tub with warm water. He poured in bubble soap. Soon the tub was full of foam. It looked like a bay full of clouds. Father placed a toy whale in the water.

"Welcome to Bubble Bay, Captain Bobby!" said Father.

Bobby climbed in. The water was warm and soft. Bubbles floated everywhere. The toy whale bobbed on the waves. Bobby felt his stubbornness melting away.

"Splish, splash, bubbles pop, worries float and never stop," sang Father.

Bobby splished with his paws. Splash! Water droplets sparkled. He made the whale dive under the bubbles. Then it popped up with a whoosh. Bobby giggled.

"Splish, splash, bubbles pop, worries float and never stop," sang Father again.

Bobby scooped up a handful of bubbles. He blew them gently. They floated up, up, up. Each bubble carried a tiny worry away. Pop! There went the grumpy mood. Pop! There went the wiggles.

Bobby washed his ears. He washed his tail. The warm water felt wonderful. He did not feel stubborn anymore. He felt relaxed and happy.

"Splish, splash, bubbles pop, worries float and never stop," sang Father one last time.

Father wrapped Bobby in a fluffy towel. It was warm from the dryer. Bobby felt like a cozy burrito. His fur was clean and soft. He smelled like bubble soap.

He put on his pajamas. They felt extra soft on clean skin. He climbed into bed. His body felt warm and tingly.

Sleep now, little beaver. Bubble Bay has washed your day away. Dream of sailing warm and foamy seas.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A warm bath can wash away a grumpy mood along with the day's dust.*

## Story 8: Bobo Learns to Listen

---

Bobo was a baby bat.

He had very big ears.

But he did not use them well.

He talked and talked.

He hardly ever listened.

One evening, Mama said, "Listen, Bobo."

But Bobo was too busy chattering.

He missed the owl's soft hoot.

Mama said, "I see you love to talk."

Bobo nodded proudly.

"But listening is a superpower too," Mama said.

She taught him the listening game.

"Quiet ears hear the world sing."

"Quiet ears hear the world sing," she said again.

"Quiet ears hear the world sing," she said one more time.

Bobo wanted to try.

He closed his mouth.

He opened his big ears wide.

At first, he heard nothing.

Then, very softly, he heard it.

Hoot, hoot.

"The owl!" he whispered.

Then he heard more.

Drip, drop, went the stream.

Rustle, rustle, went the leaves.

Buzz, buzz, went a sleepy bee.

Bobo felt amazed.

His ears felt tingly.

His heart felt happy.

"I can hear everything!" he said.

Mama smiled.

"You listened carefully," she said.

"Listening shows we care."

That night, they flew together.

Bobo listened to the wind.

Whoosh, whoosh.

He listened to the crickets.

Chirp, chirp.

He listened to Mama's wings.

Flap, flap, beside him.

He felt close to everything.

Listening made the night bigger.

And more beautiful.

Back in the cave, Bobo snuggled upside down.

He listened to Mama's lullaby.

Soft and low.

He felt happy.

He felt calm.

The cave was quiet.

Bobo closed his eyes.

He was a good listener.

He was loved.

Goodnight, Bobo.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Listening carefully helps us hear the world — and shows we care.*

Sniff sniff sniff.

Golden and dripping.
Bramble's tummy rumbled.

Too high!
He stood on his tiptoes.
Still too high!
He jumped.
Boing!
Missed!
Bramble felt frustrated.
He plopped down in the grass.
"I am too small," he grumbled.
He felt grumpy.
He felt stuck.

His friend Poppy the porcupine waddled by.
She saw Bramble's sad face.
"What is wrong, Bramble?" asked Poppy.
"The honey is too high," said Bramble.

Whoosh.

Whoosh.

Toot.

Hoo.

## Story 102: Why the Sky Blushes

---

She felt curious.

"Mama, why does the sky blush?" she asked.

"Blush?" laughed Mama.

"Yes," said Cara. "It turns pink like cheeks."

Mama smiled.

"Let me tell you the sky's secret."

Long ago, the sky was always blue.

Blue in the morning, blue at noon, blue at night.

The sky felt bored.

"I want to wear other colors," said the sky.

So the sun offered to help.

"When I go to sleep," said the sun, "I will paint you."

Every evening, as the sun sank low, it painted the sky.

First pink.

Then orange.

Then purple.

"Pretty, pretty, pretty," whispered the sky.

That was three times it whispered it.

The sky felt delighted.

Cara loved this story.

But she had another question.

"Why does it blush only at bedtime?" she asked.

"It paints the sky so we know it is time to rest."

Cara watched the sky that evening.

The sun dipped lower.

The clouds turned pink at the edges.

Then peachy.

Then soft purple.

Cara felt calm.

The whole world seemed to slow down.

The flowers closed up.

The shadows grew long and soft.

"Goodnight, sun," whispered Cara.

She felt sleepy.

The pink faded to lavender.

The lavender faded to deep blue.

Sleep now, little watcher.

Dream of sunsets that blush just for you.

## Story 103: Zara and the Tiny Ladybird

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Zara was a small girl, six years old.

She loved playing in the garden.

One morning, she saw a ladybird.

It was tiny and red with black dots.

It crawled on a green leaf.

Zara felt excited.

She wanted to pick it up.

She reached out fast.

The ladybird tumbled off the leaf.

Zara felt worried.

Her heart beat fast.

"Did I hurt it?" she whispered.

Her grandma knelt beside her.

She said, "I see you feel worried."

Zara nodded.

"It is okay to feel worried," Grandma said.

"It shows you care."

Grandma showed her how.

"Gentle hands keep little friends safe."

"Gentle hands keep little friends safe," she said again.

"Gentle hands keep little friends safe," she said one more time.

Zara held out her hand flat.

Very still.

Very soft.

The ladybird crawled onto her finger.

Tickle, tickle.

Zara giggled quietly.

"It tickles!" she whispered.

She watched the tiny legs.

She watched the shiny wings.

"You are so small," she said.

"And so brave."

She lowered her hand to a flower.

The ladybird crawled off.

It waved its wings.

Then it flew away.

"Bye, little friend," Zara said.

She felt happy.

She had been gentle.

Being gentle felt good.

That afternoon, Zara was gentle with everything.

She stroked the cat softly.

She watered the flowers carefully.

She spoke in a quiet voice.

Grandma smiled.

"You have gentle hands and a gentle heart," she said.

At bedtime, Zara snuggled under her blanket.

She thought about the ladybird.

Flying somewhere in the night.

She felt worried earlier.

Now she felt peaceful.

The garden crickets sang softly.

Chirp, chirp, hush.

Zara closed her eyes.

She was gentle.

She was loved.

Goodnight, Zara.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Gentle hands and a gentle heart keep small creatures safe.*
