# Rapunzel

*A much-loved folk tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a green valley full of flowers, there stood a tall, tall tower.

The tower was so tall that its top touched the soft white clouds. At the very top was one tiny window, and in that little room lived a girl named Rapunzel.

Rapunzel had long, golden hair that flowed all the way from her window to the ground, like a golden waterfall.

Every day, Rapunzel sat by her window and sang about the sun, and the flowers, and the blue sky.

The tower was looked after by Dame Greta, a grumpy old keeper with a frown and a loud voice. But her heart was not unkind. She brought Rapunzel her meals and her books, and always said, "Stay safe up there, my dear."

But Rapunzel wished she could come down and play in the valley, run in the grass, and meet the children of the village!

One fine evening, a kind young prince rode through the valley on his gentle white horse, and heard the sweetest singing floating down from the tiny window.

"Hello up there!" called the prince. "Your singing is beautiful! May I come up and say hello?"

Rapunzel peeped out. "Hello!" she said. "I would love a visitor. But there is no door, and the tower is too tall to climb."

"Then let down your long hair," said the prince, "and I will climb up very gently, like climbing a golden rope."

So Rapunzel let down her long golden hair, and the prince climbed up, up, up — ever so gently.

"Thank you for the lovely song," said the prince. "I am Prince Rowan." "I am Rapunzel," she said with a big smile. "Would you like to hear another song?"

And so the prince came to visit every evening. Rapunzel sang, the prince listened, and they became the best of friends.

One day, Dame Greta came up the tower steps with fresh bread. She heard Rapunzel laughing, and she peeped round the door.

"Well, well," she said. "So that is where all the singing comes from! You have a friend, Rapunzel!"

Rapunzel held her breath. Would Dame Greta be cross?

But Dame Greta looked at Rapunzel's happy face, and her grumpy frown melted away. "Oh, my dear," she said softly, "I have kept you up here far too long. A girl needs friends, and sunshine, and grass to run in."

Then Dame Greta smiled. "Rapunzel, you may come down and live in the village, and I shall bake you honey cakes."

Rapunzel clapped her hands with joy! "Thank you, Dame Greta! Thank you!"

So Rapunzel came down at last and moved into a little cottage in the village, where she made friends with all the children.

And every evening, Rapunzel stood in the village square and sang her sweet songs. All the villagers came to listen, and Dame Greta always sat in the front, tapping her foot and smiling.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart and a brave friend can open even the tallest tower door.*
