The Twelve Months

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, in a snowy village, lived a kind girl named Mira.

Mira lived with her stepmother and stepsister, who were not always kind to her.

One cold winter day, the stepmother said, "Mira, go into the forest and bring back a basket of strawberries! I want strawberries for our feast tonight!"

"But Mother," said Mira softly, "it is winter. Strawberries do not grow in the snow."

"Go anyway!" said the stepmother. "And do not come back without them!"

So Mira wrapped her thin shawl around herself and walked into the snowy forest.

The snow was deep. The wind was cold. Her feet went crunch, crunch, crunch.

Soon Mira saw a warm light among the trees. She walked towards it — and what did she find?

A big, crackling fire! And sitting around it were twelve brothers, all dressed in different colors.

One wore white like snow. One wore green like spring leaves. One wore gold like autumn wheat.

"Come and warm yourself, little girl," said the oldest, whose beard was white as frost. "I am January. These are my brothers, the twelve months of the year. Why are you out in the snow?"

Mira told them everything — about the strawberries, and the feast, and her thin shawl.

"Please do not be sad," said the brother in green — April. "You spoke to us so politely. Sit by the fire and share our bread."

Mira shared her little piece of bread with all twelve brothers, crumb by crumb.

Then April stood up and waved his hand. The snow melted in a little circle. Green leaves popped up. And there — oh, wonder! — red strawberries grew all around, sweet and ripe!

Mira filled her basket to the top. "Thank you, thank you!" she said, and hurried home.

The stepmother was amazed. "Strawberries — in winter!" she cried.

The next day, the stepsister went to the forest too. "I want strawberries and gold!" she said.

But she did not greet the brothers. She did not share her bread. She stamped her foot and said, "Give me strawberries at once!"

The brothers looked at each other. January waved his hand — and a swirl of snowflakes danced around the rude girl until she ran all the way home, cold and empty-handed.

When she got home, Mira did not laugh at her. Instead, Mira shared her strawberries and gave her a bowl of warm soup.

"Why are you kind to me?" asked the stepsister, surprised.

"Because kindness is warmer than any fire," said Mira, smiling.

And from that day on, the house was a little kinder, and the strawberries tasted a little sweeter.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kind words open doors that rudeness cannot — even in the middle of winter.
