Nasruddin and the Lake of Yogurt

A timeless Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On a warm summer afternoon, Nasruddin sat at the edge of the big blue lake. He was stirring the water with a spoon as big as a paddle.

Round and round. Swish, swish, swish — little ripples spread out across the blue water.

A villager walking by stopped and stared. "Nasruddin! What are you doing?!"

"Making yogurt," said Nasruddin, stirring away.

"Yogurt? From the LAKE?" The villager laughed so hard he nearly fell over. "Nasruddin, you silly man! The whole lake will never turn into yogurt!"

More villagers gathered. "He is stirring the lake!" they giggled. "He thinks the lake will become yogurt!"

Nasruddin's wife came hurrying down the hill. "Nasruddin!" she said, hands on her hips. "Come home! This is the silliest thing I have ever seen!"

But Nasruddin just kept stirring. Swish, swish, swish. He smiled a small, secret smile.

"Tell me, friends," he said at last, "have you ever tasted yogurt made from a whole lake?"

"No!" laughed the villagers.

"Have you ever seen a bowl of yogurt as big as a lake?"

"No!" they laughed again.

"Well then," said Nasruddin, giving the water one last big stir, "how do you know it can't be done?"

The villagers scratched their heads. Hmm. That was a tricky question!

"What if it works?" Nasruddin said, his eyes twinkling. "What do you think — could it work? Just think — the biggest, creamiest bowl of yogurt in the whole world! Enough for everyone! Wouldn't that be wonderful?"

The villagers looked at the blue lake, sparkling in the sun. And for just a moment, they imagined it — a whole lake of white, creamy yogurt.

They smiled. All of them.

Nasruddin stood up, tucked his giant spoon under his arm like an umbrella, and walked home humming a soft, sleepy tune.

That evening, he ate his dinner — plain rice and a small bowl of plain yogurt from the kitchen. The lake was still just blue water, and that was fine.

And as he ate, he smiled to himself.

And Nasruddin knew that people who dream big, kind dreams make the world a happier place.

The end. Goodnight, little dreamer. 🌙
