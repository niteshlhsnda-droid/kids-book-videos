# The Grateful Crane

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a snowy village in Japan, there lived a kind young farmer named Arjun.

One cold winter evening, Arjun was walking home through the snow when he heard a soft, sad sound. "Kruu… kruu…"

There, by the side of the road, lay a beautiful white crane. Her wing was hurt, and she could not fly.

"Oh, poor little crane," said Arjun gently. He picked her up as carefully as if she were made of glass, wrapped her in his warm scarf, and carried her home.

At home, Arjun bandaged her wing with a soft cloth and gave her warm rice and water. Day after day, he looked after her. And when spring came and her wing was better, he opened the window wide.

"Fly free, little crane," he said. "And thank you for letting me help you."

The crane flew up into the blue spring sky, circled once around Arjun's little house, and disappeared over the mountains.

That very evening, there was a knock at Arjun's door. Knock, knock!

Standing outside was a young woman with long, dark hair and kind, bright eyes. "My name is Tara," she said. "I have no home. May I stay with you and help you?"

Arjun smiled. "Of course," he said. "Everyone is welcome here."

Tara was a wonderful friend. She cooked, she cleaned, and she sang sweet songs while she worked. But best of all, she could weave the most beautiful cloth in the whole village — cloth as soft as moonlight and as bright as morning.

One day, Tara said, "Arjun, I will weave a special cloth for you. But you must promise me one thing — never watch me while I weave."

"I promise," said Arjun.

So Tara went into the little weaving room and closed the door. Click, click, click went the loom. And when she came out, she held the most beautiful cloth Arjun had ever seen.

Night after night, she wove. But Arjun began to wonder. How did she make such beautiful cloth? What was her secret?

One night, he could not help himself. He peeped through a tiny crack in the door.

And there, at the loom, was not Tara — but the beautiful white crane! She was weaving with her own soft, white feathers to make the cloth. That was why the cloth was so soft and bright.

Arjun gasped. The crane looked up and saw him. In a moment, she was Tara again, and tears shone in her kind, bright eyes.

"Arjun," she said softly, "I was the crane you saved. I came back to thank you. But now you have seen my secret, and I must fly away."

"Please don't go," said Arjun.

But Tara smiled through her tears. "Do not be sad. I will always be grateful. Every spring, I will visit you. And whenever you see a white crane in the sky, think of me."

Then she opened the window and flew away, up into the soft spring sky. But before she went, she left a gift on the windowsill — one soft, white feather, and a little note that said, "Thank you."

Arjun kept the feather by his window. And every spring, a beautiful white crane flew over his little house, circled once, and called down softly, "Kruu… kruu…" — as if to say, "Thank you, my friend. Thank you."

And Arjun would smile and wave, and his heart would feel warm all day.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind deed is never forgotten — gratitude finds its way back to you.*
