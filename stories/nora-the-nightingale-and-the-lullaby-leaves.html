Nora the Nightingale and the Lullaby Leaves

A brand-new bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a tall oak tree at the edge of a quiet forest, there lived a little nightingale named Nora.

Nora had soft brown feathers, bright black eyes, and the sweetest voice in the whole forest. Every morning, she sang a song that woke up the flowers. Every evening, she sang a lullaby that put the whole forest to sleep.

But one evening, something strange happened. Nora opened her beak to sing her lullaby — and no song came out!

She tried again. "La, la…" Nothing. Just a tiny squeak, like a rusty little gate.

"Oh no!" whispered Nora. "Where has my lullaby gone?"

She flew to her friend Lulu the ladybird, who was sitting on a red rose. "Lulu, I have lost my night song! Can you help me find it?"

Lulu thought hard. "Perhaps it fell into the pond," she said. "Let us go and look!"

So Nora and Lulu flew to the pond, where Gus the goose was floating on the water, as round and white as a cloud.

"Gus!" called Nora. "I have lost my lullaby. Have you seen it?"

Gus shook his big white head. "I have not seen it," he said. "But I know something that might help. Deep in the forest, there is a bush with lullaby leaves. The leaves hum softly when the moon shines on them. If you hold one close to your heart, your song will come back — softer and sweeter than before."

"Oh, thank you, Gus!" said Nora. "Will you come with me?"

"Of course!" said Gus, flapping his wings. "Lulu, you come too!"

So the three friends set off into the forest. The moon was rising, round and silver, and the fireflies were lighting the path with their tiny golden lamps.

At last, they came to the lullaby bush. Its leaves were soft and green, and each one hummed a little tune — hmm, hmm, hmm — as the moonlight touched it.

Nora picked one leaf, very gently, and held it close to her heart. She closed her eyes and listened to its hum. Hmm, hmm, hmm.

Then, very softly, Nora began to hum too. Hmm, hmm, hmm. And then — oh, wonder! — the hum turned into a song. A soft, sweet lullaby, floating up through the trees like a silver ribbon.

Lulu clapped her tiny wings. Gus waddled in a happy little circle. And all through the forest, the animals heard Nora's song and yawned big, sleepy yawns.

Nora sang the whole forest to sleep that night — the rabbits in their burrows, the owls in their trees, and even Gus, who fell asleep right there on the pond, floating like a soft white cloud.

When her song was done, Nora tucked the lullaby leaf under her wing. "Thank you, my friends," she whispered. "I thought I had lost my song forever. But you helped me find it again."

"That is what friends are for," said Lulu sleepily.

"And your song sounds sweeter than ever," honked Gus, very softly.

And Nora smiled, closed her bright black eyes, and fell asleep — with her lullaby leaf humming gently beside her.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When you lose your song, friends help you find it again — and it comes back sweeter than before.
