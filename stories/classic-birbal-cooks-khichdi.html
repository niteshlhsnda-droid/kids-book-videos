Birbal Cooks Khichdi

A clever Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Emperor Akbar loved a good challenge — and Birbal, his cleverest minister, loved solving them.

One frosty winter evening, Akbar said, "Birbal, I bet you cannot stand in the cold river until morning!"

Birbal smiled. "I accept, Jahanpanah."

All night long, Birbal stood in the icy river, shivering from head to toe. Far away on the palace wall, a single little lamp glowed like a firefly.

At sunrise, Birbal climbed out, blue with cold but triumphant. "I did it!"

But Akbar stroked his beard. "Hmm. That lamp's warm glow reached you all night. It kept you warm. So — you lose the bet!"

The courtiers gasped. A lamp, far, far away — warming a man in a cold river? Everyone knew that was silly. But no one dared to argue with the emperor.

Birbal only bowed. "As you say, Jahanpanah."

The next morning, the courtiers found Birbal in the courtyard doing something very strange. He had tied a pot of khichdi — rice and lentils — to the very top of a long, long bamboo pole. Far below, on the ground, burned one tiny flame.

"Birbal! What are you doing?" laughed the courtiers.

"Cooking my lunch," said Birbal calmly.

"But the flame is so far from the pot! It will NEVER cook!"

Birbal looked up at the palace window, where Akbar was watching. "If the warm glow of a faraway lamp can warm a man standing in a cold river all night," he said loudly, "then surely this faraway flame can cook my little pot of khichdi!"

Akbar's face turned red — then he burst out laughing. "Enough, Birbal! Enough! You have cooked ME with your clever words!"

He came down, hugged Birbal, and gave him the reward — plus an extra shawl for that cold night in the river.

And that evening, they shared the khichdi together — cooked properly, over a nice close flame — and laughed till the stars came out.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Clever thinking can melt even the frostiest unfairness.
