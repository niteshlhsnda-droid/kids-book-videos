# Baxter the Beaver and the Bedtime Dam

*A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a wide, winding river lived a busy beaver named Baxter.

Baxter had a flat, strong tail and big, busy teeth. He loved to build, to chew, and to stack sticks and pat mud into place.

Every morning, as soon as the sun peeped over the trees, Baxter was up and working.

Chomp, chomp, chomp went his teeth. Pat, pat, pat went his paws.

"A dam must be strong and tall," said Baxter. "A dam must be just right."

And all day long, he worked and worked and worked.

He dragged big sticks from the bank, pushed smooth stones into place, and patted mud between the gaps.

He did not stop for a rest, a snack, or even a splash in the cool river.

By the afternoon, his tail felt heavy, his paws felt slow, and his eyes felt droopy — but still, Baxter worked on.

"Baxter!" called a friendly voice. "Baxter, my friend, come and float with me!"

It was Nia, the otter, with sleek brown fur and bright, kind eyes, floating on her back in the still pond and twirling slowly in the sunshine.

"Not now, Nia," said Baxter, yawning a great big yawn. "My dam is not finished yet."

Nia swam closer and looked at the dam, then at Baxter.

"Baxter," she said softly, "your dam is already strong and tall. But your tail is dragging, your paws are slow, and your eyes are droopy."

"I am fine," said Baxter. But even as he said it, he yawned again — a yawn so big that his whole face wobbled, whiskers and all.

Nia smiled. "A good dam needs a rested builder," she said. "Come and float with me, just for a little while. The river will wait."

Baxter looked at his dam, then at the soft, still pond, and nodded, very slowly.

"Just for a little while," he said.

He slipped into the water with a gentle plop. The cool water held him up like a hug.

Baxter floated on his back beside Nia, watching the white clouds drift by and feeling the sun warm his brown fur.

"This feels nice," he whispered. "My tail feels light again. My paws feel rested."

"Rest is part of building," said Nia. "Even the river rests when it reaches a still pond."

Together, they floated and rested until Baxter's eyes were bright again and his paws felt quick again.

Then, side by side, they swam back to the dam. Baxter patted the last bit of mud into place, and Nia helped push the last smooth stone into its gap.

"There!" said Baxter. "Now it is finished. Strong, tall, and just right."

"And you finished it with rested paws," said Nia, smiling.

As the sun went down, the sky turned pink, then purple, then dark blue, and the stars came out to twinkle on the still pond.

Baxter swam home to his cosy little house and curled up in his soft nest of wood shavings.

"Goodnight, river," he whispered. "Goodnight, dam. Goodnight, Nia."

And Baxter slept soundly all night, dreaming of rivers, dams, and floating on still, still water.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Rest is part of good work — a rested builder builds best.*
