# The Emperor's New Clothes

In a town where the river curled like a silver ribbon, there lived an emperor named Felix. Emperor Felix loved clothes more than anything in the world. He had a different coat for every kind of day. Every morning he asked, "What shall I wear today?"

One bright morning, two travellers came to the palace gates with big smiles and empty hands. "We are the finest weavers in all the land," they said. "We weave cloth so fine that only wise and clever people can see it. To anyone else, it looks like nothing at all."

Emperor Felix clapped his hands. "Wonderful! Weave me a suit of this magic cloth for the grand parade!" The weavers set up two great looms in the palace hall. All day they clicked and clacked, though the looms stayed quite empty.

Soon the emperor sent his kind old helper, Marta, to see the cloth. Marta peered at the empty loom, and her heart thumped. "Oh dear," she thought. "I cannot see a single thread. Does that mean I am not wise at all?" But she smiled and said, "How beautiful!"

Then the emperor himself came to look. He stared at the empty loom, and his cheeks turned pink. "I cannot see it either," he thought. "But everyone else says it is beautiful." So he nodded and said, "Yes, very fine. Very fine indeed."

On the day of the parade, the weavers helped the emperor into his new suit — which was no suit at all. They brushed his shoulders and held up a mirror, and the emperor turned this way and that, trying to look pleased.

Then out he went along the street, with trumpets playing and banners waving. All the people lined the road, clapping and cheering. "What a splendid suit!" cried one man. "Such wonderful colours!" cried a woman. And everyone nodded, because nobody wanted to be the one who could not see it.

But a little girl named Ivy sat on her father's shoulders, swinging her legs. She looked at the emperor and frowned a small, puzzled frown. "Papa," she whispered, "the emperor isn't wearing any clothes at all!" Her father gasped. The woman beside him giggled.

The giggle spread like warm honey, from one person to the next, until the whole street was laughing, a soft, happy laugh like sunshine after rain. Emperor Felix stopped, looked down at himself, and laughed too, the biggest, warmest laugh of his life. "Ivy is right!" he cried. "There is no suit at all!"

The weavers had slipped quietly away, but nobody minded. The parade went on, with the emperor waving in his fine, soft robe and Ivy waving back. That night, Felix put on his softest old robe and curled up in bed, thinking about Ivy's honest little voice, and how saying what is true can make a whole town laugh together.

Sleep now, little Felix.
