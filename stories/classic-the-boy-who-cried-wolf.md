# The Boy Who Cried Wolf

*A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

On a green hill above a little village lived a shepherd boy named Tommy.

Every morning, Tommy took his flock of fluffy sheep up the hill to munch the sweet grass. His dog Biscuit went with him, trotting happily at his side.

But watching sheep all day was very quiet work. The sheep only went munch, munch, munch. Biscuit only went snooze, snooze, snooze.

"Oh, this is so boring," said Tommy one sunny morning. "I wish something exciting would happen."

Then Tommy had an idea. A naughty idea.

He stood up on a rock, cupped his hands around his mouth, and shouted at the top of his voice, "WOLF! WOLF! A big wolf is chasing my sheep!"

Down in the village, the farmers heard him. They dropped their baskets and their buckets and came running up the hill as fast as they could, carrying sticks and pots and pans.

"Where is the wolf?" they cried.

Tommy laughed and laughed. "There is no wolf!" he said. "I was only joking! You should have seen your faces!"

The farmers were not laughing. They walked slowly back down the hill, shaking their heads.

"That was not a kind joke, Tommy," said old Farmer Joe. "When you call for help, we come because we care."

The next day, Tommy felt bored again. The sheep went munch, munch, munch. Biscuit went snooze, snooze, snooze.

"I will do it just once more," thought Tommy. "Just once more, for fun."

So he stood on his rock and shouted, "WOLF! WOLF! A big wolf is chasing my sheep!"

Again the farmers came running up the hill. Again there was no wolf. Again Tommy laughed.

"Oh, Tommy," said Farmer Joe, very sadly. "If you keep crying wolf when there is no wolf, one day we will not know when to believe you."

Tommy felt a little funny inside. Not a happy funny. A wobbly, sorry funny. But he did not say anything. He just watched his sheep until the sun went down.

On the third day, Tommy was sitting on his rock when Biscuit suddenly jumped up, barking. Tommy looked up — and there, at the edge of the trees, stood a real wolf!

It was big and grey, with pointy ears and a long, bushy tail. It was looking at the sheep, licking its lips.

Tommy's heart went thump, thump, thump. This time it was real!

"WOLF! WOLF!" shouted Tommy, as loud as he could. "A REAL wolf! Please come and help!"

But down in the village, the farmers heard him and said, "Oh, that is just Tommy and his jokes again."

Nobody came. Nobody at all.

Tommy's knees wobbled. He looked at his sheep, all huddled together, and at Biscuit, barking bravely. He knew he had to do something himself.

"Stay back, wolf!" shouted Tommy, waving his shepherd's stick. "These are my sheep, and I look after them!"

Biscuit barked and barked, loud and brave. Tommy banged his stick on a rock — BANG, BANG, BANG!

The wolf did not like the noise one bit. It tucked its tail between its legs and trotted away into the trees, without touching a single sheep.

Tommy sat down on his rock, all shaky and relieved. "Thank you, Biscuit," he whispered. "You are the bravest dog in the world."

That evening, Tommy walked down to the village with his sheep. He went straight to Farmer Joe's house and knocked on the door.

"Farmer Joe," said Tommy, "I am sorry. I told silly stories when there was no wolf, and then when a real wolf came, nobody believed me. I will never cry wolf for fun again. A call for help is for real help."

Farmer Joe smiled and patted Tommy's shoulder. "Thank you for telling the truth, Tommy. That takes a brave heart too."

From that day on, Tommy never cried wolf unless a real wolf was there. And whenever he called, the whole village came running — because now they knew his word was true.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Tell the truth, and people will trust your word when it truly matters.*
