# Nasruddin and the Turban

*A Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

It was a blustery, windy day in Nasruddin's village, and the wind was in a very playful mood.

It ruffled the laundry on the lines. It rattled the shutters. And it whooshed down the dusty lane where Nasruddin was walking home, humming a little tune.

Nasruddin loved his turban. It was white and soft and as tall as a small cake, and it sat on his head like a happy little cloud.

Then came the biggest gust of the whole day.

WHOOSH!

Right off his head went the turban — up, up into the air it flew, spinning like a pinwheel, until it landed — plop! — in the dusty street.

"My turban!" cried Nasruddin. He ran after it.

But a few village children had seen it fall. They were quick as squirrels! A little boy snatched it up before Nasruddin could reach it.

"Mine!" giggled the boy — and threw it to a girl across the street.

"Hey! Give me back my turban!" called Nasruddin, running after it.

The girl caught it with a shriek of laughter and tossed it high to her friend. Then that friend threw it on to the next. Back and forth, back and forth the turban flew, like a big white ball in a game nobody had planned.

Nasruddin stretched up on his tiptoes — whee! The turban flew over his head. He dived to the left — whoop! It bounced off a wall and sailed right. The children squealed with delight at every miss.

"Almost, Uncle Nasruddin! Almost!" they cried, and threw it higher than before.

Nasruddin chased it up the lane and down the lane — sandals flapping, breath coming in little puffs.

"Hey! Give me back my turban!" he called again — but this time he wasn't cross. He was smiling.

Because do you know what he saw? The children's cheeks were pink with laughter. Their eyes sparkled. They were having the BEST game of the whole day — and all because of his silly runaway turban.

So Nasruddin stopped chasing. He sat right down in the dust — plop! — and started to laugh too.

Ha-ha! Ho-ho! His big laugh rolled down the lane and mixed with the children's giggles until the whole street was full of laughing.

At last the turban sailed through the air — and landed right in Nasruddin's lap. A little boy ran up, breathless and grinning.

"Thank you, Uncle," he said. "That was the funniest game ever!"

"You're welcome, little champion," said Nasruddin, ruffling the boy's hair. "Take care of that strong throwing arm!"

Nasruddin put his turban back on his head, gave it a friendly pat, and walked home, still smiling. The children waved from the corner until he turned out of sight.

At home, his wife was making dinner. She looked up and blinked.

"Nasruddin," she said, "your turban is full of dust! And where did it go? Did you lose it somewhere?"

Nasruddin smiled his widest smile.

"Oh, my turban suddenly remembered its childhood," he said. "And it decided to go off and play."

His wife laughed, and they sat down to dinner, and Nasruddin was the happiest man in the village that night.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Don't let a small loss steal your joy — sometimes it is better to laugh along.*
