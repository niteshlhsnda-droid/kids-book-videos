The Wolf and the House Dog

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Deep in a quiet, green forest lived a wolf named Wally. He was tall and grey, with a big fluffy tail and bright yellow eyes.

Wally was a kind wolf, but he was often hungry. Some days he found a few berries. Some days he found nothing at all. On cold nights, his tummy rumbled like a little drum.

"I wish I had a warm place to sleep," Wally sighed one evening, "and a full bowl of food every day."

One misty morning, Wally was trotting along the edge of the forest when he met a dog. The dog was round and plump, with a shiny brown coat and a happy wagging tail.

"Hello!" said the dog cheerfully. "My name is Bruno. I live in the big house down the lane. What are you doing out here all alone?"

"I am looking for food," said Wally. "My tummy is empty, and my bed is a pile of cold leaves."

Bruno smiled a big, friendly smile. "Then come and live with me!" he said. "My master is very kind. He gives me warm milk, soft bread, and tasty bones every single day. I sleep on a soft rug by the fire, and I never feel cold or hungry."

Wally's eyes grew wide. "Warm milk? Soft bread? Every day?" he said. "That sounds wonderful!"

"It is wonderful," said Bruno, wagging his tail even faster. "Come along. You will love it."

So Wally trotted happily beside Bruno down the lane, dreaming of warm milk and soft rugs.

But as they walked, Wally noticed something funny. On Bruno's neck there was a round, bare patch where the fur had worn away.

"What's that mark on your neck?" asked Wally.

"Oh, that?" said Bruno. "That is from my collar. My master ties me to a chain at night so I stay in the yard."

"A chain?" said Wally, stopping in his tracks. "You mean you cannot run where you like? You cannot visit the forest whenever you wish?"

"Well, no," said Bruno. "But I get warm milk and soft bread every day!"

Wally looked at the big house. Then he looked back at the green forest, where the trees whispered and the birds sang.

"Thank you, Bruno," said Wally gently. "Your house sounds warm and your food sounds tasty. But I would rather be a little bit hungry and free in my forest than full and tied up in a yard."

And with that, Wally turned around and trotted happily back into the trees, where he could run and play and sleep under the stars whenever he pleased.

That night, Wally curled up under a big oak tree. His tummy was not quite full, but his heart was happy. The moon shone softly through the leaves, and the forest sang him a gentle lullaby as he drifted off to sleep, free and safe in his own green home.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Freedom is worth more than comfort.
