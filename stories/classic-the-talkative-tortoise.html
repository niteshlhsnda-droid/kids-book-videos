The Talkative Tortoise

A Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a small round pond, under the green lily pads, lived a tortoise named Kachhua.

Kachhua loved to talk more than anything in the whole wide world.

In the morning, he talked to the tall reeds. "Good morning, reeds! Did you sleep well? Did you dream about the wind?"

At noon, he talked to the little silver fish. "Hello, fishies! Where are you swimming today? Tell me everything!"

And in the evening, he talked to his own long shadow on the water. "Hello, shadow! Are you as handsome as me? Of course you are!"

His two goose friends, Gagan and Gagni, used to smile at him. They loved Kachhua's chatter, but sometimes they said, "Kachhua, dear, your tongue is going to wear out before your legs do!"

One summer, the sun grew hotter and hotter, and the little pond shrank smaller and smaller, until one day it was only a puddle.

"Oh dear," said Kachhua. "My pond is disappearing! Whatever shall I do?"

Gagan flapped down beside him. "Do not worry, old friend," he said. "There is a big blue lake just beyond the hills. Gagni and I will carry you there."

"Carry me?" said Kachhua. "But tortoises cannot fly!"

Gagni dropped a strong stick at his feet. "You do not need to fly," she said. "You only need to hold this stick very tightly in your beak. I will hold one end, Gagan will hold the other end, and up, up, up we will go!"

"And one more thing," said Gagan, very seriously. "Once we are in the air, you must NOT open your mouth. Not to say hello, not to sing, not even to sneeze. Keep your beak shut tight until our feet touch the ground again."

Kachhua grabbed the stick in his beak. "Hmm-hmm," he said — which meant, "I promise!"

Up, up, up they rose, into the soft white clouds. Can you picture it — the whole wide world tiny as toys? The world below looked like a tiny quilt — green fields, blue rivers, and little houses like toy blocks.

Kachhua had never been so high. He had never been so happy. And he had never, ever been so full of words!

Below them, a group of village children looked up and waved.

"Look! Look!" they shouted. "A flying tortoise! A real flying tortoise!"

Kachhua felt so proud that his cheeks puffed right up. Everyone was looking at HIM. Everyone was cheering for HIM.

How could he not answer? He opened his mouth wide to say, "Thank you! Thank you all!" —

And down he went. Whoosh! Tumble, tumble, tumble — plop! — right into a big soft haystack.

The hay was scratchy. The hay was golden. The hay tickled his nose, and Kachhua could not stop laughing.

"Ha-ha-ha!" he laughed. "Next time, my beak stays shut! I promise, promise, promise!"

Gagan and Gagni swooped down and found him giggling in the hay.

"Are you all right, you silly old chatterbox?" said Gagni, and she gave him a big feathery hug.

"Better than all right!" said Kachhua. "That was the softest landing in the whole world. Now let us try again — and this time, not a single word!"

So Kachhua grabbed the stick again. Up, up, up they flew, and this time his beak stayed shut as tight as a little green zipper.

Not a hello. Not a song. Not even one tiny word.

When they landed by the big blue lake, with the sun dancing on its ripples, Kachhua opened his mouth at last and cheered in the happiest whisper, "We made it!"

And splash-splash, they slipped softly into the cool, clear water and laughed one soft, happy laugh together.

That night, under the round yellow moon, Kachhua floated on his back and whispered to the stars, "Talking is wonderful. But thinking first is even better."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Think before you speak.
