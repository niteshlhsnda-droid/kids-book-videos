# Nasruddin and the Donkey

*A merry Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little village, where the lanes were dusty and the rooftops were warm with sun, lived a cheerful man named Nasruddin, with a round smile, a big white turban, and a small grey donkey named Golu.

Golu was a good donkey. He carried Nasruddin's baskets to the market and water pots from the well. And every evening, he carried Nasruddin home, clip-clop, clip-clop, down the dusty lane.

But Golu had one great love in all the world: clover. Sweet, soft, green clover from the meadow beyond the village.

One bright morning, Nasruddin led Golu out to the field and tied him to a wooden post. "Stay here, Golu," said Nasruddin. "Eat your hay, and be a good donkey." And off he went to buy vegetables in the market.

But Golu sniffed the air. Sniff, sniff, sniff. From far away came the smell of clover — sweet and green and wonderful. Golu pulled. Golu tugged. The rope slipped off the post — plip! — and off went Golu, trot-trot-trot, following his nose toward the clover meadow.

When Nasruddin came back from the market, his baskets full of carrots and onions, he stopped and stared.

"Golu?" he called. "Golu, where are you?"

No answer. The post stood empty. The rope lay on the ground like a sleepy snake.

"Oh, no!" said Nasruddin. "My donkey has wandered off!" So he set off down the lane, calling as he went. "Golu! Golu! Where are you?"

First, he looked in the stable, behind every haystack, and under the cart.

"No Golu," said Nasruddin. "But wait — what is that? Two long ears behind the haystack!"

He tiptoed closer, and out popped — a goat! The goat looked at him, chewed thoughtfully, and said, "Baa!"

"Oh!" said Nasruddin, laughing. "You are not my donkey! You are a goat with very donkey-like ears!"

Next, Nasruddin followed a trail of little hoofprints in the dust — around the well, past the baker's shop, and straight into the baker's garden.

"Aha!" said Nasruddin. "Golu must be eating the baker's cabbages!"

But when he peeked into the garden, there was no donkey — only the baker's cow, munching cabbages with great satisfaction.

"Oh!" said Nasruddin. "You are not my donkey either! You are a cow with very donkey-like hoofprints!"

The baker came out, wiping flour from his hands. "Nasruddin," he said, "are you looking for someone?"

"My donkey Golu!" said Nasruddin. "Have you seen him?"

The baker scratched his head. "I saw a small grey donkey trotting past my shop," he said. "He was heading toward the clover meadow, and he looked very happy."

"The clover meadow!" cried Nasruddin. "Of course! Golu loves clover more than anything in the world!"

So Nasruddin hurried down the lane, past the well, past the big banyan tree, and out into the meadow. The grass was thick and green, and the air smelled sweet, like rain.

And there, under the biggest tree in the meadow, lay Golu.

His belly was round and full of clover. His ears were flopped to the sides. His eyes were closed. And he was fast asleep, snoring softly — snrrrk, snrrrk — with a smile on his face.

Nasruddin looked at him for a long moment. Then he burst out laughing.

"Golu!" he said, laughing. "I searched the whole village for you! I mistook a goat for you, and a cow for you — and all the time, you were here, napping under a tree after your clover feast!"

Golu opened one eye. He saw Nasruddin. He wiggled his ears, and he got to his feet with a happy little shake.

"Come on, you sleepy donkey," said Nasruddin, patting his neck. "Time to go home."

And so they walked home together, clip-clop, clip-clop, down the dusty lane — Nasruddin carrying his baskets, and Golu carrying nothing at all except a very full belly and a very happy heart.

That evening, Nasruddin tied Golu to his post with a very good knot. And as the stars came out, he told the whole story to his neighbours — the goat, the cow, and the donkey asleep under the tree — and everyone laughed until their sides ached.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A good laugh makes every search sweeter — and home is happiest when everyone is back together.*
