Diamonds and Toads

A beloved world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, in a little cottage at the edge of a village, lived two sisters.

The elder sister, Mira, was cross and grumbly. She never helped with the chores, and her words were sharp like thorns.

The younger sister, Lena, was kind and gentle. She swept the floor, fed the hens, and her words were soft like warm bread.

One sunny morning, their mother said, "Lena, go to the well and fetch fresh water."

Lena picked up the clay pot and skipped down the path to the old stone well.

At the well stood a poor old woman in a ragged shawl. "Dear child," said the old woman, "I am so thirsty. Will you give me a drink of your water?"

"Of course, grandmother!" said Lena. She filled her little cup and held it out with both hands. "Drink slowly. There is plenty more."

The old woman drank — and as she did, her ragged shawl shimmered and turned to silver! She was not an old woman at all, but a shining fairy!

"Kind child," said the fairy, "for your sweet heart, I give you a gift. From this day on, every kind word you speak will turn into a flower or a jewel as it leaves your lips."

Lena ran home, and as she told her mother what happened, roses and pearls tumbled from her mouth — plip, plop! — onto the floor. Everyone gasped with delight.

Mira's eyes grew wide. "I want jewels too!" she cried. She grabbed the pot and ran to the well.

At the well stood the same fairy, dressed as a fine lady this time. "Dear child," said the fairy, "may I have a drink?"

"Ugh! Fetch it yourself!" snapped Mira. "I'm not your servant!"

The fairy's eyes twinkled. "For your sharp words, I give you a gift as well. From this day on, every unkind word you speak will turn into a toad as it leaves your lips."

Mira ran home to boast — but when she opened her mouth, out hopped a fat green toad! "Ribbit!" Then another! "Ribbit! Ribbit!"

Mira burst into tears. "I don't want toads! I want diamonds!"

Lena hugged her sister gently. "Dear Mira," she said — and a rose fell from her lips — "words are like seeds. Plant kind ones, and beautiful things grow."

Mira wiped her tears. From that day on, she tried very, very hard to speak kindly. She helped with the chores. She said "please" and "thank you" and "I love you."

And do you know what happened? The fairy came back one moonlit night and smiled.

"You have learned," she said. "Kind words grow in kind hearts." She touched Mira's lips — and from then on, whenever Mira spoke kindly, tiny daisies and dewdrop pearls fell softly to the ground.

The two sisters lived happily ever after, and their cottage garden bloomed with the most beautiful flowers in the whole village — all grown from kind words.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kind words are the truest treasure — they make beautiful things grow.
