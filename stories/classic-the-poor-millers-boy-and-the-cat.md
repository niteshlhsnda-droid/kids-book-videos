# The Poor Miller's Boy and the Cat

*A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, there was a poor miller who had three apprentices. The eldest two were big and boastful, and they were always telling the miller how hard they worked.

The youngest was a small, quiet boy named Hans. Hans worked hard too, but he never boasted. He swept the mill, carried the sacks of flour, and sang softly while he worked.

One day, the miller said, "I am growing old, and soon I must give my mill to one of you. The one who brings me the finest horse shall have the mill."

The two big apprentices laughed and set off at once to find fine horses. But poor Hans had no money at all, and he did not know where to look. He walked down the dusty road with his little bundle, wondering what to do.

As evening fell, Hans came to a little cottage in the woods. Sitting on the doorstep was a small grey cat with bright green eyes.

"Good evening, young traveller," said the cat — for this was a talking cat, as cats in old tales sometimes are. "You look tired. Come in and rest."

Hans was surprised, but he was also polite. "Thank you," he said. "I am looking for a fine horse, but I have no money to buy one."

The cat purred. "Stay and work for me for seven years," she said. "Sweep my house, polish my little silver pots, and be kind — and at the end, I will give you something better than a horse."

Seven years is a long time. But Hans was patient and kind, and the little cat was good company. He swept and polished and sang his soft songs, and the cat listened, purring, by the fire.

At last, the seven years were done. "You have been a faithful friend," said the cat. "Now, take this little box, and do not open it until you reach your old mill."

Hans thanked the cat and set off home. On the way, he met his two old fellow apprentices. They were riding fine horses, and they laughed when they saw Hans on foot.

"Where is your horse, little Hans?" they teased.

Hans only smiled. When he reached the mill, he opened the little box — and out stepped the most beautiful horse anyone had ever seen! Its coat shone like moonlight, and it whinnied softly and nuzzled Hans's hand.

The miller clapped his hands in delight. "This is the finest horse of all!" he cried. "Hans, my boy, the mill is yours!"

But the best surprise was yet to come. For the little grey cat had followed Hans all the way home — and as she stepped into the mill, there was a soft shimmer of light, and the cat turned into a lovely princess!

"Thank you, Hans," said the princess, smiling. "Your seven years of kindness broke the spell. Now the mill is yours, and I will be your friend always."

And so Hans became the miller, and the princess stayed to help him bake the finest bread in all the land. The two boastful apprentices came to work for him too — and they learned, little by little, that quiet kindness is worth more than loud boasting.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Quiet kindness, kept up day after day, brings the finest rewards of all.*
