Ivy the Iguana and the Island Lullaby

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On a tiny green island in the middle of a blue sea lived a little iguana named Ivy. Ivy was bright green, with a spiky crest and a long, curly tail, and she loved to sing.

Every evening, as the sun sank into the sea, Ivy climbed onto her favourite warm rock and sang a lullaby. Her voice was soft and sweet, like wind through palm leaves.

But one evening, Ivy's voice felt wobbly. "La, la, laaa…" she sang, and then her voice squeaked and stopped. "Oh no," whispered Ivy. "My singing voice has gone all wobbly! How will my island friends fall asleep tonight?"

Ivy tried again. "La, la…" Squeak! Her voice wobbled like a jelly.

Just then, her friend Piko the parrot fluttered down. "Do not worry, Ivy," squawked Piko kindly. "Let us sing together. Two voices are stronger than one."

So Piko sang, "La, la, la," and Ivy sang, "La, la, la," and together their song floated over the island like a soft blanket.

Then Tiko the turtle paddled up to the rock. "May I join?" he asked slowly. "I cannot sing high, but I can hum very low. Hmmmm."

So Piko sang high, Tiko hummed low, and Ivy sang in the middle — and the song sounded more beautiful than ever before!

One by one, more friends joined in. The little crabs clicked their claws in time. The palm trees swayed. The waves whispered shhh, shhh, shhh against the shore.

Soon the whole island was singing the lullaby together, soft and slow. The baby birds closed their eyes. The little fish drifted to sleep in the warm lagoon. Even the moon seemed to yawn and settle down for the night.

Ivy smiled a big iguana smile. Her voice was still a little wobbly — but it did not matter at all. Because the island's lullaby was not one voice. It was everybody's voice, all together.

And every evening after that, Ivy climbed onto her rock, and all her friends gathered round, and they sang the island lullaby together, until the whole island was fast asleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When friends sing together, every voice matters — even a wobbly one.
