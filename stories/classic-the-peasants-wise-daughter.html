The Peasant's Wise Daughter

A Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once there was a king who loved riddles more than anything — more than gold, more than feasts, more than his velvet cloak.

"Answer my riddle," he would say, "and I will grant you a wish!"

One day he rode to a little village and asked the peasant folk, "Bring me three things by sunset: the richest thing, the gentlest thing, and the kindest thing."

The villagers scratched their heads. The richest thing? The gentlest thing? The kindest thing?

Only Mira, the peasant's young daughter, smiled. "I will go," she said.

All afternoon she thought and thought, walking through the golden wheat fields. By sunset she stood before the king with three small gifts.

"First," said Mira, holding up a handful of golden grain, "the richest thing. This little grain feeds the whole village — bread for the baker, seed for the farmer. What is richer than that?"

The king nodded slowly.

"Second," said Mira, holding up a puff of soft lamb's wool, "the gentlest thing. It never hurts anyone — it only keeps little lambs warm."

The king smiled.

"Third," said Mira — and she had nothing in her hands at all. She simply knelt beside a tired old farmer and helped him carry his heavy basket. "The kindest thing," she said. "A helping hand."

The whole court went quiet. Then the king laughed — a warm, delighted laugh. "No gold, no jewels — and yet you brought me the truest treasures! You are wise, little one."

"One more riddle," said the king, "the hardest of all. What travels fastest in the whole wide world?"

Mira thought for just a moment. "A kind thought, Your Majesty. It flies from heart to heart faster than any horse."

The king clapped his hands. "Wonderful!" From that day on, Mira became the kingdom's wisest helper. Whenever there was a quarrel or a puzzle, the people would say, "Ask Mira — she knows!" And she always answered with a smile, for she knew that wisdom grows best in a kind heart.

And every evening, Mira walked home through the golden fields, humming softly, rich in the only treasure that truly matters.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Wisdom and kindness are the greatest riches of all.
