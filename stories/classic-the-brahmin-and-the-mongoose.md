# The Brahmin and the Mongoose

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little village, beside a lane of mango trees, lived a kind man named Dev. Dev loved books, and he loved his family most of all.

With him lived his wife Meera, their tiny baby Aya, and a small mongoose named Miko.

Miko had soft brown fur, bright eyes, and a long, swishy tail. He loved to scamper around the courtyard, chasing butterflies and sniffing at fallen mango leaves.

Miko loved Aya most of all. When Aya slept in her little wooden cradle, Miko sat beside her like a tiny guard, quiet and watchful. When Aya woke up and gurgled, Miko's tail swished happily, and Aya giggled.

"You are a good friend, Miko," Meera would say, smiling.

One warm morning, Meera went to the well to fetch water. "Dev," she called, "I am going to the well. Please watch over Aya."

"Of course," said Dev. "I will watch her."

But soon a neighbour called from the lane, "Dev! Dev! Come quickly! A branch has fallen across my roof!"

Dev hurried out to help. "Miko," he said, "stay with Aya. I will be back in a moment."

Miko sat up straight beside the cradle, ears pricked and eyes bright. He would guard Aya, and he would do it well.

Then, from behind the big clay pot, came a soft, sliding sound. Sssss.

A snake slid into the courtyard, slow and quiet, its tongue flickering. It slid closer and closer to the cradle.

Miko saw it at once. His fur stood up. He gave a brave little chatter and leaped in front of the cradle.

"No," said Miko, in his mongoose way. "Not near Aya. Not one step closer."

The snake hissed and coiled. Miko darted left. He darted right. He was quick as a wink.

Round and round the courtyard they went, over the doorstep and around the clay pot. At last Miko chased the snake right out of the gate and far down the lane, away from the house, away from Aya.

When the snake was gone, Miko trotted back home, puffing a little. His paws and fur were smudged with dust, and one paw had a small red scrape where the snake's tail had grazed him.

Just then Dev hurried back through the gate. He saw the red smear on Miko's fur. He saw Miko standing by the cradle. And before he stopped to look, before he stopped to ask, a hot rush of anger filled him up like a storm.

"Miko!" cried Dev. "What have you done? Go away! Go away from this house and do not come back!"

Miko's bright eyes went wide. He did not understand. He tucked his tail low and padded away down the lane, alone and sad.

Dev ran to the cradle. And there was Aya, safe and sound, fast asleep, her tiny hands curled like flower buds. Aya was smiling in her sleep.

Then Dev looked again. On the ground beside the cradle, he saw the long sliding marks of the snake, going away from the house. And beyond the gate, he saw the snake itself, far down the lane, hurrying away into the grass.

Dev's heart went thump. His anger melted away like morning mist.

"Oh, Miko," he whispered. "You did not hurt Aya. You saved her. You chased the snake away. And I did not even stop to look. I spoke in anger, and I was wrong."

Dev ran down the lane, calling, "Miko! Miko! Come home!"

He found Miko sitting under a mango tree, his head down and his tail still.

Dev knelt beside him. He gently stroked Miko's soft brown fur. "I am so sorry," he said, his voice soft and warm. "You were brave and good. I should have looked before I spoke. Will you forgive me? Will you come home?"

Miko looked up, his tail swishing, swish, swish. He scampered right back home.

That evening, Dev told Meera the whole story — the snake, the chase, and the sorry that came after.

Meera gave Miko an extra mango slice. Aya gurgled and waved her tiny hands.

And from that day on, whenever Dev felt anger bubble up inside him, he took one slow breath, and he looked, and he listened, before he spoke.

That night, the courtyard was quiet. Aya slept in her cradle. Miko curled up beside her, safe and loved, and the mango leaves whispered softly in the breeze.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Always look and listen before you act in anger.*
