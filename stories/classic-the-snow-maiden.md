# The Snow Maiden

*A beloved world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Far away in a snowy forest, in a tiny cottage with a warm little fire, lived an old woodcutter named Ivan and his kind wife, Sonya.

They were happy, but they were lonely. They had no children of their own, and the winter nights were long and quiet. "Oh," sighed Sonya, "if only we had a little one to sing with."

One sparkling winter evening, they stepped outside together. The snow glittered like sugar under the moon. "Let's make a snow girl," said Ivan, with a twinkle in his eye. "Just for fun — a little friend for the evening."

So they rolled and patted and shaped. They gave her soft snow curls and a sweet little smile. Sonya pressed two rose petals onto her cheeks — soft, soft, soft. Ivan tickled her nose. "There," he laughed. "Hello, little one!"

And then — *shiver-shimmer!* — something wonderful happened. The snow girl blinked… stretched… and laughed a tiny, silvery laugh.

"Oh!" gasped Sonya. "She's alive! She's alive!"

They wrapped her in a warm blue shawl and named her Snegurochka — the Snow Maiden.

Snegurochka was sweet and kind. Her cheeks glowed like rose petals. Her hair shone like frost in the morning sun. And oh, how she loved to play!

All winter long, she slid down the snowy hills with the village children. *Whee!* went the sled. *Whoosh!* went the snow. She sang little songs in her silvery voice, and the children sang back: "Snegurochka! Snegurochka! Sing, sing, sing!"

Ivan and Sonya were no longer lonely. The little cottage rang with laughter and smelled of warm bread.

Every evening, Snegurochka would curl up by the warm little fire, and Sonya would hum a soft lullaby. "Again! Again!" Snegurochka would giggle, clapping her snowy hands. And Sonya would sing it again — soft and slow — until the fire crackled low and the stars came out to listen.

But then — slowly, softly — the days grew longer. The sun peeked out a little brighter. The first bird sang, "Spring is coming! Spring is coming!"

And Snegurochka grew quiet. She sat by the window, watching the sunshine. She loved the warmth of her new friends… but the warm sun made her feel all soft and sleepy.

"Dear one," said the kind old grandmother of the village, patting her hand gently, "nothing truly kind ever disappears. It only changes into something new."

On the first warm day of spring, Snegurochka stood in the sunny meadow. She closed her eyes. A soft breeze lifted her hair like falling snowflakes.

And very, very gently — *shimmer, shimmer* — she turned into a sparkling stream, dancing away down the hill, laughing her silvery laugh.

Where her little footprints had been, something magical happened. Up popped the first flowers of spring — tiny white snowdrops, nodding their heads in the breeze. *Hello, hello,* they seemed to say.

The village children smiled their softest smiles. They planted a whole garden of snowdrops, just for her. "Thank you, Snegurochka," they whispered. "Thank you."

And every winter, when the first snow falls — *soft, soft, soft* — the children build a little snow girl again and whisper, "Welcome back, Snegurochka."

Ivan and Sonya are never lonely now. They have the love of the whole village… and every spring, the snowdrops bloom, soft and white, just like her smile.

Sleep now, little one. Sweet dreams. 🌙

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Love never melts away — it blooms again in new ways.*
