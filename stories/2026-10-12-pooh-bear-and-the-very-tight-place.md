# Pooh Bear and the Very Tight Place

One soft morning in the Hundred Acre Wood, Winnie-the-Pooh woke up with an empty tummy and a very hungry thought.

"I know just the thing for an empty tummy," said Pooh. "A little visit to Rabbit."

So Pooh hummed his breakfast hum and ambled down the twisty path to Rabbit's house — round door, warm welcome.

"Is anybody home?" called Pooh, knocking with his paw.

"Hello, Pooh!" said Rabbit, opening the door wide. "Come in! Would you like a little something to eat?"

Pooh settled at the table, and Rabbit set out honey and bread and a nice warm pot of tea. Pooh took one little spoonful of honey… then another… then a bigger one… and then, somehow, three whole pots had quite disappeared.

"Oh, Pooh," giggled Rabbit, "you've eaten it all!"

"Thank you, Rabbit," said Pooh, rubbing his round tummy. "And now I must be going home for a small nap."

But when Pooh tried to squeeze back through Rabbit's round front door — oh dear. His tummy would not fit. He wiggled. He waggled. Stuck fast, half in and half out, like a cork in a bottle.

Rabbit pushed from inside. Pooh pulled from outside. Nothing moved at all, except Pooh's ears.

So Rabbit fetched Christopher Robin, who brought Piglet, who brought Eeyore, and they all pulled together. Pooh's front paws were out in the sunshine, his back paws still inside the house, and he stayed exactly where he was.

"He'll have to get thinner," said Christopher Robin. "About a week."

"A week!" squeaked Piglet.

Pooh sighed a big round sigh. "I will wait," he said bravely.

And his friends did not leave him. Piglet sat on the doorstep and read him his favourite story. Eeyore sat quietly beside him, which was his way of being kind. Rabbit brought tiny sips of water through the door. Christopher Robin stayed until the stars came out, promising Pooh that soon he would be slim enough to pop right through.

Day by day, Pooh's tummy grew a little smaller, and his friends kept him company — and not one of them ever said it was his fault for eating three pots of honey.

And then, on the seventh morning, Christopher Robin took hold of Pooh's front paws and gave one great pull — and POP! Out came Pooh, tumbling onto the soft grass like a ball, laughing.

"Next time," Rabbit whispered, hugging him right there on the doorstep, "just one pot."

"Just one pot," Pooh agreed. "And I shall always save room to get home."

That night, Pooh curled up in his cosy bed and drifted off with a happy hum.

Sleep now, Pooh Bear.

The end. 🌙
