# The Wolf and the Seven Little Goats

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a little house at the edge of the green meadow, lived Mother Goat and her seven little goats.

The seven little goats loved to play all day — hop, skip, and jump around the little house.

One morning, Mother Goat put on her shawl and picked up her basket.

"I am going to the market," she said. "Stay inside and do not open the door to anyone. If someone knocks, peek through the crack first and look at the feet. My feet are soft and white. The wolf's feet are big and grey."

"Yes, Mother!" said the seven little goats. "We will be careful!"

Off went Mother Goat, clip-clop, clip-clop, down the path to the market.

Soon after, there came a knock on the door. Knock, knock, knock!

"Little goats, little goats, open the door! It is your mother, back from the market!" called a rough, deep voice.

But the eldest little goat peeped through the crack in the door and saw a big grey paw.

"That is not Mother!" she whispered. "Mother's feet are soft and white. Those feet are big and grey!"

"Go away, big grey wolf!" cried all seven little goats together. "We will not open the door!"

The wolf grumbled and tiptoed away, his tummy rumbling with hunger.

But the little goats were clever. They knew the wolf might come back.

"Quick, hide!" said the eldest little goat. "The wolf must find nobody here!"

One little goat hid inside the big old clock — tick-tock, tick-tock. One hid under the bed, curled up small and still. One hid behind the curtain, quiet as a mouse. One hid behind the door, one under the table, one in the cupboard, and the littlest one hid in the basket by the fire.

Sure enough, the wolf came back. He pushed the door open and looked all around the house.

He looked in the kitchen. Nobody. He looked behind the chairs. Nobody. He looked under the table. Nobody!

The wolf sniffed and searched and searched — but the seven little goats were hidden so well that he could not find a single one.

At last, the wolf gave up. He tiptoed out of the house, hungrier than ever, and padded away into the forest.

Then the little goats heard a happy sound — clip-clop, clip-clop — Mother Goat was home!

Mother Goat opened the door and called, "Little goats, where are you?"

Out they popped, one by one — from the clock, from under the bed, from behind the curtain, from behind the door, from under the table, from the cupboard, and from the basket!

Mother Goat counted them, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven — seven happy little goats, safe and sound!

"You were so clever and so brave," said Mother Goat, hugging them all tight. "Now, warm milk and a song for my seven clever little goats!"

And as the moon rose over the meadow, the little house was full of warm milk, happy songs, and sleepy little goats.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Clever little ones who listen and stick together stay safe.*
