The Dog and the Wolf

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One cold winter evening, a thin, hungry wolf named Wally padded through the snowy forest. His tummy rumbled and grumbled. He had not found a proper meal for days.

By the light of the moon, Wally met a big, round, glossy dog named Bruno. Bruno's coat shone, his tail wagged, and he looked as well-fed as a birthday cake.

"Good evening, friend!" barked Bruno cheerfully. "You look rather thin. Have you had your supper?"

Wally sighed. "No," he said. "Food in the forest is hard to find in winter. Every creature runs faster than me."

"Then come and live with me!" said Bruno. "My master feeds me every day — warm porridge in the morning, tasty bones at noon, and a cosy basket by the fire at night. You will never be hungry again."

Wally's eyes grew round. "Warm porridge? Tasty bones? A cosy basket? That sounds wonderful! What must I do?"

"Almost nothing," said Bruno. "Just bark when strangers come, guard the house, and wag your tail when my master pats your head."

Wally trotted happily beside Bruno along the snowy path. Then, in the moonlight, he noticed something — a bare, worn patch around Bruno's neck, where the fur was rubbed thin.

"What is that mark around your neck?" asked Wally.

"Oh, that?" said Bruno. "That is where my collar sits. My master ties me to my kennel with a long rope during the day, so I stay near the house."

Wally stopped walking. "You are tied up?" he asked slowly. "You cannot run wherever you like? You cannot roam the forest when you please?"

"Well, no," said Bruno. "But I have porridge and bones and a warm basket!"

Wally looked at the moon, at the wide, snowy forest, and at the stars twinkling above the trees. He took a deep breath of cold, free air.

"Thank you, Bruno," said Wally kindly, "but I will keep my freedom. A full tummy is lovely, but being able to run under the moon and choose my own path is worth more to me than all the porridge in the world."

So Wally padded back into the forest, still thin but with his head held high. And though he was hungry that night, he sang a happy little song to the moon, because he was free.

Bruno wagged his tail and trotted home to his warm basket, and both friends were glad — each in his own way.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A comfortable life is sweet, but freedom to choose your own path is a treasure too.
