# The Old Tiger and the Gold Bangle

*A gentle Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

By a wide, slow river lived an old tiger.

His stripes had faded a little, and his walk was slow. But his eyes were still sharp, and his smile was still sweet — a little too sweet.

One morning, the old tiger sat by the riverbank, polishing something shiny in his paws.

It was a gold bangle. It glittered in the sun — glint, glint, glint!

Soon a traveller named Kabir came walking along the path, with a bundle on his back.

"Kind traveller! Dear friend!" called the tiger in his sweetest voice. "Please come closer! Look at this beautiful gold bangle. I am old now, and I cannot wear it. I want to give it to you — a gift, just for you!"

Kabir stopped. A gold bangle! For free!

He took one step closer. Then another.

But then he looked more carefully.

The tiger's teeth were long and sharp. The tiger's paws were big and muddy. And the tiger's smile was a little too sweet.

Kabir remembered his grandmother's words: "A gift that asks you to walk into danger is not a gift at all."

So Kabir stopped, right where he was, and smiled politely.

"Thank you, kind tiger," he said. "That is a very beautiful bangle. But I do not need gold to be happy. My own two hands are gift enough for me. Keep your bangle, and stay well!"

And Kabir turned and walked on down the path, safe and sound, humming a little tune.

The old tiger watched him go. He sighed a long sigh.

"Ah," said the tiger. "That one has wise eyes. Sweet words do not work on wise eyes."

He polished his gold bangle again — glint, glint, glint — and waited by the river.

But Kabir went home and told everyone in his village, "If a stranger offers you gold and asks you to come close to danger, just smile, say thank you, and walk on!"

And from that day, the children of the village sang a little song whenever they walked by the river:

"Shiny gift, sweet talk, sharp teeth — thank you, no thank you, and keep walking!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A free gift that hides danger is no gift — trust your wise eyes and walk on.*
