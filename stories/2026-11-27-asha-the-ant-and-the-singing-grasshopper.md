# Asha the Ant and the Singing Grasshopper

In a sunny meadow full of soft grass and tall daisies, there lived a little red ant named Asha.

Asha wore a tiny yellow scarf, and every morning she marched out of her anthill home with her little basket on her back.

All summer long, Asha gathered seeds — one by one, crumb by crumb — and tucked them away in her cozy pantry under the old oak root.

"A little every day," she hummed to herself, "and soon there will be plenty."

Nearby, in the same sunny meadow, lived a green grasshopper named Gus.

Gus had long springy legs, a shiny green coat, and a little brown fiddle that he played from morning until night.

"Tra-la-la!" sang Gus, hopping and twiddling his fiddle. "The sun is warm and the day is long. Why work when you can sing?"

One morning, Gus hopped over to Asha and called, "Come and sing with me, Asha! The daisies are dancing!"

Asha smiled and shook her head. "I have three more seeds to carry home first," she said. "But you may sing while I work."

So Gus played his fiddle, and Asha marched — step, step, step — all the way home with her seeds.

Day after day, the sun grew softer and the air grew cooler, and the meadow turned gold.

The daisies drooped, the leaves drifted down, and one morning Asha found frost on her little yellow scarf.

"Winter is coming," said Asha. She carried her last seed home and tucked her pantry door shut.

That night, the wind blew cold, and soft white snow covered the meadow like a blanket.

Gus shivered in his little hollow log. His fiddle was quiet, his tummy rumbled, and there was not one crumb to eat.

He hopped through the snow — hop, hop, hop — all the way to Asha's anthill, and knocked very softly on her door.

"Please," said Gus, his voice small and cold. "May I come in? I have no food, and my log is so cold."

Asha opened the door wide. "Come in, Gus," she said. "There is room by the fire, and soup enough for two."

So Gus sat by the warm fire, ate a bowl of Asha's seed soup, and played a soft, sleepy tune on his fiddle — just for her.

And when the snow fell quiet outside, Asha tucked her little yellow scarf around them both, and the two friends drifted off to sleep, warm and full and safe.

Sleep now, little Asha.

The end. 🌙
