Nasruddin and the Lost Ring

A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a small village, there lived a funny man named Nasruddin. Everyone in the village loved Nasruddin, because he always made them laugh.

One warm evening, Nasruddin was walking in his garden. The birds were singing their last songs. Tweet, tweet, tweet.

Nasruddin was looking at the stars when — oh no! His ring slipped off his finger. Plip! It fell into the tall grass.

"My ring!" cried Nasruddin. "My shiny ring is gone!"

But the garden was dark. He could not see a thing.

"Hmm," said Nasruddin. "It is too dark in here!"

So Nasruddin walked out of his garden and stood under the bright street lamp. The lamp glowed yellow and warm.

Nasruddin bent down and began to look. He looked left and right. He peeked under his shoes.

Just then, his neighbour Ali came walking by. "Nasruddin, what are you doing?" asked Ali.

"I lost my ring," said Nasruddin. "I am looking for it."

"Did you lose it here, under the lamp?" asked Ali.

"No," said Nasruddin. "I lost it in my garden."

Ali blinked. "Then why are you looking under the lamp?"

Nasruddin smiled a big smile. "Because the light is better here!" he said.

Ali laughed. "Ha, ha, ha! Your ring is in the garden, not under the lamp!"

Soon more neighbours came. Fatima came with her basket. Old Uncle Rahim came too. Little Sara came skipping.

"Why are you laughing?" they asked.

Ali told them Nasruddin's funny answer. And they all laughed too. "Ha, ha, ha!"

But then Fatima said something kind. "Friends," she said, "laughing is good. But let us help Nasruddin find his ring."

"Yes!" said little Sara. "Let us fetch a lantern!"

So they all went together into the dark garden. Uncle Rahim carried a big lantern. It glowed soft and warm. Swish, swish went the tall grass.

"Look here!" said Ali.

Little Sara bent down low. Suddenly she cried, "I see it! I see something shiny!"

There, in the soft green grass, something twinkled. Twinkle, twinkle, twinkle. It was Nasruddin's ring!

Nasruddin picked it up. It shone in the lantern light. "My ring!" he cried. "Thank you, my dear neighbours!"

Everyone smiled. Nasruddin put the ring back on his finger, safe and snug.

"Nasruddin," said Ali with a grin, "next time, look where you lost it!"

"You are right," laughed Nasruddin. "Next time I will look in the garden — with a lantern!"

And they all walked home together, laughing under the stars.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Look where you lost it, not where it is easy to look.
