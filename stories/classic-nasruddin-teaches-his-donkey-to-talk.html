Nasruddin Teaches His Donkey to Talk

A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a little town with sunny yellow walls and one very dusty road, lived a clever man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin was not rich. But he had two treasures: a quick, twinkling mind — and a small gray donkey with the softest ears in the whole town.

One morning, the king made a grand announcement from his palace balcony.

"Listen, everyone!" cried the king in his big, booming voice. "I will give a whole bag of gold — a whole bag, clinking and shining! — to anyone who can teach a donkey to talk!"

The crowd went "Oooooh!"

Mothers hugged their children. Children pointed at the palace. For a whole minute, nobody said a word — they were all too busy dreaming.

A bag of gold! Everyone dreamed of it — the baker, the tailor, the blacksmith. But everyone also knew the same thing: donkeys bray — that's their loud hee-haw sound — but donkeys do not talk.

So nobody stepped forward. Nobody — except Nasruddin.

Nasruddin walked through the crowd, right up to the palace steps, and gave the king his deepest bow.

"Your Majesty," he said in a calm, clear voice, "give me ten years, and my little donkey will speak."

All the king's helpers gasped. "GASP!"

The king clapped his hands. "Wonderful! Ten years it is — and the bag of gold will be yours!"

And so Nasruddin led his little gray donkey home, with everyone staring after him.

But on the way, his old friend hurried up beside him, looking very worried.

"Nasruddin!" he whispered. "Donkeys cannot talk! Whatever will you do?"

Nasruddin just smiled. He gave his friend a slow, secret wink.

"My friend," he said softly, "listen. In ten years, the king may pass away. I may pass away. Or — who knows? — the donkey may learn to talk!"

His friend stared. Then he laughed and laughed, right there in the dusty road — laughed till his sides ached and his eyes watered.

And Nasruddin laughed too, and patted his donkey's soft, soft ears, and the donkey swished his tail, pleased with all the happiness.

That evening, Nasruddin sat outside his little house and shared his warm bread with the donkey. A soft breeze moved through the town, carrying the smell of evening fires. The sky turned pink, then purple, then deep, deep blue. One by one, the stars came out, like tiny candles being lit.

The donkey rested his chin on Nasruddin's knee. He twitched his soft ears at a passing moth, and said —

"Hee-haw!"

Nasruddin chuckled and rubbed the soft spot between the donkey's ears. "Close enough, my friend," he whispered. "Close enough."

A night bird called once, far away. The breeze settled. And under the quiet stars, the two friends sat together, happy and calm — with no worries at all, and ten whole years stretching softly ahead of them, like a long, sleepy road.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A wise answer is better than a hasty one.
