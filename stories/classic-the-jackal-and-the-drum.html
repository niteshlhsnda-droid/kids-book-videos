The Jackal and the Drum

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green forest full of tall trees and soft moss, there lived a young jackal named Jilu. Jilu had pointy ears, a bushy tail, and a very curious nose.

One quiet afternoon, Jilu was trotting along a forest path when suddenly he heard a sound that made his fur stand on end.

BOOM-BOOM! BOOM-BOOM!

"Oh!" gasped Jilu. He jumped behind a big bush and hid, his heart thump-thump-thumping. "What is that terrible noise? Is it a giant? Is it a monster?"

BOOM-BOOM! BOOM-BOOM!

The sound rolled through the trees again. Jilu's ears wobbled. His tail tucked right under his tummy. He wanted to run all the way home.

But then he thought, "What if it is just a sound, and nothing more? I will peek. Just one tiny peek."

So Jilu crept forward, one soft step at a time. Step, step, step. He stopped behind a tree and listened.

BOOM-BOOM!

The sound was coming from a little clearing just ahead. Jilu took a deep breath and peeked around the tree.

And what did he see? Not a giant. Not a monster. Just an old drum, lying on its side in the grass.

Long ago, a travelling band of musicians had passed through the forest, and one of their drums had been left behind. Now the wind blew through the clearing, shaking the drum's loose skin, and each shake made it go BOOM-BOOM!

Jilu blinked. Then he blinked again.

"Is that all?" he whispered. "Just a drum?"

He crept a little closer. The wind shook the drum again. BOOM! Jilu jumped, but then he giggled. "It cannot hurt me. It is only a drum."

Jilu walked right up to the drum and looked at it closely. It was round and brown, with a shiny skin stretched over the top, a little loose at the edges.

Very gently, Jilu lifted one paw and tapped it. Tap. The drum said, "Boom."

Jilu laughed. He tapped it again, a little harder. Tap, tap. "Boom, boom!" answered the drum.

Soon Jilu was tapping a happy little rhythm. Tap-tap-tap, boom-boom-boom! He danced around the drum on his four paws, his tail wagging, his ears bouncing.

"Ha!" he laughed. "I was frightened of an empty drum! The sound was big, but the drum was not scary at all."

From that day on, whenever Jilu heard a loud noise in the forest, he did not run away. First, he stopped. Then he listened. Then he looked, to see what was really making the sound.

And very often, it was nothing frightening at all — just the wind in the trees, or a woodpecker tapping, or rain pattering on the leaves.

That evening, as the sun went down and the forest grew quiet, Jilu curled up in his cosy den. The drum sat silently in the clearing, waiting for the morning wind.

Jilu yawned a big, sleepy yawn. "Loud sounds are not always scary," he murmured. "Sometimes they are just drums, waiting for a friend to tap them."

And with that, he closed his eyes and drifted off to sleep, dreaming of soft drums and gentle breezes.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Loud sounds are not always scary.
