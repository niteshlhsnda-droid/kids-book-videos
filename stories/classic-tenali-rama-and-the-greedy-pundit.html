Tenali Rama and the Greedy Pundit

A clever Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the golden city of Vijayanagara, there lived a wise and merry king named Krishnadevaraya. And in his court lived the cleverest, funniest man in all the land — Tenali Rama.

One fine morning, a proud pundit came striding into the court. His name was Pandit Ghamandilal, and he was as proud as a peacock and as greedy as a hungry goat.

He bowed to the king and announced in a booming voice, "O King! I have come to DEBATE your Tenali Rama — a battle of clever words! But first — as is only proper for a great scholar like me — I demand a grand royal feast as my fee!"

The courtiers gasped. Tenali Rama's eyes twinkled. "A feast FIRST?" he said, bowing very low. "Of course, O great Panditji! The grandest feast in all Vijayanagara!"

The pundit smiled proudly and patted his round belly. "Excellent! I am VERY hungry!"

Soon the servants came, carrying big, green banana leaves. And on those leaves — oh my! Mountains of soft, white rice. Bowls of golden dal. Crispy pakoras. And jalebis, curling like orange ribbons, dripping with syrup!

The pundit's mouth watered. "At last!" he cried, rolling up his sleeves.

But wait — everything was STEAMING. The rice sent up hot clouds of steam. The dal bubbled and hissed. The jalebis sizzled. Everything was so hot, so hot, that the pundit could not touch a single bite!

He blew on the rice. Hoo-hoo! He waved his hand over the dal. Hoo-hoo! He tried to pick up a jalebi — "OW!" He burned his finger and shook it in the air!

The whole court tried not to giggle. The king hid his smile behind his hand. Tenali Rama's shoulders shook with silent laughter.

"How can I eat THIS?" the pundit huffed, his face red as a tomato. "It is too hot! Too hot!" He pushed the banana leaf away, stood up, and marched off — his belly still rumbling with hunger.

The court could hold back no longer. "Ha-ha-ha!" everyone laughed — not cruelly, but the way you laugh at a funny, silly moment.

That evening, there was a soft knock at the pundit's door. It was Tenali Rama, carrying a tray. And on that tray was the SAME feast — rice, dal, pakoras, jalebis — but now everything was just right. Warm, not hot. Fresh, and ready to eat.

"Eat, Panditji," said Tenali kindly. "A feast is for enjoying slowly, not for grabbing in a hurry."

The pundit tasted a jalebi. It melted in his mouth, sweet and soft. He ate slowly. He smiled. And then — he laughed at himself! "Ha-ha! What a fool I was!" he said. "I was in such a hurry to grab my prize that I forgot the sweetest rule of all."

Tenali Rama laughed with him, and the two of them shared the meal like old friends.

"And what is that rule, Panditji?" asked Tenali with a twinkle.

"That patience," said the pundit, licking syrup off his fingers, "makes everything sweeter. And humility is the best spice of all."

And that night, as the moon rose over Vijayanagara, Pandit Ghamandilal lay on his soft bed with a full, happy belly, and drifted to sleep dreaming of sweet, golden jalebis.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Greed spoils the sweetest things; humility is the best spice.
