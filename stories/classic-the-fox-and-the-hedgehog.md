# The Fox and the Hedgehog

*A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green meadow, there lived a quick red fox named Rusty.

Rusty loved to run. He ran through the tall grass. He ran around the old oak tree. He ran so fast that the wind whistled in his ears.

One sunny afternoon, Rusty was chasing a butterfly — flutter, flutter — and he did not watch where he was going.

SQUELCH! His paws sank into a soft, muddy hollow. The mud was sticky and deep, and it held his feet tight.

"Oh no!" said Rusty. "I'm stuck!"

Soon a cloud of tiny flies came buzzing around him. "Bzz! Bzz! Bzz!" They tickled his nose. They tickled his ears. They tickled the tip of his tail.

"Go away!" laughed Rusty, though it was not very funny. "Stop tickling me!"

Just then, a small hedgehog named Hari waddled along the path. He saw Rusty stuck in the mud, with flies buzzing all around him.

"Poor Rusty!" said Hari. "Shall I chase those flies away for you? I can gobble them up, one by one!"

Rusty thought for a moment. Then he shook his head, very slowly.

"Thank you, Hari, but no," he said. "These flies have already had their lunch. They are full and sleepy now. If you chase them away, a whole new crowd of hungry flies will come buzzing — and they will tickle me even more!"

Hari nodded. "Then I will stay with you instead," he said. He waddled off and came back with a big, green leaf, which he held over Rusty like a little umbrella, so the hot sun would not shine in his eyes.

Then he sat down beside the muddy hollow and began to sing a soft, scratchy song.

"La-la-laaa, la-la-laaa," sang Hari.

Rusty smiled. The sun grew warm. The mud began to dry. And little by little, Rusty's paws came loose — squelch, squelch, SQUELCH!

At last, Rusty jumped out of the hollow with a happy leap! "Hooray!" he cried. "I'm free!"

"And the flies are gone too," said Hari, smiling. "See? The sun sent them home to bed."

Rusty gave his friend a big, warm hug. "Thank you for staying with me, Hari. You are the best friend in the whole meadow."

From that day on, whenever Rusty ran too fast, he remembered to watch where he was going — and Hari always walked beside him, just in case.

And the two friends walked home together, side by side, as the sun set soft and golden over the grass.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sometimes the trouble you know is better than a new trouble — and a friend who stays beside you is the best help of all.*
