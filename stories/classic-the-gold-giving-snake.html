The Gold-Giving Snake

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, there lived a poor farmer named Haru and his young son, Nikku, in a little mud hut that always smelled of woodsmoke and warm earth.

Behind their hut stood an old anthill — a small mud hill where the ants lived. And in that anthill lived someone very special — the king of the snakes, with dark scales that glittered in the sunshine.

One day, Nikku said, "Father! The snake king eats the rats in our field. Can I give him a thank-you treat?"

So Nikku poured sweet, warm milk into a little clay bowl. Then he set it gently by the anthill. "Please drink, snake king," he said softly.

The snake king slid out, drank the milk — slurp, slurp, slurp — and — plink! — something dropped out of his mouth into the empty bowl. Can you guess what it was? … A shiny gold coin! Then he slid back home.

Every day Nikku brought milk, and every day the snake king left a gold coin. Soon their hut was full of mangoes and warm rotis — and they shared everything, just like they always had.

Then one day, Haru's cousin came to visit. His eyes went big and round at the pile of gold coins. Can you guess what the cousin was thinking? … "Why wait for one coin a day?" he thought. "Inside that anthill there must be piles and piles of gold!"

The next morning, while Nikku was away, the cousin tiptoed to the anthill with an empty basket. "I'll take all the gold at once!" he whispered, and began to dig with his hands.

But the snake king was already there. He lifted his head, slow and steady. "Sssstop," he said softly. "You came to a friend's home to take all his gold… so there will be no more coins for you."

The cousin set down his empty basket, pressed his hands together, said sorry, and walked quietly home.

The snake king slid away to the quiet forest after that. But every evening, Nikku still set a little bowl of milk by the old anthill — just in case. And every morning the bowl was empty — because in the quiet of the night, the snake king would slide out of the forest, drink the sweet milk all up, and smile.

And as the moon rose over the hut, Haru and Nikku drifted off to sleep, Nikku's head warm and heavy on his father's shoulder.

Goodnight, little one. The end. 🌙

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Greed spoils good things — be thankful for what you have.
