The Thief and the Watchdog

A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, at the edge of a quiet village, there stood a little farmhouse with a red roof.

Every night, a big brown watchdog named Sheru guarded the farmhouse. He had floppy ears, a waggy tail, and a very loud bark.

"Woof! Woof!" he barked at the moon. "I am Sheru, and I keep this house safe!"

Inside the farmhouse lived a kind farmer. Every evening, he patted Sheru's head and said, "Good boy, Sheru. Watch over us tonight."

And every night, Sheru sat by the gate, his ears pricked up, listening to the crickets sing and the night wind rustle through the wheat.

One dark night, when the moon was hiding behind a cloud, a thief crept up to the farmhouse. He carried a big empty sack, and in his hand he held a big, juicy bone.

"Shh," he whispered to Sheru. "Good dog. Here is a bone for you. Eat it, and let me pass quietly."

Sheru sniffed the bone. Oh! It smelled so good — meaty and rich and delicious. His mouth watered. His tail gave a little wag all by itself.

But Sheru stopped. He thought and thought.

"If I eat his bone tonight," he thought, "he will come back tomorrow night with another bone. And the night after that. And soon, he will carry away everything in the farmhouse — the farmer's grain, the farmer's pots, even the farmer's warm winter blanket!"

Sheru looked at the bone. Then he looked at the farmhouse, where the farmer was sleeping safe and sound.

"No," said Sheru. He stood up tall, threw back his head, and barked as loudly as he could.

"WOOF! WOOF! WOOF! WOOF!"

The bark echoed across the fields. The crickets stopped singing. The thief dropped the bone and ran away as fast as his legs could carry him!

"Thief! Thief!" cried the farmer, waking up. He ran to the door with his lantern. "Sheru! Good boy! You woke me just in time!"

The farmer hugged Sheru tight. He gave him a big bowl of warm milk. He gave him a soft blanket by the fire. And he gave him the biggest, juiciest bone from his own kitchen — a bone far better than the thief's bone.

"You are a loyal friend, Sheru," said the farmer, scratching behind Sheru's floppy ears. "A friend like you cannot be bought."

That night, Sheru curled up on his blanket, warm and proud. Outside, the crickets sang again. The moon peeped out from behind its cloud. And Sheru counted the twinkling stars through the window — one, two, three, four, five — until his eyes grew heavy and he drifted off to sleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A loyal friend cannot be bought — and a kind heart is worth more than a juicy bone.
