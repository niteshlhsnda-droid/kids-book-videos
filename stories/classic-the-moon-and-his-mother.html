The Moon and His Mother

A classic Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

High above the sleepy earth, where the night sky glitters like a velvet blanket, there lived a little Moon.

The little Moon was round and pale and very, very proud. Every night he climbed up into the sky and shone down on the sleeping world. *Twinkle, twinkle,* went the stars around him, and he beamed right back at them.

One evening, the little Moon came hurrying to his Mother, who lived just past the tallest cloud.

"Mother!" he cried. "I want a coat! A fine, fine coat, all velvet blue with silver buttons. Then I will look as grand as I feel."

Mother Moon smiled her soft silver smile.

"Of course, my darling," she said. "But first, I must measure you, so the coat fits just right."

*Snip, snap,* went her measuring tape. *Snip, snap,* all around his round little tummy.

"Hold still now," she laughed. "You tickle!"

Then Mother Moon sat down at her little wooden table, lit a candle, and began to sew. *Stitch, stitch, stitch,* went her needle. *Hum, hum, hum,* went her song.

She sewed through the whole long night. And when the first sleepy sunbeam peeked over the mountains, the coat was done.

"Here it is!" she said proudly. "Try it on!"

The little Moon slipped one arm in, then the other. But oh! The coat would not close. Not even a little bit. His tummy had grown so round and fat!

*Snappity-snap!* went the buttons, flying everywhere. *Ping, ping, ping!*

"Oh dear," said Mother Moon. "You have grown so big and round! I will sew you a bigger one."

So she measured him again — *snip, snap* — and sewed through the whole long night again. *Stitch, stitch, stitch. Hum, hum, hum.*

"Try this one!" she said at sunrise.

The little Moon slipped one arm in, then the other. The coat hung and flapped and sagged all around him, like a tent. *Flop, flop, flop.* He had grown slim again, like a silver boat!

"Oh no!" giggled the little Moon. "First too tight, then too loose! Mother, I am impossible to dress!"

"Oh, my silly little moonbeam," laughed Mother Moon. She tapped her chin and thought very, very hard. "You grow round, and then you grow slim, and then you grow round again — and that will never, ever stop."

"What shall we do?" asked the little Moon.

Mother Moon took down from her shelf a great bundle of something soft and shimmery. It was starlight — the softest, lightest cloth in all the sky. It shimmered and glimmered and made a sound like tiny bells. *Shhh-immer, shhh-immer.*

She wrapped it gently around her little Moon, round and round, and tied it with a moonbeam ribbon.

"There," she whispered. "A cloak of starlight. It stretches when you grow round, and gathers close when you grow slim. It always, always fits."

The little Moon twirled and swirled in his new cloak. *Shhh-immer, shhh-immer.* Oh, how it glowed! Oh, how it shone!

"Mother," he said, "it is the finest coat in the whole sky."

"Of course it is," said Mother Moon, kissing his round cheek. "It was sewn with love."

That night, the little Moon climbed up into the sky, wearing his cloak of starlight, and shone down on the sleeping world — soft, and silvery, and cozy.

And if you look up tonight, you will see him up there still, wearing his starlight cloak, snuggled in the arms of the dark blue sky.

*Shhh-immer, shhh-immer,* whispers the starlight.

*Shush... shush... shush,* whispers the night.

Goodnight, little Moon. Goodnight, dear Mother. Goodnight, twinkling stars.

Goodnight, sleepy you.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A mother's love fits every shape — just the way you are is just right.
