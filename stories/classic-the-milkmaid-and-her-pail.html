The Milkmaid and Her Pail

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Early one sunny morning, a milkmaid named Molly set off down the lane to market.

On her head she balanced a shining pail, full to the brim with fresh, creamy milk. She walked carefully, step by step, so that not a single drop would spill.

"Swish, swish," went her skirt. "Plip, plop," went the milk, very gently, inside the pail.

As Molly walked, she began to dream.

"When I sell this milk," she thought, "I shall have enough coins to buy a basket of eggs."

Molly smiled at the thought. "And when the eggs hatch, I shall have twelve fluffy little chicks, all yellow and soft."

She walked a little further, and her dream grew bigger.

"When the chicks grow up, they will lay lots and lots of eggs. I shall sell those eggs at market and buy the prettiest dress in the whole village — a dress with blue ribbons and shiny buttons!"

Molly's smile grew wider and wider.

"And when I wear my new dress to the village fair, everyone will say, 'Oh, look at Molly! Doesn't she look lovely?' And I shall toss my head like this —"

And Molly tossed her head, just a little, to practise looking lovely.

But oh no! As she tossed her head, the pail wobbled. It tipped. It tumbled.

SPLASH! All the creamy milk poured out onto the dusty lane, and the pail rolled away, empty.

Molly stood very still. Her eggs were gone. Her chicks were gone. Her dress with the blue ribbons was gone — all before they had ever been real.

For a moment, Molly felt like crying. Then she looked at the puddle of milk, and then at the empty pail, and then — she began to giggle.

"What a silly dreamer I am!" she laughed. "I was counting my chicks before they had even hatched!"

Just then, her mother came hurrying down the lane. "Molly! What happened?"

"I dreamed too big and tossed my head, and out went the milk," said Molly, still giggling.

Her mother smiled and gave her a hug. "Never mind, my dear. The cow will give more milk tomorrow. And dreams are lovely things — just keep your eyes on the path while you dream them."

Molly picked up her pail and walked home beside her mother. The sun was warm, the birds were singing, and Molly was already dreaming again — but this time, she kept her head very, very steady.

That evening, Molly helped her mother milk the cow, and she told the cow all about her dream. The cow just blinked her big brown eyes and said, "Moo."

And Molly laughed all over again.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Dreams are lovely, but keep your eyes on the path — do not count your chicks before they hatch.
