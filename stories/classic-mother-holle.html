Mother Holle

A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there was a widow who had two daughters.

The elder daughter, Rosa, was kind and hard-working. She spun wool from morning until evening, and she always helped with a smile.

The younger daughter, Petra, was lazy. She did not like to spin, she did not like to help, and she spent most of the day yawning by the fire.

One day, Rosa was spinning by the well when — plop! — her spindle slipped from her fingers and fell deep into the water.

"Oh no!" cried Rosa. "My spindle!"

Her stepmother scolded her. "Then go and fetch it, silly girl!"

So brave Rosa climbed down into the well, deeper and deeper, until — whoosh! — she tumbled out onto a beautiful meadow full of flowers.

"What a lovely place!" said Rosa, brushing herself off.

As she walked through the meadow, she came to an oven full of bread. "Take us out! Take us out!" cried the loaves. "We are baked and ready!"

Rosa opened the oven door and lifted out the golden loaves, one by one, placing them gently on a clean cloth.

Next she came to an apple tree, heavy with ripe red apples. "Shake us! Shake us!" called the apples. "We are ripe and ready to fall!"

Rosa shook the tree gently until all the apples lay in a neat pile on the grass.

At last, Rosa came to a little cottage where an old woman with kind eyes sat by the fire. "Welcome, dear," said the old woman. "I am Mother Holle. Will you help me keep house? All you must do is shake my featherbed well, so the feathers fly — that is how snow falls on the world below."

Rosa stayed and worked happily. She shook the featherbeds until the feathers flew like snowflakes, and Mother Holle was very pleased.

"You have a good heart," said Mother Holle. "Let me take you home."

She led Rosa to a great golden gate. As Rosa stepped through it, a shower of gold coins rained down over her, sparkling in her hair and on her dress!

Rosa returned home, glittering with gold, and told everyone about Mother Holle.

Petra heard the story and thought, "I want gold too!" So she jumped into the well on purpose and tumbled onto the same flowery meadow.

But Petra did not help the bread. "I might burn my fingers," she said, and walked on.

She did not shake the apple tree. "An apple might fall on my head," she said, and walked on.

At Mother Holle's cottage, Petra shook the featherbeds so lazily that hardly a feather flew. Then she sat by the fire and yawned all day.

Mother Holle shook her head. "You have not helped anyone today," she said kindly. "But everyone deserves a second chance."

She led Petra to the gate. But instead of gold, a shower of sticky black pitch rained down on Petra! It stuck to her hair and her dress, and she looked very silly indeed.

Petra burst into tears. "I am sorry," she sobbed. "I should have helped."

Mother Holle smiled gently. "Then let us wash it all away," she said. She gave Petra a warm bath, a clean dress, and a small warm loaf of bread. "Go home, dear, and try again. Kind hands are always welcome here."

Petra went home, clean and thoughtful. And from that day on, whenever the bread called or the apples called, Petra was the first to help — because she had learned that kind hands are rewarded, and that everyone deserves a second chance.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kind hands are rewarded — and everyone deserves a second chance.
