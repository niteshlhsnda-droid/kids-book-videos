# The Brave Little Parrot

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a forest so green it looked like a big cozy blanket, lived a happy little parrot named Pip. His feathers were the color of the leaves around him.

Snuggle in close, little one, and hear the bravest thing he ever did.

Every morning, Pip breathed in the smell of wet bark and ripe mangoes and thought, "There is nowhere else I want to be." He swooped between the tall trees, calling, "Good morning, everybody!" to the deer, the monkeys, and the owls still blinking from their naps.

He shared mangoes with the monkeys and sang lullabies to the baby squirrels.

Then one hot summer day, a tiny spark — no bigger than a firefly's blink — landed on a dry twig. Before long, a small fire was nibbling at the dry leaves, and gray smoke curled into the blue sky.

The deer padded away quietly. The monkeys swung up into the high branches and vanished. And the great elephants stepped away, rumbling gently, "Come along, little ones — the fire is too big for us."

But Pip did not run. He looked at the flames, then at the home he loved, and whispered, "I'm little, but this is my forest — and I'm not giving up!"

Down he darted to the silver river and dipped his wings into the cool water. Then he flew back — flutter, flutter, flutter — and shook the drops onto the flames. Plip! One drop landed on his own beak. Pip blinked, surprised, and shook harder.

A few tiny drops. That was all — but Pip did not stop. He flew to the river again. And again. And again. With every trip, his wings felt soft and sleepy-tired, and the cool river water tickled his feathers — but Pip kept flying on.

High above, a kind god watched from the soft white clouds. At first the god just smiled at the little parrot carrying his tiny drops of water — what do you think he was thinking, little one?

But as Pip flew back and forth, never stopping, never giving up, the god's smile softened into a look of wonder. His heart felt warm and full, watching the brave parrot.

"Such a brave little heart," the god whispered. "Let me help."

With a wave of his hand, he gathered dark clouds from every corner of the sky. They rolled over the forest — and the rain began to fall.

Pat, pat, patter. Splash, splash, splash. The rain came down in cool, shining streams, and wherever it touched the flames, they sighed and melted away into soft puffs of mist. The fire was out. The forest was safe.

Close your eyes, little one, and listen — what does the rain sound like as it taps the leaves?

Slowly the big animals crept back. They gave him the ripest mangoes they could find, piled in a cozy heap around him. Plop! One landed right on his tummy. But Pip was all cozy and sleepy — he curled up in his favorite tree, his feathers clean and cool from the rain, and saved his mangoes for morning.

Now the forest is quiet and cool, the leaves damp from the rain. The fireflies blink their sleepy little lights, and the owls hum their soft nighttime songs.

And Pip, the brave little parrot, is fast asleep. What do you think he is dreaming about, little one?

Goodnight, Pip. Goodnight, forest. Goodnight, little one.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even the smallest try matters — never give up on what you love.*
