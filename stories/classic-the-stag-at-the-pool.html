The Stag at the Pool

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a green forest full of tall trees, there lived a handsome stag named Stuart.

Stuart had the grandest antlers in the whole forest. They were wide and branching, with ten smooth points, and they shone in the sunlight like polished wood.

Every morning, Stuart walked down to a clear, still pool to drink. The water was so still and clear that it looked like a mirror made of glass, and Stuart could see every point of his grand antlers in it. And every morning, he stopped to admire himself in the water.

"Oh, look at my antlers!" he said. "How grand! How splendid! No animal in the forest has antlers like mine!"

But then he looked a little lower, and his smile faded.

"And look at my legs," he sighed. "So thin! So knobbly! So ugly! If only my legs were as grand as my antlers, I would be the finest stag in the world."

One sunny afternoon, Stuart was nibbling soft grass near the pool when — SNAP! — a twig broke behind him.

Out of the bushes bounded a pack of hounds — woof, woof, WOOF! — with a hunter close behind them!

"Run!" cried Stuart, and run he did!

His thin, knobbly legs flew over the grass — patter, patter, patter! They carried him faster than the wind. He leapt over a fallen log. He splashed through a little stream. The hounds barked and barked, but they could not catch him.

But then Stuart darted into a thicket of bushes — and CRACK! His grand antlers caught in the branches!

"Oh no!" cried Stuart. He tugged this way. He tugged that way. His beautiful antlers were stuck fast, and the hounds were getting closer — woof, woof, WOOF!

With one great wiggle and one great twist, Stuart pulled himself free — but he left two little points of antler behind in the bush!

He raced away on his thin, quick legs, leaping over logs and splashing through streams, until at last the hounds gave up and went home.

Safe at last, Stuart stood panting by the pool. He looked at his reflection in the water — his grand antlers, now a little less grand, and his thin, knobbly legs.

And suddenly, Stuart laughed — a great, happy, staggy laugh!

"My silly old legs!" he said. "I called you ugly, but you saved my life! My antlers nearly got me caught, but my legs carried me to safety!"

From that day on, whenever Stuart looked in the pool, he smiled at the whole of himself — antlers and legs, grand parts and plain parts, all together.

"Every part of me has its own job to do," he said. "And the plainest part of all may be the greatest gift."

And he lived happily in the green forest, running fast on his thin, knobbly, wonderful legs.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: What seems plain may be your greatest gift — love every part of yourself.
