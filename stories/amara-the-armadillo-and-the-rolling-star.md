# Amara the Armadillo and the Rolling Star

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a warm meadow full of tall grass, lived a little armadillo named Amara.

Amara had a round, shiny shell, a pointy little nose, and one very special talent: she could roll herself into a perfect ball and roll, roll, roll all the way down the hill!

Every evening, Amara rolled down the hill to the bottom, giggling all the way. "Wheee!" she cried. "Rolling is the best feeling in the world!"

One night, as Amara was rolling down the hill under the stars, she heard a tiny sound. "Plop!"

Amara unrolled herself and looked around. There, in the soft grass, lay a tiny glowing star. It had fallen right out of the sky!

"Oh!" said Amara. "Are you all right, little star?"

The little star twinkled weakly. "I fell down," it said in a tiny sleepy voice. "And now I cannot get back up to the sky. And I am too tired to shine."

"Do not worry," said Amara kindly. "I will help you. But first, you need a good sleep. A sleepy star cannot climb back to the sky."

Amara gently tucked the little star into the soft grass, like a blanket. Then she curled herself into a ball beside it and began to rock, very gently — roll a little this way, roll a little that way — like a cradle rocking in the night air.

"Rock-a-bye, little star," sang Amara softly. "Rock-a-bye, sleep tight. The moon will watch you, all through the night."

The little star yawned a tiny yawn. Its glow grew warmer and softer. Then, with a happy sigh of sleepiness, it closed its twinkly eyes and drifted off to dreamland.

Amara kept rocking, slow and gentle, until the first light of morning touched the meadow.

Then something wonderful happened! The little star woke up, stretched its pointy arms, and glowed bright and golden.

"Thank you, Amara!" cried the star. "Your rocking helped me sleep, and now I am strong enough to shine again!"

And with a happy twinkle, the little star floated up, up, up — back into the sky, where it took its place among all the other stars, shining its brightest just for Amara.

Every night after that, when Amara rolled down the hill, she waved up at her star friend. And the star twinkled back, as if to say, "Good night, Amara. Thank you for the rocking."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A gentle rhythm and a kind heart can help even a fallen star rest and shine again.*
