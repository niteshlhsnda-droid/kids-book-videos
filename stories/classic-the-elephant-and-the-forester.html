The Elephant and the Forester

A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Deep in a green forest, where the trees grew so tall that the sunlight fell in golden ribbons, lived a kind old elephant named Gajraj.

One rainy evening, a forester named Bhanu was walking home through the forest with a bundle of firewood on his back. The rain poured down, the path turned to mud, and soon Bhanu did not know which way was home.

"Oh no," he whispered, looking left and right. "Every tree looks the same. I am lost!"

Just then, he heard a soft rumbling sound — rrrmm — and out from behind the great sal trees stepped Gajraj, the old elephant. Water ran in silver streams off his big grey back.

Bhanu was frightened at first. But Gajraj only looked at him with kind, wrinkled eyes and gently touched Bhanu's shoulder with the tip of his trunk, as if to say, "Do not be afraid."

"Please, great elephant," said Bhanu, "I am lost. Can you help me find my way home?"

Gajraj nodded his huge head. He knelt down on his front knees, and with his trunk, he lifted Bhanu — bundle of firewood and all — up onto his broad back. It was like sitting on a warm, swaying hill.

Then Gajraj walked. Step, step, step — his great feet made no sound on the wet leaves. He carried Bhanu all the way to the edge of the forest, where the little lamps of the village glowed like fireflies.

Bhanu climbed down and folded his hands. "Thank you, Gajraj. You are the kindest friend in the whole forest. How can I ever repay you?"

Gajraj rumbled softly and waved his trunk, as if to say, "Kindness needs no payment."

But Bhanu wanted to say thank you. So the next morning, he came back to the edge of the forest carrying a basket of the sweetest bananas and the ripest mangoes.

Gajraj came to meet him, and his little eyes shone with joy. He picked up a banana with his trunk — curl, peel, munch! — and shared the mangoes with a family of monkeys who had come to watch.

From that day on, Bhanu and Gajraj were friends. Whenever Bhanu walked through the forest, Gajraj would rumble a hello, and sometimes they would share bananas under the sal trees while the golden sunlight fell around them like ribbons.

And Bhanu always told his children, "If someone helps you, say thank you — and remember their kindness, the way the forest remembers the rain."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Help given freely is the sweetest gift — and a thankful heart keeps friendship warm.
