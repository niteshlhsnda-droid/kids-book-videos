The Elephant and the Tailor

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a small village, there lived a kind elephant named Balu.

Every morning, Balu walked down the dusty lane to the big pond for a drink. And every morning, he stopped outside a little shop with a bright striped awning.

In that shop worked Nikhil the tailor. Nikhil sewed shirts and saris and little coats for the village children. And every morning, when Balu stopped by, Nikhil gave him a ripe yellow banana.

"Good morning, Balu!" Nikhil would say. "Here is your banana, my big friend."

Balu would curl his trunk around the banana, trumpet a happy little toot, and walk on to the pond.

This went on for a very long time. Balu and Nikhil were the best of friends.

But one morning, Nikhil woke up in a grumpy mood. His needle had broken. His thread was tangled. And a shirt he was sewing had a big crooked seam.

"Oh, what a terrible morning!" grumbled Nikhil.

Just then, Balu came down the lane, swinging his big trunk, and stopped outside the shop as always.

"Banana! Banana!" trumpeted Balu, in his happy elephant way.

But Nikhil was too grumpy to smile. "Not today, Balu," he snapped. "Can you not see I am busy?"

Balu waited. He waved his trunk, hoping for his banana.

And then — oh dear! — Nikhil did a very unkind thing. He took his sharp needle and gave Balu's soft trunk a little prick.

"Ouch!" cried Balu. His big eyes filled with tears.

Balu turned away and walked slowly to the pond. His trunk hurt. His heart hurt even more.

At the pond, Balu looked at his reflection in the water. "That was not kind," he said softly. "Nikhil is my friend. Friends do not prick friends."

Then Balu had an idea. It was not a kind idea — but it was a fair one.

Balu dipped his long trunk into the pond and filled it full of cool, muddy water. Then he walked back to Nikhil's shop.

Nikhil was sitting at his table, sewing a beautiful clean white shirt. He did not look up.

Balu lifted his trunk high — and SPLASH! — he sprayed all the muddy water right over the shop, right over the table, and right over the beautiful clean white shirt!

"Oh no!" cried Nikhil. "My shirt! My beautiful clean shirt! It is all muddy!"

Balu looked at him with his big, calm eyes. "A little prick hurt me," he said. "And a little splash has hurt your shirt. Unkindness always comes back, my friend."

Nikhil looked at the muddy shirt. Then he looked at Balu's sad eyes. And his grumpy mood melted away like morning mist.

"Oh, Balu," said Nikhil. "I am so sorry. I was grumpy, and I took it out on you. That was wrong. Please forgive me."

Balu smiled his big elephant smile. "I forgive you," he said. "But let us remember — kind words are better than sharp needles."

Nikhil laughed. He wiped his hands and gave Balu not one banana, but two! "From today," he said, "I will always have a banana ready for my best friend."

And from that day on, Balu never missed his morning banana. And Nikhil never forgot that kindness is the best thing a friend can give — much better than a prick.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness comes back to us — and so does unkindness. So let us always choose kindness.
