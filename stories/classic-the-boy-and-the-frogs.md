# The Boy and the Frogs

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little village with a crooked lane and a round green pond, there lived a boy named Ayaan.

Ayaan was a happy boy with curly hair and pockets full of smooth, round stones. He loved to skip stones across the pond — one, two, three skips! — and watch the circles spread out on the water.

One sunny afternoon, Ayaan came to the pond with a pocket full of stones. The frogs were sitting on their lily pads, singing their croaky afternoon song. "Croak, croak, croak!"

Ayaan picked up a stone and threw it — plop! — right into the middle of the pond. The frogs jumped with fright. Plop! Another stone. Plop! Another stone. The frogs hopped off their lily pads and dived under the water, one by one.

Ayaan laughed. "This is fun!" he said, and he threw another stone. Plop!

Then, from behind a big green lily pad, an old frog popped up his head. He was the oldest frog in the pond, with a wrinkled green chin and kind, round eyes.

"Little boy," said the old frog in a soft, croaky voice, "please stop for a moment. May I tell you something?"

Ayaan stopped, with a stone still in his hand. "Yes?" he said.

"What is a game to you," said the old frog, "is not a game to us. Your stones fall like thunder on our little heads. They splash into our homes. They frighten all my frog children."

Ayaan looked down at the stone in his hand. Then he looked at the pond. All the little frogs were hiding under the lily pads, peeking out with worried eyes.

"Oh," said Ayaan quietly. "I did not think about that. I was only having fun. I am sorry, little frogs."

The old frog smiled. "A sorry from the heart is a wonderful thing," he said. "And do you know what would be even more wonderful? If you helped us instead of scaring us."

Ayaan put the stone gently back on the ground. "What can I do?" he asked.

"Well," said the old frog, "our lily pads are old and torn. If you could find us some big, strong leaves, we could make a fine new raft to sing on!"

Ayaan ran to the big banana tree at the edge of the pond and picked three big, strong leaves. He laid them carefully on the water, one by one, and tied them together with soft grass.

The frogs hopped onto their new raft, one by one. Then the old frog cleared his throat, and all the frogs sang their very best song — "Croak, croak, croak! Croak, croak, croak!" — louder and happier than ever before.

Ayaan sat on the bank and listened, smiling. "Your song is much better than my stones," he said.

And from that day on, whenever Ayaan came to the pond, he brought something nice for the frogs — a flower, a smooth pebble to sit on, or a big green leaf for their raft. And the frogs always sang him their very best song.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: What is a game to you may hurt someone else — so be kind, and help instead of harming.*
