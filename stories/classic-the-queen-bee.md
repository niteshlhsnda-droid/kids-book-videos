# The Queen Bee

*A timeless Grimm fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a kingdom of rolling green hills, there lived a king with three sons.

The two older princes were clever and proud. The youngest was called Simpleton — not because he was silly, but because he was simple and kind. He smiled at everyone, shared his lunch with sparrows, and never, ever hurt a living thing.

One day, the three brothers went walking through the great forest. They came upon an anthill — thousands of tiny ants scurrying up and down, carrying crumbs much bigger than themselves.

The eldest brother grinned. "Let's kick it over and watch them scatter!"

"No!" cried Simpleton, throwing his arms wide. "Leave them be! They've worked so hard on their little home."

The brothers grumbled but walked on. Soon they reached a lake where ducks were swimming — quack, quack — with their fluffy ducklings paddling behind.

"Let's catch a duck for dinner!" said the second brother, picking up a stone.

"No!" cried Simpleton. "Leave them be! Look how happy they are, the mothers with their babies."

On they went, deeper into the forest, until they found a hollow tree buzzing with bees. Golden honey dripped from a crack in the bark.

"Let's smoke them out and take the honey!" said the eldest.

"No!" cried Simpleton. "Leave them be! They made that honey with their own hard work."

His brothers laughed at him. "You and your soft heart, Simpleton!"

But that night, something magical happened. The three brothers dreamed the same dream: a voice whispered, "The kind heart shall be king."

The next morning, they found a gray stone castle in a clearing. On its door was a notice: whoever completes three tasks shall be king — but whoever gives up and falls asleep in the garden shall sleep for a hundred years.

The brothers knocked. The tasks were read aloud:

"First: find a thousand pearls, scattered in the moss of the forest floor, before sunset."

The two older brothers searched and searched, but the pearls were tiny, and the forest was huge. They found only a hundred before their backs ached and they gave up.

Simpleton sat down in the moss and sighed. Then — rustle, rustle — out came the ants! Thousands of them, marching in lines, each carrying a pearl. In no time, a thousand pearls glittered in a pile.

"Second task: fetch the key to the princess's chamber from the bottom of the lake."

The older brothers couldn't even see the bottom. But Simpleton waded in — and up swam the ducks! Quack, quack! Down they dived, and up they came with the little golden key in their beaks.

"Third task: find which of the three sleeping princesses is the youngest and sweetest. They all look exactly alike."

Simpleton tiptoed into the dark chamber. The princesses slept like roses. How could he tell?

Then — buzz, buzz — in flew the queen bee! She landed on the lips of the sweetest princess and tasted them. "This one," she seemed to say. "This is the youngest and sweetest."

Simpleton chose her. And at that very moment, the spell broke! The princesses woke up smiling, the gray castle turned golden, and the sleepers in the garden woke up too, rubbing their eyes and laughing with joy.

And who became king? The kind-hearted Simpleton, of course — with the sweetest princess as his queen.

He ruled for many, many years, and his kingdom was the happiest in all the land. Because a king who is kind to ants and ducks and bees... is kind to everyone.

And every evening, the queen bee would come and sit on the windowsill of the palace, buzzing her soft, sleepy lullaby: bzzzz... bzzzz...

Good night, little one. Be kind to the small things, and the small things will love you back.

---

*Moral: A kind heart is the greatest treasure — kindness always finds its way back.*
