# Koda the Capybara and the River of Lights

Beside a slow, silver river, in a nest of soft reeds, lived a young capybara named Koda. He loved the evenings best, when the sun went down and the fireflies woke up, blinking like stars above the water.

Every evening, Koda floated on his back in the warm river, counting their lights. "One, two, three," he whispered, as they blinked. The river animals said the fireflies were the river's own lanterns, lighting the way home for everyone.

Tonight was the Festival of Lanterns, when every animal floated a lantern down the river until it glowed like a ribbon of stars. Koda had made his from a big green leaf and a tiny candle, tucked safely under a fern.

As the sky turned pink, Koda paddled to the bank for his lantern. But under a drooping willow, he heard a small, shivery sound. Peep. Peep. It was Bea, the littlest duckling, all alone, her soft yellow feathers wet.

"I lost my mama," peeped Bea, her voice wobbling. "The river is so dark, and I cannot see the way home." Koda looked up. The moon hid behind a cloud, and the fireflies had not woken up yet. The river was dark.

"Do not worry," said Koda, in his calmest, warmest voice. "My lantern will light the way." He set his leaf lantern on the water, and its little candle glowed gold. Bea climbed onto his back, and they drifted down the river.

But as they rounded the bend, a gust of wind rushed down the river — whoosh! — and blew out the little candle. The lantern went dark. Bea buried her beak in Koda's fur. "Now we are lost," she peeped.

Koda took a slow, deep breath. He remembered the moonflowers by the old stone bridge — pale flowers that opened only at night, where the fireflies gathered first. "Hold on tight," he whispered, and paddled gently toward the bridge.

And there they were — the moonflowers, open like little moons, and above them hundreds of fireflies, blinking and twinkling. Koda held his lantern close to a firefly's light, and the candle caught again — flicker, glow! The lantern shone brighter than before, and the fireflies followed, lighting the river like stars.

"Mama! Mama!" peeped Bea, as a warm quack answered from the reeds. There was Bea's mama, swimming toward the light! She wrapped Bea in her wings and thanked Koda happily. All along the river, lanterns floated, and the whole river glowed gold, all the way home.

That night, Koda curled up in his nest of reeds, listening to the river hum. The fireflies blinked above him, slow and sleepy, and the lanterns drifted on until the whole river dreamed in gold.

Sleep now, little Koda.

The end. 🌙
