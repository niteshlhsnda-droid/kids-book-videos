Hansel and Gretel

A gentle Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, at the edge of a dark forest, there lived a brother and sister named Hansel and Gretel.

Hansel was brave, and Gretel was clever. They loved each other very much, and they always looked after each other.

One morning, they went into the forest to pick berries for their supper. They walked and walked — and soon they did not know the way home.

"Don't worry," said Hansel. "We will find our way back."

Just then, Gretel sniffed the air. "Hansel! Do you smell that? Something sweet!"

They followed the sweet smell through the trees — and there it was! A little house made of gingerbread, with a roof of chocolate and windows of sugar!

"Oh!" cried Gretel. "A gingerbread house!"

They each took a tiny bite of the sweet wall — crunch! — when suddenly the door creaked open.

"Come in, come in, my dears," said a voice. It was an old woman with a crooked smile. "I am the witch of the gingerbread house. Come in and rest your little feet."

Hansel and Gretel stepped inside. The house was warm and smelled of cinnamon.

But the old witch was not kind at all. "Stay here in my house!" she said, and she locked the door with a big key. "You will work for me now!"

Hansel and Gretel were frightened. But Gretel whispered, "Don't worry, brother. We will think of a clever plan."

Every morning, the witch came to the door and said, "Hold out your finger, so I can see if you are growing fat!"

But clever Gretel held out a thin little stick instead of her finger. The witch felt the stick and shook her head.

"Still so thin!" she grumbled. "Still so thin!"

One day, the witch said, "I am tired of waiting! Gretel, light the big oven for me!"

Gretel's heart went thump, thump, thump. She knew she had to be very, very clever now.

She looked at the big oven and said, "Oh, dear witch, I don't know how the oven works. Please show me how to open the door."

The witch laughed. "Silly child! Like this!" And she bent down low and pushed her head right inside the oven to show Gretel. The oven was cold and dark — Gretel had never lit it, not even once.

Quick as a flash, Gretel shut the oven door — click! — and turned the key.

"Let me out!" cried the witch.

"Not until you promise to be kind!" said Gretel.

"I promise! I promise!" cried the witch. "I will be kind from now on!"

So Gretel opened the door, and the witch came out, looking very sorry indeed.

"I am sorry I was unkind," said the witch. "Please, take these gingerbread treats for your journey home."

Hansel found the key to the front door, and the two children ran out into the sunshine. Behind them, the witch waved goodbye and called, "Be good children — and come back for gingerbread any time!"

Hansel and Gretel followed the birdsong home through the forest. And when they reached their little house, their father ran out and hugged them tight.

"You are home! You are home!" he cried.

And that night, they shared their gingerbread treats, and they all fell asleep safe and sound.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever mind and a brave heart can find the way home.
