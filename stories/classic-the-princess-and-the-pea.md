# The Princess and the Pea

*A timeless tale by Hans Christian Andersen for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, there was a prince named Theo who wanted to marry a real princess.

Not just any princess — a truly real one, kind and gentle and true.

Theo travelled far and wide. He met many princesses. One could sing beautifully. One could dance beautifully. One could ride a horse beautifully. But something always felt not quite right, and Theo came home each time, still looking.

One stormy night, when the wind howled and the rain rattled the windows, there came a knock at the palace door. Knock, knock, knock!

The old king opened the door. Standing there, dripping wet, was a young girl in a muddy dress, with rain dripping from her hair.

"Good evening," she said, shivering. "I am Princess Elsie. My carriage broke down in the storm, and I am looking for a place to sleep."

"A princess?" said the queen, peering at the muddy dress. "Well, we shall see. Come in, my dear, and warm yourself by the fire."

But after Elsie had gone to bed, the queen had an idea. "I will find out if she is a real princess," she whispered. "A real princess is so delicate that she can feel even the tiniest bump."

So the queen went to the guest bedroom. She took one small, hard, green pea and laid it on the bed. Then she piled twenty soft mattresses on top of the pea. And on top of the mattresses, she piled twenty thick, fluffy quilts.

"There," said the queen. "Let us see what the morning brings."

In the morning, Princess Elsie came down to breakfast, yawning and rubbing her back.

"Did you sleep well, my dear?" asked the queen.

"Oh, not at all," said Elsie. "I hardly slept a wink! There was something hard in the bed, pressing into me all night long. My back is quite sore."

Theo and the queen looked at each other. Then they smiled the biggest smiles.

"Only a real princess could feel a tiny pea under twenty mattresses and twenty quilts!" cried Theo.

Elsie laughed. "I told you I was a real princess," she said. "Though I must say, it was the lumpiest bed I ever slept in!"

Theo and Elsie became the best of friends, and soon they were married, with music and dancing and a great big cake.

And the little green pea? It was put in a glass case in the palace museum, where visitors can still see it today — the smallest, hardest, most famous pea in the whole kingdom.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: What is true shows itself in small ways — and a kind heart is the truest sign of all.*
