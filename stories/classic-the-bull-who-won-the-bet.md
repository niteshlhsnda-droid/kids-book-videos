# The Bull Who Won the Bet

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green valley, there lived a strong brown bull named Nandi.

Nandi was the strongest bull in the whole valley. He could pull a cart full of pumpkins all by himself. He could pull a cart full of hay. He could even pull two carts at once, if someone asked him nicely.

Nandi belonged to a kind farmer named Vikram. Vikram loved Nandi very much. Every day he gave him fresh hay and clean water, and he brushed his brown coat until it shone.

One sunny day, a rich merchant named Gopal came to visit.

"Your bull is strong," said Gopal. "But is he strong enough to pull one hundred carts, all in a row?"

"Of course he is!" said Vikram proudly. "Nandi can pull anything!"

"Then let us make a bet," said Gopal, smiling. "If your bull pulls one hundred carts, I will give you one hundred gold coins. If he cannot, you will give me one hundred gold coins."

Vikram thought about the gold coins. He thought about how proud he was. And he said, "It is a bet!"

The next morning, the whole village came to watch. One hundred little carts stood in a long, long row. Nandi stood at the front, ready and strong.

Vikram was so excited that he forgot his manners. He grabbed the rope and shouted, "Move, you lazy bull! Pull! Pull! Pull!"

Nandi's ears drooped. His big brown eyes grew sad.

"Lazy?" thought Nandi. "I am not lazy. I pull his carts every day. Why is he shouting at me?"

And Nandi did not move. Not one step. Not even a little wiggle.

The carts stayed still. The villagers whispered. Gopal smiled and held out his hand.

"One hundred gold coins, please," said Gopal.

Vikram went home, very sad. He sat beside Nandi in the barn and sighed.

"I am sorry, Nandi," said Vikram. "I wanted to win the bet so much that I forgot to be kind. I shouted at you, my best friend."

Nandi looked at Vikram with his soft brown eyes. "Kind words make me strong," said Nandi, in his low, gentle voice. "Harsh words make my legs feel heavy."

Vikram hugged Nandi's big neck. "Then I will speak kindly," he said. "Will you try again, my friend?"

The next day, Vikram went back to Gopal. "One more try," he said. "Please."

Gopal laughed. "Very well. One more try."

Again the whole village came to watch. One hundred little carts stood in a long, long row. Nandi stood at the front.

This time, Vikram patted Nandi's neck gently. "My strong, good friend," he said softly. "Please pull the carts for me. I know you can do it."

Nandi lifted his head. His ears stood up. His legs felt light and strong.

"For my friend," said Nandi, "I will pull!"

And pull he did! One cart, two carts, ten carts, fifty carts — all one hundred carts rolled along behind him, smooth as a river!

The villagers cheered! "Hooray for Nandi! Hooray for kind words!"

Gopal laughed and paid the one hundred gold coins. "Your bull is wonderful," he said. "And your kind words are worth more than gold."

Vikram shared the coins with the whole village, and that night there was a big feast. Nandi got the biggest pile of hay he had ever seen.

And from that day on, Vikram always spoke to Nandi kindly — because he knew that kind words make everyone stronger.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kind words give us strength. Harsh words only make our legs feel heavy.*
