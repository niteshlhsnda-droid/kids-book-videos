Birbal and the Mango Tree

A gentle Akbar–Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the kingdom of Emperor Akbar, there lived a clever minister named Birbal.

Birbal was not the strongest man in the kingdom. He was not the richest man in the kingdom. But he was the wisest — because he always asked the right questions.

One sunny morning, two neighbours came to Akbar's court, both talking at once.

"It is MY mango tree!" cried Ramesh, the gardener.

"No, it is MY mango tree!" cried Mohan, the merchant.

The emperor held up his hand. "One at a time," he said. "Tell me what happened."

Ramesh bowed. "Your Majesty, ten years ago I planted that mango tree beside the garden wall. I watered it every day. I tied a little wooden swing to its branches for my daughter, Meena. Now it is full of sweet mangoes!"

Mohan shook his head. "Your Majesty, that tree grows on MY side of the wall! The mangoes fall on my roof! So the tree must be mine!"

The emperor looked at Birbal. "Birbal, whose tree is it?"

Birbal smiled. "Your Majesty, I will find out — with three questions."

Birbal turned to Ramesh. "Ramesh, if the tree is yours, tell me — where does its shade fall at noon?"

"On the little bench by the wall, where Meena sits to read," said Ramesh at once.

Birbal turned to Mohan. "Mohan, if the tree is yours, tell me — where does its shade fall at noon?"

Mohan scratched his head. "Um… on… on my roof?" he said. "Or on the road? I am not sure."

Birbal asked his second question. "Ramesh, what hangs from the tree's branches?"

"A little wooden swing," said Ramesh. "Meena swings on it every evening."

"Mohan, what hangs from the tree's branches?" asked Birbal.

"Um… a bird's nest?" said Mohan. "Or… a lantern? I am not sure."

Birbal asked his third question. "Ramesh, what does the tree look like in the spring?"

"In the spring, it is covered in tiny white flowers," said Ramesh, his eyes shining. "They smell like honey, and the bees hum all around them."

"Mohan, what does the tree look like in the spring?" asked Birbal.

Mohan looked down at his feet. "I… I never noticed," he whispered.

Then Birbal turned to the emperor. "Your Majesty," he said, "the tree belongs to the one who knows it and loves it. Ramesh knows the tree's shade, its swing, and its spring flowers. He planted it, he watered it, and he watched it grow. The tree is his."

The emperor nodded. "Birbal is right. The mango tree belongs to Ramesh."

Mohan hung his head. "I am sorry," he said. "I only wanted the mangoes. I did not think about who planted the tree."

Birbal smiled kindly. "Mohan, you may not own this tree. But would you like a mango sapling of your own? Plant it, water it, and watch it grow. In a few years, you will have mangoes of your very own — and you will know every leaf on the tree!"

Mohan's face lit up. "I would like that very much!"

So Ramesh shared his mangoes with everyone that day — sweet, juicy mangoes, fresh from the tree. And Mohan planted his little sapling beside his house, and watered it every single day.

And whenever the emperor walked past the garden wall, he would smile and say, "Birbal's questions found the truth — and the truth made two neighbours into friends."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: True care is knowing and loving something — and the truth makes friends.
