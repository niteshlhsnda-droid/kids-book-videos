The Straw Millionaire

A gentle Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a village in Japan, there lived a poor young man named Taro.

Taro had no money, no house, and no fine clothes. But he had a kind smile, and he always tried to help others.

One morning, Taro walked to the temple and prayed, "Please, give me a little luck, so I can make my life better."

On his way home, something tickled his foot. He looked down. It was a little piece of straw, caught on his sandal.

"A piece of straw!" laughed Taro. "Well, it is my lucky straw. I will keep it!"

As he walked along, a big fly buzzed around his head. "Buzz! Buzz!" Taro caught the fly gently, tied it to the end of his straw with a thread, and let it dance in the air.

A little boy saw the dancing fly and clapped his hands. "Oh, how wonderful! Please, can I have it? I will give you three shiny oranges!"

Taro smiled and gave the boy the straw with the fly. "Here you go, little friend." And he walked on with three juicy oranges.

Soon he met a thirsty traveller, sitting by the road with a dry throat.

"Would you like an orange?" asked Taro.

"Oh, thank you!" said the traveller. "You are so kind. In return, please take my fine silk cloth. It is the softest cloth in all the land."

Taro thanked him and walked on with the silk cloth. Soon he met a shivering horse, standing in the cold rain.

"Poor horse," said Taro. He wrapped the soft silk cloth around the horse, to keep it warm.

The horse's master came running. "Thank you for being kind to my horse!" he cried. "Please, take this horse. He is strong and gentle, and he needs a kind master like you."

Now Taro had a horse! He rode along the road, singing a happy song.

Soon he met a farmer whose field had no horse to pull the plough.

"Would you like my horse?" asked Taro. "He is strong and gentle."

"Oh, thank you!" said the farmer. "In return, please take my field. It is a good field, with rich brown earth."

Taro thanked him. And now he had a field of his own!

Taro worked hard in his field. He planted rice in the spring, and in the autumn he gathered a big, golden harvest.

He built a little house beside his field. He planted a garden full of flowers. And every evening, he sat on his porch and smiled.

"All of this," he said, "from one little piece of straw — and a kind heart."

And whenever a traveller passed by, Taro shared his rice and his oranges, just as others had once shared with him.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A kind heart and a little luck can grow into something wonderful.
