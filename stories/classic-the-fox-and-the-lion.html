The Fox and the Lion

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Tilo was a little fox with a tail as fluffy as a feather duster, and feet that went pitter-patter, pitter-patter.

One sunny morning, Tilo walked quickly to the edge of the meadow — and stopped.

There, drinking at the pond — the water glittering blue and gold in the morning sun — was the biggest animal he had ever seen. A lion! His mane was like a golden sun.

What would YOU do if you saw the biggest lion ever?

He lifted his head — and RROOOAR! — so loud the leaves trembled.

Tilo's heart went thump-thump-thump. He turned and scampered all the way home, fast as his little feet could carry him.

"That lion is the scariest thing in the whole world!" he told his mother, hiding under the blanket. "I will never go near him again!"

The next week, Tilo had to cross the meadow to pick berries. And there was the lion again, sitting in the tall grass.

Then — RROOOAR! … Oh. It was only a yawn. A big, lazy lion yawn.

Tilo froze. But this time, he did not run. He stood very still at the far edge of the meadow and watched.

The lion did not chase anyone. He did not roar at the rabbits. He just stretched in the sunshine like a sleepy cat.

"Hmm," thought Tilo. "Maybe he is not so scary after all."

Can you guess what brave thing Tilo did next?

The third time, Tilo was braver. He crept a little closer — and saw something wonderful.

A baby elephant had gotten his trunk stuck in a hollow log! He was pulling and pulling, crying, "Hee-hee-hee!"

The lion walked over softly on his big quiet paws. With one big soft paw, he held the log steady. With the other, he helped the baby elephant slide his trunk out — pop!

"Thank you!" said the baby elephant in his loud trumpet voice, and the lion smiled a big lion smile.

Tilo's eyes went wide. "He is… kind!"

Slowly, Tilo walked right up to the lion. His tail was shaking a little — but only a little.

"Hello," said Tilo in a small voice. "I am Tilo."

"Hello, little fox," said the lion, whose name was Shera. His voice was deep and warm, like honey in a big jar. "Would you like to sit with me in the shade?"

So Tilo sat beside Shera in the cool shade that smelled of soft green grass, and they watched the white clouds drift by, soft and slow.

That evening, Tilo told his mother, "The lion is not scary at all. I was only scared because I did not know him."

His mother smiled. "Once you get to know someone," she said, "the scary feeling just goes away."

And from that day on, Tilo and Shera were the best of friends.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Once you get to know someone, the scary feeling just goes away.
