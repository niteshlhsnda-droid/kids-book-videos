Iris the Inchworm and the Ivy Leaf

A sweet original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a sunny little garden, on a tall brick wall, there grew a green ivy plant.

And on the very lowest leaf of the ivy plant, there lived a tiny inchworm named Iris.

Iris was small and green, with a wiggly little body. She moved the way inchworms move — arch, stretch, arch, stretch — one little step at a time.

Every day, Iris looked up, up, up at the top of the garden wall.

"Oh, I wish I could see what is on the other side," she sighed. "I bet the whole garden looks beautiful from up there."

"But you are so tiny," said a ladybird, landing beside her. "And the wall is so tall!"

"I know," said Iris. "But little steps can climb tall walls. I will try!"

So Iris began to climb. Arch, stretch. Arch, stretch. She inched her way up the ivy, leaf by leaf.

She passed one leaf. She passed two leaves. She passed three leaves. The ivy swayed gently in the breeze — swoosh, swoosh — and Iris held on tight.

Halfway up, she felt tired. Her little legs ached. "Oh dear," she whispered. "It is such a long way."

Just then, a soft breeze lifted the ivy leaf, and up, up, up it floated, carrying Iris with it!

"Wheee!" giggled Iris, holding on tight.

At last, Iris reached the very top of the wall. And oh! What a sight!

She could see the whole garden — the red roses, the yellow sunflowers, the blue pond, and the big oak tree. She could see the sun going down, painting the sky pink and gold. She could see the moon peeping up, round and silver.

"It is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen!" whispered Iris.

But Iris did not keep the view all to herself. She slid back down the ivy — down, down, down — on a shiny drop of dew, like a little slide!

"Wheee!" she giggled again.

And she told all her friends about the view — the ladybird, the bee, the little brown ant. She told them about the roses and the pond and the pink-and-gold sky.

"Little steps climb the tallest walls," she said. "And the view is even sweeter when you share it."

That night, Iris curled up on her favourite ivy leaf. She counted the leaves around her — one leaf, two leaves, three leaves — and she smelled the sweet roses on the evening air.

And under the twinkling stars, she fell fast asleep, dreaming of the top of the wall.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Little steps climb the tallest walls — and the view is sweeter when you share it.
