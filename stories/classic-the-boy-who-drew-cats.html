The Boy Who Drew Cats

A Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, in a little village in Japan, there lived a boy named Yuto who loved drawing cats more than anything in the world.

He drew big cats and tiny cats. He drew sleepy cats curled up like soft dumplings, and dancing cats with their tails held high.

He drew on paper and on the walls of his house. He even drew in the dust of the garden path, with a little stick.

His parents loved his cats. But they said, "Yuto, you must learn to read and write, too. Go and study with the kind old priest at the quiet mountain temple."

So Yuto packed his little bag and climbed up the mountain to the temple.

The temple was very still. The only sounds were the wind sighing in the pine trees and the drip-drip of water into the stone bowl.

The priest gave Yuto a brush and some paper. "Study your lessons," he said kindly, "and you may draw your cats, too."

Yuto tried hard to study. But his brush kept wandering.

Soon the temple walls were covered with cats — cats sleeping, cats stretching, cats chasing butterflies, cats smiling with round moon eyes.

The priest looked at them all and chuckled softly. "Such wonderful cats!" he said.

That night, the wind howled through the pines. Yuto curled up in his warm blanket — but he could not sleep.

Thump. Thump. THUMP.

Something big and stompy was creeping through the temple. It was a giant goblin-rat, come to gobble up all the temple's rice!

Its eyes shone like two round lanterns. Its whiskers were as long as little sticks. Yuto pulled his blanket up to his nose and peeked out with one eye.

And just then — the painted cats on the walls opened their eyes. All at once!

Meow! Hiss!

Off the walls they leapt, one after another, their soft paws thump-thumping on the floor. The sleepy cats stretched wide. The dancing cats twirled their tails — and together they chased the goblin-rat right out the door!

"Eeeek!" squeaked the goblin-rat.

And away it ran — out the door, down the mountain path, far, far away into the dark night. It never came back.

Yuto crept out of his blanket and looked at the walls. The cats were back in their places. But they looked tired now — eyes half closed, tails drooping softly.

In the morning, the old priest smiled at the tired, proud cats. "Your cats protected us, Yuto," he said. "You drew them with all your heart, and that made them real."

Yuto drew one more cat that day — a happy one, its eyes closed in a smile. Then he curled up beside the wall and fell fast asleep, listening to its soft purring.

Purrr… purrr… purrr.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The things you love doing with all your heart can become your gift to others.
