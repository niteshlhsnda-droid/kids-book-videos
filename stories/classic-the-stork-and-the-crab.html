The Stork and the Crab

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Beside a wide, shining lake, there lived an old stork with long thin legs and an even longer beak.

Every morning he stood in the shallow water, as still as a statue, waiting for a fish to swim by.

But the old stork was growing lazy. "Standing all day makes my legs ache," he sighed. "There must be an easier way to get my dinner."

One evening, he had a naughty idea. He flapped over to where the little silver fish were playing, drooped his beak, let his feathers sag, and blinked slow, watery eyes.

"Oh, my little friends," he said in a trembly voice, "I have heard terrible news. Tomorrow, some fishermen are coming with big nets. They will catch every fish in this lake!"

The little fish gasped. "Oh no! What shall we do?"

"Lucky for you," said the stork, his beak curving into a crooked, secret grin, "I know a secret lake, deep in the forest, where the water is cool and no nets can reach. I can carry you there — one by one — in my big, strong beak." Now — what do YOU think that lake looked like?

The fish were so frightened that they believed him. "Take me first!" cried one. "Me next!" cried another.

So every morning, the stork tucked a fish into his beak and flew off toward the forest.

But he never reached any secret lake. Behind the tall grass, he gobbled each fish up — which was very, very naughty of him.

Day after day, fewer and fewer fish swam in the lake.

Now, in the lake there also lived a clever little crab named Cleo. She watched the stork fly off each morning, and she watched him come back each evening — looking plumper, and never bringing any news of the secret lake.

"Hmm," thought Cleo, clicking her claws. "Something is not right here — and it is not the lake."

The next morning, Cleo waved a claw. "Dear Stork, please take ME to the secret lake today!"

The stork's eyes gleamed. A crab would make a crunchy change from fish! "Of course, little one. Hop onto my back and hold on tight."

Cleo climbed on and wrapped her claws firmly around the stork's long neck. "Fly on!"

They flew over the trees — but Cleo looked left and looked right, and nowhere below was there any lake. Only dry grass and a bare peepal tree. Her heart thumped.

"So THIS is your trick!" she cried. "There is no secret lake! You tricked all my friends!"

Quick as a wink, Cleo gave the stork's neck the biggest pinch she could — not to hurt him badly, but just enough to make his eyes water.

"Ow-ow-ow!" cried the stork. "Stop! Stop!"

"Fly me home this instant!" said Cleo sternly. "And promise — cross your heart — no more tricks. From now on, catch your fish honestly."

"I promise! I promise!" squawked the stork. And he flew straight back and set Cleo gently down at the edge of the lake.

All the fish swam up. "Cleo! You saved us!"

From that day on, the old stork stood in the shallow water and fished honestly, like a proper stork. And whenever he felt like being lazy, he remembered Cleo's pinch — and stood up a little straighter.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Cheating may seem easy, but honesty always wins in the end.
