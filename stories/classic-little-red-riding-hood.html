Little Red Riding Hood

A timeless fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there lived a sweet little girl who always wore a red hooded cape. Everyone called her Little Red Riding Hood.

One morning, her mother said, "Grandma is feeling poorly. Please take her this basket of warm bread and honey. And remember — stay on the path, and do not talk to strangers."

"I will, Mama," said Little Red Riding Hood. And off she went — skip, skip, skip — into the woods.

On the way, she met a big wolf. "Good morning, little girl," said the wolf with a sly grin. "Where are you going?"

Little Red Riding Hood forgot her mother's warning. "To my grandma's cottage," she said. "Just past the big oak tree."

The wolf licked his lips. "Why not pick some pretty flowers for your grandma?" he said.

"Oh, what a lovely idea!" said Little Red Riding Hood. She wandered off the path to pick flowers — daisies and buttercups, red ones and yellow ones.

Meanwhile, the wolf ran — dash! — straight to Grandma's cottage.

But Grandma had heard the wolf coming! Quick as a wink, she hid in the big wooden closet and locked the door.

The wolf put on Grandma's nightcap and climbed into her bed, waiting.

Soon Little Red Riding Hood knocked. "Grandma, it is me!"

"Come in, my dear," said the wolf in a creaky voice.

Little Red Riding Hood stepped closer. "Grandma, what big ears you have!"

"All the better to hear you with, my dear."

"Grandma, what big teeth you have!"

"All the better to — "

Just then — BANG! — the door flew open! It was the kind woodcutter, with his big axe!

"Not so fast, you sly wolf!" he shouted.

The wolf jumped out of bed and ran away into the woods — yip, yip, yip! — never to be seen again.

Grandma came out of the closet and hugged Little Red Riding Hood tight.

"I am sorry, Grandma," said Little Red Riding Hood. "I left the path, and I talked to a stranger. I will listen to Mama from now on."

And she did — always.

Goodnight, Little Red Riding Hood.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Listen to wise advice — stay on the path and never talk to strangers.
