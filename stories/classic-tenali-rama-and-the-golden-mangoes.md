# Tenali Rama and the Golden Mangoes

*A clever Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the grand kingdom of Vijayanagara — where golden bells chimed at sunrise — there lived a great king named Krishnadevaraya. His court was full of courtiers in fine silk clothes, and every morning they bowed very low before the throne.

"Your wisdom shines like the sun!" said one courtier.

"Your laugh is sweeter than honey!" said another.

"Your footsteps are music to our ears!" said a third, bowing so low his turban nearly touched the floor.

The king smiled politely. But deep down, he knew these were only pretty, too-nice words — what grown-ups call flattery. They were words painted gold, but underneath the paint, there was not much sweetness at all.

Only one man in the whole court saw this clearly. His name was Tenali Rama, the king's clever jester. Tenali Rama had a quick mind, a kind heart, and a smile that always seemed to know a secret.

One sunny morning, Tenali Rama thought, "Empty flattery is like an empty mango — big and shiny outside, but nothing sweet inside. I must show everyone, gently, gently."

So he walked to the market and bought a basket of ripe, ordinary mangoes. They were yellow and round and sweet-smelling. Mmm-mmm — can you smell them too? They smelled like sunshine.

Then he carried the basket into the court and set it down before the throne with a big, swishy bow. "Your Majesty!" he announced in his biggest, grandest voice. "I bring you GOLDEN mangoes! Each one is worth a thousand gold coins!"

A hush fell over the court. Then the courtiers began to nod and murmur.

"Ohhh, so golden!" said one.

"So precious! So splendid!" said another.

"Truly fit for a king!" said a third. He had never looked closely at a mango in his whole life.

The king stroked his beard. His eyes twinkled. He knew exactly what Tenali Rama was doing, and he decided to play along.

"Golden mangoes," said the king slowly. "What a wonder! But Tenali, I must ask — can you prove to us that these are truly golden?"

Tenali Rama bowed. "Gladly, Your Majesty."

But first — what do YOU think was hiding inside?

He picked up the roundest, yellowest mango. He took out his little knife — snick! — and cut it open.

Out came the juice, drip-drip-drip, sweet and golden and sticky. And inside? Juicy, ordinary, delicious mango flesh. Just a mango — perfectly lovely, perfectly ordinary.

The court went very quiet. Then the king burst out laughing — a big, warm, rolling laugh that shook the throne.

"Golden indeed!" laughed the king.

And one by one, the courtiers began to giggle too. Their cheeks turned a little pink. Not from anger, dear listener — from a soft, friendly blush, the kind you feel when you catch yourself saying something silly.

"So golden!" one courtier repeated in a tiny voice, and everyone laughed again, gently.

Tenali Rama bowed low, his own smile soft and kind. "Your Majesty," he said, "words painted gold are still just words. True praise, like a true mango, is sweet on the inside."

The king nodded slowly, and his smile was warm. "Well said, Tenali Rama. Well said."

Then the king clapped his hands. "Bring plates! Bring plates!" he called. And the mangoes — those famous golden mangoes — were cut into soft, juicy pieces and shared with the whole court.

Nom-nom-nom. The court ate and laughed and talked, and the juice ran down their chins — even onto the fanciest of noses — and nobody minded one bit.

From that day on, whenever a courtier was about to say something too flattering, someone would whisper, "Careful… is that a golden mango?" And everyone would smile, and speak honestly instead.

And the king? The king loved his honest court best of all. That evening, he shared the very last piece of mango with Tenali Rama himself.

"Thank you, my clever friend," said the king.

Tenali Rama smiled. "Sweet dreams, Your Majesty."

And so, in the quiet palace, as the lamps glowed soft and low, the whole kingdom drifted off to sleep — honest, happy, and full of mango sweetness.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Honest words are sweeter than flattery.*
