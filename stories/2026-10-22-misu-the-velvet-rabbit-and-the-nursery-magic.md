# Misu the Velvet Rabbit and the Nursery Magic

On a crisp Christmas morning, a little boy named Veer found a velvet rabbit tucked into the top of his stocking. Misu — for that was soon his name — was round and bunchy, with a spotted brown-and-white coat, thread whiskers, and ears lined with pink satin. A sprig of holly sat between his paws, and he looked quite splendid.

For two whole hours, Veer loved Misu best of all. Then the parcels came, and in all the excitement Misu was forgotten. For a long while he lived on the nursery floor, and the fancier toys — the clockwork train, the tin drum — looked down their noses at him. Misu was only made of velvet, and he was naturally shy.

But in the corner stood Sage, an old wooden rocking horse with kind painted eyes. One quiet evening Misu whispered, "Sage, am I real?" "Real isn't about how you're made, little one," said Sage. "It's something that happens to you — when a child loves you for a long, long time, truly loves you, then you become Real."

That very night, Veer could not sleep. "Misu," he murmured, and pulled the velvet rabbit close, hugging him tight all night long. Misu had never felt so warm, so important, so loved.

From that night on, Misu went everywhere. He rode in the pram, sat at the tea parties, listened to every story. Bit by bit he grew shabby — his whiskers fell out, his pink ears turned grey with hugging, and his coat wore thin. Misu didn't mind one bit. He was loved, and that was everything.

Then spring came, and Veer played out in the garden more and more. One day a shiny new kite arrived, and poor shabby Misu was popped into the old toy box. "No one will love me now," Misu thought, and a small, real tear rolled down his cheek.

Where the tear fell, something wonderful happened. A tiny green shoot pushed up, and by morning it had grown into the most beautiful flower. Out of its petals stepped a tiny fairy — the Nursery Fairy, who looks after all loved toys.

"You are loved, Misu," she said, "and love has made you Real." She kissed his shabby velvet nose — and in a shimmer of petals he was a real, live rabbit, with soft brown-and-white fur.

Misu hopped out into the spring garden to play with the wild rabbits. He was Real at last. And sometimes, on quiet evenings, Veer would see him at the edge of the lawn, watching with kind, familiar eyes — and Veer would smile and wave.

Sleep now, little Misu, real and loved, under the kind spring moon.

The end. 🌙
