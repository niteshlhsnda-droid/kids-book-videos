# The Fox Without a Tail

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once, in a forest of tall pine trees, there lived a fox named Felix.

Felix was a fine-looking fox, with a bright red coat and the biggest, bushiest tail in the whole forest. He loved his tail. He would swish it left, and swish it right, and feel very proud indeed.

One chilly morning, Felix was trotting along a leafy path when — SNAP! — his beautiful tail got caught in a hunter's trap, hidden under the dry leaves.

Felix pulled and pulled. At last he wriggled free — but his beautiful, bushy tail was left behind in the trap.

At first, Felix's ears drooped low, and his eyes felt stingy, like tears wanted to come. Then, as he walked home, a sly little idea came into his head. …Now — what do you think he was planning?

He called all the foxes together for a meeting under the big oak tree.

"Friends!" he said, standing up very tall. "Tails are terribly old-fashioned. They catch in the thorns. They get heavy in the rain. I've given up my tail, and I feel lighter and quicker than ever! You should all give up your tails too!"

The foxes looked at one another. Some of them almost believed him.

Then wise old Grandma Fox stepped forward. She had the longest whiskers anyone had ever seen, and she had seen many clever tricks in her long life.

"Felix," she said kindly, "if tails were truly so bad, why did the trap take yours — and not you?"

Felix's ears turned pink. He opened his mouth… and shut it again. He had no answer at all.

The foxes smiled — not a mean smile, but a warm one — and trotted home, swishing their tails behind them.

And from that day on, Felix stopped pretending. He was happy to be himself, tail or no tail — and the other foxes loved him just the same.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Be happy as you are — and think twice about advice from someone who only wants company in their own bad luck.*
