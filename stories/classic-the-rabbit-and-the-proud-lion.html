The Rabbit and the Proud Lion

A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the heart of the green forest lived a young lion named Sheru.

Sheru was big and strong — his paws went thump-thump-thump on the forest floor, and his roar shook the leaves. And oh, how Sheru loved to tell everyone about it.

"Look at my mighty paws!" he would roar at breakfast. "Hear my thunder voice!" he would roar at lunch. "Nobody is as strong as ME!" he would roar at bedtime.

The parrot fluffed her feathers and tried to look small. The deer lowered his head. Even the old elephant, who was far bigger, just sighed and stepped aside.

The animals felt tiny whenever Sheru passed by.

One quiet afternoon, a little rabbit named Chintu hopped up beside him. Chintu's ears twitched, and his nose wiggled the way it always did when he had an idea.

"But Sheru," said Chintu softly, "there is ANOTHER lion in the forest. And he says HE is stronger than you."

Sheru stopped mid-step. His mane puffed up like a fluffy cloud.

"WHAT?" he roared. "Take me to him! Right now! Nobody is stronger than ME!"

"Very well," said Chintu. "Follow me."

Chintu hopped along the mossy path, and Sheru stomped behind him, grumbling under his breath.

At last they stopped beside an old stone well. It was deep and dark inside, and cool air drifted up from below.

"The other lion lives down there," Chintu whispered.

Sheru leaned over the edge and peered in. A lion's face stared back up at him — a fierce, glaring lion, with eyes wide and round.

Sheru puffed out his chest. "I AM THE STRONGEST!" he roared down the well.

And from the dark below came the answer: "I AM THE STRONGEST!"

Sheru's ears twitched. He roared even louder.

"RAAAWR!"

"RAAAWR!" roared the lion in the well, twice as fierce.

Sheru's knees began to wobble. His tail drooped. His mighty voice came out just a little bit squeaky.

"Who — who IS that?" he whispered.

Chintu giggled and patted Sheru's big paw. "It is you, Sheru. The well is only showing you yourself."

For a moment, Sheru just stood very still. Then his cheeks turned pink under his mane. He looked down at his own wobbly knees and burst out laughing — a big, rumbling, friendly laugh.

"I was roaring at MYSELF!" he said. "Oh, what a silly lion I have been."

That evening, Sheru gathered all the animals under the big peepal tree. He bowed his big head, low and slow.

"I am sorry," he said softly. "I boasted and boasted, and I made you feel small. That was not kind. From now on, I will use my strength to help, not to scare."

And he did.

When the river flooded the path, Sheru carried the little ones across on his back. When the old well lost its lid, he rolled a heavy stone over it, so nobody could tumble in. When the baby monkeys were afraid of the dark, his deep, rumbly voice sang them a lullaby.

At sunset, the animals would gather in the soft grass, and Sheru would tell the story of the lion in the well. The rabbit would giggle. The parrot would giggle. Even Sheru would giggle, his knees wobbling all over again at the funny memory.

And they all played together, happily ever after.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Cleverness and kindness beat pride.
