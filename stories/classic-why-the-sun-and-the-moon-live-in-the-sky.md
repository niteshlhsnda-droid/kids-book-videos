# Why the Sun and the Moon Live in the Sky

*A lovely African folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, when the world was still new, the Sun and his wife the Moon lived right here on the earth. They had a cozy little house — just like our house! And every morning the Sun would pop up over the hills and say, "Good morning, world!"

One of their very best friends was Water. Sploshy Water! Sun loved visiting Water's home — the great salty sea — where the little fishes and the green frogs loved to play in his warm sunshine.

One day, Sun said to Water, "Dear friend, why don't YOU come and visit ME for a change? Come to my house for dinner!"

"Are you sure?" bubbled Water. "I am as big as a thousand bathtubs! Are you sure your house is big enough for me?"

"Hmm," said the Sun, looking at his little house. "Your home is the whole big sea, and my house is just a little house... but you are my friend! Come, come — all of you are welcome!"

So Water began to flow toward Sun's little house. And guess what? Water did not come alone. Water brought ALL his family with him! In came the little silver fishes, splish-splashing. Hop-hopping came the green frogs. And the water rose in waves — the little waves splash-splashing, and the big waves swooshing in!

"Here we come!" bubbled the water, and it flowed in through the door.

First, the water came up to the Sun's ankles. Splash, splash! "Welcome!" said the Sun, dancing a little happy dance.

Then the water rose up to the Sun's knees. Splish, splash! "Welcome, welcome!" said the Sun, laughing.

Where do you think the water went next? Higher and higher — right up to the Sun's shoulders! "Oh my!" laughed the Sun, and "Oh, my stars!" giggled the Moon, as the water tickled their chins.

Up and up the water went, slosh-sloshing, till the whole little house was full of water — like one great big bathtub! So the Sun held his wife the Moon's hand, and they climbed up, up, up onto the roof. They sat on the rooftop, swinging their feet, watching the fishes swim past the chimney.

But the water kept coming! Higher and higher it rose, all the way to the tippy-top of the roof.

"What do we do now?" giggled the Moon. What would YOU do?

"Up we go!" laughed the Sun.

And so the Sun and the Moon hopped — high above the water, high above the roof, high above the swaying trees — until they were up in the deep, velvet-blue sky, where the stars twinkled like tiny lanterns.

And you know what? They liked it up there so much that they never came back down. The Sun stays up in the sky all day long, shining his warm smile down on the world. And the Moon comes out at night, soft and silvery, to tuck the whole world in to sleep.

So tonight, when you look out of your window, you will see the Moon smiling down at you, and all the twinkly stars winking, "Good night, good night." And the Sun is just resting, getting ready to say, "Good morning, world!" all over again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Welcome your friends warmly — and when a friend tells you something important, listen.*
