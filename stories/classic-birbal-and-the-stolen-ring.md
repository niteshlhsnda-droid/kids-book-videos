# Birbal and the Stolen Ring

*A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the great palace of Delhi, there lived a wise man named Birbal, the favourite friend of Emperor Akbar.

One morning, the queen came to the palace hall with tears in her eyes.

"My ruby ring is gone!" she cried. "It was my most precious treasure, and now it has vanished!"

Emperor Akbar was most upset. "Birbal!" he said. "You must find the queen's ring. You are the cleverest man in all the land!"

Birbal bowed and smiled. "Do not worry, Your Majesty. I will find the ring before the sun sets tomorrow."

That afternoon, Birbal called all the palace servants together. There were cooks and cleaners, gardeners and guards — twenty servants in all.

"Friends," said Birbal, holding up a bundle of short wooden sticks, "each of you will take one magic stick. Keep it with you all night."

The servants looked at the sticks. They were just ordinary little sticks, all exactly the same length.

"But listen carefully," said Birbal. "These are magic sticks. Tonight, the stick of the one who took the queen's ring will grow longer all by itself!"

The servants took their sticks and went away, whispering to one another.

Now, one of the servants — a young man named Ramu — had indeed taken the ring and hidden it in his room. All night long, Ramu could not sleep. He kept looking at his stick.

"What if it grows?" he thought. "Then everyone will know it was me!"

So, in the middle of the night, Ramu took a little knife and cut a tiny piece off the end of his stick. "Now it cannot grow longer than the others," he thought, and he went to sleep.

The next morning, all the servants came back with their sticks. Birbal lined them up on the table — one, two, three… all twenty sticks in a row.

Nineteen sticks were exactly the same length. But one stick was shorter than all the rest!

Birbal picked it up and smiled. "Ramu," he said gently, "your stick is the only one that is shorter. Only the one who took the ring would be afraid of a growing stick."

Ramu hung his head. "I am sorry," he said in a small voice. "I took the ring because it was so shiny and beautiful. I was wrong. Please forgive me."

And Ramu ran to his room and brought back the queen's ruby ring, shining bright as ever.

The queen smiled and put the ring back on her finger. "I forgive you, Ramu," she said. "A sorry heart is worth more than a shiny ring."

Emperor Akbar clapped his hands. "Birbal! You are clever indeed! You caught the thief without shouting, without scolding — only with a little stick and a lot of wisdom!"

And from that day on, Ramu worked honestly in the palace, and whenever he saw Birbal, he smiled — for he had learned that telling the truth is always lighter than carrying a secret.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A guilty heart gives itself away — honesty keeps you free.*
