Nasruddin's Sermon

A Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little village of white houses and dusty lanes lived Nasruddin — a kind, funny man with a round tummy, a twinkly eye, and a donkey who followed him everywhere like a shadow.

Everyone in the village loved Nasruddin, because his answers to puzzles always arrived sideways — with a wink that set the whole village laughing.

One day, the village elders came to his door. "Nasruddin," they said, "our speaker is away, and Friday is coming. Will YOU give the Friday talk at the mosque?"

Nasruddin stroked his beard. "Me? Give a talk? Well… why not!"

On Friday, the whole village gathered. Men in white caps, women with smiling eyes, children fidgeting on the mats. Nasruddin climbed the little wooden steps at the front of the mosque, looked out at all the faces, and said:

"My friends — do you know what I am going to say to you today?"

The villagers looked at one another. "No!" they called out. "We do not know!"

Nasruddin nodded wisely. "Well then — if you don't even know," he said, climbing down the steps, "what's the point of telling you?"

And he walked straight home, leaving the whole village blinking.

The elders scratched their heads. "We must answer differently next time," they decided.

So the next Friday, Nasruddin was asked again. He climbed the steps at the front of the mosque and asked, "My friends — do you know what I am going to say to you today?"

This time the villagers shouted, "YES! We know!"

Nasruddin smiled. "Wonderful! Then there is no need for me to say it at all."

And down the steps he went, leaving the whole village giggling.

Now the elders scratched their beards and frowned at one another. They held a meeting under the peepal tree. "Half of us must say YES," they decided, "and half of us must say NO. Then he will HAVE to speak!"

The third Friday came. Nasruddin climbed the steps at the front of the mosque. "My friends — do you know what I am going to say to you today?"

"YES!" shouted half the village.

"NO!" shouted the other half.

Nasruddin beamed. "Perfect!" he said. "Then let those who know tell those who don't!"

Everyone chuckled — Nasruddin had wriggled out of it once more!

And he walked home with a twinkle in his eye, while the whole village smiled all the way home.

Some people said Nasruddin was the wisest man in the village. Some said he was the silliest. But everyone agreed on one thing — it was the coziest evening the village had ever shared, and that night even Nasruddin's donkey nodded off on the warm doorstep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Sometimes the wisest answer is the one that makes everyone smile.
