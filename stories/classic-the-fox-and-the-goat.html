The Fox and the Goat

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One hot afternoon, a clever little fox named Felix was trotting through a green meadow, feeling very, very thirsty.

"Water, water," he panted. "Oh, I do wish I could find a cool drink!"

At the edge of the meadow stood an old stone well. Felix leaned over the edge to peek inside.

"There it is!" he cried. "Cool, clear water, right down at the bottom!"

But as Felix leaned a little too far — WHOOSH! — down he tumbled, SPLASH, right into the water!

Luckily, the water was not deep, and Felix was a very good swimmer. He paddled round and round in the cool well, but the walls were high and smooth, and he could not climb out.

"Oh dear," said Felix. "How will I ever get out of here?"

Just then, a gentle goat named Gina came trotting by. She was thirsty too, and she stopped beside the well.

"Hello down there!" called Gina. "Is the water nice and cool?"

"Oh, it is the loveliest water in the whole world!" called Felix, smiling his cleverest smile. "Sweet and cool and perfect! Come down and try it, Gina!"

Gina thought the water did sound lovely. So she jumped — SPLASH! — down into the well beside Felix, and took a long, cool drink.

"Ahh," said Gina. "That is good water. But now, Felix, how do we get out?"

Felix had been thinking hard. "I have a plan," he said. "Stand up tall, Gina. I will climb onto your back, and then onto your horns, and hop right out of the well."

"And then what about me?" asked Gina.

"Then I will help you, of course," said Felix. "A friend does not leave a friend behind."

So Gina stood up tall and still. Felix climbed onto her back, then carefully onto her horns, and with one great hop — BOING! — he was out of the well, standing in the sunshine.

For a moment, Gina waited. Then she heard Felix's voice from above.

"Hold on, Gina! I found a long, strong vine!"

Down came the vine, curling and green. Gina held it gently in her teeth, put her feet against the wall, and up, up, up she climbed, with Felix pulling from the top.

At last, both friends stood in the meadow again, dripping and laughing.

"Thank you, Felix," said Gina, shaking the water from her ears.

"And thank you, Gina," said Felix. "You were a brave and steady friend."

Then the two friends shared a snack of sweet clover under a shady tree, and they promised each other something important: always think before you leap — and never, ever leave a friend behind.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Think before you leap — and always help a friend back up.
