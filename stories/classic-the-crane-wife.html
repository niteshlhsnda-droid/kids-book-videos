The Crane Wife

A gentle Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a small village in Japan, there lived a kind young man named Taro.

One cold winter morning, Taro was walking home through the snowy forest when he heard a soft sound. "Help... help..."

There, beside a frozen pond, lay a beautiful white crane with a hurt wing. She could not fly.

"Oh, poor crane," said Taro, softly. "Do not be afraid. I will help you."

He wrapped the crane in his warm scarf and carried her home through the snow.

At home, Taro gave her warm water and soft rice, bandaged her wing, and sang her a gentle song.

"Thank you, kind Taro," whispered the crane. "You have a good heart."

Day by day, her wing grew strong. One bright morning, she rose into the blue sky.

"Goodbye, Taro!" she called. "Thank you!"

Taro waved until she was a white speck in the sky.

That evening, there came a soft knock at the door. Knock, knock, knock.

Taro opened the door, and there stood a lovely young woman with a gentle smile, wearing a soft white dress.

"Hello, Taro," she said, softly. "My name is Yuki. May I stay with you?"

"Of course," said Taro. "Come in, out of the cold."

Yuki was kind and clever, and she made the little house shine.

One day, Yuki said, "Taro, I can weave beautiful cloth, soft as clouds. But I must weave alone, behind a closed door. Please, promise me you will never peek."

"I promise," said Taro.

So Yuki went into the little room and closed the door. Click. All day, Taro heard a soft sound, shush, shush, shush, as she wove at her loom.

In the evening, Yuki came out with cloth more beautiful than anything Taro had ever seen.

Every day, Yuki wove beautiful cloth. But every evening, she looked a little more tired.

One day, Taro grew curious. What was Yuki doing in that little room?

He tiptoed to the door and peeked through a tiny crack.

Inside, he saw not Yuki, but a beautiful white crane, weaving with her own soft feathers.

"Oh!" gasped Taro. "It is my crane!"

The door creaked open, and Yuki stood there, smiling, though her eyes looked sad.

"Dear Taro," she said, gently, "you have seen my secret. I wove with my feathers because I loved you. Now I must fly away, but do not be sad."

Taro's eyes filled with tears. "I am sorry I peeked," he whispered. "Please do not go."

Yuki touched his cheek. "I will never be far away," she said. "Every spring, when the cherry blossoms open, I will come back to visit you. We will always be friends."

Then she stepped outside. With a soft flutter, she became a white crane again.

She left a white feather on the doorstep, like a little gift.

"Goodbye, dear Taro," she called. "See you in spring!"

Taro picked up the feather and held it close to his heart. "Goodbye, dear Yuki," he called. "I will wait for spring!"

And Taro kept the soft white feather beside his bed, where it glowed in the moonlight.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: True kindness is never forgotten — and love stays close, even when friends must part.
