# The Farmer and the Stork

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a village of golden wheat fields, there lived a farmer named Kishan.

Every year, Kishan planted his seeds with care. He watered them, he sang to them, and he watched them grow tall and golden in the sun.

But every year, a flock of grey cranes came to his fields. They pecked at the seeds. They trampled the young green shoots. They gobbled and gobbled, and left holes in Kishan's neat rows.

"Oh, those greedy cranes!" said Kishan, shaking his head. "This year, I will catch them!"

So Kishan set a fine net over his field, with a little pile of golden grain in the middle. Then he hid behind a big haystack and waited, quiet as a mouse.

In the morning, down came the grey cranes, flapping and honking. "Grain! Grain! Breakfast!" they cried.

They pecked and pecked, and — SNAP! — the net fell over them!

But among the grey cranes, there was one bird who was not a crane at all. She was Sara, a tall white stork, with long red legs and a kind, gentle face.

Sara had only come to say good morning to the cranes. She had not pecked a single seed. But now she was caught in the net, just like the rest.

"Oh dear!" cried Sara. "Mr. Farmer, please let me go! I am not a crane! I am a stork, and storks do no harm. I only eat little frogs in the pond. I never, ever eat your seeds!"

Kishan came out from behind the haystack and looked at Sara.

"That may be true, little stork," said Kishan slowly, "and your face does look very kind. But tell me this: why were you standing with the cranes, in my field, eating my grain?"

Sara drooped her long white neck. "I only came to say hello," she whispered. "I thought they were my friends."

Kishan nodded, but he did not open the net. "When you stand with birds who steal, people will think you steal too. We are known by the friends we keep."

Sara's heart sank. But then Kishan smiled a small, warm smile.

"Still," he said, "you have a kind face, and a kind voice. So I will let you go — but only you, little stork. The cranes must learn their lesson. And you must learn yours: choose your friends with care."

Kishan gently lifted Sara out of the net. She flapped her great white wings and rose into the blue sky.

"Thank you, Mr. Farmer!" she called. "I will remember! From now on, I will make friends with birds who are kind, and I will say good morning from the pond, not from a wheat field!"

Sara flew home to her nest by the pond. The frogs croaked a happy song. The moon rose round and silver.

And from that day on, Sara chose her friends wisely — the little duck, the gentle heron, and the tiny wren — and she never, ever stood with the greedy cranes again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: We are known by the friends we keep — so choose kind friends.*
