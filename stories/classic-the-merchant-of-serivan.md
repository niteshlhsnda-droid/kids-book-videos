# The Merchant of Serivan

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a land of green fields and winding roads, there lived a young merchant named Serivan.

Serivan was not rich. He had a little cart, a gentle brown pony, and pots and pans that went *clang-clang-clang* as he traveled from village to village.

"Good pots! Bright pans!" he would call in his friendly voice. "Come and trade with Serivan!"

The people liked him, because Serivan was honest. If a pan had a tiny dent, he said, "This one has a tiny dent — I will sell it cheaper." And everyone trusted his smile.

One warm morning, Serivan's cart rattled into a small village. *Rattle, rattle, clatter.*

At the edge of the village lived a poor old grandmother. Her hut was tiny, her clothes were patched, but her smile was as bright as the sun.

In her hut sat a dusty old bowl. It was dull and gray with dust, and it had been sitting in the corner for years and years.

Now, just before Serivan arrived, another merchant had come to the village. This merchant was greedy. He wore shiny rings and a shiny smile that was not really a smile.

The greedy merchant had seen the old bowl. And guess what? He knew a secret. Under all that dust, the bowl was solid gold!

But he did not tell the grandmother. Oh no.

"Old woman," he said, "this bowl is worth nothing. It is old and dusty. I will give you three tiny trinkets for it."

The grandmother looked at the trinkets. They were little glass beads — red, blue, green. *Tinkle, tinkle.*

"Only trinkets?" she asked. "For my grandmother's bowl?"

"Take them or leave them," said the greedy merchant, and he stomped his foot. *Stomp!*

Just then — *clang-clang-clang!* — Serivan's cart rattled into the lane.

The grandmother called out, "Young merchant! Come and see my bowl. The other merchant says it is worth only trinkets."

Serivan picked up the bowl. He wiped away the dust with his sleeve. *Rub, rub, rub.*

And there it was — shining gold, warm and glowing like the morning sun.

Serivan's eyes grew wide. He could have been greedy too. He could have said, "Yes, only trinkets," and kept the gold for himself.

But Serivan was honest. He looked the grandmother in the eye.

"Grandmother," he said gently, "this bowl is not worth trinkets. It is solid gold. It is worth more than everything in my whole cart."

The grandmother gasped. "Gold? My grandmother's bowl?"

"I will give you a fair price," said Serivan. "All my finest pots, all my shining pans, and all my coins — every last one."

He poured out his coins — *clink, clink, clink* — until his purse was almost empty.

The greedy merchant watched from across the lane. His face turned red as a tomato.

"GRRR!" he grumbled, and he stomped off down the road, *stomp, stomp, stomp*, shaking his shiny rings all the way home.

The grandmother held Serivan's hands. They were soft and warm.

"You have an honest heart," she said. "May your road always be smooth, and your cart always full."

Serivan smiled and bowed. *Clang-clang-clang!* Off he went, with an almost-empty cart and a very, very full heart.

And do you know what happened? People everywhere heard about the honest young merchant. They all wanted to trade with Serivan.

His cart grew fuller. His pony grew rounder. His smile grew wider.

And every night, under the twinkling stars, Serivan polished his pots and whispered, "Honesty first, always."

And the old grandmother? She bought a cozy warm shawl, a full pot of rice, and new shoes for her tired feet. She lived happy ever after.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Honesty is worth more than gold — a true heart always prospers.*
