The Wise Parrot

A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a tall, green forest lived a flock of parrots, and the wisest of them all was a parrot named Mithu.

Mithu had feathers as green as fresh leaves, a curved orange beak, and eyes that noticed everything. Every morning he flew to the top of the tallest tree, looked all around, and listened to the forest waking up.

Mithu loved his flock. There was Lila, who sang the sweetest morning songs. There was Kiki, who was always hungry. And there were the little parrot chicks, who practised flying in wobbly circles and landed in giggly heaps.

Each evening, the whole flock gathered in the great old banyan tree, and Mithu told them what he had seen — where the ripest mangoes hung, which stream was coolest, and which path the elephants liked to walk.

"Listen well, my friends," Mithu would say. "The forest tells us everything, if we listen."

One golden morning, Mithu flew to the top of the tallest tree and looked around. The sky was soft and pink. The leaves rustled gently. But far away, over the hills, Mithu saw dark clouds gathering, thick and grey, rolling closer and closer.

And then Mithu noticed something else. Down in the clearing below, a hunter had spread a wide net across the grass and hidden it under soft green leaves. It was a trap, waiting quietly for birds to land.

Mithu's heart beat fast. He flew back to the banyan tree as quick as the wind.

"Friends! Friends!" he called. "Listen to me! A big storm is coming from over the hills, and there is a net hidden in the clearing. Do not fly down to the clearing today. Not one of us must land there."

Lila tilted her head. "But the clearing has the sweetest seeds," she said.

"I know," said Mithu gently. "But the seeds can wait. Our safety cannot. The clouds are gathering, and the net is waiting. We must fly to the far side of the forest, to the quiet grove behind the river, where the trees are thick and the branches are strong."

Kiki fluffed his feathers. "Will we find food there?" he asked.

"We will," said Mithu. "There are figs there, and berries, and a stream of cool water. I saw it all yesterday. Follow me, and fly close together."

The little chicks peeped, "Will it be scary, Mithu?"

"Not if we stay together," said Mithu. "I will fly in front. Lila will sing to keep us brave. And we will all land together, safe and sound."

So up went the whole flock, wing to wing, feather to feather. Mithu flew in front, steady and sure. Lila sang a soft, brave song, and the little chicks flew in the middle, where the big parrots could keep them safe.

They flew over the river, silver in the morning light, and over the hill, where the mango trees swayed. They flew past the clearing — and not one parrot landed in the net.

Just as they reached the quiet grove, the sky rumbled. The rain came down, patter, patter, patter, on the thick green leaves. But under the strong branches, the parrots stayed dry and cosy, tucked close together.

"Look," called Mithu. "Figs and berries, just as I promised. Eat, my friends. We are safe here."

The parrots ate the sweet figs. They sipped the cool water. The little chicks snuggled into the soft feathers of their mothers and fell fast asleep, listening to the rain.

When the storm passed, the sky turned gold again. The clouds drifted away like slow white sheep. The forest smelled fresh and clean.

"Thank you, Mithu," said Lila. "You saw the danger, and you kept us safe."

"We kept each other safe," said Mithu, smiling. "You listened. You stayed together. That is the wisest thing of all."

That evening, the whole flock flew home to the old banyan tree, tired and happy. The little chicks told the story all over again — how Mithu had seen the clouds, and the net, and how they had all flown together to the quiet grove.

And as the stars came out, one by one, the parrots tucked their heads under their wings and slept, safe in the branches, dreaming of figs and gentle rain.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A wise friend who warns us in time, and friends who listen, keep everyone safe.
