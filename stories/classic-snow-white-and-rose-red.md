# Snow-White and Rose-Red

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, at the edge of a deep green forest, there lived a kind widow with her two little daughters.

One daughter had hair as white as fresh snow, so they called her Snow-White. The other had cheeks as red as roses, so they called her Rose-Red.

The two sisters loved each other dearly. They picked berries together. They sang to the birds together. And every evening they sat by the fire and listened to their mother's gentle stories.

One cold winter evening — knock, knock, knock! — someone tapped at the door.

Snow-White peeked out. There stood a great big brown bear, snowflakes sparkling on his shaggy fur!

Rose-Red squealed and hid behind her mother. But the bear spoke in a deep, kind voice. "Please don't be afraid. I am only cold. May I warm myself by your fire?"

The mother opened the door wide. "Come in, poor fellow!"

The bear shook the snow from his fur and lay down by the fire. Snow-White brushed the snowflakes from his coat. Rose-Red — brave little Rose-Red — soon climbed right onto his big, warm back and giggled!

All winter long, the bear came every evening. The girls told him stories. He told them about the forest in his rumbly voice. They became the best of friends.

But the bear always left before spring came. "I must go now," he would say sadly. "I have something important to do."

When spring arrived, the sisters were playing in the forest one sunny morning. They heard an angry, grumbly voice — "Let go of my beard, you silly bird!"

A grumpy little dwarf had caught his long beard in a tree crack, and a woodpecker was tugging it!

Snow-White and Rose-Red ran to help. They gently worked the beard free. But the dwarf stamped his feet. "Clumsy girls! You pulled my beautiful beard!" And he stomped away with a sack that clinked with gold.

The very next day, the same thing happened by the river — the dwarf's beard was tangled in fishing line! The sisters freed him again. Again he grumbled and stomped away.

The third time, they heard the dwarf shouting in fear. The big brown bear stood over him!

"Help!" cried the dwarf. "Take the girls instead! They are young and sweet — eat them, not me!"

But the bear just gave one great, gentle swat — and the dwarf's mean spell broke like glass!

For the dwarf had been a wicked enchanter who had turned a kind prince into a bear. Now the bear stood up tall — and turned into a handsome young prince with kind, twinkling eyes!

"Thank you," said the prince, bowing to the sisters. "Your brave, loving hearts broke the spell."

Snow-White married the prince, and Rose-Red married his kind brother. And their mother came to live in the palace garden, where roses bloomed all year round.

And the two sisters never forgot the winter evenings by the fire — when their dearest friend was a big, shaggy bear.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind and brave heart is the strongest magic of all.*
