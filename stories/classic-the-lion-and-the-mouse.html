The Lion and the Mouse

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the warm grasslands lived a great lion with a golden mane.

One sunny morning, the lion was taking a nap under the acacia tree. Snore... snore... snore...

A tiny mouse came scurrying by — scamper, scamper! — and ran right across the lion's big paw.

The lion woke up with a ROAR! He caught the little mouse under his paw.

"Please, please let me go!" squeaked the mouse. "I am so small, and you are so big. But one day, I might help you!"

The lion laughed a big, booming laugh. "HA HA HA! You? Help ME? Oh, that is funny!"

But the lion was in a good mood, so he lifted his paw. "Run along, little one," he said.

"Thank you, thank you!" squeaked the mouse, and scamper-scamper, he was gone.

Many days later, the lion was walking through the grass when — SNAP! — he stepped into a hunter's net!

The net pulled tight around him. He roared and struggled, but the ropes held fast. "ROAR! Help! Somebody help me!"

The little mouse heard the roar. Scamper, scamper! He ran to the net.

"Do not worry!" squeaked the mouse. "I will help you, just like I promised."

Nibble, nibble, nibble! The mouse chewed the ropes with his tiny sharp teeth. Nibble, nibble, nibble!

One rope broke. Then another. Then another!

At last, the net fell open, and the lion stepped out, free!

"Thank you, little friend," said the lion softly. "You were right. Even the smallest friend can be the greatest help."

And from that day on, the lion and the mouse were the best of friends.

Goodnight, lion. Goodnight, mouse.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness is never wasted — even the smallest friend can be the greatest help.
