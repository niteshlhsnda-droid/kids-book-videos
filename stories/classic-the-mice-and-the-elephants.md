# The Mice and the Elephants

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a meadow soft as a blanket, where the grass tickled your ankles, there lived a little mouse king named Minu.

Minu was very, very small — no bigger than your thumb! But he was brave and kind, and all the mice in the meadow loved him.

The mice had their homes in the warm earth — tiny burrows, little underground rooms, under the grass, where they slept all curled up like little furry balls.

But there was one problem. Every day, a herd of elephants walked across the meadow to drink at the lake.

And the elephants' big, heavy feet squished the burrows flat! Stomp, stomp, stomp!

"Oh no! My door!" squeaked one little mouse.
"Oh dear! My window!" squeaked another.

One evening, Minu the mouse king stood up on a little stone. "This cannot go on," he said. "I must talk to the elephant king."

So Minu scampered all the way to the lake, where the great elephant king was splashing cool water over his back. Splish, splash, splish!

"Hello, great Elephant King!" Minu called out in his small, squeaky voice.

The elephant king lowered his long, long trunk and peeked down. "Well, hello there, little one," he rumbled kindly. "What can I do for you?"

"O great king," said Minu, bowing low, "when your herd walks across the meadow, your big feet squish our little homes. Could you please walk a different way?"

The elephant king thought for a moment. Then he smiled — and when an elephant smiles, his whole face wrinkles up!

"Of course, little one," he said. "From now on, we will take another path to the lake. And tell your friends — the elephants are your friends now."

Minu's heart went pitter-patter with joy. "Thank you! Thank you!" he squeaked, and scampered home.

From that day on, the elephants walked around the edge of the meadow, and the mice danced happily in their safe little burrows.

But then, one night, the elephants got into trouble.

They were walking home through the tall grass — when suddenly — SNIFF! The elephants smelled something strange. Too late! The hunter had hidden strong, strong nets in the tall grass!

Thick ropes tangled around their legs and their trunks. No matter how hard they pulled, the nets would not let go.

The elephant king remembered his little friend. "Go find the mouse king!" he called in his big, deep elephant voice. "Only he can help us now!"

A little bird carried the message. What do you think brave little Minu did when he heard his friend needed help? He gathered hundreds and hundreds of mice — of course he did!

They scampered to the nets and went nibble, nibble, nibble — gnaw, gnaw, gnaw! All night long they chewed the thick ropes with their tiny sharp teeth.

And as the sun rose, pink and soft, the last rope snapped — and the elephants were free at last!

The elephant king lifted Minu gently in his trunk and gave him the biggest, softest hug.

"You saved us, little one," he said. "We are so glad we became friends."

And from that day on, the elephants and the mice were the best of friends — the biggest animals and the tiniest animals, all living happily together.

Now you close your eyes, little one... and drift off to sleep, cozy and safe, like a little mouse in a warm burrow...

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kindness always comes back — and no one is too small to help.*
