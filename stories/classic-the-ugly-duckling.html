The Ugly Duckling

A timeless tale by Hans Christian Andersen for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, on a sunny farm, a mother duck sat on her nest, waiting for her eggs to hatch.

Crack! Crack! Crack! One by one, five fluffy yellow ducklings tumbled out. They peeped and waddled and shook their tiny tails.

But the last egg was bigger than the rest. Crack! Out came a sixth duckling. He was big and grey, with long legs and a funny, wobbly walk.

"Oh, my," said the mother duck. "You look a little different, my dear. But you are my duckling all the same."

The yellow ducklings stared. "Why are you so big and grey?" they peeped. "You do not look like us at all."

Poor little Dusky — for that was his name — felt sad. He tried to waddle like the others. He tried to peep like the others. But he was still big and grey, and the farmyard animals teased him for it.

One morning, Dusky decided to go and see the wide world. "Somewhere," he thought, "there must be friends who like me just as I am."

He waddled past the pond. He wandered through the meadow. He slept under the stars. Autumn came, and the leaves turned gold, and then red, and then they fell. Winter came, and the pond froze, and Dusky shivered in the cold.

But Dusky kept going, and at last, spring came. The ice melted. Flowers opened. And one bright morning, Dusky came to a beautiful lake.

On the lake swam the most beautiful birds he had ever seen. They had long, graceful necks and feathers as white as fresh snow. They were swans.

"Oh," whispered Dusky. "How lovely they are. I will just watch them from here, so I do not frighten them."

But the swans saw him. They swam toward him, gliding across the sparkling water.

"Hello!" they called. "Come and swim with us!"

Dusky bent his neck low. "Please do not laugh at me," he said. "I know I am big and grey and different."

The swans looked at him — and then they laughed, but not in a mean way. They laughed with joy.

"Look at yourself in the water!" they cried.

Dusky leaned over the clear, still lake. And what did he see? Not a big grey duckling at all. He saw a beautiful young swan, with a long graceful neck and feathers as white as snow.

"Is that… me?" whispered Dusky.

"It is you!" said the swans. "You were never a duckling. You were a swan all along, growing into who you were meant to be."

Dusky spread his great white wings. He glided across the lake, and the sun sparkled on the water around him. All the other birds sang, because a new swan had come to the lake.

And Dusky was happy at last — not because he had changed into someone else, but because he had finally found where he belonged, just as he was.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Being different is not a flaw — sometimes you are just growing into who you were meant to be.
