# The Deer, the Jackal and the Crow

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green, green forest, beside a soft, grassy meadow, there lived a gentle deer named Mira.

Mira loved the morning most of all. She loved to nibble the sweet grass, to sip from the cool stream, and to listen to the birds singing in the tall trees.

One morning, a sly jackal named Jai came trotting across the meadow. He smiled his widest smile.

"Good morning, Mira!" said Jai. "You are the swiftest deer in the whole forest! You deserve the sweetest grass of all — and it grows just beyond the hill, in the farmer's field!"

Mira's ears twitched. "The farmer's field? But that is not our forest."

"Oh, it is quite safe," said Jai, waving his paw. "I go there every day. Come, come! The grass there is soft as a cloud and sweet as a mango!"

Now, high up in a mango tree, sat Koko the crow. Koko was Mira's oldest friend, and she knew Jai's tricks very well.

"Caw! Caw!" called Koko. "Mira, wait! Jai's words are sweet, but his heart is not. The farmer's field is full of snares. Stay in our meadow, where it is safe."

But Mira looked at the hill, and she thought of the sweet, sweet grass.

"Just one little taste," she said. "I will be quick as the wind."

And off she trotted, after Jai, over the hill.

The grass in the farmer's field was indeed soft and sweet. Mira nibbled and nibbled — crunch, crunch, crunch.

Then — SNAP!

A rope snare closed around her leg!

"Help!" cried Mira. "I am caught! Oh, I should have listened to Koko!"

Jai the jackal did not help at all. He danced around her, laughing. "Now the farmer will find you, and I will have the whole meadow to myself!"

But just then — flap, flap, flap! — Koko the crow flew down from the sky.

"Hold still, my friend," said Koko. "A true friend does not leave you when you are in trouble."

Koko pecked at the rope — peck, peck, peck! Her strong beak pulled and tugged at the knot. Again and again she pecked, until — POP! — the rope came loose!

Mira was free! She shook her leg and smiled. "Thank you, Koko. You are the truest friend in the forest."

But Jai was so busy laughing that he did not watch where he stepped. SNAP! The very same snare caught his own paw!

"Help! Help!" yelped Jai. "Mira! Koko! Please help me!"

Mira looked at Koko. Koko looked at Mira.

Then Mira smiled. "A kind heart forgives," she said. And together, Mira and Koko set Jai free.

Jai hung his head. "I am sorry," he said in a small voice. "I tried to trick you, and you helped me anyway."

"Then let us be friends," said Mira. "But no more tricks."

From that day on, Jai never tricked anyone again. And every morning, the deer, the crow, and the jackal shared the sweet grass of the meadow together — the safe, green meadow, right beside the forest.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sweet words can hide a snare — true friends warn you, and kind hearts forgive.*
