# The One-Inch Boy

*A gentle Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a small village in Japan, there lived a little boy who was only one inch tall.

His name was Issun-boshi, which means "one-inch boy." He was no taller than your thumb, but his heart was as big as the whole wide world.

Issun-boshi's mother made him a tiny sword from a sewing needle. His father made him a tiny boat from a soup bowl, and a tiny oar from a chopstick.

One morning, Issun-boshi put on his little straw hat and said, "Mother, Father, I am going to the big city to find my fortune!"

"Be brave, my little one," said his mother, kissing the top of his tiny head.

"Be kind, my little one," said his father, waving goodbye.

Issun-boshi floated down the river in his bowl-boat, paddling with his chopstick oar. "Row, row, row!" he sang. "A little boy can do big things!"

In the city, he met a kind lord, who gave him a job: to look after his daughter, the lovely Princess Hana.

Every day, Issun-boshi walked beside Princess Hana, his needle-sword shining at his side. He told her stories, he sang her songs, and he made her laugh.

One day, as they walked through the forest, a big ogre jumped out from behind a tree!

"ROAR!" said the ogre, waving his huge arms. "I am going to scare you both!"

Princess Hana was frightened. But Issun-boshi was not frightened at all.

"Stop right there!" he cried in his bravest voice. He waved his needle-sword high, climbed up the ogre's big shoe, ran all the way up his leg, and stood right beside his enormous ear.

"PEEK-A-BOO!" he shouted, as loud as his little voice could shout.

"Achoo! Hee-hee-hee! Stop! Stop!" laughed the ogre. The tiny shout tickled his ear so much that he laughed and laughed. He laughed so hard that he dropped his magic hammer — and off he ran, deep into the forest, laughing all the way home.

Princess Hana picked up the magic hammer. It sparkled like a star.

"Thank you for saving me, brave Issun-boshi," she said. "This hammer can grant one wish. I wish for you to grow big and tall!"

She tapped Issun-boshi gently on the head — tap, tap, tap!

And guess what? Issun-boshi began to grow! He grew and grew and grew, until he was as tall as any young man in the whole city!

Princess Hana smiled. "Now you are big on the outside too," she said. "Just as you were always big on the inside."

And so Issun-boshi lived happily ever after, brave and kind, in the big city — and every night, before bed, he thanked the little bowl-boat that had carried him so far.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Being small on the outside does not matter when your heart is big and brave.*
