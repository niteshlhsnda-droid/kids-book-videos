# Peri the Puffin and the Painted Sunrise

Peri was a small puffin chick who lived with her mother in a cozy burrow high on a rocky cliff above the sea. The burrow was lined with soft feathers, and from its round doorway Peri could see the whole wide ocean sparkling far below.

Peri had a round orange beak, a snowy white face, and bright curious eyes.

Every evening, she waddled out to the cliff edge to watch the sun sink slowly into the sea. "Where does the sun go?" she asked her mother one evening. "It rests behind the water," said Mother Puffin, "and in the morning it wakes up again."

But that night, after the sky turned the deep blue of midnight, a worried thought tucked itself into Peri's heart like a cold little stone. What if the sun forgot to wake up? What if morning never came?

She tossed and turned. At last she crept out of the burrow, tiptoed past her sleeping mother, and waddled down the winding cliff path to the shore. The stars winked above her, and the waves whispered softly.

"Sun!" she called into the dark water. "Sun, please wake up!"

An old crab clicked sideways across the sand toward her. "Little puffin," he said, "the sun has risen for me ten thousand mornings, and it has never once been late. Go back to sleep."

But Peri shook her head. "I will keep watch," she said. "If I stay awake, the sun will know I am waiting."

So she climbed onto the highest rock and stared out at the black sea. Her eyelids grew heavy, but she blinked them wide open again and again. "I am watching," she whispered.

Just then, a sleek grey head rose from the water beside the rock. It was Sammy the seal. "Little puffin," he said softly, "morning does not need your watching. It needs your dreaming. The sun finds its way whether you watch or not."

Peri yawned a great big yawn. But before she could answer, the far edge of the sea blushed the palest pink. Then came gold, and a bright orange rim lifted above the waves, and the whole sky began to bloom like a giant flower — rose and peach and soft violet, painted just for her.

Peri gasped. The sun had come back, all by itself.

Mother Puffin waddled up beside her and wrapped a warm wing around her. "See?" she said gently. "Some things you do not have to hold up. Morning knows the way."

Peri smiled a sleepy smile, and her heart felt warm and light as a feather. She waddled back up the cliff, tucked herself into her feather bed, and closed her bright curious eyes.

Sleep now, little Peri.

The end. 🌙
