# Momotaro, the Peach Boy

*A beloved Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little village in Japan, there lived an old woodcutter and his kind wife, in a small house beside a sparkling river.

One bright morning, the old woman went to the river to wash clothes. The water sparkled in the sunshine, and suddenly she saw something bobbing along — a peach, the biggest she had ever seen, round and rosy and glowing in the sun.

"Oh my!" she cried. "What a wonderful peach! My husband will love it."

She fished it out of the water and carried it home. That evening, as the knife touched the peach, it split open with a soft pop — and inside, curled up like a tiny bundle, was a baby boy!

The baby opened his eyes and smiled. He had rosy cheeks and a bright, happy laugh.

"Oh!" cried the old woman. "A baby! A beautiful baby boy!"

"We will call him Momotaro," said the woodcutter, "because he came to us from a peach." And Momotaro means "peach boy."

Momotaro grew up strong and kind. He helped his parents carry wood, he shared his rice cakes with everyone, and he always said thank you.

One day, Momotaro heard a sad story. Far away, on Ogre Island, lived a group of grumpy ogres. The ogres had gathered up all the treasure in the land — shiny gold and sparkling jewels — and they kept it all for themselves. They never shared with anyone.

"That is not fair," said Momotaro. "Treasure is meant to be shared. I will go to Ogre Island and ask the ogres to share."

His parents packed him a bag of sweet rice cakes, and Momotaro set off. Along the way, he met a brave dog, a clever monkey and a beautiful singing pheasant, and each one asked, "May I come with you?" Each time Momotaro said, "Of course," and shared a rice cake with his new friend.

Together, the four friends sailed across the blue sea to Ogre Island.

When they arrived, the ogres came out, looking big and grumpy. "Who are you?" they growled. "What do you want?"

Momotaro bowed politely. "I am Momotaro, and these are my friends. We have not come to fight. We have come to ask — will you please share your treasure with everyone?"

The ogres looked at one another. No one had ever asked them so kindly before. No one had ever offered to be their friend.

Slowly, the biggest ogre smiled. "We are sorry," he said. "We kept everything for ourselves, and it made us lonely. Yes, we will share."

And so the ogres opened their treasure chests and shared the gold and jewels with the whole village. That evening, everyone feasted together — the villagers, the ogres, Momotaro, the dog, the monkey and the pheasant.

That night, as the moon rose over the quiet village, Momotaro yawned a big, sleepy yawn. His friends curled up beside him — the dog, the monkey and the pheasant — and they all drifted off to sleep, dreaming of feasts and friends and shared treasure.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing turns strangers into friends.*
