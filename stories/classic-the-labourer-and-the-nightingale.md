# The Labourer and the Nightingale

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a village at the edge of a green wood, there lived a kind labourer named Arjun.

Every morning, Arjun walked to the fields with his basket and his lunch. Every evening, he walked home again, tired and dusty, but happy — because every evening, as the sun went down, a little nightingale named Sona sang the sweetest song in the whole world.

Sona's song floated over the fields like honey. "La-la-la, la-la-lee!" she sang. "The day is done, the stars are coming, time to rest, little ones!"

And Arjun would smile and say, "That little bird sings only for me."

One evening, Arjun had an idea. "If I catch Sona and keep her in a cage by my window," he thought, "she will sing for me all day long, whenever I like!"

So the next morning, Arjun crept quietly to the old oak tree and — very gently — caught little Sona in his soft hands.

"Do not be afraid," he said. "I will give you the finest seeds and the freshest water. And you will sing for me all day!"

He put her in a pretty little cage by his window and waited.

But Sona did not sing.

She sat in the corner of the cage, very still and very quiet, with her head tucked under her wing.

Arjun sang to her. He whistled to her. He put sweet seeds in her dish. But Sona did not sing — not one note. Not all day.

Day after day, the cage stayed silent. And Arjun grew sadder and sadder.

"Why will you not sing, little bird?" he asked. "I give you food and water and a pretty cage."

At last, Sona lifted her head and said, in a small, sad voice, "Arjun, my song is not a thing you can keep in a cage. My song belongs to the evening sky, and the tall trees, and the soft wind. When I am free, my heart is full — and that is why I sing."

Arjun looked at the little bird. He looked at the cage. And he understood.

"You are right, little Sona," he said. "I am sorry. A song is sweetest when it is free."

He opened the little door of the cage.

Sona fluttered out into the golden evening air. She flew up, up, up to the top of the old oak tree. And there, as the first stars came out, she sang — the sweetest song Arjun had ever heard.

"La-la-la, la-la-lee! The day is done, the stars are coming, time to rest, little ones!"

Arjun sat on his doorstep and listened, with a big smile on his face.

And from that day on, Sona sang every evening from the old oak tree — free as the wind, and twice as sweet.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A song is sweetest when it is free — and so is a friend.*
