# The Leaf Who Wanted to Fly

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In autumn, a little leaf named Lola lived on a tall maple tree.

She was bright orange with a pointy tip.

All summer she had watched the birds.

She felt wishful.

"Oh, I wish I could fly," she sighed.

The other leaves rustled.

"Leaves don't fly," said one.

"We just hang here," said another.

Lola felt a little sad.

But then autumn came.

The air turned crisp.

One morning, a gentle breeze came by.

"Whoosh," said the wind. "Come with me, Lola."

Lola let go of her branch.

And she flew!

Up she went, and around, and down.

"I'm flying! I'm flying! I'm flying!" she cried.

That was three times she said it.

She felt joyful.

She twirled over the garden.

She swooped past the fence.

She danced with two yellow leaves and one red leaf.

"Hello!" they called.

"Hello!" called Lola.

She felt proud of her twirls.

She felt brave in the big sky.

But soon the wind grew tired.

"Time to rest, little leaf," said the wind.

Lola floated down, down, down.

She landed softly on the grass.

She felt calm.

A small boy found her there.

"What a pretty leaf," he said.

He picked her up gently.

He carried her home in his pocket.

He pressed her into his scrapbook.

He drew a little sky around her with his crayons.

A blue sky, just for Lola.

"Now you can fly in my book forever," he said.

Lola felt cozy.

She was done flying for one day.

The wind hummed a lullaby through the trees.

Hush, hush, hush.

Lola closed her eyes in the scrapbook.

Sleep now, little leaf.

Dream of twirling through the autumn sky.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sometimes wishes come true in their own gentle way.*
