# Tenali Rama and the Milkman

*A witty Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the kingdom of Vijayanagara, there lived a milkman who sold milk in the market.

Every morning, he carried his pots of milk to the square, calling, "Fresh milk! Pure milk! The best milk in the land!"

But the milkman was not honest. Each morning, before he went to market, he poured water into his milk, so that his pots looked full, though half of each pot was only water.

The people of the city did not know. They drank the thin, watery milk and wondered why it tasted so weak.

One morning, the clever poet Tenali Rama came to the market. He bought a pot of milk from the milkman and took it home.

He poured a little into a cup and tasted it. "Hmm," said Tenali Rama. "This milk tastes like water. This milkman is cheating us all!"

Tenali Rama smiled a clever smile. "I will show the king," he said. "King Krishnadevaraya will want to know about this."

The next morning, Tenali Rama bought another pot of milk from the milkman and carried it to the palace.

"Your Majesty," he said, bowing low, "I have brought you the finest milk in the market. Please, watch what happens when I pour it out."

Tenali Rama poured the pot into a big, clear bowl. The liquid was pale and thin, like milky water.

"Your Majesty," said Tenali Rama, "real milk is thick and creamy. This is mostly water! This milkman waters down his milk every single day."

The king frowned. "Bring the milkman to me at once!" he commanded.

The milkman came trembling to the palace.

"Is it true?" asked the king. "Do you water down your milk?"

The milkman looked at his shoes and said nothing.

Then Tenali Rama poured a cup of the watery milk and held it out to the milkman. "Here," he said kindly. "Drink a cup of your own milk."

The milkman took the cup and sipped. His face twisted. "Ugh!" he cried. "It is too watery! I cannot drink it!"

Everyone in the court smiled warmly, and even the king nodded.

"You cannot drink your own milk," said the king, "yet you sell it to my people every day. That is not right."

The milkman hung his head. "I am sorry, Your Majesty," he said. "I was greedy. From now on, I promise, my milk will be pure."

The king nodded. "A promise is a good start," he said. "And because you have said sorry with all your heart, I will give you a chance. From today, you shall be the palace milkman, and every morning we shall taste your milk. If it is pure, you will keep your place."

The milkman bowed low, his eyes shining with joy. "Thank you, Your Majesty! Thank you, Tenali Rama! My milk will be pure from this day on!"

And so it was. Every morning, the milkman brought thick, creamy, pure milk to the palace, and to the market too, and the people drank it gladly.

And Tenali Rama smiled, for a clever trick and a kind heart had made the whole kingdom's milk pure again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Honest work tastes sweet — cheating only leaves a sour taste.*
