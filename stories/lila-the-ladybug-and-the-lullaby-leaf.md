# Lila the Ladybug and the Lullaby Leaf

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green garden, on a broad green leaf, lived a tiny red ladybug named Lila, with seven neat black spots on her back. Fireflies dotted the bushes like tiny golden lamps, and the night jasmine smelled sweet in the dark.

Every night Lila curled up on her leaf and fell asleep in a wink. But tonight — oh, tonight was different.

The night wind had come to play. It pushed her leaf — creak, sway! It pulled her leaf — swoosh, sway! Back and forth, back and forth went Lila's leaf, like a boat on a wobbly sea.

"Oh dear," whispered Lila, holding on tight with all six feet. "How can anyone sleep in all this wobbling?"

She decided to ask her friends for help. First she flew — buzz-buzz — to Cricket.

"Cricket," she said, "the wind won't let me sleep. Can you sing me a sleepy song?"

Cricket puffed up his chest and rubbed his wings together — chirpy-chirp-chirp! chirpy-chirp-chirp!

"Oh," said Lila, rubbing her ears. "That is a very fine song, Cricket. But it is too chirpy for sleeping."

Next she flew — buzz-buzz — to Owl, sitting big and round on his branch, with the moonlight silvering his feathers.

"Owl," she said, "the wind won't let me sleep. Can you sing me a sleepy song?"

Owl opened his round eyes wide and sang — hoo-hoo, hoo-hoo!

"Oh," said Lila, covering her ears. "That is a very grand song, Owl. But it is too hoo-y for sleeping."

Then, down by the pond, she found old Tortoise, slow and wise, tucked under his shell like a blanket.

"Tortoise," she said, "the wind won't let me sleep."

Tortoise opened one sleepy eye and smiled a slow, slow smile.

"Don't fight the wind, little one," he said. "Sing to it."

"Sing to the wind?" said Lila, tilting her tiny head.

"Try," said Tortoise, and closed his eye again. "The wind loves a soft song. It always has. And when the wind is happy, little one — it sings along."

Lila thanked old Tortoise with a little bow, and flew home through the soft night air — buzz-buzz.

So Lila curled up on her wobbly leaf. She tucked her six little feet under her spotted wings, took a deep breath, and she sang the slowest, softest song she could:

"Hmm-hmm-hmmm… hmm-hmm-hmmm…"

And do you know what happened? The wind began to listen. It softened. It slowed. The wild whoosh turned into a gentle breeze — and the breeze rocked Lila's leaf like a cradle. Sway… sway… sway… The wind hummed along, soft as a sigh: "Hmm-hmm… hmmm…"

Lila sang on, softer and softer and softer, until her song was just a sleepy whisper: "Hmm-hmm… hmmm…"

Her eyelids drooped. Her six little feet slowly let go. And she drifted off to sleep — spots and all — while the stars twinkled down like tiny night-lights.

The breeze rocked her leaf, gentle and slow, all night long. And somewhere, far away, old Tortoise smiled in his sleep, because he knew his secret had worked.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A soft song can calm even the wildest wind.*
