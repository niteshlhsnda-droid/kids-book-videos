# The Cave That Talked

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In the rocky hills lived Sheru the Lion. Sheru was old and slow now, and he had a clever trick for catching his dinner. Every evening he slipped into the cool, dark mouth of the cave. The air inside smelled of wet stone. And there he waited — still as a stone — till a small animal wandered in to rest… *pounce!* — quick as a blink, quiet as a whisper.

One evening, Jumpy the Jackal came trotting back to the cave where he slept. He stopped at the dark opening of the cave and sniffed the sandy ground. Can you guess what his little jackal nose found out?

"Hmm," thought Jumpy. "I see many footprints going *in*to this cave… but not a single footprint coming *out*."

Jumpy was clever. He did not walk in. Instead, he cupped his paws around his mouth and called out in his sweetest voice, "O Cave! Dear Cave! Can I come in tonight?"

Silence.

Now, every evening Jumpy greeted the cave, and the cave always answered back — a funny sound, like his own voice coming back to say hello. So Jumpy called again, "O Cave! Why are you so quiet today? Are you angry with me?"

Still silence. Inside, Sheru held his breath. *Stay quiet, old lion… hold still… and supper walks right in.*

But Jumpy just smiled his cleverest smile. He dropped his voice low and hollow, like an echo, and answered *himself*: "Come iiiin, Jumpy! Come iiiin!"

Sheru heard the "cave" inviting Jumpy in, and he could wait no longer. With a big, grumbly roar, Sheru rushed out of the cave — but Jumpy was already far, far away, safe and sound.

Jumpy trotted home and curled up in his warm, sandy bed. "Thank you for the warning, dear Cave," he whispered with a happy little smile. His eyes grew heavy… and he drifted off to sleep.

Goodnight, Jumpy. The end. 🌙
