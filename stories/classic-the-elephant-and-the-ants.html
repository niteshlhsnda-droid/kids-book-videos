The Elephant and the Ants

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a sunny green meadow, there lived a big elephant named Ella.

Ella was the biggest animal in the whole meadow. Her feet were as big as dinner plates. Her trunk was long and strong. When she walked, the ground went thump, thump, thump!

Near the path where Ella walked every day, there lived a family of tiny ants.

The ants had built a little hill-home out of soft earth. It had tiny rooms and tiny tunnels and a tiny front door made of a leaf.

One morning, Ella came walking down the path, thump, thump, thump! She was not looking where she stepped. Her big foot came down — right on top of the ants' little hill!

Crash! The hill-home crumbled. The tiny rooms fell in. The leaf door toppled over.

"Oh no!" cried the ants. "Our home!"

The littlest ant, a brave one named Anu, climbed up onto a pebble and called out in her tiniest, bravest voice, "Excuse me, big Ella! Please, please look where you step. You squashed our home!"

Ella looked down. She saw the tiny ants and their broken little hill.

"Ha!" she laughed. "You are so small. What can tiny ants do to a big elephant like me?"

And she walked away, thump, thump, thump, without even saying sorry.

The ants looked at their broken home. Then they looked at each other. Then they had an idea.

"She is big," said Anu. "But we are many. And we are clever. Let us all work together."

So the ants marched, one by one, two by two, up Ella's big leg. Tickly, tickly, tickly! They marched up to her trunk. Tickly, tickly, tickly!

"Hey!" said Ella. "What is that tickly feeling?"

Then the ants tickled the very tip of her trunk — the tickliest spot of all!

"Aaaah… aaaah… AAAH-CHOO!" Ella sneezed the biggest sneeze in the whole meadow!

The ants tumbled down softly onto the grass, giggling. And Ella laughed too, because a sneeze always makes you laugh a little.

"Oh, little ants," said Ella, sitting down gently, "I am sorry. I was careless and I was rude. You may be small, but you are clever and brave. Will you forgive me?"

"Yes," said Anu, "if you help us build our home again."

So Ella helped. Very, very carefully, she used her long trunk to carry soft earth and leaves. The ants showed her where to put them. And soon the little hill-home was standing again — even better than before, with a new leaf roof and a tiny flower by the door.

From that day on, Ella always looked where she stepped. And every morning, she stopped by the ants' hill to say, "Good morning, little friends!"

And the ants would wave their tiny legs and call back, "Good morning, big Ella!"

That night, Ella lay down under the stars, and the ants curled up in their cosy hill-home. The meadow was quiet and soft, and everyone slept well — big and small together.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Even the smallest friends matter — and being big means being gentle.
