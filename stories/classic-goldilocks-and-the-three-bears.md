# Goldilocks and the Three Bears

*A timeless fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a cozy cottage in the woods lived three bears — Papa Bear, Mama Bear, and Baby Bear.

One morning, Mama Bear served hot porridge for breakfast. "It is too hot to eat," she said. "Let us take a walk while it cools."

Off they went — stomp, stomp, stomp — into the woods.

Just then, a little girl with golden curls came skipping down the path. Her name was Goldilocks.

She peeked through the window. "Nobody is home," she said. "I will just look inside for a minute." And in she went — though she had not been invited.

On the table were three bowls of porridge. Goldilocks tasted the biggest bowl. "Too hot!" she said.

She tasted the middle bowl. "Too cold!"

She tasted the littlest bowl. "Just right!" And she gobbled it all up. Yum, yum, yum!

Then she sat in the biggest chair. "Too hard!" She sat in the middle chair. "Too soft!" She sat in the littlest chair. "Just right!" — CRACK! The little chair broke into pieces!

Feeling sleepy, Goldilocks climbed the stairs. She tried the biggest bed. "Too lumpy!" The middle bed. "Too bumpy!" The littlest bed. "Just right!" And she fell fast asleep. Zzz...

Soon the three bears came home — stomp, stomp, stomp.

"Someone has tasted my porridge!" growled Papa Bear.

"Someone has tasted my porridge!" said Mama Bear.

"Someone has tasted my porridge — and eaten it all up!" squeaked Baby Bear.

"Someone has sat in my chair!" growled Papa Bear.

"Someone has sat in my chair!" said Mama Bear.

"Someone has sat in my chair — and broken it!" squeaked Baby Bear.

Upstairs, they found Goldilocks asleep in Baby Bear's bed!

Goldilocks woke up, saw three bears, and — EEK! — she jumped up and ran all the way home as fast as her legs could carry her.

She never went into someone's house without being invited again. And whenever she remembered the three bears, she felt sorry — and she learned to always ask first.

Goodnight, Goldilocks.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Always respect other people's homes and things — and ask before you take.*
