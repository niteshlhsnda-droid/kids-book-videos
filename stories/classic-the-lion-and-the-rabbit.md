# The Lion and the Rabbit

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, at the edge of a green forest, there lived an old lion named Sheru.

Sheru was big and grumbly. His mane was a little gray, and his roar was still very loud. Every morning he sat on his rock and said to all the animals,

"Every day, one of you must come and visit me. And do hurry — I do not like to wait!"

The animals whispered to each other. "Oh dear," they said. "The old lion is grumpy again."

Now, in a cozy burrow under the roots of a banyan tree lived a little rabbit named Rumi. Rumi was small, with ears like soft leaves and a tail like a puff of cloud.

One morning it was Rumi's turn to visit the lion. But Rumi was clever, and she had a plan.

She took her time. She nibbled some clover. She washed her whiskers. *Nibble, nibble. Splish, splash.*

Then, very late, she hopped slowly, slowly to the lion's rock.

"RUMI!" roared Sheru. "Why are you so late? I have been waiting and waiting!"

Rumi twitched her nose and bowed very low.

"O great King Sheru," she said, "I hurried as fast as my little legs could hop. But on the path, another lion stopped me."

"ANOTHER lion?" said Sheru, his ears pricking up.

"Oh yes," said Rumi. "A bigger lion. A fiercer lion. He said, 'I am the king of this forest now. Tell old Sheru to stay home and be quiet.'"

Sheru's mane puffed up like a round gray cloud.

"A BIGGER lion? A FIERCER lion?" he roared. "ROAR! Take me to him at once!"

So Rumi hopped down the path, *hop-hop-hop*, and the old lion padded after her, *thump-thump-thump*.

She led him to the oldest well in the forest. It was deep and round, and the water at the bottom was dark and still as a mirror.

"O King," whispered Rumi, "your rival is down there. Look, but do not fall in!"

Sheru leaned over the edge and looked down into the well.

And there, in the water, he saw a lion — big, gray-maned, with wide eyes and a very grumpy face.

"It is HIM!" growled Sheru.

"It is him," whispered Rumi, hiding a giggle behind her paw.

"ROAR!" roared Sheru, and the well went *ROAR!* right back at him.

The lion in the water opened his mouth just as wide. The lion in the water looked just as fierce.

Sheru roared again. The other lion roared again. Louder and louder they went, until poor Sheru's throat was all scratchy and his voice went *croak*.

Then Sheru stopped. He looked at the lion in the water. The lion in the water looked back at him — tired, gray-maned, and very, very grumpy.

"O King," said Rumi softly, "do you see? That is what the animals see when you roar at them. A fierce, frightening face."

Sheru looked again. His roar faded away like a popped balloon. *Pffff.*

"I look... cross," he said quietly. "I look lonely."

"You do not have to be fierce to be a king," said Rumi. "Try being kind instead. Kindness is much cozier."

The old lion sat down beside the well. He thought and thought. Then he smiled — a big, warm, wrinkly smile.

"You are right, little rabbit," he said. "From this day on, I will never frighten anyone again."

Word spread through the forest like sunshine through the leaves. *Rustle, rustle — the lion is kind now!*

The animals were so happy that they planned a feast — a mango feast, just for Sheru.

They brought mangoes, fat and golden and juicy. *Squish!* *Slurp!* Sheru had never tasted anything so sweet.

The monkeys drummed on hollow logs. *Boom, boom-boom!* The parrots sang. Little Rumi sat beside the lion and ate mango slices, swinging her feet.

That night, under a round silver moon, Sheru lay on his rock — not roaring, just smiling.

"Kindness," he rumbled sleepily, "is the coziest crown of all."

And all the animals of the forest drifted off to sleep, safe and happy.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kindness is stronger than a roar — a gentle heart makes the best king.*
