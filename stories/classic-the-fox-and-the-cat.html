The Fox and the Cat

A classic Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Felix the fox loved to brag.

"I know a hundred clever tricks!" he boasted to anyone who would listen. "A hundred! I can zig, I can zag, I can double back, I can play dead, I can hide in a haystack, I can —"

"That is a lot of tricks," said Cleo the cat, licking one soft paw. "I know only one."

"Only ONE?" Felix laughed, rolling on the grass. "Poor Cleo! What is your one little trick?"

"Climbing a tree," said Cleo. "Quick as a whisper. I practice it every single day."

"Practice!" Felix scoffed. "Who needs practice when you have a hundred tricks?"

Just then — far away at first, then nearer — came a sound that made both their ears stand straight up.

Bay! Bay! Bay! The hounds were coming!

Felix's hundred tricks tumbled through his head all at once. "Zig? Zag? Double back? Play dead? Which one — which one —"

He zigged. He zagged. He doubled back. But his paws were too slow, and the baying grew louder and louder.

"Felix!" called Cleo from high in the old oak tree. "Up here! Quick!"

Felix looked up. Cleo's one little trick suddenly seemed like the cleverest trick in the whole world.

Up he scrambled — scratch-scratch-scratch — just as the hounds came bounding into the clearing below. They sniffed and snorted and circled the tree, and then, finding nothing, they trotted away into the dusk.

Felix and Cleo sat side by side on a thick branch, watching the last light fade.

"Your one trick saved us both," Felix said quietly.

Cleo purred. "One trick, practiced well, is worth a hundred tricks never tried."

Felix nodded slowly. "From today, I will practice one good trick instead of bragging about a hundred."

And he did. Every morning, Felix practiced climbing — up the oak, down the oak, quick as a whisper. Cleo watched from her sunny branch and purred her approval.

Soon Felix could climb almost as fast as Cleo herself. "Not bad," said Cleo, "for a fox with only one trick."

Felix grinned. "The best trick," he said.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: One trick learned well is worth a hundred only talked about.
