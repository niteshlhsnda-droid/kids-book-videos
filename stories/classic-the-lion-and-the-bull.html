The Lion and the Bull

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Leo the lion was the king of the forest. He had a big golden mane that shone in the sun, and a roar that rolled like happy thunder.

Benny the bull was gentle as a summer cloud. He had soft brown eyes, slow heavy hooves, and a kind, kind heart.

One sunny morning, they met at the water hole — and do you know what happened? They became the BEST of friends.

Every single day, they played together. They chased each other through the tall, tickly grass. They splashed in the cool stream — splash, splish, splash! At noon, they napped side by side under the big banyan tree, and the leaves went sh-sh-sh above them like a lullaby.

Leo shared his shady rock. Benny shared his sweetest grass. They laughed and laughed until their bellies ached.

But someone was watching them — and he was not happy at all.

Jasper the jackal sat behind a bush, and his heart felt sour and grumbly. "Why do THEY get to have all the fun?" he grumbled. "I will spoil their friendship. I will!"

So Jasper made a sneaky, whispery plan.

That evening, he padded softly up to Leo. "Leo," he whispered, looking left and right, "I heard something terrible. Benny says you are a COWARD. He says your roar is silly!"

Leo's big golden mane puffed up like an angry cat's tail. "WHAT?" he roared. "Benny said THAT?"

Then Jasper trotted quickly to Benny. "Benny," he whispered, "I heard something awful. Leo is planning to EAT you! He says bulls taste delicious!"

Benny's soft brown ears trembled. "WHAT?" he snorted. "Leo would never—"

The next morning, Leo and Benny met at the water hole, just like always. But this time, Leo's eyes were narrow. And Benny's head was low.

"Is it true?" Leo growled. "Do you think my roar is silly?"

"Is it true?" Benny snorted. "Do you want to EAT me?"

They pawed the ground. Dust rose up around their feet. They were about to fight — really, truly fight.

But then — Benny stopped.

He remembered chasing butterflies with Leo through the morning mist. He remembered sharing ripe mangoes under the banyan tree, juice dripping down their chins. He remembered laughing until their bellies ached.

"Wait," said Benny softly. "Stop. We are best friends. And best friends do not believe whispers. Best friends TALK."

Leo took a big, deep breath. "Benny," he said slowly, "did you really say my roar is silly?"

"Of course not!" said Benny. "Your roar is the best sound in the whole forest. Now tell me, Leo — did YOU really plan to eat me?"

"Never!" said Leo. "You are my very best friend."

They looked at each other. And slowly, they both understood.

"JASPER!" they said together.

Behind the bush, Jasper the jackal's ears drooped — down, down, down. He tucked his tail between his legs and slunk away into the forest, and nobody saw him for a very, very long time.

Leo sat down in the soft grass. Benny sat down right beside him. "I am sorry I believed him," said Leo quietly.

"I am sorry too," said Benny. And they promised each other: "From now on, we will always talk to each other. Always."

That evening, they watched the sun melt into soft pink and gold, side by side, just like always.

And they stayed best friends — forever and ever.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Don't believe gossip — talk to your friends.
