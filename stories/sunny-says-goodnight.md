# Sunny Says Goodnight

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High above the clouds lived Sunny the sun. Every morning she woke up bright and cheery. She shone on flowers. She warmed the playgrounds. She loved daytime more than anything.

But when evening came, Sunny did not want to set. "I am not tired!" she declared. She felt bouncy and wide awake. She kept shining and shining.

Down below, the world needed nighttime. Baby birds needed to sleep. Flowers needed to close. Little children needed the dark to dream. But Sunny would not dim.

Mother Moon floated up beside her. "Sunny," she said gently, "even suns need sleep. Let me show you the goodnight stretch."

Sunny felt grumpy. "I do not want to stretch. I want to shine!"

"Just try," said Mother Moon. "First, stretch your rays out wide. As wide as you can."

Sunny stretched her golden rays. They reached across the whole sky. It felt rather nice.

"Now pull them in close," said Mother Moon. "Tuck them in like a blanket."

Sunny pulled her rays in. The sky turned pink, then orange, then purple. Sunny felt a tiny yawn coming.

"Stretch, yawn, goodnight," sang Mother Moon.

Sunny stretched once more. She yawned a great golden yawn. Her light grew softer. Down below, the baby birds tucked their heads. The flowers closed their petals.

"Stretch, yawn, goodnight," sang Mother Moon again.

Sunny sank lower toward the sea. Her light was now a warm glow. She felt drowsy and cozy. Being the sun was hard work. She had earned her rest.

"Stretch, yawn, goodnight," sang Mother Moon one last time.

Sunny slipped below the horizon. The stars came out to take her place. Mother Moon smiled and began her night watch. Sunny dreamed sunny dreams under a blanket of ocean.

Down in the world, a little child looked out the window. "Goodnight, Sunny," she whispered. "Sleep well."

Sleep now, bright sun. You shone beautifully today. Rest now, and tomorrow you can shine again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even the brightest and busiest need to rest so they can shine again.*
