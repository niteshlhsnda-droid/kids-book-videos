Tiddalik the Thirsty Frog

An Australian folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the warm land of Australia, there was a big billabong — a wide, still pool where all the animals came to drink.

Kangaroos drank there. Emus drank there. Wombats and koalas and platypuses all drank there. The water was cool and clear, and everyone shared it happily.

But one very hot morning, a great big frog named Tiddalik woke up feeling terribly thirsty.

"Oh, I am so thirsty!" croaked Tiddalik. "I need a drink. Just one little drink."

So Tiddalik hopped to the billabong and took a sip. Then another sip. Then a great big gulp. Then another, and another, and another!

Tiddalik drank and drank and drank — until his tummy swelled up big and round like a balloon, and the whole billabong was gone!

There was no water left at all. Just wet mud and very surprised animals.

"What shall we do?" cried Waru the wombat. "There is nothing to drink!"

"My throat is as dry as the desert," said Kara the koala, fanning herself with a leaf.

"We must make Tiddalik laugh," said Edna the emu wisely. "When Tiddalik laughs, his tummy will wobble, and the water will come splashing out!"

So all the animals tried their very best to make Tiddalik laugh.

First, Waru the wombat did a funny waddle, rolling this way and that. Tiddalik did not even smile.

Then Kara the koala climbed a tree and slid down again, upside down. Tiddalik did not even giggle.

Then Paddy the platypus did a silly splashy dance in the mud. Tiddalik did not even chuckle.

Then Edna the emu did her very best: she flapped her wings, bobbed her head, and did a long-legged jiggle-dance all around the billabong.

Tiddalik's mouth twitched. His tummy wobbled. But he did not laugh.

At last, little Bibi the kookaburra flew down and sat on a branch above Tiddalik.

"Kooka-kooka-kooka!" laughed Bibi. "Look at that frog! He is so full of water, he looks like a great green hill with eyes!"

And Bibi laughed and laughed, a happy rolling laugh that bubbled up from her tummy.

Tiddalik tried very hard to stay serious. But Bibi's laugh was so jolly that first Tiddalik smiled, then he giggled, then he laughed a great big belly laugh — "HA! HA! HA!"

And as he laughed, his tummy wobbled and shook — and SPLASH! Out poured the water, back into the billabong, cool and clear and sparkling!

All the animals cheered and drank their fill. Tiddalik drank just one sensible sip, and said, "Thank you, friends. From now on, I will only drink what I need — and leave plenty for everyone."

And they all played by the billabong until the sun went down.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Take only what you need, and leave plenty for everyone else.
