The Wise Stag

A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a deep green forest, lived a great herd of deer.

Their leader was a wise old stag named Banyan. His antlers were wide and silver, and his eyes were kind and calm.

"Remember, my deer," Banyan told the herd every morning, "a wise deer looks before it leaps. Stay together, and no harm will find us."

One day, a hunter came to the forest. He set many hidden snares along the deers' path — loops of rope tucked under the soft leaves.

The next morning, the deer were trotting to the meadow. Suddenly, the youngest fawn, little Pip, stopped.

"Grandfather Banyan!" squeaked Pip. "Look! The leaves on the path are moving in a strange way. And I smell something sharp and new."

Banyan lowered his great head and looked closely. Under the leaves, he saw the glint of hidden ropes.

"Snares!" said Banyan softly. "The hunter has been here. My herd, listen well. Do not step on the path today. We will go around, through the tall grass."

But the deer were hungry, and the meadow was just beyond the path.

"Can't we just jump over them?" asked a young deer named Fleet.

Banyan shook his silver antlers. "A wise deer does not jump over danger when it can walk around it. Follow me, single file, through the tall grass. Step where I step."

So the whole herd followed Banyan, stepping softly through the tall, whispering grass. Pip walked right behind Banyan, copying every careful step.

When they reached the meadow safely, all the deer cheered.

"Hurray for Banyan!" they cried. "The wisest stag in the forest!"

The hunter came back that evening. He checked his snares one by one.

"Empty! Every single one is empty!" he grumbled. "Those deer are too clever for me."

He packed up his ropes and walked home, and he never set snares in that forest again.

That night, under the round silver moon, the deer gathered around Banyan.

"How did you know the snares were there, Grandfather?" asked little Pip.

Banyan smiled. "I listened to you, little one. You noticed the moving leaves. Wisdom is not only in old antlers — it is in young eyes too. When we listen to each other, the whole herd stays safe."

Pip snuggled close to Banyan's warm side, and all the deer slept peacefully, safe and sound, until morning.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Wisdom grows when we listen to each other — and the whole herd stays safe.
