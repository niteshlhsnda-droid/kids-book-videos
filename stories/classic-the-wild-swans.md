# The Wild Swans

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, there lived a king with eleven brave sons and one little daughter named Elina.

Elina loved her eleven brothers more than anything in the world. They played together in the garden, they splashed together in the pond, and they read stories together under the big oak tree.

But one day, a cross and jealous queen came to the palace. She did not like the princes at all. So she waved her magic wand — swish! — and turned all eleven brothers into beautiful white swans!

"Fly away, swans!" she cried. "Fly far away, where no one will ever find you!"

The eleven swans flapped their great white wings and flew away over the forest, over the river, and far, far away.

Elina cried and cried. "My brothers! Where did my brothers go?"

She ran out of the palace and into the forest, calling, "Brothers! Brothers! Where are you?"

At last, she found them by a quiet lake. Eleven white swans, floating on the blue water.

"Elina!" called the eldest swan. "We can only be ourselves at night. Every day, we must be swans. Only a sister's love can set us free."

"Tell me how!" cried Elina. "I will do anything!"

"You must sew eleven shirts out of prickly nettles," said the swan, "and you must not speak a single word until all eleven shirts are finished. Not one word — for six whole years."

Elina nodded. She did not say a word. She went straight to the nettle patch and began to sew.

The nettles pricked her fingers. Ouch! But she did not cry out. She sewed and sewed, day after day, month after month, year after year.

The seasons came and went. Spring flowers bloomed. Summer suns shone. Autumn leaves fell. Winter snows came. And still Elina sewed, without saying a single word.

Her brothers flew to visit her every evening. They would land softly beside her, and she would smile and show them the shirts — one, two, three… ten… eleven.

At last, after six long years, the shirts were almost done. Elina had finished ten shirts, and the eleventh shirt needed only one more sleeve.

But just then, some unkind people from the village came and took Elina away. "She never speaks!" they said. "She must be up to no good!"

They led her away to a quiet tower. But Elina did not speak. She kept sewing, sewing, sewing. And high above, her eleven brothers were already flying to her — whoosh!

On the very last day, as the sun began to set, her eleven brothers came flying — whoosh! — over the tower. Elina threw the ten finished shirts over them, and the last shirt over her youngest brother, Veer.

Poof! Ten brothers stood before her, tall and smiling and human again!

And little Veer? His shirt was missing one sleeve — so he kept one soft, white swan wing. But he could still run, and laugh, and hug his sister tight.

"Elina!" cried the brothers. "You saved us! Your love and your patience set us free!"

Elina opened her mouth and spoke for the first time in six years. "My brothers!" she said, in a soft, happy voice. "You are home!"

And do you know what? The eleven brothers and their little sister lived happily ever after. And every evening, Veer would flap his one soft wing and say, "Good night, sister. Good night, stars. Good night, moon."

And Elina would smile and whisper, "Good night, brothers. Good night, moon."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Love and patience can set anyone free.*
