# Lulu the Ladybug and the Dewdrop Moon

In a quiet garden, behind a little stone wall, lived a ladybug named Lulu. Her wings were bright red, with seven round black spots, and every morning she polished each spot with a dewdrop and a leaf.

Lulu loved the evening best of all. When the sun went down and the garden grew soft and blue, the moon rose over the stone wall, round and gold. Lulu would climb to the top of the tallest clover leaf to say hello.

One evening, the moon was so bright that its light fell like silver rain. And on a clover leaf below, a single dewdrop caught the light. Inside the drop there shone a tiny moon, no bigger than a berry.

Lulu gasped. She fluttered down and landed softly beside it. The little moon glowed warm and gold, and it made her whole leaf shine. "My moon," she whispered. "All mine."

Just then, Annie the ant came hurrying along the leaf, carrying a crumb twice her size. "Oh!" said Annie, stopping still. "What is that beautiful light?" Lulu hesitated. Then she moved aside, just a little. Annie peered into the drop, and her eyes went wide and bright. "It's the prettiest thing I've ever seen."

But as the night breeze stirred the clover, the leaf trembled, and the dewdrop wobbled. Lulu's heart thumped. What if it rolled away? What if it faded? She cupped her wings around it to keep it safe, and in the shadow of her wings, the little moon grew dim.

"Why is it going dark?" asked a small voice. It was Benny the beetle, who had crawled up to see. Lulu looked down. Her wings were blocking the moonlight. Slowly, she lifted them, and the little moon shone again, brighter than before.

"Come and see," said Lulu, and she stepped back so Annie and Benny could sit beside the drop. The three friends watched together, and as they watched, the light seemed to grow. It spilled across the leaf, over the clover, and all the way to the stone wall, where a sleepy moth and a tiny spider had come to look too.

Soon the whole garden corner glowed soft and gold. The little moon was still just one dewdrop, but shared by many eyes, it lit the whole night. Lulu smiled. Her moon was not smaller for sharing. It was bigger, because everyone could see it.

When the real moon began to sink, the dewdrop moon faded gently, like a candle going out. Annie yawned. Benny curled under a leaf. Lulu tucked herself beside the clover, her wings folded, her seven spots safe. "Goodnight, little moon," she whispered. "See you tomorrow."

Sleep now, little Lulu.

The end. 🌙
