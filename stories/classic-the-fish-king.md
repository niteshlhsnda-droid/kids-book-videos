# The Fish King

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a wide, blue lake lived many, many fish — big ones, little ones, stripy ones and spotty ones.

Their king was a wise old fish named Raja, with shining silver scales and a calm, kind face.

Every morning, Raja swam around the lake to say hello to everyone.

"Good morning, little Minnow!" he would call. "Good morning, Grandma Catfish!"

And all the fish would wiggle their fins happily.

But one summer, the sun shone hotter and hotter, day after day.

The lake began to shrink. The water grew warm and shallow. The reeds at the edge drooped sadly.

"Oh no," whispered the little fish. "What shall we do?"

King Raja thought and thought. Then he remembered something his own grandfather had told him long ago.

"Beyond the reeds," he said, "there is a secret stream that runs all the way to the Great River. The Great River is deep and cool, even in summer. Follow me!"

So Raja led the way. First he swam to the edge of the lake, where the tall reeds swayed.

"Keep together, little ones," he called. "Swim in a line, like beads on a string!"

One by one, the fish slipped through a narrow, shady channel between the reeds.

The water was cool and clear. It bubbled and giggled over smooth pebbles.

"Hurry, hurry!" cried the little Minnow, splashing along. "I can hear the river!"

At last, the channel opened wide — and there it was: the Great River, deep and blue and cool, stretching as far as any fish could see.

"Hooray!" shouted all the fish together. "Hooray for King Raja!"

They splashed and swirled and danced in the cool water.

That evening, as the sun set in pink and gold, Raja swam slowly around his happy family.

"A good king," he said with a smile, "does not just wear a crown. He looks after everyone — big and small."

And from that day on, whenever the lake grew low, the fish knew just what to do: follow King Raja, in a line, like beads on a string, all the way to the Great River.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A true leader takes care of everyone, big and small, and leads the way to safety.*
