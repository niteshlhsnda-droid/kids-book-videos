# The Jackal and the Elephant

*A gentle Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green forest full of tall trees and singing birds, there lived a boastful jackal named Joko.

Joko had a loud voice and a very big opinion of himself. He loved to tell everyone how clever he was.

One sunny morning, a gentle elephant named Nandini came walking through the forest. She was big and kind, with soft grey skin and long, floppy ears.

"I am going to the big pond," said Nandini, in her soft, deep voice. "I want to drink the cool water and splash my feet."

"Oh, the big pond is very far away," said Joko, waving a paw. "But I know a short cut! Follow me, and we will be there in no time at all!"

Nandini smiled. She liked Joko, and she believed him. "Thank you, Joko," she said. "Please lead the way."

Soon the path grew narrow, and the ground under Nandini's feet felt soft and squelchy.

"Are you sure this is the short cut?" asked Nandini, looking around. "This path does not look like the way to the pond."

"Of course I am sure!" said Joko, without even looking back. "Trust me! I am the cleverest animal in the forest!"

But Joko was not looking where he was going. He led Nandini straight to a muddy pond, hidden behind the tall reeds. The mud was dark and deep and very, very sticky.

"Squelch!" went Nandini's front foot. "Squish!" went her back foot. Before she knew it, she had sunk down into the mud, right up to her knees!

"Oh dear, oh dear," said Joko. "I am so sorry, Nandini. I did not know this pond was here. I was boasting, and I was not looking where I was going."

So Joko ran as fast as his legs could carry him, all the way back to the big mango tree where the forest friends were playing.

Bina the bear put down her honey pot. Titu the monkey jumped down from his branch. They all ran to help their friend.

Titu found some long, strong vines. He tied them together, one by one, until they made a very long rope.

They pulled and pulled. The mud went squelch and squish. Slowly, slowly, Nandini's feet came free, one at a time, with a big POP!

At last Nandini stood on the dry grass again. She shook the mud from her feet and gave a happy trumpet. "Thank you, my friends!" she said. "You saved me!"

Then Joko stepped forward. His head was low, and his voice was quiet.

"Nandini, I am very sorry," he said. "I boasted about knowing a short cut, but I did not know the way at all. Boasting is not being clever."

Nandini smiled and touched his shoulder gently with her trunk. "Thank you for saying sorry, Joko," she said. "That is a very clever thing to do."

From that day on, Joko never boasted again. When he did not know the way, he said so. And when his friends needed help, he was the first to run and fetch them.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Boasting is not being clever — looking carefully and telling the truth is.*
