The Merchant and the Iron Scales

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a small town in India lived two shopkeeper friends, kind Ravi and clever Dev.

Ravi sold sweet spices — cinnamon, cardamom, and cloves that smelled like a warm kitchen. Dev sold shining pots and pans.

One morning, Ravi had to travel far away to sell his spices at a big market. "Dev, my friend," he said, "please keep my big iron scales safe while I am gone."

The scales were heavy and strong, with two bright pans that hung from a bar. Dev smiled. "Of course! They'll be safe in my storeroom."

Weeks passed, and Ravi came back home, tired and happy from his long journey. He went to Dev's shop. "Thank you, my friend! May I have my iron scales back?"

Dev looked down at his feet. "Oh no! The rats ate your iron scales!"

Ravi blinked. Rats? Eating... iron? Have YOU ever seen a rat crunch on iron?

But Ravi's eyes twinkled. He smiled a slow, secret smile, and said nothing at all.

The next day, Ravi invited Dev's little son Aarav to play in his garden all afternoon. Aarav swung high on the swing, ate sweet ripe mangoes under the mango tree, and played the best game of hide-and-seek ever.

When the sun started to go down, Dev came to Ravi's house, looking for his little boy. "Where is Aarav?" he asked.

Ravi smiled cheerfully — and just then, Aarav's giggle floated out from behind the mango tree. "A hawk must have carried him away — just like the rats ate my iron scales!"

Dev stood very still. Then he laughed and laughed. "Oh, Ravi! You're too clever. Wait here!"

Dev ran home and opened his storeroom. There were the iron scales, safe and shiny, exactly where he had kept them. He carried them back and handed them to Ravi, bending low in a big thank-you bow.

Just then, Aarav came running out of the garden, giggling, with mango juice all over his cheeks. "Papa! It was the best day ever!"

The two friends laughed together and hugged. Dev shared sweet ladoos with everyone, and they sat under the mango tree till the sky turned pink.

"I promise," said Dev, "from today, I'll always be honest with you." Ravi nodded. "Honesty is the best treasure between friends."

Then everyone walked home slowly as the first stars came out. The swings swayed softly in the evening breeze. The whole town curled up, and slept safe and sound.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Honesty is the best treasure between friends.
