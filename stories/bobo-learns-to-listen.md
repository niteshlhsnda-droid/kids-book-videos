# Bobo Learns to Listen

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Bobo was a baby bat.

He had very big ears.

But he did not use them well.

He talked and talked.

He hardly ever listened.

One evening, Mama said, "Listen, Bobo."

She told him about the night sounds.

But Bobo was too busy chattering.

He missed the owl's soft hoot.

He missed the stream's gentle song.

Mama said, "I see you love to talk."

Bobo nodded proudly.

"But listening is a superpower too," Mama said.

She taught him the listening game.

"Quiet ears hear the world sing."

"Quiet ears hear the world sing," she said again.

"Quiet ears hear the world sing," she said one more time.

Bobo wanted to try.

He closed his mouth.

He opened his big ears wide.

At first, he heard nothing.

Then, very softly, he heard it.

Hoot, hoot.

"The owl!" he whispered.

Then he heard more.

Drip, drop, went the stream.

Rustle, rustle, went the leaves.

Buzz, buzz, went a sleepy bee.

Bobo felt amazed.

His ears felt tingly.

His heart felt happy.

"I can hear everything!" he said.

Mama smiled.

"You listened carefully," she said.

"Listening shows we care."

That night, they flew together.

Bobo listened to the wind.

Whoosh, whoosh.

He listened to the crickets.

Chirp, chirp.

He listened to Mama's wings.

Flap, flap, beside him.

He felt close to everything.

Listening made the night bigger.

And more beautiful.

Back in the cave, Bobo snuggled upside down.

He listened to Mama's lullaby.

Soft and low.

He felt happy.

He felt calm.

The cave was quiet.

Bobo closed his eyes.

He was a good listener.

He was loved.

Goodnight, Bobo.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Listening carefully helps us hear the world — and shows we care.*
