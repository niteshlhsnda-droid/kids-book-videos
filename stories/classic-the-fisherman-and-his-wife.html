The Fisherman and His Wife

A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, beside the wide blue sea, there lived a fisherman named Tomas and his wife Marta.

They lived in a tiny cottage with a thatched roof, and every morning Tomas went down to the sea with his fishing rod and caught fish for their supper.

One morning, Tomas felt a strong tug on his line. He pulled and pulled, and up came the most beautiful fish he had ever seen — a golden fish, shining like the sun!

"Please, kind fisherman, let me go," said the golden fish, in a small, polite voice. "I am not an ordinary fish. I am an enchanted prince! If you let me go, I will grant you a wish."

Tomas was so surprised that he nearly dropped his rod. "Well," he said, "I do not need much. But my wife Marta would love a cosy little house with a garden. Our cottage is very small."

"Go home," said the golden fish. "Your wish is granted."

Tomas let the fish slip back into the waves and hurried home. And there, instead of the tiny cottage, stood a pretty little house with a garden full of flowers!

Marta clapped her hands. "How wonderful!" she said. "But you know, dear, a house with a garden is lovely — and a house with a garden and a little shop would be lovelier still. Could you ask the fish for a shop?"

So Tomas went back to the sea and called, "Golden fish, golden fish, please come!"

The fish swam up. "What does Marta wish for now?" he asked.

"A little shop, please," said Tomas shyly.

"Go home," said the fish. "Your wish is granted."

And now their house had a little shop beside it, where Marta sold cakes and jam. The villagers came to buy, and Marta was very happy.

But after a while, Marta said, "Tomas, a shop is lovely — but a big house with many rooms would be lovelier still!"

So Tomas went back to the sea and asked again. The fish granted it, and now they had a big house with many rooms.

Then Marta wished for a castle. Then a castle with gardens and fountains. Each time, Tomas went to the sea, and each time the kind golden fish granted the wish.

At last, they lived in a grand castle, and Marta had everything she could think of. But she sat on her throne and felt — strangely — not happy at all.

"Tomas," she said quietly, "I have wished and wished, and each wish made me want one more. But I am not happier than I was in our little cottage."

Tomas sat beside her and held her hand. "I think I know why," he said gently. "Happiness is not about having more and more. It is about enjoying what we already have — and the people we share it with."

Marta nodded slowly. "You are right. Let us go back to the sea and thank the fish. I would like our cosy little house with the garden back, please."

So Tomas went down to the sea one last time and called the golden fish.

"Thank you for all your kindness," said Tomas. "But Marta and I have learned something. We would like our little house with the garden back — that was enough, and we were happy there."

The golden fish smiled a fishy smile. "That," he said, "is the wisest wish of all. Go home."

And when Tomas got home, there stood their cosy little house with the garden full of flowers. Marta was waiting at the door with two cups of tea.

"This is perfect," said Marta, breathing in the smell of the flowers. "Not too big, not too small — just right, with you beside me."

And from that day on, Tomas and Marta never wished for more than they needed. They had their little house, their garden, their friends — and hearts full of contentment.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Enough is a feast — a contented heart is richer than a castle.
