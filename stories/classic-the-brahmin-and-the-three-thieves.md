# The Brahmin and the Three Thieves

*A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a small village lived a kind brahmin named Dev, who loved his little white goat, Moti, more than anything in the world.

One day, Dev was walking home from the market with Moti trotting happily beside him, when three sneaky thieves spotted them from behind a banyan tree.

"Look at that fine goat!" whispered the first thief. "Let us trick him out of it."

So the first thief stepped onto the path ahead of Dev and said, very politely, "Good morning, sir! Why are you carrying a dog on your shoulder?"

Dev laughed. "A dog? This is my goat, Moti! You must need new spectacles!"

A little further along, the second thief appeared. "Good morning, sir! What a strange dog you have there!"

Dev frowned. "It is not a dog. It is my goat. I know my own goat!"

Then the third thief came along, shaking his head in wonder. "Good morning, sir! Everyone knows a brahmin should not carry a dog!"

Now Dev felt quite muddled. "Three people cannot all be wrong," he thought. "Perhaps my eyes are playing tricks on me!" So, feeling very foolish, he set Moti down, said, "Forgive me, little one," and hurried home without her.

The thieves clapped their hands with glee — but their glee did not last long. For Moti was a very clever goat. She looked at the three thieves, gave a loud "Baa-a-a!" and trotted straight back home after Dev, all by herself!

Meanwhile, Dev sat at home, feeling sadder and sadder. "Moti was my goat," he said to himself. "I know her soft white coat. I know her little bell. Why did I believe strangers over my own eyes?"

Just then — ding-a-ling! — there was Moti at his door, wagging her tail and ringing her bell!

Dev hugged her tight. "My clever girl! You came home to me!"

The next morning, the three thieves came to the village, hoping to sell a goat they did not have. Instead, they found Dev and Moti sitting together in the sunshine, and the whole village laughing at the story of the trick that had failed.

The thieves hung their heads. "We are sorry," they said. "Tricking people is a poor way to live."

Dev smiled kindly. "Then come and share my lunch," he said. "And if you want work, the farmer next door needs three strong helpers."

And so the three thieves became three honest helpers — and Dev never again believed strangers over his own eyes.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Trust your own eyes, and do not let other people's words change what you know is true.*
