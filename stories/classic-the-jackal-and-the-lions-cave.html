The Jackal and the Lion's Cave

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One golden morning, Jimi the jackal trotted through the forest to visit his friends. On his way, he passed a dark cave at the foot of a hill. Many footprints led up to the mouth of the cave — deer prints, rabbit prints, and little goat prints — and then every single one turned straight back again, as if their owners had changed their minds.

Jimi stopped and looked closely. "How strange," he whispered. "So many friends walk up to this cave, and every one of them turns back again. Nobody ever goes inside."

Just then, a deep, sleepy voice rumbled from inside. "Who is out there? Come in, little friend, and keep me company."

Jimi's ears wiggled. He did not go in. Instead, he called out politely, "Good morning! Who lives in this cave?"

"I am Sheru the lion," said the voice. "I am old now, and my legs are stiff. I cannot run and hunt the way I used to. I am lonely in here all by myself."

Jimi felt sorry for the old lion. "Dear Sheru," he said, "your cave is dark, and my friends are afraid to come near it, because nobody has ever seen a visitor come happily back out again. That is why they turn away."

There was a long silence. Then Sheru said quietly, "You are right, little jackal. I called out to visitors because I was lonely, but my deep voice only frightened them more. I do not want to be a scary neighbour."

"Then let us change that," said Jimi kindly. "Come out into the sunshine, and I will bring my friends to meet you. We will have a picnic instead of a fright."

Sheru padded slowly out of the cave and blinked in the bright light. He was big, but his eyes were gentle, and his mane was soft and grey at the edges.

Jimi fetched ripe mangoes, sweet bananas, and a basket of berries. Soon the deer, the rabbits, and the little goats came hopping and trotting over. At first they peeped from behind the bushes, shy as could be.

"Do not be afraid," called Jimi. "Sheru only wants friends, not a fright. Look — he is sharing the sunny meadow with us."

Sheru smiled a great lion smile. "Thank you for coming," he rumbled softly. "I have been lonely for a very long time. Will you share my picnic?"

The animals sat in a happy circle on the grass. The deer nibbled mangoes. The rabbits munched berries. The little goats crunched sweet leaves. Sheru told them stories of the old forest, and everybody laughed at his funny lion jokes.

From that day on, Sheru never called animals into his dark cave again. He sat in the sunny meadow instead, and whenever friends passed by, he waved a big paw and called, "Come and sit with me!"

And the footprints in front of the cave changed, too. Now they went right up to the cave door and back again, happily, side by side with big lion paw prints — because the cave was no longer a scary place. It was the home of a friend.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A kind word and a sunny invitation can turn a lonely neighbour into a friend.
