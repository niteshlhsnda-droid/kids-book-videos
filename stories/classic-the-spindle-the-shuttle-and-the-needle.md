# The Spindle, the Shuttle, and the Needle

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little cottage at the edge of a green forest, there lived a kind young girl.

The girl was poor, but she was happy. She had a little garden, a little kettle, and a little cat who purred by the fire.

And she had three special gifts from her godmother: a spindle, a shuttle, and a needle.

"Keep these safe," her godmother had said. "They are small, but they hold a little magic."

One morning, as the girl sat down to spin, the spindle slipped from her fingers! It rolled across the floor, out of the door, and down the garden path, spinning a shining golden thread behind it.

"Come back, little spindle!" cried the girl, running after it.

It rolled through the meadow, where the daisies nodded, and over the hill, where the buttercups danced, the golden thread shining in the sun.

The girl ran and ran, following the golden thread. At last, the spindle stopped at the door of a little house.

"What a beautiful thread!" said a voice. It was a young prince on a white horse — he had followed the golden thread all the way from his palace!

"Did you spin this thread?" he asked.

"Yes," said the girl, a little shy. "My spindle ran away, and I followed it here."

Just then, the shuttle began to hum. "Hum, hum, hum!" it sang, and it wove a beautiful carpet along the golden thread — a carpet of red and blue and gold, soft as a cloud.

And the needle began to dance. "One stitch, two stitches, three!" it sang, sewing the little house's torn roof, stitching the curtains, mending the cushions — until the whole little house was cosy and bright.

The prince looked at the golden thread, the beautiful carpet, and the cosy little house.

"What clever, magical friends you have!" he said. "Will you show me your cottage?"

"Of course," smiled the girl. "And you must stay for supper. I have fresh bread, and my cat loves visitors!"

The prince stayed for supper, and to hear the girl's stories. And he came back the next day, and the next, and the next.

The spindle spun golden thread. The shuttle wove beautiful carpets. The needle sewed the softest cushions. And the girl and the prince became the very best of friends.

When they grew up, they married, and they lived in the little cottage together. The prince brought roses for the garden. The girl baked bread in the little oven. The cat purred by the fire. And the spindle, the shuttle, and the needle kept working their quiet magic, day after day.

That night, the girl sat by the window and counted the golden threads in the moonlight — one, two, three, four, five — until her eyes grew heavy.

And under the twinkling stars, she fell fast asleep, happy and safe at home.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart, a steady hand, and a little magic can turn a small cottage into a happy home.*
