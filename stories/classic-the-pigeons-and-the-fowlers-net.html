The Pigeons and the Fowler's Net

A wise old Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

High above a green field, in a tall old tree, lived a flock of pigeons.

Their leader was Chitra, a wise old pigeon with speckled wings and kind round eyes. The young pigeons loved her, because she always knew what to do.

One sunny morning, the pigeons flew out in a fluttering cloud. *Flap-flap-flap!* They swooped and swirled over the golden field.

And there, scattered on the ground, was grain — plump, golden grain, more than they had ever seen!

"Grain! Grain!" cooed the young pigeons. "Let us dive down and feast!"

"Wait," said Chitra. "Look carefully. Does something feel... not quite right?"

But the young pigeons were already diving. *Whoosh!* Down they went, pecking happily. *Peck, peck, peck.*

Then — *SWISH!* — a great net fell over them all!

It was the fowler's net. A fowler is a man who catches birds, and he had hidden behind a bush, waiting.

"Oh no!" cried the pigeons. "We are trapped! We are trapped!"

They fluttered and flapped, this way and that, but the net held them tight.

Then Chitra's calm voice rose above the fluttering.

"Stop, my dears," she said. "Fluttering alone will not save us. But listen — I have a plan."

The pigeons went quiet. *Hush.*

"On the count of three," said Chitra, "we will ALL flap our wings together — as ONE. We will lift the whole net and fly away with it."

"Together?" whispered a tiny pigeon.

"Together," said Chitra, smiling. "One... two... THREE!"

*FLAP-FLAP-FLAP!* Hundreds of wings beat as one. The net trembled. The net wobbled. The net LIFTED!

Up they rose, higher and higher, carrying the great net with them, like a big gray cloud. *Flap, flap, flap!*

The fowler came running out from behind his bush.

"My net!" he cried. "My net is flying away!" He ran after them, *thump-thump-thump*, but pigeons are faster than running men.

Chitra led the flock to a friendly tree far away, where their tiniest friend lived.

It was Kumud, a small brown mouse with bright eyes and very sharp teeth. *Nibble, nibble* went his teeth on everything.

"Kumud!" called Chitra. "Please help us!"

Kumud looked up and smiled. "Of course!" he squeaked.

He climbed up the ropes of the net and gnawed and gnawed. *Nibble-nibble-nibble!* One rope... two ropes... *snap!*

Free! The pigeons tumbled out of the net, laughing and cooing. "Hurray! Hurray!"

Kumud bowed, and the pigeons showered him with grain. "Our hero!" they cooed.

"And from today," said Chitra, "Kumud is one of us. There will always be grain for you, little friend."

Kumud's whiskers wiggled with joy. *Squeak!* He had never felt so proud.

Now, the fowler had followed all the way. He stood under the tree, out of breath, staring up.

He had never seen anything like it. Pigeons flying together. A mouse freeing birds. Friends helping friends.

His heart felt soft and warm, like melted butter.

"From this day on," he said quietly, "no more nets. I will scatter grain for the birds — as a friend, not a catcher."

And he did. Every morning, the fowler scattered golden grain in the field. *Sprinkle, sprinkle.*

The pigeons would land and feast, and Chitra would coo, "Thank you, friend!"

That night, the moon rose round and silver. The pigeons tucked their heads under their wings. Kumud curled up in his cozy hole. And the fowler slept with a happy heart.

For the forest had learned the loveliest lesson of all.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Together we are strong — friendship can lift any net.
