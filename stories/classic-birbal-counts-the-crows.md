# Birbal Counts the Crows

*A witty Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago in India, there lived a great king named Akbar, who loved to ask tricky questions.

And in his court lived Birbal, the cleverest minister of all, who always had a twinkling answer.

One sunny morning, King Akbar was watching black crows hop about the palace gardens — caw, caw! — when a mischievous idea popped into his head.

"Birbal!" he called. "Tell me — how many crows are there in my whole kingdom?"

The courtiers gasped. "What a question! No one could ever count all the crows!"

The king stroked his beard and waited, for he loved a clever answer.

But Birbal just smiled his calm smile and folded his hands.

"Your Majesty," he said, "there are exactly nine thousand, four hundred and twelve crows in your kingdom."

"Nine thousand, four hundred and twelve?" laughed the king. "And how can you be so sure, Birbal?"

"Because I have counted them all, Your Majesty," said Birbal. "But if you count them yourself and find fewer, do not worry — it only means some of our crows have gone to visit their cousins in the next kingdom."

"And if I find MORE than your number?" asked the king, his eyes twinkling.

"Then, Your Majesty," said Birbal with a deep bow, "it means some cousin-crows have come to visit US. Our kingdom is so wonderful that even the crows bring their relatives!"

The whole court burst out laughing — ha, ha, ha! Even the king laughed until tears rolled down his cheeks.

"Well answered, Birbal!" cried King Akbar. "You have turned a trick question into a compliment for my kingdom!"

That evening, the king gave Birbal a bag of sweet jaggery as a reward, and Birbal shared every piece with the court — and saved one sweet piece for the crows in the garden.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever answer can turn a tricky question into a happy moment — wit is a gift to share.*
