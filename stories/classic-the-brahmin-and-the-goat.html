The Brahmin and the Goat

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a small village, beside a winding river, there lived a kind Brahmin named Bholu. He had a gentle smile, a soft voice, and a heart full of kindness.

One morning, a farmer gave Bholu a gift — a gentle white goat, with soft fur and bright, kind eyes. The goat's name was Moti, and she bleated softly, "Maa, maa," as if to say hello.

"Thank you," said Bholu, patting Moti's head. "I will take good care of you."

Bholu lifted Moti onto his shoulders, and together they set off for home. Moti was happy, her little legs dangling, her ears flopping in the breeze. Bholu sang a little song as he walked.

But on the road, three sly rogues were hiding behind a tree. They had seen the goat, and they wanted it for themselves.

"I have a plan," whispered the first rogue. "I will trick him."

The first rogue stepped onto the road and called out, "Good morning, sir! Why are you carrying a dog on your shoulders?"

Bholu stopped and frowned. "A dog?" he said. "This is not a dog. This is my goat, Moti."

"Oh, of course," said the rogue, smiling a sly smile. "If you say so." And he walked away.

Bholu walked on, but a tiny seed of doubt had planted itself in his mind. Why had the man called her a dog?

A little further along the road, the second rogue jumped out. "Sir! Sir!" he cried. "Why are you carrying a dog? Dogs are not for carrying!"

Bholu's heart sank. "Two people cannot both be wrong," he thought. "Can they?" He looked up at Moti again. She looked like a goat, but now Bholu was not so sure.

Then, near the village gate, the third rogue appeared. "Oh dear!" he laughed. "Look at the man carrying a dog on his shoulders! What a silly sight!"

That was enough for Bholu. His hands trembled. His smile faded. He gently set Moti down on the ground.

"Perhaps I am wrong," he said sadly. "Perhaps my eyes are playing tricks on me."

But then Bholu did something very wise. He stopped. He took a deep breath. And he looked at Moti — really looked at her.

He saw her soft white fur. He saw her small curved horns. He saw her gentle goat eyes. He heard her soft bleat: "Maa, maa."

"This is my goat," said Bholu, and his voice grew strong. "I can see her with my own eyes. I can hear her with my own ears. No one else can tell me what I see."

So Bholu picked Moti up again, settled her on his shoulders, and walked on, smiling. The three rogues called after him, but Bholu did not listen. He just sang his little song, and Moti bleated along.

When he reached home, Bholu gave Moti fresh green grass and cool water. Moti munched happily, and Bholu sat beside her, watching the evening stars come out, one by one.

"I am glad I trusted my own eyes," he whispered, and that night he slept peacefully.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Trust your own eyes.
