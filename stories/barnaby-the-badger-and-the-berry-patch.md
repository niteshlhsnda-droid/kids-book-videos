# Barnaby the Badger and the Berry Patch

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green wood, where the ferns grew tall and the stream sang softly, there lived a busy badger named Barnaby.

Barnaby had a stripy face, strong paws, and a very empty basket — because today was berry-picking day!

Deep in the wood, behind the old oak tree, grew the best berry patch in the whole forest. Its bushes were full of fat, purple-black blackberries, shining in the morning sun.

"Oh, what a berry day!" cried Barnaby, and he began to pick — pluck, pluck, pluck! One berry for the basket, one berry for his tummy. Pluck, pluck, pluck!

But Barnaby was in a hurry. He rushed from bush to bush, and he only picked the berries he could see right away.

Just then, he heard a small, soft voice. "Hello, Barnaby."

It was Molly the mole, peeping out of the soft earth. "May I pick berries with you?" she asked. "I can smell them, but I cannot see where the best ones hide."

"Of course!" said Barnaby. "But hurry — I must fill my basket before lunch!"

Molly smiled. "Barnaby," she said, "the sweetest berries are never in a hurry. Look slowly. Look under the leaves."

Barnaby stopped rushing. He looked slowly… and oh! Under the big green leaves, there were the biggest, juiciest berries of all — dark and sweet as jam!

"Wow!" said Barnaby. "You were right, Molly! I almost missed them!"

Together, the two friends picked slowly and happily. Barnaby reached the high berries for Molly, and Molly showed him the hidden berries under the leaves. Pluck, pluck, pluck!

Soon, Barnaby's basket was full to the brim. But instead of hurrying home, Barnaby had a new idea.

"Molly," he said, "let us share these berries with everyone. Let us have a berry picnic!"

So they spread a little blanket under the old oak tree, and they shared the berries with everyone. The rabbits sat in a row and nibbled politely. The birds sang a sweet thank-you song. And even a sleepy hedgehog, who had just woken up, came waddling over for a taste.

Everyone laughed. Everyone tasted the sweet, juicy berries. And everyone agreed: they were the best berries in the whole forest.

That evening, Barnaby and Molly walked home together under the round, golden moon, carrying the empty basket between them. The fireflies blinked goodnight, and the stream sang its soft, sleepy song.

"Thank you, Molly," said Barnaby. "You taught me to look slowly — and to share what I find."

And Molly smiled. "That," she said, "is the sweetest berry of all."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Berries taste sweetest when you pick them slowly — and share them with a friend.*
