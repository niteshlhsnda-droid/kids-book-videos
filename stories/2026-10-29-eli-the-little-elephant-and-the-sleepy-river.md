# Eli the Little Elephant and the Sleepy River

In a green valley, where a slow river curled like a silver ribbon, lived a little elephant named Eli. He had soft grey skin, big floppy ears, and a small trunk that wiggled whenever he was happy.

Eli loved the river, but he worried about being big. When he splashed, the water splashed back in great waves. When he walked, the ground went thump, thump, thump. "I am too big and too clumsy," Eli sighed. "One day I will wake everybody up."

One evening, as the sky turned peach and gold, Eli's friends gathered for a moonlight picnic. Mina the mouse brought berries, Bree the bluebird brought seeds, and old Tomo the tortoise brought soft, round buns. But the picnic blanket was on the far bank, and the stepping stones across the river were wet and slippery.

"I can carry the baskets," said Eli, "but what if I splash? What if I slip? What if I drop them all in the river?"

"Try one small step at a time," said Tomo, smiling. "Slow feet make a steady elephant."

So Eli took one small step onto the first stone. He placed his big feet down softly, one by one, and his trunk held Mina's berry basket high and steady.

Halfway across, a stone wobbled under Eli's foot. Plop! A berry bounced out of the basket and rolled toward the water. Eli's trunk curled down, quick and gentle, and caught it just in time. "Well caught!" cheered Bree, fluttering above him.

Then the wind blew, and river water washed over the stones. Eli's feet slid, and the seed basket tipped. Seeds scattered like tiny stars. Eli's ears drooped. "See?" he whispered. "Too big. Too clumsy."

Mina patted his trunk. "You did not drop the berries," she said. "And you are still standing. Let us pick the seeds up together."

So together they gathered every seed, one by one, while Bree sang a soft, slow song. Eli breathed in, breathed out, and took the next step even more slowly than before.

At last, Eli reached the far bank. He set the baskets down gently, without a splash. His friends spread the blanket, shared the berries, seeds and buns, and watched the moon rise over the sleepy river.

"You carried it all so carefully," said Tomo. "Your big feet walked softly. Your big trunk held on gently. Being big is not the trouble, Eli. Hurrying is."

Eli smiled, and his trunk wiggled happily, as the moonlight shimmered on the water and the crickets sang their sleepy song.

Back home, Eli curled up in the soft grass beside the river and listened to the water whispering past.

Sleep now, little Eli.
