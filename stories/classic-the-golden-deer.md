# The Golden Deer

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Deep in a green bamboo forest, beside a pond that shone like a mirror, lived a deer unlike any other. What do you think Suraj saw looking back at him from that shiny pond?

His coat was pure gold. When he walked through the morning mist, the whole forest glowed.

His name was Suraj, and he was the kindest deer in all the forest. He shared his grass with the rabbits, and helped old turtles cross the stream — and oh, those turtles were SO slow that Suraj had to take the teeniest, tiniest steps.

One rainy day, Suraj heard a cry from the river.

"Help! Help! I cannot swim!"

A woodcutter named Mohan had slipped on a wet rock and fallen into the rushing water.

At once, Suraj jumped into the river. Splash! He swam to Mohan and let the man hold on to his golden horns.

Kick, kick, kick — Suraj swam and swam, until they reached the sandy bank.

"Thank you, thank you, golden deer!" Mohan gasped. "How can I ever thank you?"

"Just one thing," said Suraj softly. "Please do not tell anyone where I live. My golden coat would make some people greedy."

Mohan put his hand on his heart. "I promise," he said.

But Mohan was poor. He had heard the king was offering gold coins to anyone who found the golden deer.

One morning, Mohan wanted the gold coins more than he wanted to keep his promise. Mohan walked through the palace's tall golden doors and told the king where Suraj lived. Can you picture those tall, shiny doors?

The king and his men rode into the forest on their horses — thump, thump, thump went the hooves on the soft ground. When Suraj saw them coming, he did not run. He stepped out from the bamboo — calm and still, glowing in the sunlight.

"Why have you come for me?" he asked the king.

"Your coat is worth a great many gold coins," said the king.

Then Suraj looked at Mohan, and Mohan's face turned red as a sunset.

"I saved this man's life in the river," said Suraj gently. "I asked only that he keep my home a secret. And he traded my kindness for gold coins."

The king was quiet for a long time. Then he said, "Deer, you are kinder than any man I know."

He turned to Mohan. "Gold coins can be spent and lost. But a friend who saves your life — that is the real treasure."

Mohan felt so ashamed that tears rolled down his cheeks. "Forgive me, Suraj," he whispered. "I will never be greedy again."

Suraj stepped forward and rubbed his soft nose against Mohan's hand. "I forgive you," he said.

From that day on, the king said the bamboo forest would be a safe, quiet home for every animal, forever. And Mohan became the keeper of the forest, planting soft new bamboo shoots in the quiet mornings.

And every evening, the golden deer glowed in the sunset — the kindest heart in the whole green forest.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart shines brighter than gold.*
