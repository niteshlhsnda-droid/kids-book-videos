# The Fir Tree

*A gentle Hans Christian Andersen tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a quiet green forest, there grew a little fir tree.

He was small and soft, with feathery green branches and a tiny point at the top.

Every morning, the sun tickled his needles. Every evening, the moon sang him a soft, silver song.

But the little fir tree was not happy.

"Oh, I wish I were tall!" he sighed to the morning breeze. "I wish I could grow up high and see the wide, wide world."

The tall trees around him smiled kindly. "Little one," said the old oak, in a deep, soft voice, "enjoy being small. The sun is warm, and the birds sing sweet songs just for you."

But the little fir tree shook his branches. "I want to be big and grand," he said. "I want to see faraway places and meet new friends."

So he stretched and stretched, reaching up toward the bright blue sky.

Summer came, and the little fir tree grew a little taller. His branches grew thick and green, and his needles smelled fresh like rain.

Autumn came, and golden leaves danced past him in the wind.

Then winter came, soft and white, with snowflakes falling like tiny stars.

One snowy morning, a kind family came walking through the forest, holding hands and singing a happy song.

"Look!" said the little girl, pointing with joy. "There is the perfect tree! He is just the right size, with lovely green branches!"

"Yes!" said her father, with a warm smile. "This is the tree for our home."

The little fir tree felt happy all over. "Me? They chose me?" he whispered, in wonder.

Carefully, the family carried him home, wrapped snugly in a soft blanket.

Inside their cozy house, there was a warm fire glowing in the corner, and the smell of sweet cookies floated through the air.

"Now," said the mother, with a smile, "let us dress our tree."

The children hung shiny red balls on his branches. They wound golden ribbons round and round, soft as silk.

They hung little candy canes that smelled of sweet peppermint, and tiny bells that tinkled a happy tune.

Then the father lifted the little girl high, and she placed a bright star on the very top.

"Twinkle, twinkle!" she laughed. "Now you shine, dear tree!"

The little fir tree glowed with joy. He had never felt so loved, so bright, so happy.

That evening, the children gathered around him and sang soft songs. They told him stories, and they laughed, and the golden star twinkled above them all.

"Oh," whispered the little fir tree, happily. "This is even better than being tall. I am loved, and I am shining, and I am home."

The mother tucked a soft blanket around the little girl, and the father turned the lights low.

The tree stood in the corner, glowing gently, with his star shining bright, like a friend watching over the sleeping house.

And the little fir tree knew, deep in his green heart, that he was the happiest tree in all the world.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The happiest place is not far away — it is right where you are loved.*
