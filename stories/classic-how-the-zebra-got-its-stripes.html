How the Zebra Got Its Stripes

A timeless African folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the hot, golden land of Africa, all the zebras were plain white.

White as milk. White as clouds. White as the soft sand by the river. And a little zebra, the smallest of the herd, wished with all her heart that she could look a little different.

"Oh, I wish I had a coat like the leopard," she sighed, watching the leopard's golden spots. "Or feathers like the flamingo, pink as sunset!"

But the sun was hot, and the little zebra was hotter. The white coat shone bright in the sun, and the little zebra felt as warm as a baked potato.

One afternoon, she trotted to the tallest tree on the savanna. Its branches spread wide, and its leaves made lacy shadows on the ground.

"Ahh," sighed the little zebra, stepping into the cool shade. "This feels lovely."

She stood very still under the tree. The breeze rustled the leaves — swish, swish, swish. The sun shone through the branches, and the lacy shadows fell across her white coat.

And something wonderful happened. The shadows stayed!

When the little zebra stepped out of the shade, she looked down at herself — and gasped!

Black stripes! Beautiful black stripes ran across her white coat, just like the shadows of the leaves!

"Look at me!" she cried, galloping back to the herd. "The sun and the shadows have painted me!"

The other zebras gathered round. They sniffed her stripes. They touched them with their soft noses.

"Oh, how pretty!" said one. "How cool you look!"

"Please, sun," cried all the zebras together. "Paint us too!"

So all the zebras stood under the tall trees, one by one. The sun shone through the leaves, and the shadows painted stripes on every zebra — long stripes and short stripes, wide stripes and thin stripes.

And do you know the most wonderful part? No two zebras had the same stripes! Every zebra's pattern was different, like a picture drawn just for them.

The little zebra ran to the river and looked at her reflection in the clear water. There she was — white and black, stripey and proud.

"Thank you, sun," she said. "Thank you, shadows. Thank you, tall tree."

That evening, the herd lay down together in the soft grass. The little zebra counted her stripes — one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten — and then she counted ten more, and ten more, until her eyes grew heavy.

And under the twinkling stars, all the stripy zebras fell fast asleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The sun and the shadows painted a gift — and every zebra wears a pattern all its own.
