# The Lion and the Woodpecker

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a wide green forest, there lived a lion named Raja. His paws were as wide as dinner plates, and the ground thumped softly under each step. His roar could shake the leaves off the trees.

He never chewed — his dinner slid down his throat whole.

One evening, at dinner, he gulped down his meal in three big bites. Gulp. Gulp. Gulp.

Then he stopped.

Something was wrong. A bone — white and pointy — was stuck right in his throat.

Raja coughed and coughed. He tried to swallow. The bone would not move.

"Help!" he roared. "HELP!"

The rabbits hid under the bushes. The deer tiptoed far away.

Everyone heard the lion. But no one dared come close.

No one, that is, except one very small bird.

Pippa the woodpecker was tapping on an old tree trunk when she heard the roar. Tap-tap-tap. A pause. Tap-tap-tap.

She tilted her red head and listened.

"That's Raja!" she chirped to herself. "And he sounds hurt — really hurt."

Woodpeckers tap-tap-tap on tree trunks all day long — and Pippa tapped the loudest of them all. She was not one bit afraid.

She fluttered down through the leaves and landed on a branch near the lion. Her heart went thump-thump-thump.

"Raja," she said in her tiny voice, "what is wrong?"

"A bone! Stuck — right here!" the lion groaned, his big golden eyes all watery. "Little bird, please — can you pull it out?"

Pippa looked at the lion's huge open mouth — dark inside, and as big as a cave.

She took a deep breath — and you can take one with her. In… and out.

"I can help!" she said. "Look at my beak — long and strong, just right for pulling. But you must promise me one thing first."

"Anything!" said the lion. "Anything at all!"

"Promise me a reward," said Pippa, "and I'll hop in."

"I promise," said Raja. "I promise — cross my heart!"

So Pippa did the bravest thing she had ever done. She hopped right into the lion's mouth.

The lion held very, very still.

She spotted the bone, took it in her long beak, and pulled as hard as she could.

Pop! Out came the bone.

Raja swallowed. He swallowed again — and his throat felt smooth and clear.

He breathed a huge sigh of relief. "Ahhh. That feels better."

Pippa hopped out, landed on the grass, and gave her feathers a shake. "Now," she said, looking up at the big lion, "my reward, as you promised?"

Raja looked down — at her dusty red head, the beak still trembling a little, the bird no bigger than his paw. His cheeks felt warm. He felt a little shy. "You're right, little one," he said softly. "A lion keeps his promises. And you — you were the bravest of us all."

So Raja did something no lion had ever done before. He fetched his dinner, still warm, shared it with Pippa, and thanked her with the biggest smile in the forest.

From that day on, Raja never forgot his promise to the little woodpecker.

That evening, as the sun sank low and lazy, the big lion and the tiny bird sat together under the old tree. They watched the sky turn pink and gold — the oddest, happiest pair of friends in the whole forest.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Keep your promises — even lions must.*
