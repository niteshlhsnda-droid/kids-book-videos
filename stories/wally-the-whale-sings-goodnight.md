# Wally the Whale Sings Goodnight

*A cozy original bedtime tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a round, blue bay lived a little whale named Wally. The water was calm, and the moonlight sparkled softly on the waves.

Wally spent whole mornings splashing sun-warm waves with his big flat tail. Whole afternoons, he chased silver fish that darted and flashed like scattered coins. But at night, when the sun set and the bay grew quiet, Wally could not sleep.

The bay was too still. Too silent. Wally floated on the dark water, wide awake. He listened and listened, but all he heard was quiet.

"Maybe," Wally whispered, "the night just needs a little song."

So he sang. Softly at first, a small, wobbly goodnight song. Hmmm, hmm, goodniiiiight.

His song floated over the water like a ribbon of warm honey, slow and golden, curling around the rocks.

Nearby, Tilly the turtle lay snuggled inside her shell. She heard the humming, smiled in her sleep, and began to hum along — still half asleep. Hmmm... hmm... goodniii...ght.

Now another friend was singing too, and the song was softer than ever.

Dolly the dolphin was drifting beneath the waves. He heard the humming above him and clicked back — one soft click, then another, like tiny bells. Click-click... click-click...

The seahorses heard it too. They were swaying in the seagrass, back and forth, back and forth, like ribbons in the water. Sway... sway... goodniiiiight.

Then the jellyfish floated up from the deep, their round tops glowing like lanterns, and hummed along in tiny, shaky voices. H-h-hmm... h-h-hmm... goodniiiiight.

Olly the octopus was curled in his rock cave. He wrapped all eight arms around himself like a warm blanket and sang one long, sleepy note. Hmmm... goodniiiiight.

And a starfish, clinging to a gray stone, tapped a slow rhythm with one tiny arm. Tap... tap... goodniiiiight.

One by one, the whole bay joined in. The little fish swished their tails in time. The crabs tapped the sand, very, very quietly. Far above, the moon watched quietly, and the waves rocked Wally gently, the way a cradle rocks a baby.

Rock... rock... goodniiiiight.

Wally listened to all his friends singing his small song back to him. The bay didn't feel quiet anymore. It felt warm and full — the humming brushed his cheeks, soft and warm, tucking him in on every side.

His song slowed down. His eyelids grew heavier. The humming drifted on without him now, soft as the tide.

Hmmm... hmm... goodniiiiight...

A few last bubbles floated up from Wally's blowhole, soft as a sigh, and drifted away across the moonlit water.

And Wally the whale drifted off to sleep, wrapped in the bay's slow lullaby.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A shared song makes the dark feel friendly.*
