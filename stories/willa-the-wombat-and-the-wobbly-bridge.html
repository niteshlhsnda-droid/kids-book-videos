Willa the Wombat and the Wobbly Bridge

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green valley in Australia, where the gum trees whispered in the wind, there lived a little wombat named Willa.

Willa had a round, furry body, a shiny black nose, and the softest grey fur you ever touched. She lived in a cosy burrow under a big old gum tree, with her little brother Wally and her friend Joey the wallaby.

Every morning, Willa and Wally walked to the berry bush on the other side of the creek. The berries there were the sweetest in the whole valley — round and purple and bursting with juice.

But to reach the berry bush, they had to cross the wobbly bridge.

The wobbly bridge was made of old wooden planks and two long ropes. When you stepped on it, it went wobble-wobble-wobble! And below it, the creek went bubble-bubble-bubble over the smooth stones.

Wally loved the wobbly bridge. He bounced across it like a little rubber ball. "Wobble-wobble!" he laughed. "It is the best bridge in the world!"

But Willa did not love the wobbly bridge. Not one bit.

"It wobbles too much," said Willa, hugging her paws tight. "What if I fall in the creek? What if a plank breaks? What if I wobble right off?"

So every morning, Willa stayed on her side of the creek while Wally bounced across and brought her back a few berries in his paws.

"It is not the same," sighed Willa, eating her berries. "I want to pick my own berries. I want to cross the bridge myself."

One sunny morning, Joey the wallaby came hopping by. "Good morning, Willa!" he said. "Are you coming to the berry bush today?"

Willa looked at the wobbly bridge. It swayed gently in the breeze. Wobble-wobble-wobble.

"I want to," said Willa in a small voice. "But I am scared."

Joey smiled his kind wallaby smile. "Being scared is all right," he said. "Brave does not mean not scared. Brave means taking one small step at a time. Shall we cross together?"

Willa took a deep breath. "One small step at a time," she repeated.

Wally took her left paw. Joey took her right paw. And together, they stepped onto the bridge.

Wobble-wobble-wobble!

Willa's heart went thump-thump-thump. But she held on tight to her friends' paws, and she counted out loud, "One… two… three…"

One plank. Two planks. Three planks.

The bridge wobbled. The creek bubbled below. But Willa kept counting, and her friends kept holding her paws.

"Four… five… six…"

And then — she was across! She was standing on the other side, with the berry bush right in front of her!

"I did it!" cried Willa. "I crossed the wobbly bridge!"

Wally cheered. Joey cheered. And Willa picked the biggest, juiciest berry she could find and popped it into her mouth. It was sweet and purple and perfect.

"That was the best berry ever," said Willa, "because I picked it myself!"

That evening, as the sun went down over the valley, Willa crossed the bridge all by herself. Slowly. Carefully. Counting, "One… two… three…" all the way.

And when she reached the other side, she turned and waved at the bridge. "Thank you, wobbly bridge," she said. "You taught me that brave is just one small step at a time."

Then she waddled home to her cosy burrow, where her mother was waiting with warm milk and a soft blanket, and she fell asleep dreaming of berries and bridges and brave little steps.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Brave doesn't mean not scared — it means taking one small step at a time.
