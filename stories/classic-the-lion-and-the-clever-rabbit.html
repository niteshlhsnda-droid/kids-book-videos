The Lion and the Clever Rabbit

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a wide green forest, beside a slow, sparkling river, lived a great lion named Leo.

Leo was the biggest animal in the forest, and his roar was the loudest sound anyone had ever heard.

"ROAR!" went Leo, every single morning.

And every single morning, all the animals trembled.

"Why must he roar so loud?" squeaked Pip the mouse.

"Why must he chase us so fast?" whispered Kiki the deer.

The animals were tired of being frightened. So one day, they gathered under the tallest tree and talked it over.

"Let us send Leo one meal each day," said Tortoise, slowly and wisely. "Then he will not need to chase us at all."

Everyone agreed. Each day, one animal would walk to Leo's cave with a basket of food.

One morning, it was the turn of Remy the rabbit.

Remy was small. Remy was soft. But Remy was very, very clever.

As Remy hopped along the path, he thought and thought. "Leo's roar is big," he said to himself, "but my thinking can be bigger."

When Remy reached the cave, Leo was waiting, and Leo was grumpy.

"Where is my food?" roared Leo. "You are late! And you are so small!"

"I am sorry, great Leo," said Remy, bowing low. "I was coming with a basket of carrots, but on the way I met another lion!"

"Another lion?" roared Leo. "There is no other lion in MY forest!"

"Oh, but there is!" said Remy. "He lives in a deep well near the river. He says HE is the king of the forest. He ate my carrots!"

Leo's eyes went round. "Show me!" he roared. "Show me this other lion at once!"

So Remy hopped, hop, hop, to the deep well near the river. Leo stomped, stomp, stomp, right behind him.

"Look inside, great Leo," said Remy. "There he is!"

Leo leaned over the edge of the well and looked down into the water.

And there — staring right back up at him — was another lion!

The other lion had Leo's big eyes. The other lion had Leo's big mane. The other lion had Leo's big teeth.

"ROAR!" shouted Leo.

And the other lion shouted "ROAR!" right back.

Leo jumped back, surprised. "He is copying me!"

"Try this, great Leo," said Remy. "Say something kind. Kind words make kind friends."

Leo was not sure. But he leaned over the well again, and this time he said, softly, "Hello, friend."

And the lion in the water seemed to say, "Hello, friend."

Leo smiled a little. "He looks friendly now."

Then Leo said, "I am sorry I roared."

And the lion in the water seemed to say, "I am sorry I roared."

Remy smiled. "You see, great Leo? The lion in the well is only your own reflection. Whatever you give him, he gives right back. A roar comes back as a roar. A kind word comes back as a kind word."

Leo sat down on the grass. He thought for a long, long time.

"I have been roaring at everyone," he said at last. "No wonder everyone is frightened of me."

From that day on, Leo changed. He still had a big voice, but he used it to say good morning. He still was the biggest animal in the forest, but he used his size to help — to reach the high branches for the monkeys, and to carry baskets for the little ones.

And the animals were not frightened anymore. They waved when Leo walked by, and Leo waved back with his great big paw.

"Good morning, friends," Leo would say.

And all the animals would say, "Good morning, Leo!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: What you give comes back to you — a kind word makes a kind friend.
