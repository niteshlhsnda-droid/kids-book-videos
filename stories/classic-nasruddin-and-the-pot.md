# Nasruddin and the Pot

*A timeless Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little village with dusty lanes and sunny courtyards, there lived a funny, clever man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin loved jokes. He loved laughing. And he loved making his neighbours laugh too.

One day, Nasruddin knocked on his neighbour Ali's door.

"Dear Ali," he said, "may I borrow your cooking pot? I want to cook a big pot of rice for my family."

"Of course!" said Ali, with a smile. He fetched his shiny pot and handed it over. "Take good care of it."

"I will!" said Nasruddin.

Nasruddin cooked his rice. It smelled warm and delicious. His family ate together, and everyone said, "Mmm! So tasty!"

The next morning, Nasruddin walked back to Ali's house, carrying the pot. But inside the big pot, there sat a tiny little pot!

Ali blinked. "Nasruddin, what is this? I lent you one pot, and you have brought back two!"

Nasruddin smiled a wide, twinkling smile. "Wonderful news, Ali! While your pot was staying at my house, it had a baby!"

Ali laughed and laughed. "A baby pot! Oh, Nasruddin, you and your jokes!"

"Keep them both," said Nasruddin, laughing too. "A mother and her baby belong together."

Ali kept the tiny pot, and every time he saw it, he smiled.

A few weeks later, Nasruddin knocked on Ali's door again.

"Dear Ali," he said, "may I borrow your biggest pot? I want to cook a feast for all my friends!"

"Of course!" said Ali. "You always bring my pots back safe — and sometimes with a surprise!"

Ali gave him the biggest pot he had. It was round and deep, and it shone in the sun.

Nasruddin cooked his feast. His friends came, and they ate and laughed and told stories until the stars came out.

But the next morning, Nasruddin did not come back. Or the morning after that. Or the morning after that.

Ali scratched his head. "Hmm," he said. "Where is my big pot?"

On the fourth morning, Ali went to Nasruddin's house and knocked on the door.

"Nasruddin, where is my big pot?" he asked.

Nasruddin put on his saddest face. "Oh, Ali, I have very sad news," he said. "Your big pot has died."

Ali stared at him. "Died? A pot cannot die! Pots are not alive!"

Nasruddin tried hard to keep a straight face. But his eyes were twinkling, and his mouth was wobbling, and suddenly he burst out laughing.

"I am joking, Ali! I am only joking!" he cried. "You believed me when I said your pot had a baby. So why not believe me when I say it has died?"

Ali looked at him for a moment. Then he burst out laughing too. "Oh, Nasruddin! You tricked me again!"

Nasruddin ran inside and came back carrying the big pot. And guess what was inside it? The pot was full of sweet, sticky sweets, warm from the pan!

"Here is your pot, safe and sound," said Nasruddin. "And here are some sweets, to say thank you for lending it to me."

Ali and Nasruddin sat down together and shared the sweets. They laughed and laughed, until they were giggling too hard to speak.

And from that day on, whenever Ali lent Nasruddin a pot, they both smiled and said, "No babies, and no dying — just a pot, and maybe some sweets!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A joke is best when everyone can laugh together at the end.*
