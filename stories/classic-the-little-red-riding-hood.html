Little Red Riding Hood

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a little house at the edge of a green forest, there lived a sweet little girl named Riya.

Riya loved her red cloak with its soft red hood, and she wore it every single day.

So everyone in the village called her Little Red Riding Hood.

One sunny morning, Mother packed a basket with warm cakes and a little pot of honey.

"Please take these cakes to Grandma," said Mother. "She is not feeling well today, and the cakes will make her smile."

"Yes, Mother!" said Riya, and she skipped out of the door with her basket swinging.

"Stay on the path, my dear," called Mother, "and do not stop to talk to strangers!"

Riya promised, and she walked along the path, singing a happy song.

Soon Riya met a big grey wolf standing under a tall oak tree.

"Good morning, little girl," said the wolf in a smooth voice. "Where are you going with that lovely basket?"

"I am going to Grandma's house," said Riya. "She is not well, and I am taking her cakes."

"How kind you are!" said the wolf, with a sly smile. "Why not pick some pretty flowers for her too? Grandma loves flowers!"

Riya looked at the flowers. Oh, they were so pretty! She bent down to pick a pink one, then a yellow one, then a blue one.

While Riya picked flowers, the wolf tiptoed away down the path, quicker than a shadow.

He knocked on Grandma's door — knock, knock, knock!

"Who is there?" called Grandma.

"It is me, Riya," said the wolf, trying to sound like a little girl. "I have brought you cakes!"

Grandma opened the door, and the wolf stepped inside with a big scary growl.

But Grandma was not frightened at all. She picked up her broom and waved it in the air. "Shoo! Shoo, you naughty wolf!" she cried.

Just then, a kind woodcutter named Mohan was walking past the house, carrying his axe on his shoulder.

He heard Grandma's voice and Riya's song floating through the trees.

"That sounds like trouble!" said Mohan, and he hurried to the door.

Mohan stepped into the doorway and stood tall. "Leave this house at once, wolf!" he said in a strong, brave voice.

The wolf heard that brave voice, and his smile melted away.

With a frightened yelp, the wolf turned and ran — out of the door and deep into the forest.

Grandma put down her broom and gave Mohan a big warm hug.

"Thank you, kind Mohan," she said. "You came just in time!"

Just then, Riya came skipping up the path with her basket and her bunch of flowers.

"Grandma! Grandma!" she called. "I have brought you cakes and flowers!"

Grandma opened her arms wide, and Riya ran into them for the biggest hug of all.

Mohan stayed for tea, and they all shared the warm cakes and the honey.

"From now on," said Riya, "I will stay on the path, just like Mother said."

And Grandma smiled, because Riya was safe, and warm, and home.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Stay on the path, listen to kind advice, and good friends will always help you home.
