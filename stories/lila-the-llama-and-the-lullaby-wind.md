# Lila the Llama and the Lullaby Wind

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High on a green hill, where the clouds came down to say hello, lived Lila the llama.

Lila had the softest wool in the valley, the gentlest eyes, and — most special of all — a hum. Every night as the sun melted into the hills, Lila would sit on her favorite rock and hum:

"Loo-loo-loo… loo-loo-loo… sleep, little world, sleep…"

The night wind loved Lila's lullaby. It would scoop up her hum in its invisible arms and carry it down, down into the valley — to the lambs in the meadow, the baby rabbits in their burrow, the tiny owlets blinking in their tree.

"Loo-loo-loo…" sighed the wind through the grass, and one by one, little eyes closed.

But one evening, the air was perfectly still. Not a leaf trembled. Not a blade of grass whispered.

Lila hummed her lullaby, but with no wind to carry it, the tune just floated up and faded among the stars.

"Oh no," whispered Lila. "How will the babies fall asleep tonight?"

She thought and thought. Then she took a deep breath and hummed LOUDER — and she added words:

"Loo-loo-loo, the moon is bright, loo-loo-loo, the stars are light, close your eyes, my dears, good night…"

Her voice rolled down the hill like warm honey. It reached the meadow. It reached the burrow. It reached the tall, tall tree.

And do you know what happened? The wind woke up! It had been napping behind the hill. "What a beautiful song!" it sighed, and it gathered up Lila's lullaby and carried it farther than ever before — over the river, over the next hill, to every sleepy baby in the land.

That night, Lila learned something wonderful: even when help doesn't come, your own gentle voice is enough to begin. And help often wakes up to join you.

So now, every night, if you listen very carefully when the wind moves through the trees — loo-loo-loo — that is Lila, singing you to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Your gentle voice is enough to begin — kindness carries farther than you know.*
