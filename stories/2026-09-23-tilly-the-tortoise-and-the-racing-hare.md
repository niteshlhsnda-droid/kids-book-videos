# Tilly the Tortoise and the Racing Hare

In a green meadow, where the buttercups danced in the breeze, lived a little tortoise named Tilly. Tilly was small and round, and she carried her cozy shell on her back wherever she went.

Now, Tilly was not fast. Her four short legs went *plip… plop… plip… plop…* across the grass, slow and steady, slow and steady.

Hare lived in the same meadow, and Hare was very, very fast. *Zoom!* went Hare. *Zip! Zap! Zoom!* His long ears flapped as he bounced over the hills.

"Why do you walk so slowly, Tilly?" giggled Hare one sunny morning. "I could hop to the old oak tree and back before you took three little steps!"

Tilly just smiled a soft tortoise smile. "I may be slow," she said kindly, "but I keep on going. Shall we race to the tall oak tree?"

Hare laughed so hard he almost tumbled over. "A race? With you? Ha! Of course!" And so the friends agreed: the race would start at the buttercup patch and finish at the old oak tree.

Early the next morning, the friends gathered to watch. Mouse squeaked, "On your marks…" Squirrel twitched his tail. "Get set…" And Robin sang out, "GO!"

*Zoom!* Hare shot off like a furry arrow. *Plip… plop… plip… plop…* went Tilly, one little step at a time.

Hare looked back and laughed. "Tilly is so far behind! I have plenty of time for a little nap." So he curled up under a shady mushroom, yawned a big hare yawn, and fell fast asleep. *Zzzzz…*

But Tilly did not stop. *Plip… plop… plip… plop…* She walked past the mushroom where Hare was snoring, past the buttercups, past the pebbles — one slow step after another.

The old oak tree grew closer. Closer. Closer still. And at last — *step* — Tilly's little foot touched its roots.

"We did it," she whispered, and smiled.

Then Hare woke with a start. He blinked his sleepy eyes and *zoomed* to the oak tree as fast as his legs could carry him. But there was Tilly, already sitting quietly in the shade.

"I — I mean, *you* won!" cried Hare in surprise. "How?"

Tilly's eyes twinkled. "You are the fastest, dear Hare," she said. "But I just kept going, step by step, all the way."

That evening, as the sun painted the sky pink and gold, Hare sat beside Tilly under the oak tree. "You taught me something today," he said softly. "Being fast is fun, but finishing what you start is what matters."

And from that day on, Hare never laughed at slow walkers again. Sometimes he even walked beside Tilly, *plip… plop… plip… plop…*, and discovered that a slow walk through the meadow is a lovely thing indeed.

**The moral:** Slow and steady, step by step, finishes the race. 🌱
