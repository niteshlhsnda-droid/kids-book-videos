The Capseller and the Monkeys

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a land of sunny villages and dusty lanes, there lived a kind old man. He was a capseller. Every morning he stacked his caps in a tall, tall pile on his head. Red caps, blue caps, green caps, and yellow caps. Then he walked from village to village, calling out in a happy voice, "Caps! Caps! Lovely caps for sale!"

One hot afternoon the sun was high and bright. The capseller was very tired. His feet were dusty, his legs wobbly. "I must rest for a little while," he said. He saw a big tree with cool, shady leaves. He sat down under it, put his caps beside him, and closed his eyes. Soon he was fast asleep. Snore, snore, snore.

But high up in the tree, some cheeky monkeys were watching. They swung down on their long tails, one by one. Swish! They took the red caps. Swish! They took the blue caps. Swish! They took the green caps and the yellow caps too. Then they jumped back up into the tree and put the caps on their own heads. They chattered and giggled. "Hee, hee, hee!"

After a while the capseller woke up. He rubbed his eyes and looked beside him. "Oh no!" he cried. "Where are my caps?" He looked up and saw the monkeys wearing them. "Give me my caps back!" he shouted. He shook his fists in the air.

But the monkeys only copied him. They shook their little fists in the air too. "Hee, hee, hee!"

The capseller waved his arms and stamped his feet. The monkeys waved their arms and stamped their feet too.

He put his hands on his hips. "Come down here, you naughty monkeys!" The monkeys put their hands on their hips too. "Hee, hee, hee!"

Hot and tired and cross, he scratched his head. "Oh, what shall I do?" The monkeys scratched their heads too.

Then the capseller had an idea. A quiet, clever idea. He took off his own cap — the one cap he still had — and threw it on the ground. "There!" he said. "Take that too!"

And down came the caps, one by one. Plop! Plop! Plop! The monkeys had copied him once more, and thrown their caps to the ground.

The capseller laughed and laughed. He picked up all his caps and stacked them on his head again. "Thank you, little monkeys," he said with a smile. And off he walked down the dusty lane, calling out, "Caps! Caps! Lovely caps for sale!" The monkeys watched him go, and the evening breeze was soft and warm.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Cleverness beats shouting.
