# The Goose with the Golden Eggs

*A classic Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once there was a farmer named Finn who lived in a little cottage with a red roof.

Finn was not rich, but he was happy. He had a vegetable garden, a spotted cow, and one plump white goose named Goldie.

Every morning, Goldie waddled out of her straw nest — waddle, waddle — and laid one egg for Finn's breakfast.

One morning, Finn picked up the egg and gasped. It was heavy, and yellow, and shiny all over!

"A golden egg!" he cried. "My goose has laid a golden egg!"

He ran to the market and sold the egg for a whole bag of coins. That night he bought warm bread, sweet honey, and a soft new blanket.

The next morning — another golden egg! And the next morning — another!

Soon Finn had new shoes, a painted cart, and a bell for his cow. But the more he had, the more he wanted.

But instead of feeling thankful, Finn began to feel greedy.

"One egg a day is too slow," he grumbled. "If Goldie has gold inside her, why wait? I want ALL the gold right now!"

So one evening, he crept to Goldie's nest and shook her awake. "Give me all your golden eggs at once!" he cried.

Poor Goldie was so frightened that she flapped her great white wings — flap, flap, flap! — and flew up, up, up over the hills, far away.

She never came back.

Finn waited the next morning. No golden egg. He waited the morning after that. No golden egg, ever again.

Finn sat in his empty cottage and sighed. "If only I had been patient and thankful," he said, "I would still have my goose and her golden eggs."

And from that day on, whenever Finn felt greedy, he remembered Goldie flying over the hills, and he smiled and shared what he had.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Greed spoils a good thing — be thankful for what comes to you, little by little.*
