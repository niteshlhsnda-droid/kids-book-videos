The Cock and the Pearl

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little farmyard, where the sun was warm and the hay was soft, there lived a cheerful cock named Kuku.

Kuku had bright red feathers on his head, a shiny tail, and a very loud voice. Every morning, he woke the whole farm with his song: "Cock-a-doodle-doo!"

First the cow would moo and stretch. Then the horse would neigh and shake his mane. Then the little chicks would peep and hop out of the henhouse, all in a row.

"Good morning, farm!" Kuku would cry. "It is time to find breakfast!"

Kuku loved to scratch in the soft earth, looking for tasty grains of corn. Scratch, scratch, scratch went his feet, all morning long.

One sunny morning, Kuku was scratching near the old barn when — clink! — his foot hit something hard and shiny.

"What is this?" said Kuku. He pecked at it, and it rolled into the sunlight.

It was a pearl! A big, round, shiny white pearl, glowing like a tiny moon!

Kuku looked at the pearl. The pearl looked back at Kuku, all shiny and bright.

"Oh, what a pretty thing you are!" said Kuku. "You shine like the moon, and you are smooth as a pebble in the stream. Anyone would be proud to have you!"

Just then, his friend Munia the hen came clucking over. "Oh my!" said Munia. "What a shiny thing! Is it a magic egg? Is it a little moon that fell from the sky?"

"It is a pearl," said Kuku. "A very fine, very shiny pearl."

"How wonderful!" clucked Munia. "You must be the richest cock in the whole world!"

But Kuku tilted his head to one side and thought.

"And yet… what can I do with a pearl? I cannot eat it. It will not keep me warm at night. It will not sing me a morning song."

Kuku looked at the pearl once more, and then he smiled.

"I would rather have one little grain of corn," he said, "than a hundred shiny pearls!"

And with that, Kuku scratched the earth again — scratch, scratch, scratch — until he found a plump, golden grain of corn! And then he found another one, just for Munia!

"Breakfast!" cried Kuku happily. And he shared the grain with his friend Munia the hen, who clucked with delight.

The pearl stayed in the farmyard, shining in the sun. And Kuku never missed it — not even a little bit.

That evening, as the sun went down and the farm grew quiet, Kuku and Munia sat together on the fence and watched the sky turn pink and gold.

"Goodnight, farm," said Kuku, with a happy little yawn. "What a fine day."

For Kuku knew what truly mattered: a full tummy, a warm nest, and a good friend to share breakfast with.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: What you truly need matters more than what only shines.
