Akbar and Birbal: The Mango Saplings

A timeless Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One golden evening, Emperor Akbar rode out of his palace with his clever minister Birbal. The sky was turning soft and pink, and the road smelled of rain and jasmine.

By the roadside, they saw a very old man. His back was bent, and his hands were brown with earth. He was digging little holes in the ground and planting tiny green baby mango trees, one by one.

Akbar pulled his horse to a stop and smiled, a little puzzled. "Chacha! You are so old! You will never live to taste these mangoes. Why do you work so hard?"

The old man looked up and smiled. His eyes were full of light. "Huzoor, my father planted the mango trees I eat from today. I plant these for my children's children — and for every traveler who passes this way."

Birbal nodded wisely and stroked his beard, a small smile hiding at the corner of his mouth. Akbar's heart felt warm all at once, like tea on a cold day.

"You are right, Chacha," said Akbar. "And every good deed deserves a thank-you." He took a small bag of gold coins and placed it in the old man's hands.

Rahim Chacha burst out laughing. "Huzoor, look! My trees are already giving fruit — before they are even grown!"

Akbar laughed too, a big, happy laugh that rolled across the fields. "Then here is more fruit for your garden!" And he gave him another bag of gold coins.

Birbal leaned in with a twinkle in his eye and whispered, "Wah, Chacha — your trees give gold before mangoes!"

The old man waved goodbye as the royal party rode home under the rising moon. His little baby mango trees stood in a neat green row, swaying softly in the evening breeze.

That night, Akbar told the story at dinner with a warm heart, and everyone smiled, warm and cozy.

And far away, under a sky full of twinkling stars, Rahim Chacha watered his baby mango trees. He sat down beside them, closed his eyes, and fell asleep with a smile on his face, dreaming of sweet, golden mangoes.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Do good for others today, even if the reward comes tomorrow.
