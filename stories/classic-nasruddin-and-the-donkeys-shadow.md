# Nasruddin and the Donkey's Shadow

*A timeless Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One hot summer day, Nasruddin wanted to ride to the next village, so he rented a donkey from his neighbour, Karim.

"One silver coin for the donkey," said Karim. "Ride him there and back, and bring him home safe."

Off went Nasruddin, clip-clop, clip-clop, under the blazing sun. At midday, the sun was so hot that Nasruddin stopped under a big tree, hopped off the donkey, and sat down in the donkey's cool shadow to eat his lunch.

Just then, Karim came puffing along the road. "Nasruddin!" he cried. "You rented my donkey — but you did not rent his shadow! If you want to sit in the shadow, that will cost you another silver coin!"

Nasruddin nearly choked on his bread. "The shadow comes with the donkey!" he laughed. "You cannot sell a donkey and keep his shadow!"

"I can, and I will!" said Karim, folding his arms. "One more coin for the shadow!"

The two men argued and argued, back and forth, until a crowd gathered to watch. At last, Nasruddin stood up, dusted off his clothes, and said, "Very well, Karim. Let us ask the wise judge in the village to decide."

So off they went to the judge, with the donkey trotting behind them and the whole crowd following.

The judge listened carefully to both sides. Then he smiled and said, "Karim, you are right. The shadow is yours, and Nasruddin must pay for sitting in it."

Karim beamed with triumph. "Ha! One more coin, please!"

But the judge held up his hand. "Not so fast. Nasruddin, you rented the donkey for the whole day. The sun moves, and so does the shadow. If Karim owns the shadow, then Karim must follow his donkey all day long, wherever it goes, to keep his shadow safe!"

Karim's face fell. "Follow the donkey all day? In this heat?"

"Exactly," said the judge. "Or you may let Nasruddin sit in the shadow for free, as any good neighbour would."

Karim laughed out loud. "Free it is! Sitting in the shade is much better than chasing a donkey all day!"

And so Nasruddin finished his lunch in the cool shadow, Karim shared the bread, and the two men rode home together, laughing all the way.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A silly argument often has a silly answer — and a kind one is best of all.*
