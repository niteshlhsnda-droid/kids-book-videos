# The Deer Who Saved His Herd

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green forest full of tall banyan trees, there lived a great herd of deer.

Their leader was a wise old deer named Arjun. He had soft brown fur, kind dark eyes, and wide branching antlers like the arms of a tree.

Every morning, Arjun led his herd to the sweet grass meadow. Every evening, he counted them home again, one by one.

"One, two, three," he would say softly, "all safe, all home."

But one day, the king of the land, King Vikram, came hunting in the forest.

"From now on," said the king, "one deer from this herd must come to my palace each morning."

The deer were frightened. Their legs trembled. Their soft ears drooped low.

"Do not be afraid," said Arjun in his calm, deep voice. "We will take turns, and we will look after one another. No deer will be left alone."

And so it went, day after day. Each morning, one deer walked bravely to the palace, and the rest of the herd waited quietly under the banyan trees.

Then one morning, it was the turn of a young mother deer named Lila. And Lila was going to have a baby very, very soon.

"Oh, Arjun," whispered Lila, her eyes full of tears. "My little one is coming any day now. If I go, who will look after my baby?"

All the deer looked at Arjun. Arjun looked at Lila. Then he stood up tall and straight.

"I will go in your place, Lila," he said gently. "You stay here, safe in the shade. Your baby needs you."

"But Arjun," cried the herd, "you are our leader! You are the wisest of us all!"

"A leader looks after his herd," said Arjun with a soft smile. "That is what leaders are for."

So Arjun walked slowly through the forest, past the tall grass and the singing stream, all the way to the king's palace.

When King Vikram saw the great deer standing before him, he was very surprised.

"Where is the young mother deer?" asked the king. "Why have you come instead?"

Arjun bowed his great antlers low and said, "Your Majesty, Lila is going to have a baby very soon. A baby needs its mother. So I have come in her place."

The king stared at Arjun. No one had ever spoken to him so bravely — or so kindly.

"Wait," said the king slowly. "You would give yourself for another deer? Even for a little one who is not yet born?"

"Yes," said Arjun. "That is what a herd is for. We look after one another."

King Vikram was quiet for a long, long time. Then he smiled — a warm, wide smile.

"Then you shall not be harmed, noble deer," said the king. "And neither shall Lila, nor her baby, nor any deer in your herd ever again. From today, no deer in this forest will be hunted. They are all safe, by my word."

Arjun bowed again, lower this time, and his heart felt as light as a feather.

Back in the forest, the whole herd cheered — softly, so as not to wake the birds. Lila nuzzled Arjun's cheek with her soft nose.

"Thank you, Arjun," she whispered. "You saved us all."

That evening, under the great banyan tree, the deer lay down together in the soft grass. The moon rose round and silver. The fireflies blinked like tiny lanterns.

And Arjun, tired and happy, closed his kind eyes and drifted off to sleep, knowing his herd was safe at last.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A true leader looks after others first — kindness can change even a king's heart.*
