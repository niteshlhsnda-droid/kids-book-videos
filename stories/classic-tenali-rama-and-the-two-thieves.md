# Tenali Rama and the Two Thieves

*A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a sunny town in the south of India, there lived a very clever man named Tenali Rama.

Tenali Rama loved jokes, stories, and riddles. But most of all, he loved helping people. Whenever there was a tricky problem, the king would say, "Call Tenali Rama! He will know what to do."

One night, two thieves tiptoed through the town. They carried a heavy bag of gold coins they had taken from a kind shopkeeper. The coins did not belong to them, and the thieves knew it. But they did not want the guards to catch them with the bag.

"Quick!" whispered one thief. "Let us hide the gold in Tenali Rama's garden. No one will look for it there."

So they dug a little hole under Tenali Rama's mango tree, dropped the bag inside, and patted the soft earth flat. Then they ran away into the dark.

The next morning, Tenali Rama stepped into his garden with a watering can. He loved his mango tree, and he watered it every day.

But that morning, he stopped. "Hmm," he said, looking at the fresh pile of earth. "My mango tree did not look like this yesterday. Someone has been digging here."

Tenali Rama was very clever. He did not dig up the hole. Instead, he smiled a small, secret smile.

"I know!" he said. "I will wait and see who comes back for it."

That night, the two thieves tiptoed back into the garden. They carried a little spade, ready to dig up the gold.

But Tenali Rama was waiting for them, watering can in hand, humming a happy tune.

"Oh, good evening, my friends!" he said with a big smile. "You have come just in time! I found the gold seeds you planted under my mango tree."

"Gold seeds?" said the thieves, looking at each other in surprise.

"Yes, yes!" said Tenali Rama. "I have been watering them all day, so they will grow into a fine gold tree. But a tree grows faster when its gardeners dig it up to check! Come, come, let us dig together and see if it has sprouted!"

The thieves looked at each other. A gold tree! That sounded wonderful. They forgot all about hiding. They took up their spade and dug, dug, dug — until they lifted the heavy bag of gold coins right out of the hole with their own hands.

"There it is!" cried Tenali Rama cheerfully. "Thank you for digging it up for me!"

Just then, the town guards came marching into the garden. Tenali Rama had asked the guards to wait nearby, because he knew the thieves would come back.

"These coins belong to the shopkeeper," said Tenali Rama gently. "Taking what is not yours only brings worry. You spent all night hiding, and all day worrying. That is no way to live."

The thieves hung their heads. They were not hurt, and no one shouted at them. But they felt very, very sorry.

"We are sorry," they said. "We will never take what is not ours again."

Tenali Rama gave the bag of gold back to the shopkeeper, who thanked him with a warm smile. And the thieves promised to earn their living honestly, by working hard like everyone else.

That evening, Tenali Rama watered his mango tree once more, and chuckled to himself.

"Gold does not grow on trees," he said. "But a clever idea, and an honest heart, can grow anywhere."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Cleverness and honesty can solve a problem without a single harsh word.*
