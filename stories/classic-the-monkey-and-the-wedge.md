# The Monkey and the Wedge

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a forest near a village, where the morning light peeked through the tall leaves, there lived a family of playful monkeys. Their leader was Monty, the kind of monkey who couldn't walk past a shiny leaf without picking it up, sniffing it, and giving it a lick — and then pulling a funny face.

One morning, two woodcutters came to the forest. Thunk! Thunk! went their axe, as they split a huge log. Then, to hold the crack open, they hammered in a little wooden wedge — like a tiny triangle. Then they strolled off to eat their lunch, leaving the wedge in the log.

As soon as they were gone — swing! swoosh! — down came the monkeys.

"What is this funny wooden tooth?" giggled Monty, climbing onto the log. He sat right over the crack, his long tail dangling into the split.

With both hands, he grabbed the wedge. "I wonder what happens if I pull THIS!" What do you think will happen, little one?

"Monty, don't!" cried the other monkeys. "The woodcutters left it there for a reason!"

But Monty never listened. He tugged with all his might — and POP! Out came the wedge!

SNAP! The log closed tight — like a door slamming shut! — and Monty's tail was caught fast in the crack!

"OWWW!" cried Monty, hopping on the log, pulling this way and that. But the log would not open.

Just then the woodcutters came back. "Oh dear," said one of the woodcutters, smiling kindly. "Let's get that poor tail of yours free." He eased the log back open and set Monty's tail free.

Monty rubbed his sore tail. "No more grabby-grab!" said Monty. "From today I look first, think first — THEN I touch!"

Then he curled his long tail around a nearby branch, closed his eyes, and drifted off to sleep.

Goodnight, Monty. The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Do not meddle with things you do not understand — curiosity needs care.*
