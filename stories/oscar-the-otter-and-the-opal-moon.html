Oscar the Otter and the Opal Moon

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a blue river that wound through a quiet valley, there lived a little otter named Oscar.

Oscar had smooth brown fur, bright, twinkling eyes, and a long tail that splashed happily in the water. He loved to swim, to slide down the muddy banks, and to float on his back and watch the clouds drift by.

One evening, just as the sun was setting and the sky was turning soft pink and gold, Oscar was swimming home when he saw something sparkling on the riverbank.

It was a pebble. But this was no ordinary pebble. It was smooth and round, and it shimmered with all the colours of the rainbow — pink and blue and green and gold. It glowed softly, just like the moon.

"Oh, what a beautiful pebble," whispered Oscar. "You shine like a little opal moon."

Oscar picked up the pebble gently in his paws. Its light was soft and warm, and it made him feel safe and cosy, like a hug.

Oscar tucked the pebble into his paws and smiled. "I will carry you home," he said, "and your light will keep me company all night."

But as Oscar swam on toward his burrow, he heard a small, worried voice. It was Daisy the duckling, standing on the riverbank and looking at the darkening sky.

"The night is coming," said Daisy in a tiny voice, "and I do not like the dark. I cannot find my mummy's nest."

"Do not worry, Daisy," said Oscar kindly. "Come with me. My little opal moon will light the way."

Oscar held up the glowing pebble, and its soft light shone on the path. Daisy waddled beside him, feeling braver with every step.

A little further on, they met Finn the frog, sitting on a lily pad and blinking his big eyes.

"I am trying to find my home," said Finn, "but everything looks so dark and strange."

"Follow our light," said Oscar. "The opal moon will show us the way."

Finn hopped along behind them, and the pebble glowed a little brighter.

Then they met Hazel the hedgehog, who was curled up under a bush, peeping out nervously.

"I was looking for my cosy burrow," said Hazel, "but the night feels so big."

"Stay close to us," said Oscar with a smile. "The light will guide us all home."

So Oscar, Daisy, Finn, and Hazel walked together along the riverbank. The opal pebble glowed softly in Oscar's paws, lighting every step, and the night did not feel big or dark at all. It felt gentle and kind, like a warm blanket.

One by one, they found their homes. Daisy found her mummy's nest, soft and warm. Finn found his lily pad, floating quietly on the water. Hazel found her cosy burrow, snug beneath the bush.

"Goodnight, friends," said Oscar. "Sleep well."

"Goodnight, Oscar," they all called back. "Thank you for sharing your light."

Oscar swam back to his own burrow and placed the opal pebble beside him. It glowed softly all night long, like a little moon watching over the river.

Oscar curled up in his burrow, closed his eyes, and smiled. He knew that his friends were all safe at home, and that the gentle light would be there for them whenever they needed it.

And as the real moon rose high above the river, Oscar drifted off to sleep, warm and happy, dreaming of soft, shimmering light.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Sharing your light makes the dark feel gentle for everyone.
