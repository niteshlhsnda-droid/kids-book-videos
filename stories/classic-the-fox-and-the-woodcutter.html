The Fox and the Woodcutter

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a forest full of tall pine trees, there lived a clever red fox named Sona.

Sona was quick and smart. She could run faster than the wind, and she knew every path in the forest — every hollow log, every mossy stone, every secret shortcut.

One crisp morning, Sona was trotting home with a basket of blackberries when she heard loud voices behind her.

"There she is! Catch the fox! Catch the fox!"

Two hunters, Arman and Samar, were running after her, waving their arms and shouting.

Sona ran as fast as her little legs could go. She darted left, she darted right, and at last she saw a small wooden hut at the edge of the forest.

Inside the hut lived Bhanu the woodcutter. Bhanu was chopping wood for his fire, and his axe went chop-chop-chop.

"Please, please help me!" cried Sona, tumbling through the door. "The hunters are chasing me! Please hide me!"

Bhanu was a kind man. "Quick!" he said. "Hide behind that big pile of firewood. Stay very still and very quiet."

Sona squeezed behind the firewood and held her breath. She could hear her own heart going thump-thump-thump.

A moment later, the two hunters arrived at the door.

"Woodcutter!" called Arman. "Have you seen a red fox? She ran this way!"

Bhanu looked the hunters in the eye. "No," he said. "I have not seen any fox."

But as he said the words, his hand pointed — just a tiny little point — toward the pile of firewood!

Sona saw the pointing hand from her hiding place. Her heart went thump-thump-thump even faster!

The hunters looked around the hut. They looked at the table. They looked at the fire. But they did not see the little pointed hand. And they did not find Sona.

"Thank you," said Samar. "We will look in the meadow." And off they went.

When the hunters were gone, Sona crept out from behind the firewood. She shook the dust off her red coat and looked at Bhanu.

"Thank you for hiding me," said Sona. "But I must tell you something. Your words said 'no' — but your hand said 'yes.' Your hand pointed right at me!"

Bhanu's face turned red. "Oh, Sona," he said. "You are right. My mouth said one thing and my hand said another. That was not honest of me. I am sorry."

Sona smiled. "It is all right," she said. "You still kept me safe. But remember — our words and our actions should say the same thing."

Bhanu nodded. "I will remember," he said. "From today, my words and my hands will always agree."

To say thank you, Sona shared her blackberries with Bhanu. And Bhanu shared his warm bread with Sona. They sat by the fire together, eating blackberries and bread, as the sun went down over the pine trees.

And from that day on, whenever Bhanu spoke, his hands did exactly what his words promised — and Sona visited him every morning, just to say hello.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Our words and our actions should say the same thing.
