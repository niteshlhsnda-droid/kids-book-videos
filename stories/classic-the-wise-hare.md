# The Wise Hare

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Under a tall, leafy banyan tree, in a quiet green forest, lived a little hare named Hana.

Hana had soft grey fur, long ears that twitched at every sound, and a cozy burrow under the tree's great roots.

Every day, Hana nibbled the sweet grass under the banyan tree. Every evening, she watched the sky turn pink and gold as the sun went down.

One warm afternoon, Hana was resting under the banyan tree when — plip! — a ripe fruit dropped from the branches and landed on a dry leaf with a loud TAP!

Hana jumped a mile high! Her ears shot straight up! Her heart went thump, thump, thump!

"Oh no! Oh no!" cried Hana. "The sky is falling! The sky is falling!"

And Hana ran. She ran as fast as her little legs could carry her, right out of the forest.

On the path, she met Didi the deer.

"Hana! Why are you running so fast?" asked Didi.

"The sky is falling! The sky is falling!" cried Hana. "I heard it with my own ears! Run! Run!"

Didi's eyes went wide. "The sky is falling? Oh no!" And Didi began to run too.

Then they met Bholu the bear.

"Why are you running?" asked Bholu.

"The sky is falling! The sky is falling!" cried Hana and Didi together. "We heard it with our own ears! Run! Run!"

Bholu's eyes went wide. "The sky is falling? Oh no!" And Bholu began to run too.

Soon, a whole crowd of animals was running — the deer, the bear, the monkey, the peacock, and the elephant. Everyone was shouting, "The sky is falling! The sky is falling!"

Just then, along came the Lion King, walking slowly down the forest path.

He looked at the running, shouting crowd and said, in his calm, deep voice, "STOP."

Everyone stopped. Everyone was puffing and panting.

"Who told you the sky is falling?" asked the Lion King.

"Hana told us!" said Didi.

"Didi told us!" said Bholu.

"Bholu told us!" said the monkey.

And everyone looked at Hana.

"Little hare," said the Lion King kindly, "did you SEE the sky falling?"

Hana's ears drooped. "N-no," she said in a small voice. "But I heard a great big TAP! It was so loud!"

"Then let us go and look," said the Lion King. "Come with me, Hana. Let us see what really happened."

So Hana hopped, slowly, back to the banyan tree, with the Lion King walking beside her and all the animals following behind.

They looked up. The sky was blue and calm. The banyan tree was green and still.

They looked down. And there, on a dry leaf, lay a ripe fruit — the very fruit that had fallen with a TAP!

"It was only a fruit," said Hana, blushing pink under her fur. "The sky was not falling at all. I heard a sound, and I made up the rest in my head."

The Lion King smiled. "A sound can sound scary," he said. "But before we tell others, we should stop and look. A good friend checks before she shares a fright."

Hana nodded. "I am sorry I made everyone run," she said. "Next time, I will look first."

That evening, all the animals sat under the banyan tree together. Hana shared the ripe fruit with everyone, and the Lion King told a story about the stars.

And from that day on, whenever Hana heard a strange sound, she would take a deep breath, look carefully, and only then decide what to do.

And whenever her friends heard a scary story, they would ask, "Did you see it yourself?" — just like the Lion King taught them.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Before sharing a scary story, stop and look — check what really happened.*
