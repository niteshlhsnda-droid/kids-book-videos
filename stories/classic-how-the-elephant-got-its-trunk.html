How the Elephant Got Its Trunk

A playful Just-So folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, before your grandmother's grandmother was born, elephants did not have long trunks at all. Their noses were tiny — no bigger than a round button, like the buttons on your shirt. Can you imagine? A great big elephant with a teeny-tiny button nose!

In those days there lived a baby elephant who was curious about everything. He asked, "Why are leaves green?" and "Where does the river go?" — to his mama, his papa, and even the sleepy old tortoise, who blinked up at him from a cozy puddle of warm mud. And his very favorite question of all was, "What does the crocodile eat for dinner?" ... Before we find out — what do YOU think the crocodile eats for dinner?

"Nobody knows, sweetie," said his mama. "And you must never go near the wide green river to find out."

But the little elephant's questions grew and grew inside him, like bubbles in a fizzy drink. So one warm morning, with the sun toasting his back and the tall grass tickling his ankles, off he went, thump-thump-thumping down to the wide green river all by himself.

There, floating like a bumpy green log, was the crocodile, snoring soft bubbles through his afternoon nap.

"Excuse me," said the baby elephant politely. "What do you eat for dinner?"

The crocodile opened one sleepy eye. "Come closer, little nose, and I'll tell you." Uh-oh — should the little elephant go closer?

The baby elephant leaned in close, and the crocodile gently held the baby's tiny button nose in his soft, flat mouth. Not a bite at all — just a soft, squishy hold, like holding a mitten.

"Let's play tug-with-the-nose!" said the crocodile with a wink. "You pull back, and I'll pull too. Ready?"

The baby elephant giggled and gave a tug. The crocodile tugged back. The baby pulled a little harder. And his tiny button nose went s-t-r-e-t-c-h... longer... and longer... and LONGER!

Plip! The crocodile let go. The baby elephant sat down with a thump and looked at his nose. It was long! It wiggled and waved in the breeze!

"My nose!" he shouted. "It's all stretched out!" Then he discovered something wonderful. He could curl it this way and that way. He sucked up river water and let out a soft little sneeze — atchoo — spraying a cool, tickly shower over his own toes. He giggled softly.

He pointed his new trunk at a tree. Up, up it reached, and it plucked the sweetest leaves right off the highest branch — cool, crackly leaves, fresh from the top of the tree. "Mmm!" he said, munching. "This nose is the BEST!"

Then he took a deep, sleepy breath and gave a soft little trumpet — toot... toot... toooot. ... The birds hummed along softly, and even the old grumpy crocodile smiled.

"Thank you for the game!" the baby elephant called, and he wrapped his long, warm nose around the crocodile's bumpy green back in a big squishy hug.

Off he trotted home, his new trunk swinging proudly side to side. His mama's eyes grew round. "My little one! Look at your nose — long as a rope and wiggling like a garden hose!"

That night, the whole family nestled together on the cool night grass under a sky dotted with tiny white stars, and the baby elephant told them everything. "I am glad," his mama whispered, hugging him close, "that your questions helped you grow."

And high above them, the moon peeked out — round and creamy as a scoop of vanilla ice cream — smiling down at the little elephant, whose new trunk swung and curled like a ribbon in the night breeze.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: What do you think the little elephant's questions helped him grow?
