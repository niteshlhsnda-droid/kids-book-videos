Birbal and the Goldsmith

A clever Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a grand palace in India, there lived a wise king named Akbar. His best friend was a clever man named Birbal. Birbal was kind and very wise.

One sunny morning, a merchant came to the palace. He bowed low before the king.

"My king," said the merchant, "I ordered a gold necklace for the queen from the goldsmith Sona. I gave him fine gold to make it. But the necklace came back light. Some gold is missing! Sona must have taken it."

The king frowned. "Is this true, Sona?" he asked.

The goldsmith Sona stepped forward. "No, my king," said Sona softly. "I did not take any gold. My hands are honest hands. I made the necklace with all the gold I was given."

"Then who took the gold?" asked the king.

Then Birbal smiled his gentle smile. "My king," he said, "let us find the truth with a simple test. Truth can be weighed, just like gold."

Birbal turned to Sona. "Sona, here is a lump of gold," he said. "It weighs just as much as a small apple. Please make a tiny gold bird from it."

Sona bowed. "Yes, Birbal," he said.

Sona went to his little workshop. Clink, clink, clink went his hammer. Tap, tap, tap went his tools. He worked all morning.

By the afternoon, Sona came back. In his hands sat a tiny gold bird. It was so pretty that everyone smiled.

Birbal placed the bird on the palace scales, and the scales balanced perfectly. The bird weighed just as much as the lump of gold.

"See, my king," said Birbal. "Sona's hands are honest. No gold goes missing."

The king nodded. "Then where did the queen's gold go?" he asked.

Birbal turned to the merchant. "My friend," he said kindly, "you told us you gave Sona all the gold. But did you keep some back for yourself?"

The merchant's face turned red. "I... I..." he stammered.

Birbal smiled again. "Let us weigh the gold you kept in your own box," he said.

The guards fetched the merchant's box from his home. Inside lay a small pile of gold. Birbal weighed it on the scales. It was just the amount that was missing from the queen's necklace!

"I am sorry," said the merchant in a small voice. "I kept the gold back. I blamed poor Sona. Please forgive me."

The king looked at Sona. "Sona, you are an honest man," he said. "Your hands are clean, and your heart is true. From today, you will make all the queen's jewels."

"Thank you, my king," said Sona.

And Birbal smiled, because the truth had been found.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Truth can be weighed — an honest worker's hands are clean.
