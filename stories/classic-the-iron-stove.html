The Iron Stove

A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a kingdom far away, there lived a young prince who had been shut inside a great iron stove by a spell.

The stove stood in the middle of a dark forest, and whenever a traveller passed by, a kind voice called from inside: "Please, will you help set me free?"

One day, a princess named Lila was walking through the forest, picking wildflowers, when she heard the voice.

"Who is in there?" she asked.

"A prince," said the voice, "locked in by a spell. To free me, someone kind must stay with the stove, polish it, and speak to it kindly for three days, without once being unkind."

"I can do that," said Lila at once. "Kindness is my favourite thing to do!"

So Lila stayed in the forest. She polished the iron stove until it gleamed in the sunlight. She brought it wildflowers. She told it stories and sang it soft songs.

On the first day, the stove grew warm with happiness. On the second day, it began to hum a little tune. On the third day — creak! — the iron door swung open, and out stepped the prince, smiling and free!

"Thank you, Lila!" cried the prince. "Your kindness broke the spell!"

But just as they were celebrating, a grumpy old troll stomped out of the trees. "Bah!" he grumbled. "That prince was supposed to stay in my stove forever!"

Lila was not frightened. She smiled at the troll and said, "You must be very lonely, living all alone in this forest. Would you like to come to our picnic? We have honey cakes and lemonade."

The troll blinked. No one had ever invited him to a picnic before. His grumpy face softened into a shy smile. "Honey cakes?" he said. "Well... I do like honey cakes."

So the prince, the princess, and the troll sat down together under a big oak tree and shared honey cakes and lemonade. And the troll, who had been grumpy for a hundred years, laughed for the very first time — a great, booming, happy laugh that shook the leaves!

From that day on, the troll was the forest's friend, not its fright. The prince and Lila visited him every week, and the iron stove stood empty in the clearing, polished bright, as a reminder that kindness opens even iron doors.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness can open even the heaviest iron door — and soften even the grumpiest heart.
