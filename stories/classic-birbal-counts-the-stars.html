Birbal Counts the Stars

A witty Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One warm evening, Emperor Akbar sat on his palace terrace, watching the stars pop out one by one — twinkle, twinkle, twinkle.

Beside him stood his clever minister Birbal, stroking his own thoughtful chin.

"Birbal," said the Emperor, stroking his long beard, "tell me — how many stars are there in the sky?"

The courtiers gasped. What a question! The royal astronomer turned pale. The royal mathematician began counting on his fingers — and ran out of fingers.

Birbal smiled his calm, clever smile.

"Your Majesty," he said, "there are exactly as many stars in the sky as there are hairs on my sheep."

The Emperor blinked. "Your sheep?"

"Yes," said Birbal. "I counted every hair myself. If anyone doubts my number, let them count the sheep's hairs — and then they will know the stars too!"

There was a moment of silence. Then the Emperor threw back his head and laughed — HA-HA-HA! — and the whole court laughed with him, even the pale astronomer.

"Birbal!" chuckled Akbar, wiping a happy tear. "No one can count the hairs on a sheep — and no one can count the stars. Your answer is perfect because it cannot be wrong!"

He clapped his hands. Servants came running with a tray of star-shaped cookies, warm and dusted with sugar.

"For the man who counted the stars," said the Emperor, giving Birbal the biggest cookie.

Birbal bowed, took a happy crunch — and shared the rest with all the courtiers.

That night, as Birbal walked home under the twinkling sky, he looked up and whispered, "Good night, stars. You are too many to count — and that is what makes you wonderful."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever answer can turn an impossible question into a shared laugh.
