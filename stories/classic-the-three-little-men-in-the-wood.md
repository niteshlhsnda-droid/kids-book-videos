# The Three Little Men in the Wood

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a little cottage at the edge of a great wood, there lived a kind girl named Mina.

Mina had a stepmother who was not kind at all, and a stepsister named Rupa, who was unkind too.

One cold winter morning, the stepmother said, "Mina, go into the wood and pick me a basket of strawberries!"

"But Mother," said Mina, "it is winter! There are no strawberries in winter!"

"Go anyway," said the stepmother, "and do not come back without them."

So Mina put on her warm shawl, took a little basket and a small piece of bread, and walked into the snowy wood.

She walked and walked through the sparkling snow, until she came to a little clearing, where three tiny men were sitting on a fallen tree.

"Good morning!" said Mina with a smile. "What a cold, beautiful day it is!"

They were no taller than Mina's knee, with long white beards and red pointed hats.

"Good morning to you, kind girl," said the first little man. "Where are you going with your basket?"

"I am looking for strawberries," said Mina, "though I do not think I shall find any in the snow."

The little men smiled at one another.

"Sit with us a little while," said the second little man. "You look hungry."

Mina sat in the soft snow and opened her bread. Before she took a bite, she broke it into four pieces and gave one piece to each little man.

"Thank you for sharing with us," said the third little man. "Because you have a kind heart, we will give you three gifts."

"Whenever you speak," he said, "little gold coins will fall from your lips."

"You will grow more and more lovely every day," said the second.

"And one day," said the first, "a kind king will ask you to be his queen."

Mina thanked them with all her heart. As she walked home, she saw something red under the snow — strawberries! Big, sweet, red strawberries, growing in the middle of winter!

She filled her basket and ran home, and as she told her story, tiny gold coins went plink, plink, plink onto the floor!

The stepmother's eyes went wide. "Rupa!" she cried. "Go into the wood at once! The little men will give YOU gifts too!"

So Rupa marched into the wood with a basket and a big piece of cake. She did not say good morning at all.

"Give me my gifts," she said rudely. "And be quick about it!"

The little men looked at one another sadly.

"Because you spoke unkindly," said the first little man, "whenever you speak, little grey toads will hop out of your mouth — until you say something kind and mean it."

Rupa stamped her foot. "How dare—" she began, and two little grey toads hopped out and sat on her shoes, going, "Ribbit!"

Rupa ran home in tears, and every time she tried to speak, the toads went, "Ribbit!" But Mina put her arms around her stepsister and said, "Do not worry, Rupa. Just try saying something kind."

Rupa took a deep breath. "I am sorry I was rude," she said — and as she spoke, one toad turned into a tiny green leaf and floated away!

"Please forgive me," said Rupa — and another toad turned into a leaf!

"Thank you for helping me," said Rupa — and the very last toad turned into a leaf and danced away on the wind.

From that day on, Rupa tried very hard to be kind. And one fine spring morning, a kind young king asked Mina to be his queen. Rupa came to the wedding in her prettiest dress, saying the kindest words of all, and they all lived happily, and kindly, ever after.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kind words are the sweetest gift of all.*
