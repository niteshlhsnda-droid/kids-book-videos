The Crow and the Pitcher

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

The sun was a big, hot ball in the sky.

High up in the blue sky flew a thirsty crow named Kalu. His wings went flap, flap, flap, and his throat felt scratchy and dry.

"Water," croaked Kalu. "I need water."

He flew over fields of golden wheat and a quiet buffalo pond. But the pond was far away, and Kalu was so tired he could hardly flap.

Then he saw it — a small farmhouse with a sunny front step. And on the step stood a tall clay pitcher.

"Water!" croaked Kalu, and down he swooped.

He landed on the rim of the pitcher and peeked inside. Yes! There was water at the bottom, cool and clear. Kalu stretched his neck down, down, down.

Too low. His beak could not touch it, no matter how long he stretched.

He tipped the pitcher with his wing. Wiggle, wobble — but it was too heavy. Not a drop came out.

"What will Kalu try next?" you might wonder.

Kalu hopped down to the path and looked around. And what did he see? Small, smooth pebbles, shining like marbles in the sun.

A little idea popped into his head — pop! like a soap bubble. Can you guess what his clever idea was?

Kalu picked up a pebble in his beak and flew back to the pitcher. Plop! He dropped it in. Splash!

Then another. Plip!

Then another, and another. Plop-plop-plip!

Each pebble went down, down, down… and each time, the water rose — just a tiny bit. Higher, higher, higher!

At last the water reached the top. Kalu bent down and took a long, cool drink. Gulp, gulp, gulp.

"Ahhh!" he sighed. It was the sweetest drink he had ever tasted.

Kalu was no longer thirsty. He was no longer tired. With a happy flap, he flew up to the big mango tree and settled on a shady branch.

The leaves went hush-hush in the evening breeze, and the birds sang their soft, sleepy song.

Kalu closed one eye… then the other… and dozed off, dreaming of cool water and shining pebbles.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Little by little, patience and a clever idea can fix the biggest problem.
