# Nasruddin Rides His Donkey Backwards

*A funny Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One sunny morning, Nasruddin woke up smiling. "Today is market day!" he said, patting his little grey donkey, Dhanno. "Come on, old friend — off we go!"

He climbed onto Dhanno's back… and sat down facing the TAIL!

That's right — backwards! His nose pointed at Dhanno's wiggly tail, and his back faced the road ahead!

Clip-clop, clip-clop went Dhanno's hooves, down the dusty lane toward the village market.

The village children saw him first. "Look! Look!" they giggled. "Nasruddin is sitting the wrong way! Nasruddin, you are facing the wrong way!"

Nasruddin grinned and waved. "Wrong way? Oh no, my little friends — this is the RIGHT way!" he said. "Dhanno watches where we are GOING. I watch where we have BEEN. Between us, we see EVERYTHING!"

The children laughed and ran along beside him.

Clip-clop, clip-clop. Nasruddin swayed gently in his backwards seat, watching the road behind them. And — oh! — what did he see? A tiny white lamb, all alone, bleating sadly behind a bush. "Baa! Baa!"

"Whoa, Dhanno, stop!" cried Nasruddin. He hopped off, scooped up the little lamb, and looked around. There, at the edge of the lane, stood its worried mother sheep, calling and calling.

Nasruddin carried the lamb to its mother, and she nuzzled her baby with joy. "Baa!" said the lamb, happily this time.

"If I had been facing forward," Nasruddin told the children, "I would never have seen that little lamb!"

The children nodded. Clip-clop, clip-clop. On they went.

Then Nasruddin's backwards eyes spotted something on the road behind them — a soft red scarf, dropped in the dust! He hopped off and picked it up. "Whose is this?" he called.

Old Granny Amma came hurrying up. "My scarf! My best red scarf!" she cried, wrapping it around her shoulders. "Thank you, Nasruddin!"

A little further on — SWOOSH! A gust of wind blew his friend Rahim's round cap right off his head! It tumbled and rolled BEHIND them, down the lane. But Nasruddin saw it at once. "Your hat is escaping, Rahim!" he laughed.

Dhanno trotted back, Nasruddin grabbed the cap, and Rahim clapped it back on his head. "Nasruddin," he said, "your backwards eyes miss nothing!"

By the time they reached the market, the whole village was laughing and clapping. "Nasruddin's backwards ride is the cleverest ride of all!" they said.

Nasruddin bowed from his backwards seat. "Remember, my friends," he said with a wink, "sometimes you must look BACK — to see what, and who, needs your help."

And that evening, as the sun sank low and golden, Nasruddin rode home on Dhanno — still backwards, still smiling — swaying softly to the clip-clop of the hooves.

At home, he curled up in his cozy bed. And that night he dreamed of riding backwards through fields of golden wheat, watching the whole wide world drift softly by… drifting… drifting… to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Looking back helps you remember the way — and help others.*
