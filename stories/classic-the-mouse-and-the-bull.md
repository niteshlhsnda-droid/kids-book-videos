# The Mouse and the Bull

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green meadow, beside a tall oak tree, there lived a big brown bull named Boris.

Boris was the biggest animal in the meadow. He had strong legs, a shiny nose ring, and a voice like thunder. "MOOOO!" he would say, and all the little animals would hop out of his way.

In a tiny hole under the oak tree, there lived a little grey mouse named Milo.

Milo was the smallest animal in the meadow. He had tiny paws, a long thin tail, and a very big curiosity.

One sunny afternoon, Boris lay down under the oak tree for a nap. He closed his eyes. He folded his big legs. And soon he was snoring — HRRR, HRRR, HRRR!

Milo peeped out of his hole. "Oh, what big feet!" he whispered. "I wonder what they feel like?"

Milo tiptoed up to Boris. He sniffed one big foot. Then — NIBBLE, NIBBLE, NIBBLE! — he took a tiny bite of Boris's toe, just to see what it tasted like.

"OUCH!" roared Boris. He jumped up with a snort. "Who bit my foot?"

Milo squeaked and ran. Boris charged after him — THUMP, THUMP, THUMP! — shaking the whole meadow.

But Milo was quick, and Milo was small. He darted left. He darted right. And ZOOM! — he slipped into his tiny hole under the oak tree.

Boris pushed his big nose against the hole. He pushed and he pushed. But his nose was too big, and the hole was too small.

"Come out, little mouse!" bellowed Boris. "I am big and strong, and I will catch you!"

Milo peeped out from the dark. "Please, Mr. Bull," he squeaked. "I am sorry I nibbled your toe. I was only curious. I did not mean to hurt you."

Boris stopped pushing. He looked at the tiny mouse with his big brown eyes. And slowly, he began to laugh.

"HA-HA-HA!" laughed Boris. "You are so small, and you were not afraid of me at all!"

"I was a little afraid," said Milo, smiling. "But being small does not mean being scared."

Boris sat down in the grass. "You know, little mouse," he said, "my ear has been itchy all week, and I cannot reach it with my big hoof. Could you help me?"

"Of course!" said Milo. He climbed up onto Boris's ear and scratched it gently with his tiny paws. Boris closed his eyes and sighed a happy sigh.

"Ahh, that feels wonderful," said Boris. "Thank you, Milo. From today, you are my friend. And whenever you like, you may ride on my back, and I will show you the whole meadow!"

So Milo rode on Boris's back every day. He saw the tall grass, the blue sky, and the whole wide meadow from high up in the air. And Boris always had someone small to scratch his itchy ear.

And whenever a big animal said, "I am too big to need a small friend," Boris would laugh and say, "MOOOO! The smallest friend can help in the biggest way!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The smallest friend can help in the biggest way.*
