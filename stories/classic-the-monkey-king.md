# The Monkey King

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green forest beside a wide, laughing river grew the biggest mango tree in the world.

In that tree lived a troop of happy monkeys, and their king was Monkey King Momo — the bravest, kindest monkey of all.

Every day the monkeys swung through the branches, munching sweet, juicy mangoes. Slurp! Yum!

One morning, a ripe mango fell — plop! — into the river and floated all the way to the king's palace downstream.

"That mango is the sweetest I have ever tasted!" cried the human king. "Bring me more!"

So the king's men marched to the forest with big drums to chase the monkeys away.

The monkeys were frightened. "Oh no! What shall we do?" they chattered.

The river was too wide to jump. The men were coming closer. Boom, boom went the drums!

Then Monkey King Momo had an idea.

He climbed to the tallest branch on the river side and stretched his long, strong body across the water to a tree on the other bank.

"Quick!" he called. "Climb over my back — one by one — like a bridge!"

One by one, the little monkeys scampered across their king's back — patter, patter, patter — safe to the other side.

Last of all came tiny baby Mimi, who was shaking with fear.

"Hold my hand," said Momo softly, and he helped her across, step by careful step.

The human king had come to watch, and his eyes grew wide.

"What a brave and kind king!" he said. "He risked himself to save his people. No one shall ever harm these monkeys again."

And from that day on, the king planted mango trees all along the river, so the monkeys would always have sweet mangoes to eat.

Every evening, Monkey King Momo sat in the mango shade, and his troop groomed his fur and told him how much they loved him.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A true leader cares for others before himself — and courage comes from a loving heart.*
