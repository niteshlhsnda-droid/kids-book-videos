The Crows and the Owl

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a big green forest, there lived two families of birds who never, ever spoke to each other.

The crows woke up at sunrise. "Caw-caw-caw!" they called, black and shiny in the morning light.

The owls woke up at sunset. "Hoo-hoo-hoo!" they called, soft and round in the silvery moonlight.

The crows never visited the dark cave where the owls slept all day. And the owls never visited the sunny treetops where the crows played.

Long, long ago, the crows and the owls had a big quarrel. Nobody remembered quite why — but ever since, the day birds stayed away from the night birds, and the night birds stayed away from the day birds.

Then one evening, a clever old crow named Grandpa Caw said, "This quarrel is silly. I am going to end it."

At dusk, when the sky turned pink and gold, Grandpa Caw flew to the owls' cave. His heart went thump-thump-thump. It takes courage to visit someone who has been angry with you for a hundred years!

Can you feel your heart thumping, little one? Thump-thump-thump.

"Hello, friends," Grandpa Caw called softly. "I come in peace."

The owl chief, Chief Hoot, blinked his big round eyes. "A crow… in our cave? Why have you come?"

"I have come to ask one question," said Grandpa Caw kindly. "Why do we fight?"

Chief Hoot thought and thought. Then he laughed a soft, round laugh. "Hoo-hoo! It began long, long ago, over something very silly. Our grandmothers argued about whose song is sweeter — 'caw-caw' or 'hoo-hoo'!"

Grandpa Caw laughed too. "What a silly thing to fight about! Your hoo-hoo is lovely at night, and our caw-caw is lovely in the morning."

"And do you know what?" said Chief Hoot shyly. "I have always liked your caw-caw in the morning. It tells me the sun is coming."

Then Grandpa Caw had an idea. "Will you meet our chief, Chief Swoop, at sunrise tomorrow? Let us talk, and end this quarrel forever."

At sunrise, the two chiefs met under the tallest tree. Can you picture them — the black crow and the soft brown owl, sitting side by side?

They talked. They laughed at the old, silly quarrel. And they made a promise.

"The forest is big enough for all of us," said Chief Swoop. "Crows will rule the day, and owls will rule the night."

"And at dawn and dusk," said Chief Hoot, "we will greet each other kindly — 'Good morning!' and 'Good evening!'"

From that day on, whenever the sun rose, the crows called out, "Good morning, owls!" And whenever the moon rose, the owls called out, "Good evening, crows!"

And the forest felt warmer, softer, and happier than ever before.

And if you listen at dawn, little one, you can still hear them greeting each other — caw-caw and hoo-hoo, friends forever.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A kind word and a little courage can end even the oldest quarrel.
