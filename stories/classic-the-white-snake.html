The White Snake

A timeless Grimm fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, there was a king who was famous all over the land for being very, very wise.

Every evening, after dinner, the servants brought him a special dish — covered with a silver lid so no one could see inside. And every evening, the king would send everyone away, lift the lid, and eat it all by himself.

Now, the king's young servant, a kind boy named Sam, was very curious. "What could be under that lid?" he wondered. "It must be something wonderful!"

One evening, Sam couldn't resist. When no one was looking, he lifted the silver lid — just a tiny peek.

Inside lay a steaming bowl, and curled in the broth like a white ribbon — a little white snake.

Before Sam could even think, the smell of the broth made his mouth water — and quick as a wink, he tasted one tiny spoonful.

And suddenly — oh, wonder! — the whole world began to TALK!

The sparrows outside the window were chirping, "Cold tonight! Cold tonight! Fluff your feathers!"

The mice in the pantry were squeaking, "Crumbs! Crumbs! Under the third shelf!"

Sam clapped his hands over his mouth. "I can understand the animals!" he whispered. "Every single one!"

Now, soon after this, the queen lost her most beautiful ring, and the king accused poor Sam of stealing it.

"I didn't take it!" said Sam. "But give me one day, and I'll find your ring."

Sam sat in the courtyard, sad and worried. Just then, he heard quacking. The ducks were gossiping by the pond!

"Did you hear?" quacked one duck. "The queen's ring! It's lying in the mud under the big rock — I saw it glinting this morning!"

Sam ran and found the ring. The king was so pleased that he gave Sam a reward — and also a warning: "Leave my kingdom and seek your fortune elsewhere."

So Sam set off on an adventure. On the road, he heard tiny voices crying for help — some fish were flapping in a drying puddle!

"Don't worry!" said Sam. He scooped them up gently and carried them to the river. "Swim free, little ones!"

"Thank you!" bubbled the fish. "We won't forget this!"

Further on, Sam heard an ant king wailing. A horse's hoof was about to squash the anthill! Sam quickly led his horse around it. "Build in peace, little builders!" he said.

"Thank you!" chimed the ants. "We won't forget this!"

Then, near a tall tree, Sam heard sad peeping. Baby ravens had fallen from their nest and were hungry. Sam took bread from his own bag and fed them, crumb by crumb. "Eat up, little ones!"

"Thank you!" peeped the ravens. "We won't forget this!"

At last Sam came to a grand city, where the princess had set three hard tasks for anyone who wished to marry her.

First: find her golden ring, lost in the sea. Sam walked to the shore, sad — but up swam the fish! Down they dived, and up they came with the ring held high.

Second: pick up ten sacks of grain scattered on the meadow before sunrise. Sam sighed — but out marched the ants! All night they worked, grain by grain, and by dawn every sack was full.

Third: bring an apple from the Tree of Life. Sam didn't know the way — but the ravens he'd fed flew ahead, caw-caw-caw, and led him straight to it.

The princess smiled her sweetest smile. "You must have a kind heart," she said, "for the whole forest helped you."

And so Sam married the princess, and they lived happily — with ducks in the pond, ants in the garden, and fish in the river, all of them dear friends.

And on quiet evenings, Sam would sit by the window and listen to the animals' soft bedtime chatter — the gentlest lullaby in the world.

Good night, little one. Be kind to every creature, big and small.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness to every creature, big or small, always comes back to you.
