Ivy the Inchworm and the Ivy Lantern

A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Ivy the inchworm loved to measure everything.

She measured leaves. One inch. Two inch. Three inch. She measured twigs. She measured raindrops. She even measured her own tiny shadow.

"One inch, two inch, three inch," Ivy hummed, as she walked in her slow, wiggly way. Her little body curled up and stretched out, up and down, like a tiny green wave.

One dark evening, the sun went down behind the big ivy wall. The sky turned a soft, sleepy purple. The stars began to peep out, one by one. Ivy's friends were playing hide-and-seek in the ivy leaves.

But then it got too dark to see.

"Where is the way home?" squeaked a little ladybird, bumping into a leaf. "I cannot see the path!"

"I am lost too," buzzed a small bee, rubbing her sleepy eyes. "Everything looks the same in the dark."

Ivy felt a little scared. But she took a big, brave breath. "Do not worry," she said. "We will go home together. Small steps. One at a time. I will measure the way for us."

Just then, Ivy saw a tiny light. It was a glow-worm! The glow-worm glowed soft and green, like a tiny star sitting on the ground.

"Dear glow-worm," said Ivy, "will you help us? My friends cannot find their way home."

The glow-worm glowed a little brighter. "Of course," he said. "I am happy to help. That is what my light is for."

Ivy gathered the soft glow into a little lantern made of leaves. She tied it with a thin strand of spider silk. She hung it from a tiny twig. The lantern glowed warm and gold, like a little moon.

"Follow me," said Ivy. "One inch, two inch, three inch."

Step by step, Ivy led her friends through the ivy. The lantern swayed gently. Its light danced on the leaves. The ladybird followed. The bee followed. They walked in small, safe steps, all together, and nobody was scared any more.

At last, they saw the little red door of home. It glowed in the lantern light, round and warm and welcoming.

"Home!" cried the ladybird. "We are home!"

The friends snuggled into their cosy beds. Ivy put her lantern by the door, so its light would stay soft all night long. The glow-worm curled up beside it, glowing gentle green.

"Thank you, Ivy," whispered the bee. "Your small steps brought us home."

Ivy smiled a sleepy smile. "One inch, two inch, three inch," she hummed, and closed her eyes. The lantern glowed softly, and all the friends slept safe and sound.
