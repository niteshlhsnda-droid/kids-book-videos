# Akbar and Birbal: Who Is Greater?

*A merry Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago in India, there was a great emperor named Akbar.

Emperor Akbar ruled from a grand palace of pink stone, with tall white domes and fountains that went *splash, splash, splash* all day long. He had elephants with painted trunks, horses with silver bells — *jingle, jingle* — and a court full of wise ministers.

The wisest of all was Birbal. Birbal was not big or strong, but he had the quickest, kindest brain in the whole kingdom. Whenever the emperor asked a hard question, Birbal always had just the right answer.

One fine morning, Emperor Akbar sat on his golden throne, munching a juicy mango. *Slurp, slurp.* The whole court sat around him, fanning themselves in the warm air.

Suddenly the emperor clapped his hands. "I have a question!" he announced. "A very big question!"

Everyone sat up straight.

"Tell me," said Akbar, wiping the mango juice from his beard. "Who is greater — the king... or God?"

*Gasp!* went the courtiers. *Oh!* went the ministers. What a question! The king was asking if anyone was greater than himself!

Some courtiers looked at their shoes. Some courtiers coughed. *Ahem, ahem.* Nobody wanted to answer first.

Then Birbal stepped forward, smiling his calm, gentle smile.

"Your Majesty," said Birbal, folding his hands, "what a lovely question! And the answer is simple. God is greater."

"Ho ho!" laughed Akbar. "And why is that, my clever Birbal?"

Birbal smiled and looked up at the blue sky through the open courtyard.

"Because, Your Majesty," he said softly, "you are a great king. But your palace has walls. You look after your kingdom — and you do it wonderfully! But God... God looks after your palace AND the farmer's little hut, AND every sparrow's nest, AND every ant's home — all at the very same time."

The court was very quiet. Even the fountains seemed to go *splash... splash...* more softly.

"And," Birbal went on, "He does it all without asking for anything back. No taxes. No presents. Not even a mango." Birbal winked. "He does it simply out of love."

For a moment, Emperor Akbar said nothing.

Then his round face broke into the biggest, happiest grin. "Ha ha ha!" he laughed, slapping his knee. "Birbal! Birbal! What a wonderful answer!"

All the courtiers laughed too, with great relief. *Hee hee hee. Ha ha ha.* The whole court rang with laughter.

"You are right," said Akbar, nodding his royal head. "A king looks after one kingdom. God looks after everything — the kings, the farmers, the sparrows, and the ants — all at once. That is greater indeed!"

Then Akbar clapped his hands again. "Bring mangoes!" he cried. "The sweetest mangoes in the palace — for Birbal, and for every single person in this court!"

*Plop, plop, plop,* went the golden mangoes onto silver plates. *Slurp, slurp,* went the whole happy court.

Birbal bowed low. "Thank you, Your Majesty."

"You are welcome," said Akbar, tossing him the biggest mango of all. "A king may be great — but a clever, honest minister is a treasure!"

That evening, as the sun melted into the golden sky, the whole court went home full of mangoes and laughter.

And Birbal walked home under the twinkling stars, humming a happy little tune. *Hum, hum, hum.*

For the bravest answer is the true one — and the truest answer is said with a smile.

*Twinkle, twinkle,* went the stars above. *Snore... snore... snore,* went the sleepy palace below.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The bravest answer is the true one — and honesty, said with a smile, makes everyone happy.*
