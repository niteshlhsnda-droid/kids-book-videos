# The Monkey King and the Mango Tree

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green forest beside a wide, slow river stood the tallest mango tree anyone had ever seen.

Its branches stretched up to the sky, and in summer they hung heavy with round, golden mangoes that smelled sweet in the warm sunshine.

In that forest lived a troop of monkeys, and their king was a kind monkey named Milo.

Milo loved mangoes. But what he loved even more was sharing them.

"One for you, one for you, and one for you!" he would call, tossing a soft, ripe mango to every monkey in the troop, from the smallest baby to the oldest grandmother.

Nobody in the forest ever went hungry while Milo was king.

One sunny morning, the mangoes were riper than ever. Milo picked the biggest one, sniffed it, and smiled. "Mmm! Sweet!"

But before he could share it, the mango slipped from his hands. Plop! It fell into the river and bobbed away down the stream, like a little golden boat.

Down, down the river it floated, all the way to a town where a king named Aman lived.

King Aman picked up the mango and took one bite. His eyes went wide. "This is the sweetest mango I have ever tasted! Where did it come from?"

He sent his helpers up the river to find the tree. The helpers came marching into the forest, talking loudly and clapping their hands.

The monkeys were frightened. They chattered and hid behind the leaves. "What do we do? What do we do?"

Milo held up a paw. "Do not worry, my friends. Stay close to me."

But the helpers kept coming closer, and the only safe place was across the wide river.

Milo looked at the river. He looked at the mango tree. Then he looked at his troop — and he had an idea.

He climbed to the top of the mango tree, which stood right at the river's edge, and grabbed a long, strong branch that reached across the water. Then he stretched himself across the river, holding the branch with his hands and planting his feet on the near side of the river.

"Walk across my back, one by one," he called. "Gently now. I will hold steady."

Now — what do YOU think that felt like, walking across a bridge made of your kind king?

One by one, the monkeys crossed. First the babies, holding tight to their mothers. Then the grandmothers, stepping slowly. Then all the rest, pat-pat-pat, across Milo's back.

Milo held steady. His arms ached a little, but his heart felt warm. When the very last monkey was safe on the other side, Milo hopped down and shook his arms with a happy wiggle.

"All safe!" he cried, and the whole troop cheered.

King Aman had been watching from the riverbank. His heart felt soft and sorry.

"Forgive me," he said. "I only came for mangoes, but I see now that this tree is your home. I will not take it from you."

Milo smiled. "Then come and share the mangoes with us," he said.

So King Aman sat down under the mango tree, and Milo tossed him a soft, ripe mango. They ate together, and the king promised to guard the forest and plant new mango trees beside the river.

From that day on, whenever the mangoes ripened, Milo shared them with his troop, with the king, and with everyone who passed by.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind leader thinks of others first, and sharing makes sweet things sweeter.*
