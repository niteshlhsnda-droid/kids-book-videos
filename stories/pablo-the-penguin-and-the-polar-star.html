Pablo the Penguin and the Polar Star

A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Far away, where the snow sparkles white and the sea is soft and blue, lived a little penguin named Pablo.

Pablo was small and round, with a soft black back, a white tummy, and two little orange feet that went waddle, waddle, waddle across the ice.

One evening, Pablo waddled out to watch the sky, and oh, what a wonderful sight — the aurora was dancing! Green and pink ribbons of light swirled above the snow, like a great soft blanket waving in the wind.

"Oh, how beautiful!" whispered Pablo. "The sky is dancing just for me."

But slowly the ribbons began to fade, the green to grey and the pink to pale blue, until the sky was dark and the dancing lights were gone.

Pablo blinked. It was very dark now. He turned around once. He turned around twice. But he could not see the way home.

"Oh no," whispered Pablo. "I have waddled too far, and now I cannot find my family's igloo. It is dark, and I am all alone."

But then Pablo took a deep breath and looked up at the sky.

And there, high above the snow, was one bright, shining star. It was the Polar Star, twinkling kindly, as if it were winking just at him.

"Hello, little star," said Pablo. "Will you help me find my way home?"

The star twinkled, twinkle, twinkle, as if to say, "Follow me, little penguin. Step by step."

So Pablo began to waddle, keeping the bright star just ahead of him. Waddle, waddle, waddle went his little orange feet across the snow.

First he passed the sleeping seals. They lay in a soft heap on the ice, snoring gentle little snores, snore, snore, snore.

Then he climbed over a snowy hill, crunch, crunch, crunch, and at the top he stopped and looked around.

The star was still there, twinkling brightly, and below it, far away, Pablo saw something wonderful — a warm golden light glowing like honey in the dark!

"That is my home!" cried Pablo. "That is my family's igloo! I can see the light!"

He waddled faster now, waddle, waddle, waddle, down the hill and across the flat, snowy ice.

At last he reached the igloo, and the door opened wide. There stood his mother and his father, smiling and holding out their wings.

"Pablo!" they cried. "You are home! We were waiting for you!"

Pablo waddled inside, and oh, how warm and cosy it was! There was a lovely fish supper waiting for him, and a soft nest of feathers, just his size.

"Thank you, Polar Star," whispered Pablo, as he snuggled into his nest. "You were only a little light, but you led me all the way home."

His mother tucked the feather blanket around him, his father sang a soft, sleepy song, and outside the snow fell gently, hush, hush, hush, as the Polar Star twinkled above the igloo, keeping watch all night long.

Pablo closed his eyes and smiled. He was home. He was safe. And he was loved, more than all the stars in the sky.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Even the smallest light can lead you home.
