# Finn the Fox and the Sleepy Stars

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a cozy den under the roots of an old oak tree lived a little fox cub named Finn, with a big fluffy tail and ears like two small leaves.

Every night, Mama Fox sang Finn a lullaby, and Finn fell asleep, warm and snug.

But tonight, Finn's eyes would not close. They blinked. They blinked again. They stayed wide, wide open.

He wriggled and stretched. He fluffed his big tail into a pillow — but his eyes stayed wide, wide open.

"Mama," whispered Finn, "I cannot fall asleep."

"Hush, little one," said Mama Fox softly. "Lie still, and listen to the night."

So Finn lay still on the soft moss at the mouth of the den. The moss felt cool under his chin. He looked up at the sky.

The sky was full of stars — hundreds and hundreds of tiny, twinkling stars, scattered like sugar across a dark blue blanket.

"I know!" said Finn. "I will count the stars! One... two... three..."

But as he counted, something funny happened. The first star gave a tiny yawn — and winked out. Then the second star curled up — and winked out. Then the third, and the fourth...

The fifth star gave such a big yawn that Finn yawned too — a great big yawn. "Excuse me," Finn whispered, and giggled behind his paw.

One by one, the stars were falling asleep!

"Oh!" whispered Finn. "Even the stars are sleepy."

Then the moon leaned a little closer, round and silvery, and began to sing — a soft, slow lullaby, with no words at all. Just "loo... loo... loo..."

The lullaby floated down like silver dust and settled over the den, soft and warm. Even Mama Fox began to hum along, very quietly.

In the branch above, Old Owl folded her wings. Her feathers puffed up round and cozy. "Whoo... goodnight," she murmured, and tucked her head under her wing.

In the bushes, Hazel the hedgehog curled into a prickly little ball. Her prickles settled flat and soft. "Goodnight," she sighed.

In the grass, Pippa the mouse yawned the tiniest yawn. Her whiskers stopped wiggling. "Goodnight," she squeaked, and curled her tail around her nose.

Somewhere far away, a nightingale sang one soft note — and then was quiet.

One by one, the night animals were falling asleep.

Finn felt his eyelids grow heavy — but he wanted to see the last star.

Finn nestled deeper into the warm grass. Above him, only one last star was still twinkling — the littlest star of all. It twinkled... and twinkled... and twinkled... slower... and slower...

And then it winked out too.

"Goodnight, little star," whispered Finn. "Sleep tight."

His tail curled around his nose. His breathing slowed... slowed...

And with the moon's soft lullaby humming all around him, Finn closed his eyes — and drifted off to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Rest comes to those who lie still and listen to the night.*
