# Hans in Luck

*A gentle Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Hans had worked for his master for seven long years, and at last it was time to go home to his mother.

"You have been a good worker, Hans," said his master, and he gave Hans a lump of gold as big as Hans's head. "This is your reward."

Hans thanked him, tied the gold in a cloth, and set off down the road, singing a happy song. But the gold was very heavy, and soon his shoulders ached and his feet dragged.

Clip-clop, clip-clop! Along came a farmer riding a fine horse.

"Good morning!" called Hans. "How lucky you are, riding along while I must carry this heavy gold!"

The farmer smiled. "Would you like to trade? My horse for your gold?"

"Gladly!" said Hans. He handed over the gold and climbed onto the horse. Now he rode like a prince, and the wind sang in his ears.

But soon the horse began to trot faster and faster — trot-a-trot-a-trot! — and bumpety-bump, Hans tumbled right off into the grass.

"Ow," said Hans, rubbing his head. Just then, a boy came along leading a cow.

"Good morning!" said Hans. "How lucky you are! A cow gives sweet milk and never throws you off."

"Trade with me, then," said the boy. "Your horse for my cow."

"Gladly!" said Hans. He led the cow along, dreaming of fresh milk for his mother's breakfast.

But by noon, the sun was hot, and Hans was very thirsty. He tried to milk the cow — but the cow was not in a milking mood, and she gave him a swish of her tail instead.

Just then, a butcher came down the road with a fine, plump pig.

"Good morning!" said Hans. "How lucky you are! A pig needs no milking."

"Trade with me," said the butcher. "Your cow for my pig."

"Gladly!" said Hans, and he trotted off with the pig under his arm, feeling very clever indeed.

A little further on, he met a boy with a white goose under his arm. "Good morning!" said Hans. "How lucky you are! What a fine, fat bird!"

They traded — the pig for the goose. And Hans walked on, hugging his goose and whistling.

At the village pond, Hans stopped to rest. He set the goose down by the water and bent to take a drink. But the goose — honk! — waddled straight into the pond and swam away, flapping its wings.

Hans watched it go. He had no gold, no horse, no cow, no pig, and now no goose.

And do you know what Hans did? He laughed! He laughed and laughed until tears ran down his cheeks.

"I am the luckiest fellow alive!" he cried. "No heavy gold to carry, no horse to throw me, no cow to milk, no pig to feed, no goose to chase — nothing but the open road and my mother's house at the end of it!"

And he skipped all the way home, light as a feather and happy as a lark, and his mother hugged him tight and said he was the wisest trader in the whole wide world.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A light heart is the greatest treasure — happiness is not measured in gold.*
