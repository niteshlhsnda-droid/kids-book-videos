The Hare and the Elephants

A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, beside a big blue lake, there lived a family of little hares.

The hares had soft grey fur and long, wiggly ears, and they loved to hop in the green grass.

Every evening, they nibbled sweet grass and watched the moon rise over the lake.

But one hot summer, a great herd of elephants came to the lake to drink.

The elephants were big, and the elephants were many, and their great feet went thump, thump, thump!

"Thump, thump!" went the elephants, and the little hares' homes were squashed flat.

"Oh dear!" cried the little hares. "Our homes are gone! Our grass is gone!"

"What shall we do?" they asked. "The elephants are so big, and we are so small!"

Then the wisest, oldest hare of all stepped forward. His name was Tinku.

Tinku had white whiskers and kind, twinkling eyes, and he had seen many summers come and go.

"Do not be afraid, little ones," said Tinku. "I have a clever plan."

That night, when the moon was round and bright, Tinku hopped to the lake.

The great elephant king was drinking at the edge of the lake. His name was Gajraj.

Tinku bowed low and said, in his bravest voice, "O great King Gajraj, I bring you a message from the moon!"

"The moon?" said Gajraj, in his deep, rumbly voice. "What does the moon say?"

"The moon lives in this lake," said Tinku, "and she is very upset."

"She says your great feet shake her water and spoil her beautiful home."

"She asks you, most kindly, to drink from another lake, far away."

King Gajraj looked down into the water. There, in the lake, he saw the moon's face, round and bright.

And as his great trunk dipped into the water, the moon's face wobbled and shook.

"Oh!" said Gajraj. "The moon is truly upset! I must not trouble her anymore."

King Gajraj bowed his great head low, low, low, right down to the water.

"Dear Moon," he said, "please forgive me. I will take my herd far away, and we will never trouble you again."

And just like that, the moon's face grew still and calm and bright once more.

"You see?" whispered Tinku. "The moon is happy again!"

King Gajraj lifted his trunk and called to his herd, "Come, my friends! We must find another lake!"

The great elephants turned around, gently, gently, and walked away through the trees.

Thump, thump, thump — softer and softer, until they were gone.

The little hares peeked out from behind the bushes.

"Hooray!" they cried, hopping high into the air. "Our home is safe again!"

That evening, the hares nibbled their sweet grass and watched the moon rise over the lake.

Tinku smiled his wise old smile. "The moon is smiling at us," he said.

"And we are safe, all thanks to a clever plan and a kind heart."

And the moon smiled down on the little hares, all night long.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A wise mind and a kind heart can move even the biggest hearts.
