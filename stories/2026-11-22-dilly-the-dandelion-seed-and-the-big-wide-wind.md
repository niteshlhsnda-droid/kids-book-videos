# Dilly the Dandelion Seed and the Big Wide Wind

In a sunny meadow, where the bees hummed their sleepy songs and the grass tickled the sky, there stood a tall, proud dandelion. On top of it sat a hundred fluffy white seeds, all round and soft as snowflakes. The littlest seed was Dilly, with a tiny brown heart and a crown of feathery fluff.

Dilly loved her home on the tall stem. From up high she could see the whole meadow — the purple clover, the buzzing bees, and the silver brook that sang as it ran. "I never want to leave," she said, holding tight to her stem.

But one morning, the wind came dancing through the meadow, whispering, "It is time to fly, little seeds." One by one, Dilly's sisters let go of the stem and floated away like tiny white parachutes, twirling and laughing in the breeze.

Dilly held on tighter. The sky looked so big and so wide, and the meadow below looked so small. "I'm not ready," she whispered. "What if I fall? What if the wind forgets about me?"

Mama Dandelion bent close, her yellow petals warm as sunshine. "Oh, my little one," she said softly, "the wind never forgets. It is kind, and it will carry you gently. And wherever you land, the earth will tuck you in."

Just then, Ruby the ladybug landed on the stem beside her, her red wings shining in the sun. "I was scared the first time I flew too," said Ruby. "But the sky held me up, just like a soft blanket. Close your eyes, little Dilly, and trust the wind."

Then a big, bouncy gust came rushing through the meadow and tugged at Dilly's fluff. Pop! Her stem let go. Dilly tumbled and spun through the air — oh! She was flying! Up and over the purple clover she went, twirling like a tiny dancer.

Dilly opened her fluffy arms wide, and the wind held her up, soft and sure. She floated over the silver brook, over the garden wall, and past the rooftops, where the windows were starting to glow like warm honey. The whole world twinkled below her.

As the moon climbed into the sky, the wind grew gentle and slow, and it set Dilly down, soft as a feather, in a quiet garden bed. A little rain pattered down like tiny kisses, and the soft, dark earth wrapped around her like a blanket.

Dilly snuggled deep into the warm earth and closed her eyes. In the spring, she would grow tall and green, and one day she would send her own little seeds flying. But for now, it was time to rest and dream of sunshine.

Sleep now, little Dilly, tucked in the soft, dark earth.

The end. 🌙
