# The Dog and His Bone

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a town with twisty little lanes, there lived a happy little dog named Moti.

Moti was brown as toast, round as a bun, and his tail went *waggle, waggle, wag* all day long.

One fine morning, the kind baker of the town gave Moti a present.

"Here you are, Moti," said the baker, smiling. "A big juicy bone — the best bone in the whole town."

"Oh, thank you! Thank you, thank you!" barked Moti, and his tail went *waggle, waggle, wag.*

The bone was long and smooth and smelled like the oven. It smelled like warm dinners and cozy afternoons. It was the nicest thing Moti had ever held.

Moti picked up the bone ever so gently in his mouth. He held it tight and safe. And off he trotted, trot, trot, trot, on his way home.

His path took him over a little bridge. Under the bridge ran a little stream, and the water in the stream went *babble, babble, boop.* Birds sang in the reeds, and the sun made the water sparkle like tiny stars.

Halfway across the bridge, Moti stopped. He looked down at the water.

And guess what he saw?

Another dog! Another dog looking right back at him. And that other dog was holding a bone too.

"Oh!" thought Moti. "That bone looks bigger than MY bone!"

He looked again. Yes, yes, that other bone looked bigger. Much bigger. Round and juicy and oh-so-bigger.

Moti's eyes grew big. His tail stopped waggling. He forgot all about his own fine bone.

"I want THAT bone," thought Moti. "I want the bigger one!"

So Moti opened his mouth — wide, wide, wide — to say "SNAP!" and snatch the bigger bone.

But you cannot hold a bone with an open mouth.

*Plop!* Down went his own real bone into the stream. *Splish! Splash! Splosh!* Away it bobbed, down the stream, gone, gone, gone.

"Oh no!" cried Moti. "My bone! My beautiful bone! Come back!"

But the bone only floated on, round and round, until the stream carried it far away, out of sight.

And the other dog? The other dog with the bigger bone?

There was no other dog. Not really. There was only the water, smooth as a mirror, showing Moti his very own self looking back.

Moti blinked. He blinked again.

"Oh," said Moti, in a very small voice. "That was just... me."

He sat down on the bridge, and his tail went *thump, thump* on the wood. He felt very, very silly. His big juicy bone was floating away, and there was nothing to munch at all.

"Never mind, never mind," he whispered to himself, with a little sniff.

Just then, along came the kind baker with his basket of warm rolls.

"I saw it all, little one," said the baker softly. He patted Moti's soft head. "Here — a warm roll, just for you."

"Thank you," whispered Moti, and munched the warm roll. *Munch, munch, munch.* It was soft and toasty.

"Be happy with what you have, little one," said the baker, with a kind smile. "Wanting more can make you lose it all."

Moti nodded. "Be happy with what I have," he said to himself. "Be happy. Be happy."

So Moti trotted home, trot, trot, trot, and curled up in his cozy corner, all warm and round. His blanket was warm. The moon peeped through the window. He munched his warm roll, *munch, munch, munch,* and his tail went *waggle, waggle, wag* — slow and sleepy.

And that night, Moti dreamed sweet dreams of bones — big bones and small bones, all of them his, all of them wonderful.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Be happy with what you have — wanting more can make you lose it all.*
