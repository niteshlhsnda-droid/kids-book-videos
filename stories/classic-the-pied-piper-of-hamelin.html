The Pied Piper of Hamelin

A gentle world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a little town called Hamelin, there lived kind people, cozy houses, and — oh dear — a great many rats.

Rats in the kitchens! Rats in the cellars! Rats nibbling the cheese, the bread, and the cakes!

"We must do something about the rats!" said the mayor, stamping his foot.

"We have tried everything!" said the baker.

"We have tried traps and cats and brooms!" said the miller.

Just then, down the road came a tall stranger with a twinkling smile and a silver pipe in his hand.

"I am the Pied Piper," he said, "and I can pipe the rats away — with music!"

The mayor clapped his hands. "Wonderful! Pipe them away, and we will give you a grand feast and a bag of gold coins!"

So the Piper stood in the middle of the town square, lifted his silver pipe to his lips, and began to play.

Tra-la-la! Tra-la-loo! The music danced through the streets like a ribbon of light.

And out of the kitchens came the rats. Out of the cellars came the rats. Out of every nook and cranny came the rats — hundreds and hundreds of them!

They followed the music — scamper, scamper, scamper — out of the town, down the hill, and all the way to the big green meadow by the river.

There, the Piper played one last sweet note, and the rats scampered off into the tall grass, to make their new home far, far away from Hamelin.

"Hooray!" cried the people. "The rats are gone!"

But then — oh dear. The mayor forgot his promise. He forgot the feast. He forgot the bag of gold coins.

"Why should we pay?" grumbled the mayor. "The rats are gone, and that is that!"

The Piper was sad. So he lifted his silver pipe once more and played a different tune — a merry, twinkling tune, full of laughter and adventure.

Tra-la-la! Tra-la-loo!

And out of the houses came the children! Out of the schools came the children! Out of every garden came the children — skipping and dancing behind the Piper!

"Wait!" cried the mothers. "Where are you going?"

"We are following the music!" laughed the children. "It is the loveliest music we have ever heard!"

The Piper led the children — dancing, dancing, dancing — all the way to the big green meadow by the river. There they found a wonderful surprise: a meadow picnic! Blankets on the grass, baskets of bread and cheese, and games of every kind.

"This is the best day ever!" cried the children.

The mothers and fathers ran after them. The mayor ran after them too, puffing and panting.

"Oh, Piper," said the mayor, out of breath. "We were wrong. We forgot our promise. Please bring our children home — and we will keep our word!"

The Piper smiled. "A promise is a promise," he said. "And music is best when everyone is happy."

So the Piper played one more tune — a soft, sweet, homeward tune. And all the children skipped home, hand in hand with their mothers and fathers.

That evening, the whole town of Hamelin sat down to a grand feast — bread and cheese and cakes and apple pie. The Piper sat at the head of the table, and the mayor gave him the bag of gold coins.

"From now on," said the mayor, "Hamelin always keeps its promises."

And the Piper played a happy tune, and everyone danced — even the mayor!

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Always keep your promises — a promise is a promise.
