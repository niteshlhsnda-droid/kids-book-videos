Akbar and Birbal and the Farmer's Well

A timeless Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a village not far from the Emperor's palace, there lived a poor farmer named Gopi.

Gopi worked hard in his fields every day. His hands were rough from work, and his smile was wide and warm like sunshine. But his fields were thirsty — the soil lay cracked like a dry biscuit, and his little plants hung their heads, yellow and bending low.

So one day, Gopi took all the coins he had saved and bought an old well from a rich man named Seth.

The well was big and round, with cool water sparkling deep inside. Gopi grinned so wide his cheeks hurt, and hopped happily on the stone rim. Now his thirsty plants could drink the water and grow big!

But the next morning, the rich man Seth came marching to the well. He folded his arms and spoke in a voice as sour as an unripe mango: "I sold you the well, Gopi. But I did not sell you the water! If you want my water, you must pay me for every bucket!"

Poor Gopi's smile faded. He had spent all his coins already. He did not know what to do.

So Gopi walked all the way to the palace, where the marble floor felt cool under his bare feet, and told his sad story to the Emperor, Akbar. Akbar listened carefully. Then he called for his cleverest friend, Birbal.

"Birbal," said the Emperor, "help us make this fair."

Birbal walked up to the rich man with a gentle smile. "Seth," he said, "you say the well belongs to the farmer, but the water inside it belongs to you. Is that right?"

"Yes! That's right — my water!" said Seth, puffing up his chest.

Birbal nodded slowly. "Then it is simple. If the water is truly yours, you must take it out of Gopi's well. Every last drop — tonight!" What do you think Seth did then?

The rich man opened his mouth. Then he closed it. Then he opened it again.

He peered down into the deep well, watching the water sparkling far below. There was water enough down there to fill thousands and thousands of buckets — far more than any man could carry. How could he ever take it all out in one night?

The Emperor smiled, and so did everyone at the palace. And Seth had no answer at all.

With his shoulders hanging low, he turned and walked away — and never came back to the well.

That evening, Gopi lowered his bucket into the well and pulled it up, full of cool water. He drank, and it tasted like honey to him.

But Gopi loved to share, so he did not keep the water for himself. He shared it with his whole village — the grandmother next door, the little children, everyone who was thirsty.

From that day on, the well was always full of water — and the village was always full of smiles.

And when the sun went down, soft and golden, Gopi would sit by his well and listen to the gentle splash of the water. He felt peaceful and glad, and stayed there until the stars came out, one by one.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Clever tricks can never beat simple fairness.
