# The Tiger and the Persimmon

*A beloved Korean folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a little village in Korea, there lived a mother and her small daughter Munni.

One cold winter night, Munni would not go to sleep. She tossed and turned, and she cried, "I am not sleepy! I am not sleepy!"

Her mother rocked her and sang her a song, but Munni still cried. Her mother told her a story, but Munni still cried.

At last, her mother said, "Hush now, Munni! If you do not go to sleep, the tiger will come!"

Outside the house, in the dark forest, a great tiger was prowling. He heard the mother's words, and he licked his lips.

"A child who will not sleep!" thought the tiger. "I shall wait until the house is quiet, and then — yum!"

But Munni was not afraid at all. She cried even louder, "I am NOT sleepy!"

Her mother smiled a tired smile. Then she had an idea. She took a soft, sweet, dried persimmon from the pantry and held it up.

"Munni, look!" said her mother. "If you stop crying and go to sleep like a good girl, you shall have this sweet persimmon in the morning!"

Munni stopped crying at once. She sniffed the sweet persimmon, smiled a sleepy smile, and — in two minutes — she was fast asleep, dreaming sweet dreams.

But outside, the tiger had heard everything. And the tiger did not know what a persimmon was!

"A persimmon!" whispered the tiger, his eyes wide. "It must be a terrible, fierce creature! It made the child stop crying in a single moment! If a persimmon is that scary, I do not want to meet one!"

The tiger's knees began to knock. He backed away from the house, step by step, until — CRASH! — he bumped into a big tree!

Now, in that tree lived a clever thief who had climbed up to hide from the village watchman. When the tiger bumped the tree, the thief lost his balance and fell — plop! — right onto the tiger's back!

The tiger was sure the persimmon had jumped on him! "HELP!" roared the tiger. "The persimmon has got me!"

And off he ran, faster than he had ever run in his life, through the forest and over the hills, with the thief clinging to his back and shouting, "Whoa! Slow down! Whoa!"

The thief was just as frightened as the tiger. "This tiger is wild!" he thought. "I must get off!"

At last, the tiger ran under a low branch, and the thief grabbed it and swung himself up into the tree, safe and sound.

The tiger kept running all the way to the deep mountains, and he never came back to that village again.

In the morning, Munni woke up, rubbed her eyes, and said, "Mother, may I have my persimmon now?"

"Of course, my dear," laughed her mother, giving her the soft, sweet fruit. "And did you sleep well?"

"Yes!" said Munni, munching happily. "I dreamed of a tiger running away from a persimmon!"

Her mother laughed and laughed. "What a funny dream, Munni! And what a brave girl you are — you were not afraid of the tiger at all."

And from that night on, whenever Munni would not sleep, her mother only had to say, "Persimmon!" — and Munni would giggle herself straight to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Things we do not understand can seem scarier than they really are — and a good laugh chases fear away.*
