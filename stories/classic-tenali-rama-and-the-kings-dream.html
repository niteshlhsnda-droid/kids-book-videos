Tenali Rama and the King's Dream

A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago in the kingdom of Vijayanagara, there lived a clever poet named Tenali Rama. He was the king's favourite, because he could make anyone laugh — even on the grumpiest of days.

One morning, King Krishnadevaraya woke up very, very angry. His face was red, and his crown sat crooked on his head.

"Tenali Rama!" he boomed in his biggest, angriest voice. "Last night I dreamed that you slapped me on the cheek! How dare you — even in a dream!"

Can you believe it? The king was truly, truly cross… about a dream!

All the king's helpers held their breath. Nobody said a single word.

Morning sunlight streamed through the tall palace windows, and the cool marble floor shone like water.

But Tenali Rama only bowed low, as still and calm as pond water.

"Your Majesty," he said softly, "what a wonder! For last night, I dreamed that you gave me a hundred gold coins."

The king blinked. "A hundred gold coins?" he said.

"Yes, Your Majesty," said Tenali Rama. "It felt so real — I can almost feel the coins in my pocket right now."

The king looked at Tenali Rama's gentle, smiling face. And slowly — very slowly — his angry frown began to melt, like ice in the morning sun.

First his mouth twitched. Then his shoulders began to shake. And then — ha-ha-ha! — the whole court rang with the king's laughter.

"Oh, Tenali!" laughed the king, laughing so hard that happy tears came to his eyes. "If my dream was only a dream, then so was yours. But your dream has made me so happy that I shall make it come true!"

And he gave Tenali Rama a hundred real gold coins, right there in the court.

That night, the king slept with a smile on his face — and dreamed only sweet dreams.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A kind word and good humor can melt even the biggest anger.
