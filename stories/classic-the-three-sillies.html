The Three Sillies

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a little village with a little red cottage, there lived a mother named Meena and her daughter Ritu.

One evening, Ritu went down to the cellar to fetch a jug of milk. As she walked down the steps, she saw a big wooden mallet hanging on the wall.

"Oh dear," said Ritu, stopping on the step. "What if this mallet falls down one day and bumps my little baby on the head?"

Ritu sat down on the step — and began to cry. "Boo-hoo-hoo!"

Her mother Meena came to see what was wrong. Ritu told her about the mallet, and the baby she did not even have yet.

"Oh dear!" cried Meena. "What if the mallet falls and bumps the baby?" And she sat down on the step — and began to cry too. "Boo-hoo-hoo!"

Soon a young man named Aman came to the cottage. He was kind and clever, and he hoped to marry Ritu one day.

"Why are you both crying?" asked Aman.

Ritu and Meena told him about the mallet, and the baby, and the bump on the head that had not happened at all.

Aman tried not to laugh. But a little chuckle slipped out.

"You are both very silly," he said gently. "But I love you, Ritu, so I will make you a promise. I will travel the wide world, and if I find three people sillier than you two, I will come back and marry you."

"Three sillies?" said Ritu, wiping her eyes. "That is a big promise!"

"It is," said Aman, putting on his walking shoes. "And I will keep it."

So Aman walked down the lane and into the wide world.

First, he came to a village where a man was jumping up and down in a field, trying to jump into his trousers. Jump! Miss! Jump! Miss!

"Why do you not just put them on with your hands?" asked Aman.

"Because," said the man, puffing, "jumping is much quicker!"

Aman smiled. "That is one silly," he said to himself, and walked on.

Next, he came to a farm where a man was pulling a rope, trying to lift a big brown cow up onto the roof of his house.

"Why are you lifting a cow onto the roof?" asked Aman.

"Because," said the man, pulling hard, "there is sweet green grass growing on the roof, and I want my cow to eat it!"

Aman smiled. "That is two sillies," he said to himself, and walked on.

Next, he came to a cottage where a woman was carrying sunshine into her house in a basket. She ran out into the garden, scooped up the sunlight, ran back inside, and tipped the empty basket onto the floor.

"Why are you carrying sunshine in a basket?" asked Aman.

"Because," said the woman, "my house is dark inside, and I want the sunshine to stay!"

Aman laughed out loud. "That is three sillies!" he cried. "Three sillies, sillier than Ritu and Meena!"

So Aman hurried home to the little red cottage. Ritu and Meena were still sitting on the cellar step — but they were not crying any more. They were laughing at themselves.

"You kept your promise!" said Ritu, smiling.

"I did," said Aman. "And now I will keep my other promise too."

So Aman and Ritu were married, and Meena took the wooden mallet down from the wall and put it safely in a box. And whenever anyone in the cottage started to worry about something that had not happened at all, they all laughed together and said, "Do not be silly — be happy!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Do not cry over troubles that have not happened — laughter and a clear head are better than worry.
