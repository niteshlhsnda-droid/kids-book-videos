# Tink the Kitten and the Worry Box

In a little blue house at the edge of a garden lived a kitten named Tink. Tink had soft silver fur, round curious eyes, and a very small worry.

Every evening, before bed, Tink would think about her day. One day she worried that she had spilled the milk. Another day she worried that her friend Sona might not come to play again. And one day she worried that the thunder she had heard would come back in the night.

Each worry was small, like a pebble. But Tink kept them all in a little wooden box under her bed. She pushed each pebble-worry inside and closed the lid very tight, because she thought that was what brave kittens did — they kept their worries to themselves.

But pebbles add up. The box grew heavier and heavier. One night, when Tink tried to fall asleep, she could hear the box creaking under her bed. "That box is getting so heavy," she whispered. She pushed it with her paw. It barely moved. Her worries had grown so heavy that she could hardly sleep at all.

The next morning, Tink's grandmother came to visit. Grandmother had a long, soft tail and kind, crinkly eyes. She saw Tink dragging the heavy box behind her and said, "Little one, what are you pulling?"

"My worry box," said Tink. "I keep my worries in here. But it is too heavy now."

Grandmother sat down beside her. "Would you like to show me one?" she asked gently.

Tink opened the lid a crack. "This one is about the spilled milk," she said. "It has been in there for weeks."

Grandmother smiled. "Oh, little one, the milk was wiped up long ago. That worry doesn't need to live in your box anymore." And as she spoke, the worry lifted out of the box like a soap bubble and floated away through the window.

One by one, Tink showed Grandmother her worries. The one about Sona? Sona was waiting at the garden gate that very morning, calling her name. The one about the thunder? Grandmother explained that thunder was only the clouds talking in their sleep.

With every worry Tink spoke aloud, the box grew lighter and lighter. By the end, it was so light that Tink could lift it with one paw. And it was empty — but it was also, somehow, the prettiest empty box in the whole garden, painted with tiny stars.

That night, Tink put a soft feather in the box instead of a worry. She left it under her bed, light as air. And when a new worry came — as worries sometimes do — she knew just what to do. She would tell someone she loved, and watch it float away like a bubble.

Sleep now, little Tink.

The end. 🌙
