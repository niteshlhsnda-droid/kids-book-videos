# Birbal and the Sticks

*A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in the grand city of Agra, lived the great Emperor Akbar and his clever minister Birbal. Birbal was famous for solving every puzzle with a smile.

One morning, a worried merchant named Seth Ram came to the court. "Your Majesty," he said, bowing low, "someone has taken a bag of my gold coins from my shop! I have four helpers, and I do not know which one took it."

"Do not worry," said Akbar. "Birbal will find the truth."

Birbal called the four helpers to the court. They all looked honest. They all said, "I did not take it!"

Birbal smiled and thought for a moment. Then he said, "Bring me four sticks, all exactly the same length."

The sticks were brought. Birbal gave one stick to each helper and said, "Take these sticks home tonight and keep them safe. Tomorrow, bring them back. The stick of the one who took the gold will grow one finger longer by magic!"

The helpers looked puzzled, but they took their sticks and went home.

That night, three of the helpers slept soundly, their sticks lying beside their beds. But the fourth helper — the one who had taken the gold — lay awake, worrying.

"If my stick grows longer, everyone will know!" he whispered to himself. "I know what to do — I will cut a little piece off my stick, so that when it grows, it will still be the right length!"

So he snipped a tiny piece off the end of his stick, hid the gold bag under his bed, and went to sleep, feeling very clever indeed.

In the morning, all four helpers came back to the court with their sticks. Birbal laid the four sticks side by side on a table.

Three sticks were exactly the same length. But the fourth stick was shorter — shorter by one finger!

Everyone gasped. "How can that be?" asked Akbar.

Birbal smiled. "Your Majesty, sticks do not grow by magic. But a worried heart gives itself away. The one who cut his stick is the one who took the gold."

The fourth helper hung his head. "Forgive me," he said softly. "I took the coins because I wanted new shoes for my little daughter. I am sorry."

Akbar looked at him kindly. "Return the gold," he said, "and say sorry to Seth Ram. And Seth Ram —" he turned to the merchant — "this man was wrong to steal, but his heart was thinking of his daughter. Will you forgive him, if he works honestly to pay you back?"

"I will," said Seth Ram, and he shook the helper's hand.

The helper returned the gold, apologised, and worked hard in the shop from that day on — honestly, and with a smile. And Seth Ram even gave his little daughter a pair of bright new shoes, as a gift.

Akbar laughed and clapped Birbal on the shoulder. "Birbal, your wisdom works like magic — without any magic at all!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A guilty secret is hard to hide, but an honest sorry can make things right again.*
