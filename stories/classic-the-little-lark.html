The Little Lark

A wise Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a golden wheat field, under the warm summer sun, lived a mother lark named Lila.

Lila had built a soft nest of dry grass between the tall wheat stalks. In the nest sat her three tiny chicks — Chip, Chirp, and Chee.

Every morning, Lila flew off to find seeds for breakfast, and every morning she told her chicks, "Stay in the nest, my darlings. Listen carefully, and tell me everything the farmer says."

One morning, the farmer walked into the field with his son.

"Look at this wheat," said the farmer. "It is ripe and golden. It is time to cut it. I will ask our neighbors to come and help me reap it tomorrow."

The chicks listened with wide, frightened eyes.

When Lila came home, the chicks cried, "Mama! Mama! The farmer is coming tomorrow with his neighbors to cut the wheat! We must fly away tonight!"

Lila listened and smiled calmly. "Do not worry, my little ones. If the farmer is waiting for his neighbors, he will not come tomorrow. We are safe. Sleep now."

The next day, the farmer came again — all alone. The neighbors had not come.

"Hmm," said the farmer. "My neighbors are busy. No matter. I will ask my cousins to come and help me reap it tomorrow."

The chicks trembled again. "Mama! Tomorrow the cousins are coming!"

But Lila shook her head. "If the farmer is waiting for his cousins, he will not come tomorrow either. Sleep easy, my darlings."

Sure enough, the next day the cousins did not come either.

On the third day, the farmer stood in the field, looking at his golden wheat. He stamped his foot.

"Enough waiting!" he said. "If I keep waiting for others, my wheat will rot in the field. Tomorrow I will come with my son, and we will reap it ourselves — with our own hands!"

The chicks' eyes grew big. "Mama! This time he said he will do it himself!"

Lila nodded quickly. "Yes! This time he means it. When someone says 'I will do it myself,' it is time to fly. Come, my darlings — tonight we move to the meadow by the river."

That very evening, Lila carried her chicks one by one to a cozy new nest in the soft green meadow.

The next morning, the farmer and his son came with their sharp sickles and cut the golden wheat — but the lark family was already safe and singing in the meadow.

"Remember, my little ones," said Lila as they sang into the sunset, "words are only wind until someone does the work themselves."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Don't wait for others to do the work — what you do yourself truly gets done.
