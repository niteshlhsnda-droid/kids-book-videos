Birbal Catches a Thief

A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in the great city of Agra, there lived a kind merchant with a round tummy and a warm, booming laugh.

One market day, the merchant's bag of gold coins went missing from his shop. Vanished! Gone!

Only three helpers had been near the shop that morning — Gopal, Hari, and little Chotu.

"I did not take it," said Gopal.

"I did not take it," said Hari.

"I did not take it," whispered little Chotu, his eyes wide.

The merchant went straight to Emperor Akbar's court. "Your Majesty, my gold is gone! Please help me."

Akbar stroked his beard and turned to his cleverest minister. "Birbal, what shall we do?"

Birbal smiled his calm, clever smile. "Bring me the three helpers — and three sticks."

When Gopal, Hari, and Chotu arrived, Birbal gave each of them a smooth wooden stick. All three sticks were exactly the same length.

"Tonight," said Birbal, "keep your stick beside your bed. By morning, the thief's stick will grow one whole inch — all by itself!"

The three helpers bowed and went home, each hugging his stick.

That night, Gopal slept soundly. Hari slept soundly. But little Chotu could not sleep at all.

Chotu had taken the bag. And now he was frightened. "If my stick grows an inch by morning, everyone will know!" he thought.

So in the dark, Chotu took a knife and cut one inch OFF his stick. Snip!

Morning came. Birbal lined up the three sticks side by side.

Gopal's stick — just right. Hari's stick — just right. But Chotu's stick was one whole inch SHORTER than the others!

"Chotu," said Birbal gently, "a stick cannot grow. But a guilty heart gives itself away."

Chotu's eyes filled with tears. "I am sorry," he whispered. "I took the bag. Here it is." And he returned every single coin.

The merchant hugged him. "Thank you for telling the truth," he said. "The truth is worth more than gold."

And from that day on, Chotu never took what was not his — and Birbal's clever sticks became the talk of Agra.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A guilty heart gives itself away — telling the truth sets you free.
