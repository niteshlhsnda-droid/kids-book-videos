# Fern the Fox Cub and the Twinkling Star

In a cozy den under the roots of an old oak tree, at the very edge of a wide meadow, lived a small fox cub named Fern. She had soft russet-red fur and a fluffy tail with a cream-white tip that bounced whenever she ran.

Fern loved the daytime. She chased butterflies and splashed her paws in the stream. But when the sun went down and the meadow turned dark and quiet, Fern felt very small. The shadows looked tall. She always wished the day would never end.

One evening Mama Fox nuzzled Fern's ear. "Tonight, little one, we are walking to Grandma Fox's burrow, all the way across the meadow."

Fern's heart went thump-thump. "But Mama," she whispered, "it will be dark."

"Then we will walk by starlight," said Mama, taking Fern's little paw in hers.

They stepped out into the soft, dusky blue evening as the sun dipped behind the hill. Crickets began their evening song, and tiny fireflies switched on their lanterns, blinking hello. Fern stayed close to Mama's fluffy tail.

Then Mama lifted her nose and looked up. "Look, Fern," she said softly. "The first star."

High above the hill, one small star was twinkling, like a tiny lantern in the sky. Mama sat down in the grass and sang in her gentle voice, a song her own mother had sung to her. "Twinkle, twinkle, little star, how I wonder what you are. Up above the world so high, like a diamond in the sky."

Fern stared up at the star. It twinkled again — and this time it seemed to wink right at her. A giggly feeling bubbled up inside her, and she waved her little paw at the sky. "Hello, star," she whispered.

Then more stars popped out, until the sky was sprinkled with twinkling lights, like sugar on dark-blue velvet. An owl hooted a sleepy good-evening from the oak tree, and the moon peeked over the hill, round and golden, like a kind, sleepy face.

Fern looked around in wonder. The night was not empty after all. It was full of quiet friends — the stars twinkling hello, the fireflies dancing with their lanterns, the moon smiling down, and her mama's warm paw in hers. The dark did not feel big and lonely anymore. It felt like a soft, cozy blanket tucked all around the world.

They walked on, and Fern skipped, her cream-tipped tail bouncing. When they reached Grandma Fox's burrow, it was warm and glowing, with honey cakes on a leaf plate.

Later, curled in a soft moss bed beside Grandma and Mama, Fern peeked through the round doorway. There was her star, still twinkling. "Goodnight, star," she whispered. "See you tomorrow night."

Sleep now, little Fern.

The end. 🌙
