# Nasruddin and the Moon in the Well

*A funny Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One soft summer night, Nasruddin — the funny wise man — went to the village well to draw a bucket of cool water.

He leaned over the dark round well — and stopped. Down in the water, something round and glowing was floating.

Nasruddin blinked. He blinked again. "Oh no!" he whispered. "The moon has fallen into our well!"

Have you ever seen the moon's face in water? It looks so round and real, doesn't it?

Poor Nasruddin's heart thumped like a drum. Without the moon, the whole sky would be dark! Without the moon, how would the owls find their way? How would the children see their dreams?

"I must save the moon!" he said, and ran home as fast as his slippers would go — *flip-flop, flip-flop*!

He came back with a long rope and a hook on the end. He lowered the hook down, down, down into the well — *splash!* — and began to fish for the moon.

"Don't worry, little moon," he whispered into the dark. "Uncle Nasruddin is coming to help you."

He tugged and tugged. "Come on, moon! Up you come!" But the moon would not move. It just shimmered and smiled at him from the water.

Nasruddin planted his feet. He pulled harder. He pulled and pulled — and SNAP! The rope broke in two!

Nasruddin tumbled backward onto the soft grass — *thump!* — with his feet in the air and his turban all crooked.

He looked up. And what did he see? The moon! The real moon! Round and golden, smiling safely in the sky, just where it belonged.

Nasruddin sat up. He looked back down into the well — and there was the moon again, smiling up at him from the water, just for company.

He waved at it. "You can stay there, my friend," he said. "But your real home is up in the sky."

Then he scratched his beard. And then he grinned the biggest grin.

"Well," he said, "at least I set it free!"

Just then, the villagers came out to see what all the noise was about. Nasruddin pointed up at the moon. "I rescued it!" he said. "It was stuck in our well, and now it is free!"

And do you know what the villagers did? They did not laugh AT Nasruddin. They laughed WITH him — *hee-hee-hee!* — because his heart was so big and his mistake was so funny.

Grandma Zoya patted his shoulder. "Dear Nasruddin," she said softly, "the moon was never lost. It was only visiting the well's water."

They all sat together on the grass until the stars came out, one by one, like tiny lamps.

And that night, everyone in the village slept under the rescued moon, feeling safe and happy.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sometimes what looks like big trouble is just a reflection — look twice, and laugh kindly.*
