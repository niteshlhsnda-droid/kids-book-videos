# Akbar and Birbal: The Three Questions

*An Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a grand palace with gardens full of roses and fountains, there lived a wise emperor named Akbar.

Akbar loved questions. He loved to ask, "Why is the sky blue?" and "Where does the river go?" And his dearest friend and cleverest minister, Birbal, loved to answer them.

One golden morning, Akbar sat in his garden, watching the sun rise and listening to the birds. A thought floated into his mind, soft as a cloud.

"Birbal," said Akbar, "I have three questions for you today."

Birbal smiled. "Ask away, Your Majesty."

Akbar held up one finger. "First question: Where is God?"

Birbal smiled. "Your Majesty, may I have a cup of milk?"

Akbar laughed. "Milk? For a question about God? Bring Birbal some milk!"

A servant brought fresh, warm milk. Birbal stirred it slowly and said, "Your Majesty, tell me — where is the sweetness in this milk?"

Akbar peered into the cup. "I cannot see it," he said. "But I know it is there, because the milk tastes sweet."

"Exactly!" said Birbal, beaming. "God is like the sweetness in the milk. You cannot point to one little corner and say, 'God is only here.' God is everywhere, in everything, the way sweetness is in every sip."

Akbar clapped his hands. "Wonderful!"

Then Akbar held up two fingers. "Second question, Birbal: What is God doing?"

Birbal said, "Your Majesty, may we churn some butter together?"

Akbar laughed again. "Butter? Very well — bring the churn!"

The servants brought a big clay pot of cream and a wooden churn. Akbar and Birbal took turns stirring, round and round. Slowly, the cream began to change. The rich butter rose to the top, and the thin buttermilk sank to the bottom.

"Look, Your Majesty!" said Birbal. "The churn turns and turns. What was low rises up, and what was high settles down."

Akbar nodded.

"That," said Birbal, "is a little picture of what God does. God gently lifts up those who feel small and low. And God softly reminds those who feel big and proud to stay kind and gentle. Like the churn, God keeps the whole world turning, kindly and fairly."

Akbar smiled. "Another lovely answer, Birbal."

Then Akbar held up three fingers. "Last question, and the hardest one. What does God eat?"

Birbal said, "Your Majesty, let us go to the little temple at the edge of the garden."

So the two friends walked to the little temple, where a poor old woman was placing a single flower and a small bowl of rice before the little statue of God, with folded hands and a heart full of love.

"See her offering?" said Birbal softly. "It is small — one flower, a little rice. But her heart is so full of love that the offering is richer than a king's feast."

Akbar watched, and his eyes grew gentle.

"God," said Birbal, "does not hunger for feasts or sweets or gold. God eats the love in every offering. When you give with love — even a single flower, even a kind word — God tastes the love, and that is the sweetest food."

Akbar was quiet for a moment. Then he smiled a wide, warm smile. "Birbal," he said, "you have answered all three questions beautifully."

That evening, Akbar and Birbal sat in the garden as the sun went down, sharing mangoes and watching the fireflies dance. And Akbar thought how lucky he was to have a friend who could make big questions feel as simple and sweet as milk, and butter, and a single flower.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: God is everywhere, lifts the lowly with kindness, and loves the love in every gift.*
