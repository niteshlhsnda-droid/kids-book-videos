The Wise Little Girl

A timeless Russian folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a snowy village in Russia, there lived two brothers.

One brother was rich. He had a big house, a fat horse, and a cart full of shiny coins.

The other brother was poor. He had a small house, an old coat, and a little daughter named Anya, who had the brightest eyes in the whole village.

One spring morning, the poor brother's mare had a little foal — a soft, wobbly-legged baby horse with a white star on its forehead.

But the foal had been born under the rich brother's cart, which was standing in the yard.

"That foal is mine!" cried the rich brother. "It was born under my cart!"

"No!" said the poor brother. "It is my mare's foal!"

They argued all morning. At last they went to the Tsar, the ruler of the land, to ask him to decide.

The Tsar listened, and he stroked his long beard. "I will set you a riddle," he said. "Whoever answers it shall have the foal. Tell me — what is the fastest thing in the world? What is the softest thing in the world? And what is the dearest thing in the world?"

The rich brother went home and asked his wife, his friends, and his servants. But no one knew the answers.

The poor brother went home, sad and tired, and told his little daughter Anya.

Anya smiled. "Father, don't worry," she said. "I know the answers."

The next morning, the two brothers stood before the Tsar.

The rich brother said, "The fastest thing is my horse! The softest thing is my feather pillow! The dearest thing is my gold!"

The Tsar shook his head. "No," he said. "That is not right."

Then little Anya stepped forward. She was small, but her voice was clear as a bell.

"Your Majesty," she said, "the fastest thing in the world is a thought — for in one moment, a thought can fly all the way to the stars. The softest thing in the world is a baby's hand — for nothing is softer than a little hand holding yours. And the dearest thing in the world is sleep — for after a long day, nothing is dearer than closing your eyes and dreaming."

The Tsar's eyes went wide. Then he smiled a big, warm smile.

"Little one, you are wise indeed!" he said. "The foal belongs to your father — for it is his mare's baby, and a wise heart has spoken the truth."

He gave the foal to the poor brother. And he gave Anya a plate of honey cakes and a soft red ribbon for her hair.

That evening, Anya rode home on the little foal, her ribbon fluttering in the wind. The white star on the foal's forehead shone like a tiny moon.

And from that day on, whenever the Tsar had a hard question, he would smile and say, "Ask the wise little girl — she will know!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A wise heart sees what is true — and even the smallest voice can speak the wisest words.
