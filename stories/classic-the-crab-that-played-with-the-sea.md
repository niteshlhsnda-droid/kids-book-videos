# The Crab That Played with the Sea

*A Just So story by Rudyard Kipling, retold for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, when the world was still brand new and everything was soft and dreamy, there lived a great big Crab. His name was Pau Amma.

Pau Amma lived in the middle of the sea, and he was huge — as big as a small mountain. His claws were as wide as doorways, and his shell was smooth and shiny.

And Pau Amma had a favorite game.

He loved to play with the sea.

*Up!* he would push with his great claws, and the water would rush up and up and up.

*Down!* he would press, and the water would sink down and down and down.

*Up, down. Up, down.* All day long, Pau Amma played with the sea, and the sea went *whoosh, whoosh* — rushing in, rushing out, in a hurry, in a flurry.

But the sea's wild splashing made the rivers jump their banks. The little fish could not rest. The turtles could not sleep.

Now, high in the mountains lived the Eldest Magician, the wisest of them all. When he saw the poor tired fish and the splashy, sploshy sea, he sent his magic down the rivers.

First, he asked the kind Man and his little daughter for help.

"Will you go and talk to Pau Amma?" he asked. "Ask him to play gently with the sea."

The Man's daughter was brave and kind. She had a long braid and a smile like sunshine. She made a beautiful net of soft silk, fine as spiderwebs, and she walked all the way to the middle of the sea.

"Dear Pau Amma," she said in her softest voice, "the Eldest Magician says the sea is too tired to play so roughly. Please... play gently?"

But Pau Amma was having such fun. He waved his great claws.

"Bah!" he said. "I am the biggest crab in the sea. I play how I like!"

And he pushed the sea *up, up, up* — *whoosh!* — and pressed it *down, down, down* — *splash!*

The little daughter was not angry. She just smiled, and she let her silken net float down onto the water.

*Flutter, flutter,* went the silken net, soft as a feather.

Then the Eldest Magician called to Pau Amma in a voice like deep water.

"Pau Amma," he said, "if you will not play gently, then you must sleep."

And the Eldest Magician's magic lifted the middle of the sea into a great big hill — *swish!* — and set it down upon Pau Amma, gentle as a blanket, tucking him into the seabed.

Pau Amma's great claws settled. His shiny shell rested. And he fell into a deep, deep sleep.

"Shhh," said the sea, softly. "Shhh."

The fish uncurled and drifted off to sleep. The turtles yawned big turtle yawns and dozed.

But the Eldest Magician was kind. He did not want the sea to be still forever.

So he called to the Moon, bright and round in the night sky.

"Dear Moon," he said, "twice a day, will you pull the sea softly up? And twice a day, will you let it sink softly down?"

The Moon smiled a silver smile and said, "I would love to."

And so, ever since that day, the Moon pulls the sea *up* — gently, gently — and the sea comes tiptoeing up the beach. *Shush... shush...*

And the Moon lets the sea *down* — softly, softly — and the sea slips back again. *Shush... shush...*

That is why we have tides, little one. Twice a day, in and out, in and out, like the whole sea is breathing.

*Shush... shush... shush...*

Can you hear it? That is Pau Amma dreaming deep below, and the Moon watching over him, and the sea rocking gently, just like a cradle.

*Shush... shush... shush...*

Time to sleep, little one. The sea is sleeping too.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Big strength is best used gently — and everything rests easier when we play softly.*
