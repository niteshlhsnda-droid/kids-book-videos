The Brahmin and the Three Rogues

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a small green village, there lived a kind Brahmin named Dev.

One morning, Dev went to the market to buy a goat for a feast, to share a good meal with his family.

He found a fine, soft, white goat. "Just the goat for us," he said with a smile.

Dev paid, lifted the goat onto his shoulders, and set off for home along the dusty road.

Under a big banyan tree by the road, three rogues sat chatting. When they saw Dev with the goat, their eyes lit up.

"Look at that fine goat," whispered the first rogue. "Let us trick him and take it for ourselves."

The first rogue ran ahead and stood in the middle of the road.

"Good morning, Brahmin!" he called out. "Why are you carrying a dog on your shoulders?"

Dev laughed. "A dog? My friend, this is a goat! A fine, soft goat!"

The rogue shrugged. "Oh, if you say so," he said, and walked away.

Dev walked on, but a small thought buzzed in his head like a little bee. *A dog? No. It is a goat.*

A little further on, the second rogue waited by the road.

"Good morning!" he called. "Why are you carrying a dog on your shoulders?"

Dev frowned. "It is not a dog! It is a goat!"

The second rogue shook his head. "Are you sure? It looks just like a dog to me."

Now the little thought buzzed louder. Dev looked at the goat. Surely it was a goat?

Then, near the edge of the village, the third rogue waited, looking very worried.

"Oh no!" he cried. "A dog on your shoulders! What will your family say?"

Dev's heart sank. Three people had told him the same thing. *Could they all be wrong? Could I be wrong?*

He looked at the goat once more, and doubt clouded his eyes.

With a sigh, Dev set the goat down under a tree and walked home, sad and confused.

The three rogues laughed quietly, took the goat, and shared it among themselves.

When Dev reached home, his wife asked, "Where is the goat, dear?"

Dev told her everything — the three men, the dog, the doubt.

His wife listened, then laughed a soft laugh. "Oh, Dev!" she said. "Three words cannot change what your own eyes saw. You bought a goat. You held a goat. It was a goat all the way!"

Dev thought about it. She was right — his eyes had seen a goat, and his hands had felt one. Only the words had made him doubt.

"I will not be fooled again," he said. "Next time, I will trust my own eyes."

That evening, the family shared a simple meal of rice and lentils, laughing and talking, and Dev felt warm and happy inside.

And from that day on, if anyone tried to trick him with words, Dev would smile and say, "My eyes know what they see."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Trust your own eyes — repeated words cannot change what is true.
