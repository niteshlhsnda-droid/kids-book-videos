The Mouse Merchant

A clever Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Snuggle in close, little one. Tonight I will tell you about a clever young man named Rahul — and a mouse no bigger than the tip of your thumb.

Rahul was poor. He lived in a little hut with mud walls and a thatch roof at the edge of the village, and his pockets were always empty. One day, he walked to the noisy market — all clattering pots and the sweet smell of spice dust — to ask the wise old merchant for advice.

"Dear sir," Rahul said, "I have nothing. Not one coin, not one seed. How can I ever earn money of my own?"

The old merchant smiled, and his eyes twinkled. "Even the tiniest beginning can grow into something wonderful," he said, "if you are clever, and honest, and willing to work."

Just then, he saw something tiny lying on the market road — a little mouse, no bigger than the tip of your thumb, that had quietly gone to sleep forever. Nobody wanted it. People stepped around it and wrinkled their noses.

But Rahul remembered the merchant's words. He picked the mouse up carefully with a leaf. "This is my tiny beginning," he whispered.

Now — what do you think a clever boy could do with a tiny mouse?

Rahul did not throw the mouse away. He carried it to a kind farmer who kept a cat. "Would your cat like a little gift?" Rahul asked politely. The farmer laughed at the clever idea, and traded Rahul a small handful of warm, roasted grain. Picture the cat now — licking its whiskers, round and content, purring like a little motor: rrrr.

Rahul nibbled a few grains himself — they were warm and crunchy — and saved the rest. Then he sat by the road, ground the grain into fine flour between two smooth stones, and mixed it with water.

He baked little flat cakes over a crackling fire. Oh, they smelled like warm toast on a cold morning! A hungry traveler's tummy went rrrumble — louder than a little drum! He stopped, took one look, and traded a small bundle of sticks for two cakes.

Rahul carried the sticks to the market, where a potter needed firewood for his oven — and gave Rahul a small clay pot, still cool from the kiln and smelling faintly of wet earth, in exchange. Rahul filled the pot with sweet well water and sold it, cup by cup, to thirsty shoppers on a hot day.

Step by step, trade by trade, his tiny beginning grew. His one clay pot became two clay pots, the two pots grew into a basket of mangoes, and the basket of mangoes became a little wooden cart with a red cloth the color of sunset fluttering over the top.

Close your eyes and imagine all the clever little trades in between — how many do you think it took?

It took many days of hard work, because good things take time. But at last, Rahul had a real little shop at the corner of the market. It had jars of grain and pots of honey that smelled warm and sweet, and a painted sign that read "Rahul's Corner." People came from all around, because Rahul was fair and kind.

The wise old merchant visited one day. He looked around the shop — the honey jars glowing warm and golden in the lamplight — and chuckled. "And to think," he said softly, "it all began with a mouse nobody wanted."

That night, Rahul closed his little shop and sat on his doorstep. The market was quiet. A gentle breeze rocked the little cloth roof of his shop, and the stars came out one by one.

He thought of the mouse, the grain, the cakes, the pots, and the mangoes — every small, honest step that had led him to his cozy little shop. He felt warm and thankful, all the way down to his toes.

So you see, little one, even the smallest chance can grow into something wonderful, when you are clever, honest, and willing to try.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Cleverness and honest hard work can turn the smallest chance into something wonderful.
