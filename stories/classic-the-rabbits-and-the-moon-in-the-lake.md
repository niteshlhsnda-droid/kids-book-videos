# The Rabbits and the Moon in the Lake

*A gentle Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a soft green valley, beside a clear blue lake, there lived a family of little rabbits.

The littlest rabbit was named Rosy, with soft grey fur and long pink ears.

Every morning, the rabbits hopped out of their burrows to nibble sweet green grass.

But one hot summer, the rain did not come, and the little streams dried up.

Then, one dusty afternoon, a great herd of elephants came down the hill.

At the front walked King Ello, the biggest elephant of all.

"We are so thirsty," said King Ello, in his deep, rumbly voice. "We have walked a long, long way. Please let us drink from your lake."

The rabbits trembled. "If all the elephants come to the lake every day," whispered Rosy, "they will splash and stomp, and our soft burrows will fall in!"

Grandpa Hop, the old rabbit, nodded. "We must think," he said. "A clever plan is better than a loud fight."

That night, Rosy sat by the lake and looked up at the sky. The round moon was rising, soft and silver.

Suddenly, she had a bright, moonlit idea!

The next morning, Rosy hopped up the hill to meet King Ello.

"Dear King Ello," she said, in her bravest little voice, "the lake belongs to the Moon."

"The Moon?" said King Ello, surprised.

"Yes," said Rosy. "Every night, the Moon comes down to sleep in our lake. If you splash and stomp, you will wake the Moon, and the Moon will be very sad."

King Ello looked puzzled.

"May I see the Moon in the lake?" he asked.

"Come tonight," said Rosy. "Come quietly, and you will see."

That night, the moon rose round and bright. King Ello tiptoed down to the lake, as quietly as a great elephant can.

He looked into the still water — and there it was! The Moon, smiling up at him.

"Oh!" said King Ello, softly. "The Moon really does sleep here!"

Just then, Rosy hopped beside him and whispered, "Please do not wake the Moon. Drink only a little, and walk very, very softly."

King Ello nodded his great head. He drank one gentle sip of cool water.

But then he lifted his trunk — and SPLASH! It touched the water, and the Moon wobbled!

"Oh no!" cried King Ello. "I have woken the Moon! I am so sorry, dear Moon. I am so sorry, little rabbits."

"Do not worry," said Rosy, kindly. "The Moon is not angry. But now you know — this lake is a quiet, sleepy place."

King Ello bowed his great head low. "I am sorry I frightened you," he said. "From now on, we will drink from the big river instead. We will leave your lake soft and still."

The rabbits whispered, "Hip, hip, hooray!"

King Ello led his herd away, walking softly down the hill.

Rosy smiled at the moon. "Thank you, Moon," she whispered.

That night, the rabbits hopped home to their burrows, safe and happy.

The moon shone down, soft and silver, and the whole valley fell asleep under its gentle glow.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever, gentle idea can solve a big problem — and saying sorry makes everything soft again.*
