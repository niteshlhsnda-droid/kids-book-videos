# The Puddle Mirror

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

After the rain stopped, the street was full of puddles.

Little Zinnia put on her yellow boots.

Splish, splash, went her boots.

She felt joyful.

Then she found the biggest puddle of all.

It was round and smooth like a plate.

Zinnia looked down.

"Oh!" she gasped.

She could see herself in the puddle.

"Hello, me," she giggled.

She felt silly.

Then she looked closer.

She could see the sky in the puddle too.

And a fluffy cloud.

And the top of the big tree.

"The puddle is a mirror," said Zinnia.

"A mirror, a mirror, a mirror," she said.

That was three times she said it.

She felt amazed.

Zinnia had an idea.

She lay on her tummy at the edge of the puddle.

Careful not to get wet.

She watched the cloud drift across the puddle sky.

"Where are you going, cloud?" she whispered.

The cloud did not answer.

It just floated by, slow and white.

Then a bird flew over.

Zinnia saw it in the puddle first.

A real bird, flying in the puddle sky!

She felt surprised and happy at once.

"Look, Mama!" she called. "There is a whole sky down here!"

Mama came and looked.

"You are right," said Mama. "A tiny sky, just for you."

Zinnia made a face at her puddle self.

Her puddle self made a face back.

She stuck out her tongue.

Her puddle self stuck out its tongue too.

She felt giggly all over.

Then the sun came out fully.

The puddle began to shrink.

"Where is it going?" asked Zinnia.

She felt a little sad.

"Back up to the sky," said Mama.

"To become a cloud again."

Zinnia waved at the puddle.

"Goodbye, mirror," she whispered.

"Goodbye, goodbye, goodbye."

That was three times again.

She felt peaceful.

That night, Zinnia looked out her window.

She wondered if her puddle was a cloud now.

Sleep now, little splasher.

Dream of tiny skies in silver puddles.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Look closely, and ordinary things hold whole worlds.*
