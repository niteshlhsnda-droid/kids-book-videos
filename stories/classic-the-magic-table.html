The Magic Table

A Brothers Grimm fairy tale, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, there lived a poor old carpenter with three sons — Aarav, Vihaan, and Kabir.

"I have nothing to give you but my blessing," the old father said one day, "so go out into the world, learn a good trade, and come back to me."

Aarav became a carpenter like his father. When his training was done, his kind master gave him a wonderful gift — a plain wooden table with magic words.

"Just say, 'Table, be set!'" said the master, "and the table will cover itself with the tastiest dinner you can imagine."

Aarav hurried home. On the way, he stopped at an inn for the night. The innkeeper rubbed his hands together when he saw the magic table, and his eyes gleamed. While Aarav slept, the innkeeper swapped it for an ordinary table that looked just the same.

Poor Aarav reached home and cried, "Table, be set!" — but nothing happened. His old father patted his shoulder. "Never mind, my boy. You are home, and that is feast enough."

Next, Vihaan the miller came home, leading a funny grey donkey. "Watch this!" said Vihaan. "Bricklebrit!"

The donkey sneezed — ACHOO! — and out tumbled shiny gold coins, clinkety-clink!

But Vihaan had stopped at the same greedy inn. And the innkeeper had swapped the magic donkey for an ordinary one. When Vihaan said "Bricklebrit!" at home, the donkey only sneezed — ACHOO! — and no coins came out at all.

"Never mind, my boy," said the old father, hugging him. "You are home, and that is treasure enough."

Then came Kabir, the youngest, who had learned to shape wood on a spinning wheel. His master had given him a bumpy wooden stick in a sack, with the magic words, "Stick, out of the sack!"

"Stick, out of the sack!" said Kabir — and out hopped the stick, which began to dance a jolly jig and tickle everyone it touched! The old father laughed until tears rolled down his cheeks.

But Kabir had heard about the greedy innkeeper — and Kabir had a plan. Can you guess what it was?

That night at the inn, Kabir put the stick's sack beside his bed and pretended to sleep. Sure enough, in tiptoed the innkeeper, reaching for the sack.

"Stick," whispered Kabir, "out of the sack!"

Out hopped the stick — and gave the innkeeper a soft tickle-tap on the shoulder.

"Oh! Enough, enough!" chuckled the innkeeper, sitting up. "I will give back the table and the donkey — and I will never steal again."

So the innkeeper returned the magic table and the magic donkey, and promised never to steal again.

Kabir brought everything home. That evening, the old father said, "Table, be set!" — and the table was piled high with steaming curries, warm bread, and honeyed sweets. Vihaan said, "Bricklebrit!" — and gold coins danced across the floor. And Kabir's stick did a happy jig in the corner, tickling nobody at all.

They feasted together, laughed together, and the old father's heart was fuller than his pockets had ever been.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Cleverness and honesty together can set right any wrong.
