# The Eagle and the Jackdaw

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High up on a rocky mountain, there lived a great eagle named Ellie.

Ellie was big and strong, with wide brown wings and sharp, clever eyes. She could soar high above the clouds and swoop down as fast as the wind.

One morning, Ellie swooped down to the green valley below. She saw a little lamb standing in the meadow, soft and woolly and white.

"Would you like a ride, little one?" asked Ellie kindly.

The little lamb nodded. So Ellie picked him up very, very gently in her strong claws, flew him high above the valley so he could see the whole world — the river, the trees, the tiny farmhouses — and then set him down softly back in the meadow, safe and sound.

"Thank you!" bleated the little lamb happily.

Now, sitting on a fence nearby, a jackdaw named Jax was watching. Jax was a small black bird with a grey hood, and he was very proud of himself.

"Ha!" said Jax. "If an eagle can carry a lamb, so can I! I am just as clever as any eagle!"

So Jax fluffed up his feathers, spread his wings, and swooped down at a big, fluffy ram standing in the meadow.

"Here I come!" cried Jax.

He grabbed the ram's woolly back with his claws — and oh no! His claws got all tangled up in the thick, curly wool!

The ram shook and shook. "Baa! Baa!" But Jax could not get free. His feet were stuck fast in the wool, and his wings flapped and flapped, but he could not fly away.

Just then, the shepherd came walking over the hill. He saw the little jackdaw all tangled up in the ram's wool, and he laughed a kind laugh.

"Oh, you silly little bird," said the shepherd. "You are not an eagle. You are a jackdaw!"

He gently untangled Jax's claws from the wool and set him down softly on the grass. Then he carried him home to show his children.

"Look, children," said the shepherd. "This is Jax the jackdaw. He tried to do what an eagle does!"

The children laughed, and they gave Jax some crumbs and a little bowl of water. Jax ate the crumbs and felt a little bit silly — but he was safe, and he was glad.

"An eagle is an eagle," thought Jax, "and a jackdaw is a jackdaw. I do not need to be an eagle. I just need to be the best jackdaw I can be."

That evening, Jax flew home to his nest in the old oak tree. The sun went down, soft and golden. And Jax tucked his head under his wing and fell asleep, dreaming of the sky — his own sky, just right for a jackdaw.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: You do not need to copy others — just be the best you that you can be.*
