# The Doves and the Hunter

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High above the green forest, a flock of soft grey doves sailed through the morning sky. Flap-flap-flap went their wings, all in a row, like a ribbon dancing in the wind.

Their leader was Coco, the wise old dove-king. He had the softest coo and the kindest eyes, and every dove in the flock trusted him with all their heart.

Each morning he led them over rivers and fields, showing the young ones where the sweetest berries grew.

One sunny morning, Coco spotted something glittering far below. "Look, my friends!" he cooed. "Golden grains, scattered on the green grass!"

Can you see them, little one? The grains shone like tiny suns, and they smelled warm and sweet, like fresh bread from an oven.

The doves' tummies rumbled. Rumble-rumble. Down they swooped — swoosh! — and landed beside the golden grains.

But the moment their feet touched the ground — SNAP! A great heavy net dropped over them all!

"Oh no! We are trapped! Trapped!" cried the doves. They fluttered and flapped and pushed, but the net would not move. It was heavy, heavy, heavy. The little doves huddled close together, shivering.

Coco took a deep, slow breath. "Calm, my friends. Stay calm," he said in his steady, soft voice. "One dove alone cannot lift this net. But ALL of us together? Let us beat our wings as one."

So the doves stopped fluttering. They took a breath together. And then — flap-flap-FLAP! — every wing beat at the very same time. The net trembled… it swayed… and then it lifted, just a feather's width!

Can you flap your arms along with them? Flap-flap-FLAP!

Slowly, slowly, the net began to rise. Up went one corner… up went another… and UP they all flew, carrying the great net with them, high into the soft blue sky!

Far below, the hunter scratched his head. "My net is flying away!" he cried. But the doves were already far too high to catch.

Coco led them to a quiet corner of the forest, where the old peepal tree stood. Under its roots lived his dear old friend — Tino, the kind little mouse, in a cozy little home with a round door and a soft leaf bed.

"Help, Tino! Please help us!" cooed the doves.

Tino looked up with his bright black eyes. "Don't worry, friends," he squeaked. "Nibble-nibble-nibble!" His tiny teeth went to work on the net's knots.

Nibble… nibble… nibble… One knot came loose. Then another. Then another.

One by one, the doves stepped out of the net. They stretched their wings, every one of them, and smiled their soft smiles.

"Thank you, dear Tino!" said Coco, bowing low. And every dove bowed low too.

That evening, as the first stars came out — twinkle, twinkle — the doves flew home together, safe and sound and very, very happy.

The end. 🌙

*Moral: Together we are strong — unity can lift any trouble.*
