The Crows and the Snake

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

High in a mango tree, where the leaves smelled sweet and the wind hummed softly, Kiki and Kuku the crows had built the coziest nest in the world.

It was round and soft, lined with feathers, and in it lay three tiny eggs — white as moonlight, and very, very precious.

"Shh," Kiki would whisper, looking at the eggs. "I will keep you safe and warm, my little ones."

But one dark night, while the crows were asleep, a long green snake named Slink slid up the tree — sss-sss-sss — and stole one of the eggs away.

In the morning, Kiki counted. "One… two… THREE? No — one egg is gone!"

The next night, Slink came again. Sss-sss-sss. Another egg was gone.

Kiki fluttered her wings. "If this goes on, we will have no babies at all! What shall we do?"

Kiki had a friend — Hara, the old grey heron, the wisest bird at the lake.

"Caw! Hara!" cried Kiki, flapping down beside him. "A snake is stealing our eggs, and our beaks are too small to fight him. Oh, Hara — help us!"

Hara thought for a long, long time. Then he stood up very tall and said, "Your beaks are too small to fight him. But your wings can trick him. Listen carefully…"

That afternoon, by the river, a shepherd boy was giving his goats a bath. He had taken off his shiny gold bangle and laid it on the riverbank — glint, glint, glinting in the sun.

Kiki swooped down, quick as a wink, and picked up the bangle in her beak.

"Hey! My bangle!" cried the shepherd boy. He chased after her.

But Kiki was faster. She flew straight to the mango tree. And — plip! — she dropped the bangle right beside the dark hole where Slink the snake lived.

The shepherd boy came running, huffing and puffing. He picked up his bangle. And then he saw the snake's eyes, gleaming in the hole.

"A snake!" he shouted. He ran to fetch his friends from the field, calling, "Come and see! Come and see!"

All that noise was too much for Slink. He slithered quietly out of the hole and slid away through the grass — over the meadow, past the river, and far, far into the hills.

And he never, ever came back.

That night, Kiki and Kuku tucked their one last egg under their warm wings. And it was safe, and it was warm, and all was quiet.

Then they sang the softest lullaby.

"Thank you, Hara," they whispered. "Our beaks are small, but our plan was big."

Hara yawned a great heron yawn, folded one leg up like a sleepy stork, and went back to sleep in the moonlit lake.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever plan wins over a strong enemy.
