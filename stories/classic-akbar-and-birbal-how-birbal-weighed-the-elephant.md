# Akbar and Birbal: How Birbal Weighed the Elephant

*A merry Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, there lived a kind and curious king named Akbar. He lived in a grand palace with marble courtyards and gardens full of marigolds.

One sunny morning, a friend from far away sent Akbar a gift. A wonderful elephant! The elephant was big and round and gray, with gentle eyes, skin wrinkly like an old tree trunk, and a trunk that swished back and forth. Swish, swish, swish.

Akbar was delighted. "What a fine fellow!" he said, and fed the elephant a sweet, round melon. The elephant trumpeted happily. *Toot-toooot!*

That afternoon, Akbar gathered all his courtiers in the great hall, where soft footsteps echoed on the cool stone floor. "Tell me, wise men," said Akbar, stroking his beard, "how heavy is this elephant?"

The courtiers scratched their heads. They scratched and scratched.

"Oh no," said one. "No scale in all the kingdom is big enough!"

"Oh dear," said another. "We would need a scale as big as a house!"

They hummed and hummed. Some of them glanced sideways at Birbal, the king's cleverest friend, and smiled sneaky little smiles. *Birbal won't know either,* they thought. *He won't know either.*

But Birbal just smiled. He looked at the elephant. He looked at the river Yamuna, shining silver beyond the palace gates. And he had an idea.

"Your Majesty," said Birbal, bowing low, "please fetch me the biggest boat in the kingdom."

"A boat?" said Akbar, surprised. "What will you do with a boat?"

"You shall see," said Birbal with a twinkle in his eye. "You shall see."

So the servants brought the biggest boat in the kingdom. It bobbed on the Yamuna river. Bobble, bobble, bob.

Birbal gently led the elephant onto the boat. The elephant climbed aboard with a happy *toot-toooot!* The boat sank a little deeper into the water under his great weight.

"Now," said Birbal, "watch the water." He took a piece of charcoal and drew a careful mark on the side of the boat — exactly where the water touched it. A neat black line, all the way around. Can you picture it in your mind?

Then Birbal led the elephant back onto the riverbank, where a keeper gave him a whole bunch of ripe bananas. *Munch, munch, munch.* The elephant waved his trunk with joy.

"Now," said Birbal, clapping his hands, "bring sacks of grain! Heavy sacks, many sacks!"

The servants carried sack after sack onto the empty boat. Thump, thump, thump. The boat sank deeper and deeper. Birbal watched the mark on the side, closer and closer to the water.

"Stop!" he called out, just as the water touched the charcoal line again. "Just there! Perfect!"

Akbar leaned forward. "Birbal, what does it mean?"

Birbal grinned. "Your Majesty, the boat sinks to the very same mark. That means the sacks of grain weigh exactly as much as the elephant. Now weigh each sack of grain on a small scale. Add all the weights together — and you will know the weight of the elephant!"

The courtiers gasped, and their sneaky little smiles went plip! — all gone.

Akbar clapped his hands and laughed with delight. "Ha ha ha! Well done, Birbal! Well done!"

And do you know what Akbar did next? He gave Birbal a necklace of pearls. And he gave the elephant another bunch of bananas — the sweetest, ripest bananas in all the kingdom. The elephant munched them happily, swishing his trunk. Swish, swish, swish.

That night, the moon rose over the Yamuna. The elephant slept soundly in his cozy stable, dreaming banana dreams. And in the palace, everyone was smiling, because clever Birbal had solved the biggest puzzle of all — the weight of an elephant.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Clever thinking can solve even the biggest problems.*
