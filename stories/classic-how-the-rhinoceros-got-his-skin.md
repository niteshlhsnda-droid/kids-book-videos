# How the Rhinoceros Got His Skin

*A playful Just So story by Rudyard Kipling, retold for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a warm, sunny land, there lived a rhinoceros named Rocky.

Rocky was big and grey, with a horn on his nose and a very rude way of behaving. He never said please. He never said thank you. And he always took what he wanted.

Every morning, Rocky stomped to the little cake shop of Rustom the baker. Rustom baked the softest, sweetest cakes in the whole land.

"Give me a cake!" said Rocky, pushing his big nose through the window.

And every morning, kind Rustom gave him a cake. Rocky ate it in one big gulp — GULP! — and stomped away without a single thank-you.

One day, Rustom said to himself, "Rocky must learn manners. A rude rhino is not a happy rhino."

So Rustom baked a very special cake. He filled it with tiny, tickly cake crumbs — crumbs that would make Rocky itch and scratch, just a little, until he learned to say please.

The next morning, Rocky stomped to the shop. "Give me a cake!" he said.

"Here you are," said Rustom, smiling. "And today, the cake is extra sweet!"

Rocky gobbled the cake in one big gulp — GULP! — and stomped away.

But soon, his tummy began to feel funny. "Itch, itch, itch!" said Rocky. "Something is tickling me from the inside!"

Rocky scratched his tummy. He scratched his back. He scratched his ears. But the tickle would not go away!

"Oh, this is dreadful!" cried Rocky. He ran to the nearest tree and rubbed his back against it — RUB, RUB, RUB! — until his smooth grey skin became all wrinkly and folded.

"My skin!" cried Rocky. "Now it is all wrinkles!"

Rustom the baker walked over, smiling gently. "Rocky," he said, "those wrinkles are a reminder. A reminder to say please, and to say thank you, and to be kind."

Rocky looked at his wrinkly skin. Then he looked at kind Rustom. And his big grey face turned a little bit pink.

"I am sorry, Rustom," said Rocky, in his quietest voice. "Please — may I have a cake? And thank you — thank you for always sharing your cakes with me."

Rustom smiled his warmest smile. "Of course, Rocky. And here is a cake just for you — soft, sweet, and made with kindness."

Rocky ate the cake slowly this time, enjoying every bite. "Mmm," he said. "This cake tastes even better when I say please and thank you!"

From that day on, Rocky always said please and thank you. And his wrinkly skin stayed with him, to remind him — and everyone else — that good manners feel better than a stolen cake.

And that is how the rhinoceros got his skin.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Good manners feel better than a stolen cake — please and thank you make everything sweeter.*
