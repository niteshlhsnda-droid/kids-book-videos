# The Lark and Her Young Ones

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a wide, golden wheat field, a mother lark named Lila had built her nest.

The wheat stood tall all around, and inside the soft nest lay her three tiny chicks — Pip, Pop, and little Piu. They were fluffy and warm, and they loved to hear their mother's sweet morning song.

One day, the farmer walked through the field, looking at the ripe wheat.

"Tomorrow," he said to his son, "I will ask our neighbors to come and help us cut this wheat."

That evening, Lila told her chicks what she had heard.

"Oh no, Mother!" cried Pip. "Will we have to fly away?"

"Not yet, my dears," said Lila in her calm, soft voice. "Go to sleep."

Days passed, and the wheat was still standing. The farmer came again.

"Our neighbors are too busy," he sighed. "Tomorrow I will hire some workers to help me cut the wheat."

"Oh no, Mother!" cried Pop. "But where would we even go?"

"Not yet, my dears," said Lila, tucking them under her soft wing. "Sleep now."

"Peep," said little Piu, snuggling deepest of all.

More days passed. Then the farmer came once more — and this time there was no sigh in his voice. It was low and steady, like he had already decided.

"The wheat must come down," he said. "Tomorrow my son and I will cut it ourselves."

Lila listened — and this time she stood up straight.

"Now, my dears," she said softly, "now we must go. When someone says others will do the work, it may never get done. But when he says, 'I will do it myself' — then it really will be done, my dears."

That very night, under the silver moon, Lila flew with Pip, Pop, and little Piu to a new nest in the meadow, far from the wheat field.

And the next morning, as the sun rose, the farmer and his son cut the wheat far away — while Pip, Pop, and little Piu slept safe and snug in their new nest in the meadow.

The end. 🌙
