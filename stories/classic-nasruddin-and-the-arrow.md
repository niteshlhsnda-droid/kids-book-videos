# Nasruddin and the Arrow

*A timeless Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a cheerful village with a market square and a tall mulberry tree, there lived a funny, kind man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin loved to make people laugh. He laughed at his own mistakes most of all, and that made everyone love him even more.

One sunny morning, the village held an archery contest in the meadow. All the best archers came with their bows and arrows, and a big round target was painted on a board.

"Whoever hits the middle of the target wins a basket of sweet figs!" cried the village elder.

One by one, the archers took their turns. TWANG! went their bows. THUNK! went their arrows. Some arrows landed near the middle, and everyone cheered.

At last it was Nasruddin's turn. He picked up his bow, closed one eye, took a deep breath — and TWANG!

His arrow flew up, up, up… and landed far away in a haystack, nowhere near the target at all!

Everyone held their breath. Would Nasruddin be upset?

But Nasruddin just grinned. He walked over to the haystack, took out a pot of paint, and painted a big round target right around his arrow!

Then he turned to the crowd and bowed. "Look!" he said proudly. "A perfect hit! Right in the middle of my own target!"

For a moment, everyone was quiet. Then the whole meadow burst into laughter — HA HA HA! — even the village elder laughed until his sides ached.

"You are the funniest archer in the village, Nasruddin!" cried a little girl, wiping tears of laughter from her eyes.

Nasruddin laughed too, and he shared his figs — for he had brought a basket of his own — with everyone in the meadow.

"My friends," he said, smiling, "sometimes we miss the target we aimed for. But if we can laugh at ourselves, we never really lose."

And that evening, as the sun went down golden over the meadow, everyone agreed: Nasruddin had not won the archery contest, but he had won the best prize of all — a meadow full of laughter.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When you miss, laugh kindly at yourself — laughter is a prize too.*
