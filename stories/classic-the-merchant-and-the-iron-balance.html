The Merchant and the Iron Balance

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a busy little town, lived a kind merchant named Nikhil.

Nikhil sold pots and pans, ribbons and rice, and everything in between. His shop smelled of fresh spices, and his smile was as warm as the morning sun.

One day, Nikhil had to travel far away to buy new goods for his shop.

"I need someone to look after my big iron balance," said Nikhil. "It is heavy and precious. It weighs all my rice and spices."

His friend Farhan lived next door. "Leave it with me," said Farhan with a smile. "I will keep it safe until you come home."

So Nikhil left the iron balance with Farhan and set off on his journey.

Many weeks passed. Nikhil came home with new ribbons and rice, and he hurried to Farhan's house.

"My friend," said Nikhil, "please give me back my iron balance."

Farhan scratched his head and looked at the floor.

"Oh dear," said Farhan slowly. "I am sorry, Nikhil. The mice ate your iron balance."

"The mice ate it?" said Nikhil. "Mice do not eat iron!"

"These mice were very, very hungry," said Farhan, trying not to smile.

Nikhil did not shout. He did not argue. He just nodded and said, "What a pity. Well, these things happen."

Farhan felt very pleased with himself. He had kept the iron balance, and he thought his clever lie had worked.

The next morning, Nikhil met Farhan's little son, Ayaan, on his way to school.

"Ayaan," said Nikhil kindly, "does your mother know you may play by the river until sunset?"

"Yes!" said Ayaan happily. "Mother said I may play by the river until sunset!"

"Then would you like to come and see the river with me today? There are ducks to feed and boats to watch."

"Oh yes, please!" said Ayaan.

So Ayaan went with Nikhil to the river. They fed the ducks, they watched the boats, and they shared sweet oranges. When the sun began to set, Nikhil walked Ayaan safely home.

But when they reached the house, Farhan came running out, waving his arms.

"Where is my son?" cried Farhan. "Where is Ayaan?"

Nikhil looked very sad. "Oh, my friend," he said slowly, "a great hawk swooped down from the sky and carried Ayaan away."

"A hawk carried away my son?" cried Farhan. "Hawks do not carry away little boys!"

Nikhil smiled gently. "And mice do not eat iron balances," he said. "Yet you told me the mice ate mine."

Farhan's face turned red. He understood at once.

"Forgive me, Nikhil," said Farhan. "I told a silly lie, and I kept your balance. You have taught me a lesson without shouting a single word."

Farhan hurried inside and brought out the big iron balance, shiny and safe. Then he hugged his son Ayaan tight.

"From today," said Farhan, "I will always tell the truth, even when it is hard."

And the two friends laughed, shook hands, and shared sweet oranges under the evening sky.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A lie may seem clever, but the truth always finds its way home.
