# Thumbelina and the Swallow's Song

In a sunny garden lived an old woman who wished for a tiny child. A fairy gave her a tiny barley seed. She planted it in a flowerpot and watered it.

That night, a tulip opened with a soft pop. Inside sat a tiny girl, no taller than her thumb, with golden hair and bright blue eyes. The old woman named her Thumbelina, and loved her dearly.

Thumbelina slept in a walnut shell, under a violet petal blanket, and sailed a blue bowl in a tulip-petal boat. She sang sweet songs, and the garden birds stopped to listen.

But one summer evening, a big old toad hopped by as Thumbelina slept among the flowers. "What a pretty little thing," he croaked. He carried her to a lily pad in the middle of the pond.

Thumbelina was frightened and lonely. But the little fishes had heard her songs. They nibbled the lily pad's stem until it floated free, and Thumbelina drifted away on her leaf boat.

Autumn came, and the leaves turned gold and red. Cold and hungry, Thumbelina knocked at a field mouse's door under the cornfield. The mouse took her in, gave her warm soup, and let her stay all winter. She thanked her with songs each evening.

The mouse had a neighbour, a mole who lived in a dark tunnel and never saw the sun. He liked Thumbelina's singing, and wished her to stay underground with him for ever. She smiled, but her heart sank. She loved the sky, the flowers, and the birds' songs.

One day, in the mole's tunnel, Thumbelina found a swallow lying still and cold on the ground. His wing was hurt, and he could not fly south. Gently, she covered him with dry grass, brought him crumbs and water, and sang softly until he fluttered his wing.

All winter, in secret, Thumbelina cared for the swallow. When spring came, he was strong again. "Little friend, you saved me," he sang. "Climb onto my back, and I will carry you where flowers bloom all year."

Up, up, up they flew, over the sea, to a land of white flowers and golden light. There lived a tiny prince with clear wings, who smiled when he heard her sing. He gave her shining wings of her own, and a home in the prettiest white flower.

Thumbelina sang her sweetest song, and the flower folk gathered round to listen. She was no longer lonely, or afraid. She had found a place where her small, kind heart could shine.

That night, Thumbelina curled up in her flower, the swallow singing a soft lullaby beside her, and the stars twinkling above.

Sleep now, little Thumbelina.
