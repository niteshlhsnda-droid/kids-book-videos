Nasruddin and the Key

A timeless Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One soft evening, Mullah Nasruddin came home from the market with a basket of warm bread.

Nasruddin reached into his pocket for his door key.

He felt in both pockets. No key.

He looked in his basket, under the warm bread. No key.

"Oh dear," said Nasruddin. "Where has my little key gone?"

At the corner of the street stood a tall lamp, glowing warm and bright.

"Ah," said Nasruddin, "the light is much better over there."

So he walked to the lamp and began to search the ground on his hands and knees.

He looked all around the lamp post.

Just then, his neighbour, kind old Fatima, came walking by.

"Nasruddin," she said, "what are you looking for under the lamp?"

"I have lost my key," said Nasruddin, "and I am looking for it here."

"Did you lose it here?" asked Fatima.

"No," said Nasruddin, "I lost it near my door. But it is dark over there, and the light is so much better here."

Fatima laughed.

"Oh, Nasruddin," she said. "You are a funny man. Come, I will help you look. But we must look where you lost it, not only where the light is bright."

Soon little Yusuf came by, carrying a small lantern that glowed like a firefly.

"What are you looking for?" asked Yusuf.

"My door key," said Nasruddin. "It is small and silver, and it opens my little blue door."

"Then let us look by your door," said Yusuf, holding his lantern high.

So the three friends walked back to his little blue door.

Yusuf held the lantern up over the doorstep.

Fatima looked by the flower pots.

Yusuf looked under the doormat.

Nasruddin looked behind the big clay pot by the door.

And there it was!

The small silver key was lying beside the clay pot, twinkling in the lantern light.

"My key!" cried Nasruddin. "I must have dropped it when I came home with the bread."

He put the key in the lock, turned it gently, and the little blue door swung open.

"Come in," he said. "Let us share my warm bread and some sweet tea."

They sat on soft cushions by the window.

Nasruddin broke the bread into three pieces and poured sweet tea into three little cups.

"Thank you for helping me," said Nasruddin. "I looked where the light was bright because it was easy."

Fatima smiled. "That is what neighbours are for, Nasruddin."

"And the key was waiting for you all along," said Yusuf, "right by your door."

"You are both right," said Nasruddin, laughing.

Outside, the moon rose over the rooftops.

The lamp at the corner glowed softly, like a sleepy star.

Nasruddin yawned a small, sleepy yawn and waved goodnight to his friends.

"Goodnight, my friends," he said. "Thank you for the light and the laughter."

Then he locked his little blue door, curled up in his soft bed, and fell asleep with a smile, dreaming of warm bread and kind neighbours.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: It is easy to look where the light is bright, but wiser to look where you lost what you love.
