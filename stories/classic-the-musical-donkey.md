# The Musical Donkey

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Gadha the Donkey lived in a village, where he carried heavy sacks of grain all day — sackcloth rough on his back, and the dusty smell of wheat in his nose. But deep inside, Gadha was an artist. "I have the most beautiful voice in the whole village!" he told everyone. "One day, the whole world will hear me sing!"

One night, a thief came sneaking into the village. He piled stolen grain sacks on Gadha's back and whispered, "Quiet now! Not a sound, or we will both be caught!"

They tiptoed past the sleeping houses. And Gadha felt a song rising up in his throat.

"Oh, what a lovely night for a SONG!" said Gadha.

"NO!" hissed the thief. "If you sing, the villagers will wake up!"

"But my throat is all tickly!" said Gadha. "A great singer has to SING!"

And before the thief could stop him, Gadha lifted his nose to the moon and sang his loudest song. What do you think it sounded like? … HEE-HAW! HEE-HAW! HEE-HAWWW!

Windows banged open one by one! Little squares of yellow lamplight spilled across the dark road, and sleepy voices shouted, "A thief! Catch the thief!" The villagers came running out.

The thief dropped the sacks and ran into the dark. Gadha the donkey stood in the middle of the road, blinking in the lamplight.

The villagers laughed and laughed. "What a singer!" they chuckled, patting Gadha. "But little donkey — even the sweetest song has its time. Midnight, when everyone is asleep, is NOT it!"

Gadha drooped his long ears. "You're right," he said softly. "From now on, I'll sing only in the daytime — when everyone is awake to enjoy it!"

And he did. Every morning, Gadha sang his bright braying song — HEE-HAW! — under the warm golden sun, and everybody in the village smiled. For even the sweetest song knows the right time to sing.

Goodnight, Gadha. The end. 🌙
