# Birbal and the Ring

*A timeless Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a grand palace with tall white pillars, there lived a wise emperor named Akbar.

Akbar loved wisdom. And nobody in his palace was wiser than his friend Birbal.

One morning, a merchant named Kabir came to the palace, holding his hands tight with worry. "Emperor Akbar!" he cried. "Something terrible has happened! My precious ring is gone!"

The ring was gold, with a ruby that glowed like a tiny sunset. Kabir had kept it safe in a small wooden box in his shop.

"Who could have taken it?" asked Akbar.

Kabir hung his head. "Only my three helpers were in the shop yesterday — Ria, Dev, and Tara. One of them must have taken it. But I do not know which one, and I do not want to blame the wrong person."

Akbar nodded. "Then we will ask Birbal."

Birbal listened to the whole story, stroking his chin. Then he smiled his quiet, clever smile.

"Bring me three sticks," he said, "all exactly the same length."

The palace gardener brought three straight sticks, each as long as Birbal's arm.

Birbal gave one stick to Ria, one to Dev, and one to Tara.

"Listen carefully," said Birbal. "These are special sticks. Tonight, keep your stick beside you as you sleep. By morning, the stick of the one who took the ring will have grown a little longer — all by itself!"

Ria, Dev, and Tara looked at one another with wide eyes. Then each of them went home with their stick.

That night, Ria placed her stick beside her bed and went straight to sleep. Tara did the same.

But Dev could not sleep at all. He tossed and turned. He kept peeking at his stick.

Dev was the one who had taken the ring. He had slipped it into his pocket when no one was looking, and now his heart was thumping with worry.

"What if my stick really grows?" he whispered to himself. "Then everyone will know!"

So, in the middle of the night, Dev took a tiny pair of scissors and snip! He trimmed a little bit off the end of his stick. "Now it will look normal in the morning," he thought. Then he hid the ring under his pillow and tried to sleep.

The next morning, Ria, Dev, and Tara came back to the palace, each holding their stick.

Birbal laid the three sticks side by side. Ria's stick was just the same. Tara's stick was just the same. But Dev's stick was shorter than the other two!

Birbal looked at Dev kindly. "Dev," he said softly, "your stick did not grow. You trimmed it because you were afraid it would. That tells me you took the ring."

Dev's eyes filled with tears. He pulled the gold ring from his pocket and held it out to Kabir.

"I am so sorry," said Dev. "I saw the ring shining, and I wanted it. I was wrong to take it. Please forgive me."

Kabir took his ring back. "I forgive you, Dev," he said. "Thank you for telling the truth."

Akbar clapped his hands. "Well done, Birbal! You did not shout or punish anyone. You used wisdom — and the truth came out on its own."

Dev never took anything that was not his again. And from that day on, whenever anyone in the palace had a puzzle, they said, "Ask Birbal!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The truth has a way of showing itself, and saying sorry makes things right again.*
