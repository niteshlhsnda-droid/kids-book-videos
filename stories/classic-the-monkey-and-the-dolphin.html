The Monkey and the Dolphin

A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, a little monkey named Chiku lived in a tall green tree beside the sea.

One day, Chiku climbed onto a big wooden ship to look for bananas. "Ooh! Ooh!" he chattered. "So many ropes! So many sails!"

But the ship began to sail away — splash, splash, splash — out into the deep blue sea!

"Oh no!" cried Chiku. "Take me back! Take me back!"

Then a great wave came — WHOOSH! — and washed poor Chiku right off the ship and into the sea.

Chiku splashed and spluttered. "Help! I can't swim!"

Just then, a kind grey dolphin named Dolly swam up beside him. "Hold on, little friend!" she said. "Climb onto my back, and I will carry you to the shore."

Chiku climbed onto Dolly's smooth, shiny back. "Thank you, thank you!" he said.

As they swam along, Dolly leapt over a wave — splash! — and Chiku laughed and held on tight. A school of little silver fish darted past them, sparkling in the sun like tiny mirrors.

"This is wonderful!" cried Chiku. "I have never ridden on a dolphin before!"

Dolly smiled. "I thought you were a sailor from the ship," she said. "Tell me, do you know the great city of Athens?"

Now, Chiku had never been to Athens. He had never even left his tree! But he wanted to sound very clever.

"Oh yes!" he said, puffing out his chest. "I know Athens very well! And my best friend there is a man named Piraeus!"

Dolly stopped swimming. She looked at Chiku with her big, kind eyes.

"Little monkey," she said gently, "Piraeus is not a man. Piraeus is the name of the harbour where the ships come in. You have never been to Athens at all."

Chiku's cheeks turned pink. "I… I made it up," he whispered. "I'm sorry, Dolly. I only wanted you to think I was clever."

Dolly smiled a soft dolphin smile. "A little lie can sink a friendship," she said. "But a sorry can mend it. Hold on tight."

And Dolly swam on through the blue waves — swish, swish, swish — all the way to a warm, sandy shore.

"There you are," she said, setting Chiku gently on the sand. "Next time, just tell the truth. The truth is always the best story."

Chiku waved and waved until Dolly was a tiny grey speck on the sea.

"Thank you, Dolly!" he called. "I will always tell the truth from now on!"

And he ran home to his tall green tree — a little wiser, and a lot more honest.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A lie can sink a friendship — the truth is always the best story.
