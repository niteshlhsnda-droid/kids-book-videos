Tenali Rama and the Persian Horse

A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in the grand kingdom of Vijayanagara, there lived a wise king named Krishna.

King Krishna loved horses. And more than anything, he wished for a fine white Persian horse — the fastest, gentlest horse across the sea.

One day, the king called his three courtiers. "Take these bags of gold," he said, "and bring me a white Persian horse."

The courtiers bowed low. "At once, Your Majesty!"

Months passed. Then a whole year. But no horse came clip-clopping into the palace.

"Where is my horse?" asked the king.

The first courtier shuffled his feet. "The ship sank, Your Majesty."

The second courtier looked at the floor. "The horse ran away, Your Majesty."

The third courtier just smiled a slippery smile and said nothing at all.

Now, the king's cleverest minister was a witty man named Tenali Rama, with twinkling eyes and a quicker tongue.

Tenali Rama stepped forward and bowed. "Your Majesty, give ME some gold, and I will bring you a white Persian horse."

The king gave him a bag of gold. Tenali Rama went home — and spent the days eating mangoes and napping in the sun.

After a month, the king asked, "Tenali Rama, where is MY horse?"

Tenali Rama smiled his brightest smile. "Your Majesty, my horse is traveling with your courtiers' horses. They will all arrive together!"

The whole court went very, very quiet.

Then the king began to laugh — a big, booming laugh that shook the palace windows. "HA! Tenali Rama, you have caught them with words!"

The three courtiers turned as red as ripe tomatoes. One by one, they returned every single gold coin.

Tenali Rama returned his bag too — not one coin missing. "I only borrowed it to borrow the truth," he said with a wink.

And the king? He used the gold to buy a REAL white Persian horse — and he let Tenali Rama name her.

Tenali Rama named her Moonlight. And on soft evenings, the king rode Moonlight slowly around the palace gardens, smiling all the way.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever word can catch what sharp eyes miss.
