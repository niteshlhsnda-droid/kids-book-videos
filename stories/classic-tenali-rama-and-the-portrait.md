# Tenali Rama and the Portrait

*A clever Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

King Krishnadevaraya was a kind and mighty king, but he had one eye that did not see as well as the other. He loved art, and one day he called all the painters of his kingdom to the palace.

"Paint my portrait," said the king, "so that everyone can see how handsome their king is!"

The painters were worried. If they painted the king exactly as he was, with one eye different from the other, would the king be angry? If they painted him with two perfect eyes, would that be a lie?

One painter painted the king with both eyes wide open, exactly as he was. The king frowned. "You have made me look odd," he said.

Another painter painted the king with both eyes perfect and sparkling. The king frowned again. "You have not painted the truth," he said.

Then it was Tenali Rama's turn. Tenali was the king's cleverest courtier, and he loved to paint too. He bowed low and said, "Your Majesty, please give me one day."

The next morning, Tenali brought his portrait to the court and uncovered it with a flourish. Everyone gasped — it was beautiful!

Tenali had painted the king sitting proudly on his horse, holding a bow and aiming an arrow at a distant target — with one eye closed, just as any fine archer would!

"Wonderful!" laughed the king. "You have painted me as a brave archer, and my eye looks closed because I am aiming! It is true, and it is handsome at the same time!"

All the courtiers clapped and cheered. The king hung Tenali's portrait in the grand hall, where everyone could admire it.

"Tenali," said the king, smiling, "you always find the kindest way to tell the truth."

And Tenali bowed and grinned. "A good portrait, Your Majesty, shows the best of a person — and the best of you is very easy to see."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever mind finds a kind way to tell the truth.*
