# The Owl and the Crows

*A gentle Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In the middle of a great green forest stood the oldest banyan tree in the whole wide world.

Its branches spread wide like open arms. Its leaves whispered in the wind. And in its hollow trunk, high above the ground, lived a family of owls.

Old Owl Ollie lived there with his wife, Oona, and their little owlet, Ollie Junior — who was called OJ for short.

The owls loved the nighttime. When the moon came up, they opened their big round eyes and said, "Hoo! Hoo! What a lovely night!"

But in the very same banyan tree, on the sunny branches below, lived a family of crows.

Old Crow Cora lived there with her husband, Carl, and their little crowlet, Coco.

The crows loved the daytime. When the sun came up, they opened their shiny black wings and said, "Caw! Caw! What a lovely day!"

Now, you might think the owls and the crows were happy neighbours. But they were not.

The owls said, "Crows are too noisy in the morning! Their caw-caw wakes us up when we are trying to sleep!"

The crows said, "Owls are too noisy at night! Their hoo-hoo wakes us up when we are trying to sleep!"

So the owls grumbled about the crows. And the crows grumbled about the owls. And nobody in the banyan tree was happy.

One day, Old Crow Cora flew up to the top of the tree and called a meeting.

"Neighbours!" she cawed. "We cannot go on grumbling. We all live in the same tree. Let us find a way to share it."

Old Owl Ollie blinked his big round eyes. "Share it? But you are awake when we are asleep!"

"Exactly!" said Cora. "You sleep in the day, and we sleep in the night. So the tree is big enough for all of us — if we are quiet when the others sleep."

Coco the little crowlet hopped forward. "But what if I want to sing in the morning?" she asked.

"Then sing softly, like a lullaby," said Cora with a smile.

And OJ the little owlet asked, "But what if I want to hoot at night?"

"Then hoot softly, like a bedtime song," said Ollie with a smile.

So they made a promise — a tree promise. In the morning, the crows would speak in soft voices, so the owls could sleep. At night, the owls would speak in soft voices, so the crows could sleep.

And it worked! The mornings grew gentle. The nights grew gentle. The banyan tree grew peaceful.

One evening, as the sun was going down and the moon was coming up, the owls and the crows sat together on one big branch — owls on one side, crows on the other — and watched the sky change colour together.

"Look," said Coco. "The sky is pink and purple!"

"And soon it will be full of stars," said OJ.

"Day is lovely," said Cora.

"Night is lovely," said Ollie.

"And this tree," said Coco and OJ together, "is lovely for all of us!"

From that day on, the banyan tree was known all through the forest as the Tree of Two Songs — the soft caw of the morning, and the soft hoo of the night.

And whenever a new bird came to the forest and asked, "How do you all live together so happily?" the owls and the crows would answer together:

"We share the tree, we keep our voices soft, and we remember — good neighbours make a good home."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Good neighbours share kindly and keep the peace for each other.*
