Tenali Rama and the Royal Astrologer

A clever Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a grand palace in India, there lived a kind king named Krishnadevaraya. One morning, the royal astrologer came rushing in, waving his arms.

"Oh, Your Majesty!" he cried. "I have read the stars, and I have terrible news. Bad luck will visit the palace tomorrow!"

The king's eyes went wide. "Bad luck? Tomorrow? Oh dear, oh dear! What shall we do?"

But one man did not look worried at all. It was Tenali Rama, the king's cleverest friend. He smiled a big, twinkly smile.

"Your Majesty," he said, bowing low, "please do not worry. If bad luck is coming tomorrow, it will not visit you. It will visit the astrologer himself!"

The astrologer puffed up his chest. "Nonsense! Nothing bad will happen to me!"

"Oh, it will," said Tenali Rama, counting on his fingers. "Tomorrow, his turban will fall into a puddle. A cheeky monkey will steal his lunch. And he will trip over his own long robes — not once, but twice!"

The king giggled. The astrologer stomped his foot and marched away, muttering, "We shall see. We shall see!"

The next morning, the astrologer woke up feeling very proud. "Today," he said to himself, "nothing will go wrong. The stars will keep me safe."

He put on his finest turban and walked out, humming. But the road was wet, and — splish! — his foot slipped, and his turban tumbled off his head and plopped into a puddle!

"Oh no!" cried the astrologer, fishing it out. It was dripping and muddy.

He hurried on, grumbling. At midday, he sat under a tree to eat his lunch of rice and sweet mangoes. He had just taken the first bite when — whoosh! — a little monkey swung down from the branches, snatched the whole lunch basket, and scampered away, chattering with glee!

"My lunch!" wailed the astrologer. "That thieving monkey!"

Hungry and soggy, he stomped back toward the palace, thinking about his lost lunch. He did not see the edge of his own robe. He tripped — bump! — into a pile of hay. He picked himself up, took two more steps, and tripped again — bump!

When he arrived at the court, dripping and muddy and hungry, the king burst out laughing. "Tenali Rama was right about everything!" the king chuckled. "Your turban fell in a puddle, a monkey stole your lunch, and you tripped over your robes!"

The astrologer hung his head. His cheeks turned pink. "Your Majesty," he said softly, "I must tell you the truth. I was only guessing about the bad luck. I made it up to sound important."

The king smiled kindly. "Then from today, no more scary guesses. We will trust our own good sense instead."

And Tenali Rama winked at the king. "The stars are lovely to look at," he said, "but a clever mind is the best guide of all."

That evening, the palace was peaceful again. The king sipped his warm milk, and Tenali Rama went home under a sky full of twinkling stars. And the astrologer, a little wiser now, went to bed early and slept like a baby.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Clever thinking beats scary predictions.
