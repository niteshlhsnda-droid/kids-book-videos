The Tiger and the Golden Bracelet

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, beside a slow, sparkling river, there lived an old tiger.

His fur had turned gray and silver with age, and his paws were too tired to run anymore. But his smile was still full of big teeth.

One sunny morning, the old tiger was swishing his paws in the cool water. Tink! Something shiny bumped against his paw. What do YOU think it could be?

It was a golden bracelet — a shiny ring you wear on your wrist — glittering like a tiny sun.

"Oh my," said the tiger, holding it up. "What a lovely treasure. But gold is no good to an old tiger. I think I will give it away!"

So he sat up tall on the riverbank and held the bracelet high. In his deepest, kindest voice, he called out, "A gift! A free golden bracelet for anyone who wants it!"

A little deer stepped out of the trees, nibbling the soft grass.

"A gift?" she said.

"Yes! Come closer, little one," purred the tiger.

The deer looked at the bracelet. Then she looked at the tiger's big teeth. Then she looked at the bracelet again.

She giggled the way you giggle when you're a little bit scared. "Um... maybe another day!" And off she skipped into the trees.

Next came two ducks, waddling down to the river.

"A golden bracelet! And it's free-free!" quacked the first duck.

"Come and take it," said the tiger, smiling a wide, wide smile.

The ducks looked at each other. The first duck whispered, "Those teeth are very big." The second duck whispered, "Very, very big."

And they waddled right back the way they came. Splash, splash, splash.

Then along came Tito, a young jackal — like a little wild dog — with bright eyes and springy feet.

The bracelet caught the sunlight and twinkled. Twinkle, twinkle. Tito had never seen anything so shiny in his whole life.

"Oh!" said Tito. "Is that really for me?"

"For you, little friend," said the tiger in a voice as smooth and warm as honey. "Just come to the edge of the water."

Tito took one hop forward. The gold sparkled. He took another hop. Should he go any closer — what do YOU think?

Just then, a slow, deep voice, dry as leaves rubbing together, called from behind a mossy rock. "Wait, little one. Wait."

It was Kuba, the tortoise — a slow old turtle who lives on land — blinking his sleepy eyes. "Tito," he said in a voice as soft as pond water lapping a stone, "why would a tiger give away gold?"

Tito stopped. His ears perked up. He looked at the tiger, then at the teeth, then at the gold.

He thought... and thought... and thought.

"You know what?" said Tito. "Forget the bracelet — I'm going home!" He turned around and trotted home, his paws thudding softly on the cool, mossy path, till he caught the warm-earth smell of his den.

The old tiger gave one sleepy sigh, then padded softly away, all the way down the riverbank.

But Tito was curled up warm in his den, and Kuba the tortoise closed his eyes with a slow, sleepy smile.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: If a stranger's gift seems too wonderful, stop and think first — just like Tito did.
