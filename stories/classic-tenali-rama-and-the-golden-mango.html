Tenali Rama and the Golden Mango

A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the palace garden of Vijayanagara, there stood a very special mango tree.

Its mangoes were golden, round, and sweet, and they glowed like little suns in the green leaves.

King Krishna Deva Raya loved that tree more than any other tree in his kingdom.

But every morning, the king's gardener found one mango missing.

"Your Majesty," said the gardener, "someone is taking our golden mangoes in the night!"

The king was sad. "Who would do such a thing?" he asked. "Those mangoes are for all the children of the city."

So the king called for Tenali Rama, the cleverest man in Vijayanagara.

"Tenali," said the king, "find the mango thief for me. But remember — be kind. No one must be hurt."

Tenali Rama bowed. "I will find your thief, Your Majesty. And I will find him with kindness."

That night, Tenali Rama set no trap and hid behind no bush.

Instead, he tied tiny silver bells to the lowest branches of the mango tree.

Then he placed a basket of the sweetest golden mangoes by the garden gate.

On the basket he put a note: "For the friend who comes at night — take this basket, and leave the rest for the children."

Then Tenali Rama waited just outside the gate.

Late that night, a merchant named Gopal crept into the garden.

Gopal loved golden mangoes. He had taken one every night for a week, and always wanted one more.

But tonight, as he reached up for a mango, a little silver bell went tinkle, tinkle, tinkle!

Gopal froze. His heart went thump, thump, thump.

Then he saw the basket by the gate, full of golden mangoes, with the note on top.

Gopal read the note twice. His face turned red.

"I have been stealing mangoes," he whispered, "and they left me a gift instead."

Just then, Tenali Rama stepped out from behind the gate, carrying a small lamp.

"Good evening, friend," said Tenali Rama. "Sit with me and share a mango."

Gopal sat down under the tree, feeling small and sorry.

"I took your mangoes every night," he said. "I am sorry. I was greedy, and I forgot the children."

Tenali Rama cut a golden mango in half and gave the bigger half to Gopal.

"Everyone makes a mistake sometimes," he said. "What matters is what we do next."

Gopal took a bite. It was the sweetest mango he had ever tasted.

"From today," he said, "I will guard this tree and share its mangoes with all the children."

The next morning, Tenali Rama told the king everything.

The king laughed. "You caught the thief with bells and kindness!" he said. "That is the cleverest catch I have ever heard."

And so it was. Every summer, Gopal picked the ripe golden mangoes and shared them with all the children of Vijayanagara.

No mango was ever stolen again, because there were plenty for everyone, and plenty to share.

That evening, Tenali Rama sat under the tree and watched the children eat their golden mangoes, their faces sticky and happy in the warm light.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness catches a greedy heart better than any trap, and sharing makes sweet things sweeter.
