# The Old Woman and the Empty Jar

*A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little village with winding stone lanes, where the evening air smelled of warm bread and jasmine, there lived an old, old woman named Nani-ji.

Nani-ji's house was small and cozy. It had a creaky wooden door, a window full of marigolds, and shelves lined with jars of pickles and honey.

But her most favorite jar of all was empty.

It was a big clay jar in the corner of her kitchen. Long, long ago, it had held sweet, sweet grape juice, pressed from the village grapes.

The grape juice was gone now — finished long, long ago, at weddings and happy feasts. But Nani-ji kept the jar anyway.

Every evening, when the sun turned the sky soft pink and orange, Nani-ji would shuffle over to the jar. She would lean down, close her eyes, and breathe in — long and slow.

"Ahhhh," she would sigh, with the sweetest smile on her wrinkled face. "Smells just like the old days."

Her grandson, little Chintu, watched her do this every day. One evening he finally asked, "Nani-ji, why do you smell an empty jar? There's nothing inside!"

Nani-ji laughed her soft, crinkly laugh. "Come here, my little moon. Smell it yourself."

Chintu leaned in and sniffed so hard his nose nearly touched the bottom — and giggled.

And oh! From the empty jar rose a warm, honey-sweet smell — like sunshine and grapes.

"It smells... sweet," Chintu whispered. "But the jar is empty!"

What sweet smell would YOU keep in a jar, little one?

"The juice is gone," said Nani-ji, "but its sweetness stayed behind. It soaked into the clay, deep, deep down. Now whenever I miss the happy old days, I come and breathe in this smell, and I remember."

She closed her eyes and hummed. "I remember the wedding feast — drums going dhum-dhum, marigolds swinging overhead, everyone twirling in red and gold. I remember your mother's first birthday cake. I remember your grandfather's laugh — big and warm, filling the whole kitchen."

Chintu sat very still and listened. And as Nani-ji told her stories, the empty jar didn't feel empty at all. It felt full — full of memories, full of sweetness, full of love.

That night, Chintu found a small empty jar of his own and kept it on his windowsill.

"What's that for?" asked Nani-ji.

"For keeping my sweet smells," said Chintu. "Today it will hold the smell of your mango pickle. And tomorrow, the smell of the rain. So I can smell them later, and remember."

Nani-ji hugged him tight. "You are a wise little boy," she said. "The sweetest things never disappear, my love. They just wait in quiet corners — for us to remember."

And from that evening on, Chintu kept his own little jar on his windowsill.

That night, the two of them sat together in the quiet kitchen. No talking. No stories. Just breathing in, slow and deep — in... and out... — the warm, honey-sweet smell wrapping around them like a soft blanket.

In... and out... In... and out...

Good night, little one. Breathe in deep… and let a sweet smell carry you off to sleep.

---

*Moral: Sweet memories linger — keep the happy moments close to your heart.*
