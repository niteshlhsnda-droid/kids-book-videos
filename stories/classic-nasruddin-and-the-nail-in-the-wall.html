Nasruddin and the Nail in the Wall

A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once, in a little village where the rooftops were flat and the lemons grew round, there lived a clever man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin was kind, and Nasruddin was clever, and Nasruddin was — how shall we say — a little bit mischievous. His eyes twinkled like two stars whenever a funny idea popped into his head.

One spring morning, Nasruddin needed some money. So he put his little house up for sale.

A neighbor named Farid came to look at it.

"This is a fine house," said Farid. "Strong walls. Sunny windows. I will buy it."

"Wait, wait," said Nasruddin, and his eyes twinkled. "I will sell you the house. But there is one small condition — one little rule."

"What condition?" asked Farid.

"I am keeping one thing," said Nasruddin. "Just one nail. The nail in the middle of the wall in the sitting room — a short, gray, stubby little nail. The house is yours, but the nail is still mine."

Farid laughed. Ha! A nail! What a funny joke!

"Of course, Nasruddin," said Farid. "The house is mine. The nail is yours. Come and visit your nail whenever you like!"

They shook hands. The money changed pockets. The house changed owners.

Knock, knock. The very next morning, there was a knock at the door.

It was Nasruddin.

"Hello, Farid!" he said cheerfully. "I have come to visit my nail."

Farid opened the door. Nasruddin walked in, patted the nail on the wall, and hung his coat on it.

"Just visiting my nail," he said with a smile. "Thank you, goodbye!"

Knock, knock. The morning after that, there was a knock at the door again.

It was Nasruddin again.

"Hello, Farid!" he said cheerfully. "I have come to oil my nail. A nail must stay shiny, you know."

He climbed on a stool, rubbed the nail with a soft cloth until it went *shiny-shiny-shine*, and hung his market bag on it.

"Just oiling my nail," he said with a smile. "Thank you, goodbye!"

Knock, knock. The morning after that, there was a knock at the door again.

It was Nasruddin again.

"Hello, Farid!" he said cheerfully. "I have come to feed my nail lunch."

Farid's eyes went wide. "Feed your nail lunch?"

"Well," said Nasruddin, "the nail gets hungry. And I get hungry. So I brought my lunch basket."

He hung the basket on the nail, sat down on the floor beneath it, and ate his lunch — *munch, munch, munch*. What do you think was in the basket?

Knock, knock. The morning after that, there was a knock at the door again. And the morning after that. And the morning after that.

Nasruddin came to visit his nail. He came to oil his nail. He came to show his nail to his friends, and they all giggled at the shiny little nail. He hung his coat on the nail. He hung his bag on the nail. He hung his lunch basket on the nail. And once he even took a little nap under it, snoring softly — *snrrre-snrrre-snrrre* — while the nail kept watch from the wall.

Poor Farid could not eat his breakfast in peace. He could not drink his tea in peace. He could not take a nap in peace.

Knock, knock. Nasruddin again.

"Come to visit my nail?" asked Farid, before Nasruddin could say a word.

Nasruddin grinned, his eyes full of sparkle.

"Nasruddin," said Farid, holding up his hands, "I am not angry. But I cannot sleep! I cannot eat! Please — take back your nail."

Nasruddin stroked his beard. "Take back my nail?"

"Yes," said Farid, and he started to laugh. "Take back your nail. And take back your whole house too! Keep it all, my friend. Just let me rest!"

Nasruddin let out a warm, rolling chuckle. He counted the money back into Farid's hand, coin by coin — *clink, clink, clink*. He pulled the nail out of the wall and tucked it into his pocket.

And so Nasruddin got his house back, and Farid got his quiet back, and the nail got a nice long rest in Nasruddin's pocket.

That night, the village was soft and sleepy. The moon rose round as a lemon over the rooftops. And somewhere, in a little house with strong walls and sunny windows, Nasruddin hung up his coat, blew out his lamp, and fell fast asleep — *snrrre-snrrre-snrrre*.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Think carefully before you agree — small promises can grow big.
