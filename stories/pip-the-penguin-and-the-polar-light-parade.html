Pip the Penguin and the Polar-Light Parade

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Far, far away, where the sea turns to ice and the snow sparkles like sugar, there lived a little penguin named Pip.

Pip had a round black-and-white tummy, tiny flippers, and feet that went waddle, waddle, waddle across the ice. But Pip's favourite thing in all the world was not waddling. It was sliding.

Wheee! Down the icy hill he would go, on his tummy, faster and faster, until he landed with a soft flump in a puff of snow. Then he would waddle right back up and do it all over again.

One cold evening, just as the sun was sinking low and the sky was turning deep, deep blue, Pip's mama called, "Pip, come inside! It is bedtime soon."

"Just one more slide, Mama!" said Pip. "One more, please!"

Mama smiled. "One more slide," she said. "And then look up at the sky."

Pip took a deep breath and — wheee! — down the hill he slid, arms out wide, snowflakes tickling his cheeks. And when he landed with a soft flump at the bottom, he looked up.

Oh! Oh, my!

The night sky was waking up. First came a soft green glow, like a ribbon of light unrolling across the stars. Then came pink, and purple, and a shimmer of gold. The colours swirled and swayed and danced, high above the ice.

"The polar lights!" gasped Pip. "The polar lights are here!"

Pip had heard about the polar lights from the old seal who lived by the shore. "When the sky dances," the old seal had said, "it is the polar-light parade. The lights march across the night like ribbons in a parade, and everyone who watches them feels sleepy and safe."

And now the parade was beginning.

First came the green ribbons, rippling softly, like curtains blowing in a gentle breeze. Then came the pink ribbons, twirling and curling like dancers in long dresses. Then came the purple ribbons, swirling high and low, and the gold sparkles that twinkled like tiny bells.

Pip sat down on the ice with a happy sigh. He tucked his flippers around his round tummy and watched.

Soon, Suki the seal pup came flopping over the ice, flip, flop, flip, flop. "Pip!" she called. "Are the lights dancing tonight?"

"They are!" said Pip. "Come and watch with me."

So Suki flopped down beside Pip, and together they watched the ribbons dance.

Then Hoot the snowy owl came gliding down from the moonlit cliff, soft as a falling feather. "May I watch the parade too?" he hooted.

"They are!" said Pip. "Come and watch with us."

So Hoot settled on a lump of ice beside them, and together they watched the ribbons dance.

The lights danced slower now, and softer too, like a lullaby made of colour. The green ribbons swayed, slow, slow, slow. The pink ribbons curled and faded. The purple ribbons melted into the dark, and the gold sparkles twinkled, twinkle, twinkle, like sleepy eyes blinking.

Pip yawned a big, round yawn. Suki yawned too. Hoot blinked his big eyes, slow as anything.

"The parade is going to sleep," whispered Pip. "And I think… I think I am going to sleep too."

"Me too," whispered Suki, snuggling into the snow.

"Me too," hooted Hoot, tucking his head under his wing.

High above them, the last ribbon of green light swirled once more, gentle as a goodnight kiss. Then the sky grew quiet and dark, and the stars came out, one by one, twinkling down at the little friends on the ice.

And there, under the soft, sleepy sky, Pip the penguin curled up warm and snug, and drifted off to sleep, dreaming of ribbons of light dancing just for him.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The most beautiful wonders are the ones we share with friends.
