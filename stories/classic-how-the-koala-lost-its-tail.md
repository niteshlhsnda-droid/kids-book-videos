# How the Koala Lost Its Tail

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the land of Australia, koalas had long, long tails. Long, fluffy tails that swept behind them like soft grey scarves.

And one little koala named Kobi was very, very proud of his tail.

"Look at my tail!" Kobi would say to everyone. "Is it not the longest, fluffiest tail in the whole gum tree?"

Every morning, Kobi would climb to the very top of his gum tree and brush his tail with a little twig, until it shone in the sun.

One hot summer, the rain stopped falling. The creeks grew low. The water holes grew smaller and smaller, until there was only one little water hole left in the whole valley.

All the animals came to drink at the little water hole — the wombats, the wallabies, the kangaroos, and Kobi the koala.

But Kobi pushed in front of them all. "Mine! Mine!" he cried. "This water hole is MINE! Go away! All of you!"

He would not share even one sip. He sat by the water hole all day, guarding it with his long, fluffy tail wrapped around him.

His friends were very thirsty, and very sad. "Please, Kobi," said Wally the wallaby. "Just one little sip?"

"No!" said Kobi. "Mine!"

Now, old Grandma Wombat was the wisest animal in the valley. She shook her head slowly.

"Kobi," she said, "a tail is not for showing off. And water is for sharing. If you keep being unkind, your tail will teach you a lesson."

Kobi laughed. "My tail will never teach ME anything!"

But that very afternoon, Kobi climbed up his gum tree to brush his beautiful tail. He was in such a hurry that he did not look where he was going — CRACK!

His long tail caught on a dry branch. Kobi tumbled down, down, down — and landed with a soft thump in the grass below.

He was not hurt, not one little bit. But when he looked behind him — oh no! His long, fluffy tail had snapped short! Now he had only a tiny, stubby tail, like a little grey button!

Kobi sat in the grass and cried. "My beautiful tail! My beautiful tail!"

His friends came running. Wally the wallaby brought him cool water. Grandma Wombat patted his head gently.

"Do not cry, little Kobi," said Grandma Wombat. "Your tail is short now, but your heart can still grow big. Share the water hole with your friends, and you will see."

So Kobi did. He shared the water hole. He shared his shady gum tree. He shared his sweetest gum leaves with everyone.

And do you know what? His friends loved him more than ever — not for his tail, but for his kind heart.

And that is why, even today, koalas have only short, stubby tails. And every koala remembers: it is not your tail that makes you special. It is your kindness.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing makes the heart bigger than any tail.*
