The Louse and the Bedbug

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the fold of a soft, warm blanket lived a teeny-tiny bug named Mili.

Mili was no bigger than a crumb. Every night, she sipped one small sip of warmth from the blanket, curled up in the cozy fold, and slept like a baby.

One evening, her old friend Buntu came to visit. Buntu was a round little bug with shiny cheeks and a very big appetite.

"What a cozy blanket!" said Buntu, bouncing on the soft folds. "May I stay for just one night?"

"Of course," said Mili kindly. "But remember our rule — one small sip, and then sleep. Never be greedy."

That night, Buntu sipped one small sip. It was warm and lovely.

Then he sipped another. And another. And one more, just because it tasted so good.

Soon Buntu was sipping and sipping, wiggling and giggling, until the blanket shook and rustled — rustle, rustle, rustle!

The little boy sleeping under the blanket woke up. "Itchy!" he mumbled, and scratched his arm.

In the morning, his mother shook the blanket out in the sunshine — shake, shake, shake! — and hung it over the line to air.

Mili and Buntu tumbled out into the soft green grass below. Plop, plop!

"Oh no," whispered Buntu, his shiny cheeks turning pink. "My greed shook us right out of our home."

Mili patted his shoulder. "It is all right, Buntu. Let us find a new home — a better one."

So the two friends crawled under a big, cool leaf, where the morning dew glittered like tiny stars.

Mili showed Buntu how to sip one small dewdrop — just one — and then curl up for a nap.

From that day on, Buntu never took more than his share. And every morning, the two friends woke up in their leafy home, cozy and safe and happy.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Take only what you need — greed spoils even the coziest home.
