The Rat that Ate Iron

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a busy little town, there lived a merchant named Arjun.

Arjun was a kind man with a big smile and the cleverest heart in town. He always sang a small song while he worked, and his songs made everyone in the market smile back.

One day, Arjun had to travel far away for his work. He called his friend Kabir.

"Kabir, my friend," said Arjun, "will you keep my big iron pot safe while I am away? It is a pot of hard, heavy metal — I cannot carry it."

"Of course!" said Kabir, patting the pot. "It will be as safe as my own house!"

So Arjun set off down the dusty road, singing his little songs under the warm sun.

But Kabir was not a good keeper of promises. He looked at the heavy iron pot, and a greedy idea tiptoed into his mind.

He sold the iron pot — every bit of it — and jingled the coins in his pocket. Then he felt nervous, because Arjun would come back soon.

When Arjun came home, he went to Kabir's house and smiled. "Kabir, my friend, may I have my iron pot back?"

Kabir rubbed his hands and put on a sad, sorry face. "Oh, Arjun! Something terrible happened. A rat came and ATE your iron! The whole pot — gone!"

Now, rats can nibble at many things. But eat a whole heavy pot of iron? Arjun knew better.

But Arjun did not get angry. He only smiled his soft smile and said, "Ah, I see. Well, that is what rats do."

Kabir felt very relieved. He thought he was very clever.

A few days later, Kabir's little son Rohan was playing by the door. Arjun was walking past, carrying a bag of sweet jalebis from the riverside fair.

"Kabir, my friend," Arjun called, "may little Rohan come to the fair with me? I will buy him sweets, and I will bring him home before the sun goes down."

Kabir, who was feeling quite pleased with himself, laughed and said yes.

So Rohan skipped happily to the fair with Arjun. He ate round, golden jalebis — sticky and sweet — and giggled at the dancing monkeys. All the while, Rohan was safe and sound, right by Arjun's side.

Now here is the clever part. At sunset, Arjun brought Rohan to Kabir's door — but Rohan was nowhere to be seen!

Can you guess where he was hiding? Behind Arjun's big shawl — safe and sound, quietly munching his last jalebi!

Arjun knocked, and when Kabir opened the door, Arjun made his face very, very serious.

"Kabir, my friend! Something terrible happened!" said Arjun. "At the fair — a great, great hawk came flapping down from the sky and carried Rohan away!"

(But don't worry — Rohan was safe behind the shawl all along.)

Kabir's eyes grew round. "What? That cannot be! Hawks cannot carry little boys!"

Arjun nodded slowly. "Can they not?" he said gently. "Well — in a land where rats eat iron, hawks can carry boys."

Kabir stared. Then his face turned red. Then he laughed out loud!

"You clever, clever man!" cried Kabir. "Wait here!" And he ran to the market with his coins and bought the iron back, and set the heavy pot at Arjun's feet.

Arjun swept his shawl aside, and there was Rohan — safe, sticky-faced, grinning, with jalebi crumbs on his shirt. "More jalebi, please!" he piped up.

They all laughed together. Nobody was hurt. Nobody was angry for long.

That evening, Kabir sat beside Arjun and said softly, "Forgive me, my friend. A lie grows bigger and bigger, until you need a whole sky of hawks to hide it."

From that day on, Kabir kept his promises — every single one. And Arjun sang his little songs again, while the town hummed along, softly, softly, under the stars.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Dishonesty is repaid in its own coin — but a clever, honest heart always wins.
