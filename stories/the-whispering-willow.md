# The Whispering Willow

*A cozy original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Beside a quiet pond, where the water lay still and glassy, stood a willow with bark wrinkled like an old elephant's knees and roots curled thick and gray above the earth. It was the oldest tree in the forest, with long, soft leaves that hung down like green curtains.

Every evening, when the sun dipped low and golden, a breeze brushed softly through those long leaves. And the willow would sing. Not a loud song — a whisper-song, the softest lullaby in the whole forest.

*Hush, little leaves, sway in the breeze... sleep, little friends, the night is soft and deep.*

Every night, the forest babies came to listen. Three fluffy ducklings waddled over from the pond and tucked their beaks under their wings. A fawn with spots like tiny stars curled up on a patch of moss. Two baby bunnies snuggled close together, their little noses wiggling slower and slower. And a hedgehog rolled up at the willow's roots like a sleepy little ball.

The willow whispered its song, and one by one, their eyes grew heavy. Soon the whole circle under the tree was breathing slow and deep.

But one evening, the wind came racing. It yanked the long green curtains of leaves up and flapped them like washing on a line — *whoooosh!* — slapping leaf against leaf until the soft whisper-song was swallowed, the way a shout in a hallway swallows a hum.

The ducklings lifted their heads. The fawn's ears stood up. The bunnies wiggled their noses, still heavy and sleepy, and the hedgehog uncurled just a little.

The babies might have been scared — but no, they were far too sleepy for that, and they only missed their song.

Then the smallest duckling did something wonderful. "I know it," she whispered. "I know the song by heart." Then she opened her beak and hummed — softly, from memory. *Hush, little leaves...*

The other ducklings joined in, humming together. Then the fawn added a warm, low hum. The baby bunnies hummed with tiny shivery breaths. And the hedgehog's hum was the teeniest of all — so small you could barely hear it.

Their little hums floated up through the branches, soft and together. And as the wind began to slow, the willow heard them.

The willow listened to their tiny song — and something warm and happy moved through its oldest leaves.

So the willow sang again — a whisper so gentle it felt like being rocked. The leaves swayed slow, slow, slower. The pond lay still. The stars came out, one after another.

*Hush, little leaves, sway in the breeze... sleep, little friends, the night is soft and deep.*

The ducklings tucked their beaks away. The fawn sighed into the moss. The bunnies pressed their cheeks together. The hedgehog curled up tight and warm.

And under the whispering willow, everyone drifted off to sleep — the babies, the breeze, and the big old tree itself.

The end. 🌙

*Moral: When we help each other, everyone can rest.*
