# Tenali Rama and the Tank of Milk

*A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the shining city of Vijayanagara, there lived a kind king named Krishnadevaraya.

A big festival was coming. "Tomorrow morning, we will share sweet, milky water with everyone in the city!" said the king. "It will be cool and creamy!"

The king called his courtiers — the people who helped him rule — to the palace hall.

"Listen, everyone!" he said. "Tonight, every house must pour one pot of milk into the great royal tank. By morning, the tank will be full of sweet milk for all to share."

Soon everyone in the city knew.

"One pot of milk for the tank!" said the baker, as warm bread smells floated by.

"One pot of milk for the tank!" said the gardener, watering her pink flowers.

"One pot of milk for the tank!" said the teacher, as his little pupils clapped.

But as the sun sank low, a small, sneaky thought tiptoed into many homes.

"If I pour water instead of milk," thought the baker, the gardener, and the teacher too, "just my one pot of water will not be noticed. The tank will still look milky and white!"

That night, under the soft evening stars, the people crept to the tank.

Splash! went the baker's pot. Splash! went the gardener's pot. Splash! went the teacher's pot.

But Tenali Rama, the king's cleverest friend, held a pot of fresh, creamy milk.

He did not think a sneaky thought. He just smiled his quiet little smile and poured his milk in, plip-plop, until his pot was empty.

"There," he said softly. "That is my share, and it is real milk."

The next morning, the king hurried to the great royal tank.

He dipped a silver cup in and took a sip.

"Oh!" cried the king. "This is not milk at all! This is only water!"

The baker, the gardener, and the teacher all looked at their shoes.

Then Tenali Rama stepped forward, bowing low. "Your Majesty," he said gently, "may I tell you what happened?"

"Please do," said the king.

"Last night, many people thought the same sneaky thought," said Tenali Rama. "Each one thought, 'Just my one pot of water will not be noticed.' So the tank is full of water — not one pot of milk in it, except mine."

The king stared at the tank. Then he began to smile. Then he began to laugh — ha, ha, ha!

"Oh, Tenali Rama!" laughed the king. "You are the only one who poured real milk! When everyone thinks, 'Nobody will notice me,' everybody is noticed in the end!"

The people laughed too, a little shyly, their cheeks turning pink.

"Let us fill the tank again," said the king, "this time with real milk, one real pot from every house."

And so they did, pot by pot, until the tank was full and creamy.

That evening, under the soft moon, the king whispered, "Good night, sweet tank. Tomorrow, everyone will taste the milk — because everyone poured it honestly, together."

And in the quiet city, under the silver moonlight, the tank stood full and still.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Honesty matters even when nobody is watching — one real pot beats a hundred secret splashes.*
