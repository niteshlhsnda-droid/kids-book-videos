Nasruddin and the Moon

A funny Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One clear night, Nasruddin was walking home past the old well at the edge of the village. The moon was round and silver, floating in the dark sky like a shining coin.

Nasruddin stopped to draw some water. He leaned over the well — and gasped.

"Oh no!" he cried. "The moon has fallen into the well!"

There it was, at the bottom of the dark water — the round, silver moon, rippling softly.

"We cannot leave the poor moon down there in the cold water," said Nasruddin. "I must fish it out!"

He ran home and fetched a long rope with an iron hook on the end. Back at the well, he lowered the hook down, down, down into the water, and began to fish for the moon.

He swung the rope left. He swung it right. At last — clink! — the hook caught on something hard. It must be the edge of the moon!

Nasruddin pulled with all his might. He pulled and pulled — and SNAP! The rope broke, and Nasruddin tumbled backwards onto the grass with a great big THUMP.

He lay there for a moment, rubbing his head. Then he looked up at the sky.

And there was the moon — round and silver, floating safely back in the dark sky, right where it belonged!

Nasruddin sat up and clapped his hands with joy. "Well done, Nasruddin!" he said to himself. "That was hard work, but I did it. I have put the moon back in the sky!"

He walked home very proudly, swinging the broken rope over his shoulder.

The next morning, he told everyone in the tea shop, "Last night, the moon fell into the well, and I fished it out and put it back in the sky — all by myself!"

And all his friends laughed and laughed, and Nasruddin laughed loudest of all. For he knew, deep down, that the moon had never left the sky at all — but it had been a grand adventure all the same.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Look up before you leap in — sometimes the thing you chase was never lost at all.
