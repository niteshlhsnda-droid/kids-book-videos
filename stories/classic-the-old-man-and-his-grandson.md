# The Old Man and His Grandson

*A kind Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once there was a very old man named Dadu. His hands trembled when he held his cup, his eyes were dim, and his feet shuffled slowly across the floor.

Dadu lived with his son, his son's wife, and their little boy, Chintu, who was four years old. Every evening they all sat down to supper together at one big table.

But Dadu's hands shook so much that sometimes his spoon slipped, and his soup spilled on the tablecloth. And sometimes his cup tipped, and his milk splashed on the floor.

His son's wife frowned. "He makes such a mess," she whispered. "Let us give him his supper in the corner, behind the stove, in a little wooden bowl. Then the table will stay clean."

So from that day on, poor Dadu sat all alone in the corner behind the stove, eating his supper from a wooden bowl, while the rest of the family ate at the big table. Dadu looked at his bowl and said nothing, but his eyes were sad.

One evening, little Chintu was playing on the floor with some small pieces of wood. He was sawing and hammering and stacking them with great care.

"What are you making, my little one?" asked his father, smiling.

"I am making a little wooden bowl," said Chintu proudly. "When you and Mother are old, like Dadu, I will give you your supper in it, in the corner behind the stove."

His father and mother looked at each other. Their faces turned red, and their smiles faded away. They understood at once: their little boy was copying exactly what they had done.

That very evening, they took Dadu gently by the hand and led him back to the big table. They set his place right beside Chintu, with a proper cup and a proper plate.

"Dadu, please eat with us," they said softly. "We are sorry we put you in the corner. You belong here, with your family."

Dadu's eyes twinkled. "Thank you," he said, and he smiled the biggest smile.

From then on, if Dadu spilled a little soup, nobody frowned. Chintu helped him hold his cup steady, and Dadu told Chintu wonderful stories of when he was a little boy himself.

And every evening, the whole family ate together at the big table — happy, noisy, and together, just as a family should be.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Treat grandparents with love and kindness, just as you would like to be treated one day.*
