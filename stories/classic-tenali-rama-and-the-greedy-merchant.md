# Tenali Rama and the Greedy Merchant

*A gentle Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the kingdom of King Krishnadevaraya, there lived a clever court jester named Tenali Rama.

Tenali Rama loved jokes. Tenali Rama loved riddles. And most of all, Tenali Rama loved helping people.

One year, the rains came late to the kingdom. The fields were dry. The wells were low. And grain — rice and wheat for everyone's dinner — became scarce and precious.

Now, in the town market there lived a merchant named Seth Dhaniram. Seth Dhaniram had a great big storehouse, full of grain — sacks and sacks of it, piled up to the roof!

But Seth Dhaniram was greedy. "If grain is scarce," he chuckled to himself, "I will sell it for more and more money!"

So he raised his prices. And raised them again. And again!

Soon, the poor families of the town could not afford even a single sack of rice. Mothers went home with empty baskets. Children went to bed hungry.

When Tenali Rama heard about this, he frowned. "This is not right," he said. "Grain is for everyone. I must teach Seth Dhaniram a lesson — with a trick, not a fight."

So Tenali Rama put on his finest clothes and walked into Seth Dhaniram's shop, smiling from ear to ear.

"Seth ji! Seth ji!" cried Tenali Rama. "Wonderful news! The king is building a great new granary for the palace! He needs grain — mountains of grain! And he will pay DOUBLE the price for it!"

Seth Dhaniram's eyes sparkled. "Double the price?" he said. "Oh, wonderful! I have sacks and sacks of grain! I will sell it all to the king!"

That very evening, Seth Dhaniram loaded every last sack of grain onto his carts and hurried to the palace gates.

But waiting at the palace gates was not the king. Waiting there were all the mothers and fathers of the town — with empty baskets!

Tenali Rama stepped forward. "Seth ji," he said, "the king's granary is the people's granary. The king will buy your grain — at the FAIR price, the price everyone can afford. And it will go straight to the families who need it."

Seth Dhaniram's face fell. "But… but I wanted double the price!"

Tenali Rama smiled kindly. "Seth ji, grain is like the rain. If you hold it all in one place, the fields stay dry. But if you let it fall where it is needed, everyone eats — and everyone is happy. Would you rather have a storehouse full of grain, or a town full of friends?"

Seth Dhaniram looked at the mothers with their empty baskets. He looked at the children, peeping from behind their mothers' saris. And his heart melted, just a little.

"You are right," he said quietly. "I have been greedy. Let the grain go to the people — at the fair price."

So the grain was weighed, and paid for fairly, and shared among all the families of the town. That night, every kitchen smelled of fresh rice and warm bread.

And Seth Dhaniram? He went home with an empty storehouse — but a full heart. From that day on, he sold his grain at fair prices, and the people of the town called him their friend.

And Tenali Rama? He went home laughing. "A trick with a kind heart," he said, "is the best trick of all."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing fairly makes the whole town happy — greed helps no one.*
