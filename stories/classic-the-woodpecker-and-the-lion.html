The Woodpecker and the Lion

A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a deep green forest, there lived a little woodpecker named Tappy.

Tappy had a bright red cap, a sharp little beak, and a very kind heart. All day long, she went tap-tap-tap on the trees, looking for tiny bugs to eat, and singing her cheerful song.

In the same forest lived Raja the lion — big, strong, and very proud. Raja had the loudest roar in the whole forest. "ROAR!" he would roar, and all the animals would tremble.

One morning, Raja was eating his breakfast when — CRUNCH! — a hard little bone got stuck in his throat.

"Ow! Ow! Ow!" cried Raja. He coughed. He spluttered. He rolled on the grass. But the bone would not move.

"My throat hurts!" roared Raja. "Somebody help me!"

The elephant shook his head. "Too scary," he said, and walked away.

The monkey shook his head. "Too scary," he said, and swung away.

But little Tappy flew down and landed on a branch near the lion.

"I will help you," she said in her small, brave voice.

"You?" laughed Raja, though it hurt to laugh. "You are so tiny!"

"My beak is tiny too," said Tappy. "And tiny is just right for this job."

Raja opened his mouth wide — WIDE — and Tappy hopped right in. Tap, tap, tap! She wiggled the bone. She tugged the bone. And — POP! — out it came!

Raja swallowed with joy. "Ahhh! That feels wonderful!"

Tappy fluttered out and landed on a branch, hoping for a kind word.

But Raja just licked his lips and said, "Hmph! You did what any bird would do. Do not expect a reward from a lion."

And with that, he padded away into the tall grass, without even a thank-you.

Tappy's little heart sank. But then she thought, "I did not help for a reward. I helped because he was hurting. And now he can roar and laugh again. That is enough for me."

She flew back to her tree, and went tap-tap-tap, singing her cheerful song.

But Raja could not stop thinking about the little bird. All afternoon, her words echoed in his ears: "I helped because you were hurting."

That evening, Raja walked all the way back to Tappy's tree.

"Tappy," he said in his gentlest voice — which, for a lion, was still very loud. "You were right, and I was wrong. A thank-you costs nothing, and it means everything. Thank you, little friend."

Tappy smiled her brightest smile. "You are welcome, Raja."

And from that day on, whenever Raja saw Tappy in the forest, he would bow his great head and say, "Good morning, my little friend." And Tappy would sing her song a little more cheerfully than ever.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness needs no reward — and a thank-you costs nothing but means everything.
