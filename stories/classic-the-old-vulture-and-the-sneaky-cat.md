# The Old Vulture and the Sneaky Cat

*A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

At the top of the tallest banyan tree lived an old, old vulture named Greybeak.

Greybeak's wings were tired now. He could no longer fly far, so he sat in his big nest and watched the clouds drift by — slow and white, like sleepy sheep.

In the branches below him lived many small birds — sparrows, finches, and one tiny tailor-bird named Pip.

Every morning, the small birds flew off to gather seeds and berries. Every evening, they came home chattering about their day.

One morning, a sleek cat named Slinky came padding up to the tree. His voice was smooth as honey.

"Dear little birds," he purred, "I have given up all my old ways. Now I am a holy cat. Let me guard your nests while you are away — no harm will ever come to them."

The birds looked at one another. A guard for their nests! How wonderful!

"Thank you, Slinky," they chirped. And off they flew.

But that night, when the birds counted their berries, some were missing. The next night, more seeds were gone. And the next — even more!

Little Pip the tailor-bird was the first to wonder. "Who is taking our food?" she whispered.

So one evening, instead of flying far away, the birds hid quietly behind the big leaves — and watched.

In the moonlight, they saw Slinky creep to the nests, stuff his cheeks with berries, and lick his whiskers. Caught red-pawed!

"Slinky!" cried the birds. "You said you were holy! You said you would guard our nests!"

Slinky's smooth voice shook a little. "I... I am sorry," he said, and his ears drooped. "My words were sweet, but my actions were not."

Old Greybeak flapped down slowly. "Little ones," he said kindly, "sweet words are easy. Watch what someone DOES — that tells you who they truly are."

Slinky hung his head. "Will you forgive me?"

"We forgive you," said Pip. "But trust must be earned, not just spoken."

So Slinky left the banyan tree and went to learn honest ways. And every evening after that, the birds took turns guarding their own nests — together.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sweet words are easy — trust what someone does, not just what they say.*
