Tenali Rama and the Brinjal Thief

A clever Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a sunny kingdom long ago, there was a grand palace with a garden behind it. The garden was full of flowers and fruit trees, and one very special patch of brinjal plants.

The brinjals were big and shiny and deep purple, like little lanterns hanging in the green leaves. Every morning, the gardener, old Gopal, walked down the rows and counted them. "Forty brinjals for the king's table," he would say, smiling.

But one morning, Gopal came to the garden and stopped. He counted again. Then he counted once more. "Thirty-nine!" he cried. "One brinjal is missing!"

The next morning, another brinjal was gone. And the next morning, another.

"Someone is picking my brinjals in the night!" said Gopal, wringing his hands. "Who could it be?"

The king heard about the missing brinjals, and he called for his cleverest minister, Tenali Rama. "Tenali," said the king, "someone is stealing brinjals from my garden. Can you find out who?"

Tenali Rama smiled his slow, clever smile. "I will try, Your Majesty," he said. "But first, let me visit the garden."

That evening, Tenali Rama walked through the garden. He looked at the brinjal plants, and at the soft earth path between the rows. He bent down and touched the ground.

"The earth is soft," he said to himself. "Soft earth remembers every foot."

Then he had an idea. He fetched a big sack of flour from the palace kitchen.

"What is the flour for?" asked Gopal.

"You will see," said Tenali Rama, smiling.

As the sun went down, Tenali Rama sprinkled the flour all along the garden path — a soft, white carpet from the gate to the brinjal patch. It looked like the path had been dusted with moonlight.

"Now we wait," said Tenali Rama. "And we must be very quiet."

Gopal and Tenali Rama hid behind the jasmine bushes and waited. The garden grew dark. The stars came out. The jasmine smelled sweet in the cool night air.

At last, when the moon was high, they heard a tiny sound — pad, pad, pad — soft footsteps on the floury path. A small figure tiptoed through the garden, went straight to the brinjal patch, reached up, picked one shiny purple brinjal, and tucked it under his arm.

"Got you!" said Tenali Rama, stepping out from behind the bushes.

The figure jumped! It was Kiran, the young kitchen helper, his face red as a tomato.

"I am sorry!" said Kiran, holding out the brinjal. "Please do not be angry."

"Why did you take the brinjals, Kiran?" asked Tenali Rama gently.

Kiran looked down at his feet, which were white with flour. "Because I love brinjal curry," he whispered. "My mother used to make it when I was little, and it tasted like home. I wanted to make my mother's curry, just once. I did not think anyone would miss one little brinjal."

"One little brinjal?" said Gopal. "You took three!"

"I know," said Kiran. "And every time, I felt worse. I am truly sorry."

Tenali Rama looked at Kiran's sorry face, and at Gopal's surprised face, and he smiled.

"Kiran," he said, "if you had asked, Gopal would have given you brinjals. Asking is always better than sneaking."

"You are right," said Kiran. "From now on, I will ask before I take anything."

The next morning, Tenali Rama brought Kiran and Gopal to the king, and told the whole story — the flour on the path, the white footprints, and the boy who missed his mother's curry.

The king listened, and then he laughed a kind laugh. "Kiran," said the king, "from today, you may have two brinjals every week for your mother's curry. But you must ask Gopal first. And Gopal, from today, Kiran will help you water the brinjal plants."

"Gladly!" said Kiran, his face lighting up like the morning sun.

So from that day on, Kiran helped Gopal in the garden, watering the brinjal plants. And every week, Gopal picked two shiny purple brinjals and handed them to Kiran with a smile, and the whole kitchen smelled of warm spices and home.

And never again did a single brinjal go missing in the night.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: If you want something, ask for it — asking is always better than sneaking.
