# The Swan and the Crow

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

At the edge of a green forest lay a blue pond. The water was cool and clear, and lotus flowers floated like pink stars.

On that pond lived a white swan named Lila. Every morning she glided across the water, slow and smooth, her long neck high and her feathers shining in the sun.

In a tall tree beside the pond lived a grey crow named Kalu. Every morning he watched Lila from his branch and sighed sadly.

"Oh, how I wish I could swim like Lila!" he said. "She is so graceful, and I am only a grey crow."

One sunny morning, Kalu flapped down from his tree and landed on the bank — thump!

"Good morning, Lila!" he called. "Today I shall swim like you!"

Lila smiled kindly. "Good morning, Kalu," she said. "The water is lovely today. But be careful — swimming is not quite like flying."

But Kalu was not listening. He took a deep breath, spread his wings, and jumped in — SPLASH!

Water went everywhere. Kalu flapped and flapped, kicked and kicked, and went round and round, his grey feathers all wet and floppy.

"Help!" squawked Kalu. "The water is wobbly! My wings are heavy! I am going round and round!"

Lila glided over, quick as a breeze, and held out her strong white wing. Kalu grabbed it with his beak.

"Hold on tight," said Lila. "I have got you."

Slowly, gently, she pulled him to the bank. Kalu climbed out, dripping wet, and shook himself — shake, shake, shake! Drops of water flew everywhere like tiny diamonds.

"Thank you, Lila," said Kalu in a small voice. "I wanted to be graceful like you, but I only made a wobbly muddle."

Lila smiled. "Dear Kalu," she said, "you do not need to be a swan to be wonderful. Can a swan fly as fast as you? Can a swan swoop and turn the way you do?"

Kalu looked up at the sky. "No," he said slowly. "I suppose not."

"Then let me show you a few gentle strokes," said Lila, "and you can paddle with me, just for fun — just to enjoy the water."

So Lila showed him how to float, soft and still, and how to paddle, slow and easy. Kalu tried — one stroke, then another. He did not glide like Lila, and he wobbled and splashed a little. But he laughed, and the water laughed with him.

"Look!" he cried. "I am floating! I am paddling! I am not a swan, but I can swim a little!"

"You see?" said Lila. "You are a wonderful flyer, and now a happy paddler too. Being yourself is best of all."

That afternoon, Kalu flew up into the sky, swooping and turning, faster than any swan could fly. The wind rushed past his feathers, and he felt light and free and happy.

Down below, Lila glided across the blue pond, her white feathers shining in the sun. And each was glad to be exactly who they were.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: You do not need to be someone else to be wonderful — being yourself is the best thing of all.*
