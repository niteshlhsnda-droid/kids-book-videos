# Alfred and the Burnt Cakes

Once upon a time, long ago in England, there lived a king named Alfred.

Now, a king wears a golden crown — but Alfred was a tired, worried king. A great army had come to his shores, and his soldiers had been driven back, back, back. Alfred had to run and hide, so that no one would find him.

He set aside his crown and kingly cloak, dressed in the plain brown clothes of a farmer, and walked deep into the dark forest, where a swineherd and his kind wife lived in a small wooden hut.

"Please," said Alfred, "I am a soldier whose road is long. May I rest by your fire tonight?"

The goodwife saw his gentle eyes, and she felt sorry for him.

"You may," she said. "I am baking cakes for supper, and I must go and milk the cow. Will you watch the cakes? Do not let them burn."

"I will watch them," promised Alfred.

But as soon as the goodwife stepped out, Alfred began to think. He thought about his soldiers, far away in the hills, and how frightened his people must be. He thought and he thought, his forehead wrinkled with worry — and all the while, the cakes sat in the oven, growing hotter and hotter.

The goodwife hurried back, her pail of milk swinging. And what do you think she smelled? Smoke!

She pulled open the little oven door. There they were — black on the bottom, burnt and smoky, quite spoiled for supper.

"Oh dear," said Alfred, with a start. "I forgot. I am so sorry."

"Lazy fellow!" she scolded, hands on her hips. "You were asked to do one small thing, and you did not do it!"

She grumbled as she scraped the burnt cakes away. But seeing how truly sad he looked, her voice softened.

"Well," she sighed, "supper will be thinner tonight. But sit down, soldier. We shall share what we have."

Then there was a clatter of horses outside. His captains burst into the hut, knelt in the doorway, and cried, "Our king! We have found you at last!"

The goodwife's eyes went round as the moon. "King? You are the king?"

Alfred smiled a tired, warm smile. "Tonight I am only a guest who burned your supper," he said.

The goodwife blushed red as an apple. "Forgive me, Your Majesty! I scolded you!"

"Do not be sorry," said Alfred. "You were right to scold me. A king must watch the cakes like anyone else."

That very winter, Alfred won back his land. But he never forgot the little hut in the forest, or the goodwife who had scolded a king — and shared her thin supper with him all the same.

Sleep now, King Alfred, keeper of cakes and keeper of a kind heart.
