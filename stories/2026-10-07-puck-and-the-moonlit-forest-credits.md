# Credits — "Puck and the Moonlit Forest"

- **Original title:** *Tales from Shakespeare* — the "A Midsummer Night's Dream" tale
- **Author:** Charles Lamb (1775–1834) and Mary Lamb (1764–1847), retelling William Shakespeare's play; first published 1807
- **Illustrator:** none (all illustrations generated fresh for this adaptation)
- **Source URL:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/573 (Project Gutenberg; the queue's Tier 1 item #5, verified 2026-10-07 — listed as "Public domain in the USA", original 1807, out of copyright worldwide)
- **License:** Public domain
- **Adaptation note:** Retold in simple, warm language for ages 4–7 (~446 words). Lamb's tale of the fairy court is distilled to a single gentle bedtime arc: Puck the tiny mischievous fairy is sent to fetch a moonflower petal, his sneeze scatters the petals across the forest, and he gently mends his own mess — apologizing to the owl, thanking the spider, and earning the dance back for Titania. The play's love-potion plot is replaced with the petal-scattering, which carries the shown-not-stated lesson: when your fun makes a mess, mending it gently is the finest trick of all. Ends with a goodnight closing ("Sleep now, little Puck.").
