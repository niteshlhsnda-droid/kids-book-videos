The Fox and the Horse

A gentle Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, there lived a farmer and his old horse.

The horse had worked hard all his life. He had pulled the plough through the fields, spring after spring. He had carried heavy sacks of grain to the mill. He had been a good, strong, faithful horse.

But now the horse was old. His legs were slow, his back was stiff, and he could not pull the plough any more.

One morning, the farmer said, "Old horse, you cannot work any more. If you can bring me a lion, you may stay. If not, you must go away."

The poor old horse walked slowly out of the yard, his head hanging low. "A lion?" he thought. "How can an old horse like me catch a lion?"

As he walked along the road, he met a clever little fox.

"Why are you so sad, old friend?" asked the fox.

The horse told him everything. The fox twitched his ears and smiled.

"Don't worry," said the fox. "I have a plan. Lie down here on the grass and pretend to be fast asleep. Pretend you cannot move at all. I will do the rest."

The horse lay down in the grass and closed his eyes. He breathed slowly — in… out… in… out — just like a horse fast asleep.

Soon, a big lion came padding down the road. He saw the horse lying in the grass.

"A horse, all alone, and fast asleep!" growled the lion, licking his lips. "What a fine dinner!"

But before the lion could pounce, the little fox darted out of the bushes.

"Wait, dear lion!" cried the fox. "This horse is my friend. But look — his tail is all tangled! Please, let me tie it up for you, so it will not trip you while you eat."

The lion was so busy thinking about his dinner that he said, "Oh, very well. Hurry up!"

Quick as a wink, the fox tied the lion's tail to the horse's tail with a strong rope — knot, knot, knot!

Then the fox whispered in the horse's ear, "Now! Up you get — and run!"

The horse jumped up and galloped down the road — and the lion went bumping along behind him, over the stones and through the puddles!

"Stop! Stop! Let me go!" roared the lion. But the fox just laughed and waved.

At last, the horse galloped right into the farmer's yard. The farmer came out and clapped his hands.

"A lion! You have brought me a lion!" cried the farmer. "You clever old horse!"

The farmer untied the rope, and the lion ran straight back to the forest, shaking his head. "Never again!" he growled. "Never trust a fox with a rope!"

And the farmer patted the old horse's neck. "You may stay with me for the rest of your days," he said. "You shall have soft hay, fresh water, and the warmest stable in the village."

The horse neighed happily. And every evening, the little fox came to visit, and the two old friends shared apples under the apple tree.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever friend and a brave heart can solve even the biggest problem.
