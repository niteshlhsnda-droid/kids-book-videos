The Wind and the Sun

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

High up in the sky lived two great friends: the Wind and the Sun.

One breezy morning, the Wind puffed out his cheeks and said, "I am the strongest thing in the whole world! Nothing can stand against me!"

The Sun smiled her warm, golden smile. "Are you sure, dear Wind?" she said. "Strength is not always about blowing the hardest."

"Oh, it is!" said the Wind. "Watch this!"

Just then, a traveller named Theo came walking along the road below, wearing a big, warm coat. He was on his way to visit his grandmother, and he whistled a happy tune as he walked.

"Let us have a contest," said the Wind. "Whoever can make Theo take off his coat first is the strongest. I will go first!"

The Sun nodded. "Very well. You try first."

The Wind took a deep breath and blew — WHOOOSH!

He blew so hard that the trees bent and the leaves flew round and round. Theo's hat nearly flew off his head!

"Oh my!" cried Theo, clutching his hat. "What a wild wind!"

But instead of taking off his coat, Theo pulled it tighter around himself. He buttoned every button and wrapped his scarf twice around his neck.

The Wind blew harder — WHOOOOSH! — until the dust danced on the road and the branches swayed.

But the harder the Wind blew, the tighter Theo held his coat. At last the Wind was all out of breath, and he puffed, "I give up! I cannot make him take it off!"

Now it was the Sun's turn. She did not blow or bluster at all. She simply shone — soft and warm and golden.

Her rays fell gently on the road, on the trees, and on Theo's shoulders. The air grew warm and sweet. The birds began to sing.

"Ahh," said Theo, smiling. "What lovely sunshine!"

Soon Theo felt so warm that he unbuttoned his coat. Then he slipped one arm out, then the other, and folded his coat neatly over his arm.

"There," said Theo happily. "That is much better!"

The Wind stared. "You did it without even trying hard!" he said.

The Sun smiled. "That is the secret, dear Wind," she said. "Gentleness can do what force cannot. A warm smile opens what a hard blow only shuts tighter."

The Wind thought about that. Then he tried something new. Instead of a wild gust, he sent a soft, cool breeze that rustled the leaves like a lullaby.

"That is lovely," said the Sun, and they both laughed together in the sky.

From that day on, the Wind still loved to blow — but on warm days, he blew softly, just enough to make the flowers dance, while the Sun shone down and made the whole world golden.

And Theo arrived at his grandmother's house feeling warm, happy, and just right.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Gentleness and warmth can do what force cannot.
