# The Cat and the Mouse

*A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little cottage at the edge of a green field lived a cat named Kiki and a mouse named Mimi.

Kiki was orange and fluffy, with a long, swishy tail. Mimi was small and grey, with a tiny pink nose that went twitch, twitch, twitch.

Kiki and Mimi were nervous around each other.

Kiki thought, "Mice are for chasing. That is what cats do."

Mimi thought, "Cats are for running away from. That is what mice do."

So every morning, Kiki chased Mimi around the cottage — round the table, under the chair, behind the curtain — and every morning, Mimi darted into her tiny mouse-hole just in time.

But one day, something different happened.

A big dog named Bruno came bounding into the field. Bruno was huge and shaggy, with a bark like thunder: WOOF! WOOF!

Mimi was nibbling a crumb in the field when she heard the bark. "Eek!" she squeaked. "A dog! A big dog!" And off she scampered, as fast as her tiny legs could carry her.

At the very same moment, Kiki was padding through the tall grass — when SNAP! A rope loop on the ground tightened gently around her paw. It was a snare, left there by mistake. She could only sit in the grass, feeling frightened and stuck.

"Help!" cried Kiki. "I am caught! I cannot get free!"

Mimi heard her. She stopped scampering. She peeked out from behind a buttercup.

"The cat is caught," thought Mimi. "And the dog is coming."

But then Mimi looked at Kiki's frightened face, and her little heart went soft.

"Kiki," she called, "I will help you! But you must help me too."

"Anything!" said Kiki. "Anything at all!"

So Mimi scampered over and began to nibble the rope with her tiny, sharp teeth. Nibble, nibble, nibble. Gnaw, gnaw, gnaw. The rope was thick, and the work was slow, but Mimi did not stop.

Meanwhile, Bruno came bounding closer. WOOF! WOOF!

"Quick, Mimi, quick!" whispered Kiki.

"I am hurrying!" said Mimi. Nibble, nibble, nibble — SNAP! The rope broke apart, and Kiki's paw was free!

"Thank you, Mimi!" cried Kiki. And then she did something she had never, ever done before. She stood up tall, puffed out her fluffy fur, and ran straight at Bruno, crying, "Shoo! Shoo! Leave my friend alone!"

Bruno stopped. He looked at the puffed-up, brave, fluffy cat. And he thought, "Oh. This cat is not afraid of me at all."

So Bruno turned around and trotted away, his floppy ears bouncing, to chase butterflies instead.

Kiki and Mimi sat down together in the grass, breathing hard, safe at last.

"You saved me," said Kiki softly.

"You saved me," said Mimi softly.

And they both laughed — a small, happy, giggly laugh.

From that day on, everything was different. Kiki did not chase Mimi anymore. Instead, they shared crumbs in the sunshine, and Kiki let Mimi ride on her soft, warm back around the cottage.

They were friends. True friends. The kind of friends who help each other when help is needed most.

That evening, as the sun went down and the field turned gold, Kiki curled up by the cottage door, and Mimi curled up beside her, safe and warm. And they both fell asleep, side by side.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When we help each other, even unlikely friends become true friends.*
