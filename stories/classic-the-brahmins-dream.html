The Brahmin's Dream

A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a little village with mango trees and marigold flowers, there lived a kind brahmin named Bholu.

Bholu was a cheerful fellow who loved to dream. He dreamed while he walked. He dreamed while he ate. He dreamed most of all while he sat in the sun.

One morning, a neighbor gave Bholu a clay pot full of golden honey. "For your kindness," she said.

Bholu hugged the pot. "Thank you! Thank you!" He carried it home and hung it from a beam above his bed, so he could look at it while he rested.

That afternoon, Bholu lay on his bed, gazing up at the honey pot — and he began to dream.

"If I sell this honey in the market," he thought, "I will get ten silver coins."

"With ten silver coins I will buy two goats."

"The goats will have babies — four goats, then eight goats, then a whole herd of goats!"

"I will sell the goats and buy a cow. The cow will give sweet milk every day."

"I will sell the milk and buy a horse. I will ride the horse to the king's palace!"

"The king will say, 'Bholu, you are so wise!' And he will make me his chief minister!"

Bholu's dreams grew bigger and bigger. He imagined himself in fine silk clothes, riding a tall horse, waving to cheering crowds.

"And then," he thought, "when the king's dancers dance for me, I will tap my foot like this —"

And Bholu lifted his foot and KICKED it high in the air —

CRASH!

His foot struck the honey pot! It tumbled from the beam and smashed on the floor. Golden honey spread everywhere — on the bed, on the floor, on Bholu's toes!

Bholu sat up. His fine silk clothes were gone. His horse was gone. His goats and cow were gone.

All that was left was a broken pot and a very sticky Bholu.

For a moment, Bholu felt sad. Then he looked at the honey dripping from his toes — and he began to laugh! He laughed and laughed until his tummy hurt.

"What a dreamer I am!" he chuckled. "I was counting my goats before I even sold my honey!"

Just then his neighbor peeked in. "Bholu? Are you all right?"

Bholu grinned, holding up a sticky finger. "I am wonderful! But I have learned something. And I have a whole floor of honey — come, let us share it before the ants find it!"

So Bholu and his neighbor and all the children of the lane sat together and licked honey from their fingers, laughing at Bholu's big, beautiful dream.

And from that day on, whenever Bholu caught himself dreaming too far ahead, he would smile and say, "First the honey, Bholu — then the goats!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Enjoy your dreams, but live in today — first the honey, then the goats.
