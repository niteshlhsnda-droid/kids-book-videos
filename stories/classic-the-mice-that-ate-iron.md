# The Mice That Ate the Iron

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a busy market town, lived a kind merchant named Aman.

Aman had to travel far away to buy silk for his little shop. Before he left, he packed a big iron scale — the heavy scale he used to weigh his silk.

"I cannot carry this heavy scale on my journey," said Aman. "Dear friend Dev, will you keep it safe in your house until I come back?"

"Of course, my friend!" said Dev. "It will be safe with me."

Aman waved goodbye and set off on his long journey.

Now Dev looked at the shiny iron scale. "Hmm," he thought. "Iron is worth good money. If I sell this scale, no one will ever know."

So Dev sold Aman's iron scale at the market. He put the coins in his pocket and whistled a happy tune.

Many months later, Aman came home. He knocked on Dev's door.

"Welcome back, my friend!" said Dev.

"Thank you!" said Aman. "Now, may I have my iron scale back, please?"

Dev scratched his head and made a very sad face.

"Oh, Aman," he sighed. "Terrible news! While you were away, the mice came. They were very, very hungry. And they ate your whole iron scale. Crunch, crunch, crunch — all gone!"

Aman blinked. "Mice... ate my iron scale?"

"Yes!" said Dev quickly. "Every last bit. Even the little weights. Those mice have very strong teeth!"

Aman smiled a small, clever smile. "I see," he said. "Well, these things happen. Do not worry about it, my friend."

Dev felt very proud of his clever trick.

The next morning, Dev's little son Veer was playing outside. Aman was passing by on his way to the market.

"Hello, Veer!" said Aman kindly. "Would you like to come with me to see the river? The ducks are swimming there today."

"Yes, please!" said Veer, clapping his hands.

Aman took Veer's hand, and off they went to the sparkling river.

When Dev came home that evening, his little boy was not there.

"Where is Veer?" cried Dev, running to Aman's house. "Where is my son?"

Aman opened the door calmly. "Oh, Dev," he said. "Terrible news! While we were at the river, a big hawk swooped down from the sky. It picked up Veer in its claws and carried him away!"

Dev's eyes grew as round as mangoes. "A hawk carried away my boy? That is impossible! Hawks cannot carry little boys!"

Aman tilted his head. "Cannot they? But Dev, if hungry little mice can eat a whole iron scale — crunch, crunch, crunch — then surely a big strong hawk can carry a little boy?"

Dev's face turned red. Then pink. Then he laughed and laughed until tears ran down his cheeks.

"You are right, you are right!" said Dev. "No mice ate your scale. I sold it. I was greedy and foolish. Forgive me, my friend!"

Just then, Veer came running out from behind Aman, giggling. "Papa! I was hiding! We only wanted to teach you a lesson!"

Dev hugged his son tight. The very next day, he bought back the iron scale and returned it to Aman. He even added a bag of sweet jaggery to say sorry.

And from that day on, Dev never, ever told a fib again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever lie always meets a cleverer truth — so always be honest.*
