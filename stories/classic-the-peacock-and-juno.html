The Peacock and Juno

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a sunny garden lived a peacock named Percy.

Oh, Percy was beautiful! His tail opened like a great green-and-gold fan, and every feather had an eye that shone blue and bronze in the sunlight.

But Percy was not happy. Every evening, he heard the nightingale sing — a sweet, silver song that floated over the garden like moonlight.

"My voice is harsh and loud," sighed Percy. "The nightingale sings like an angel, and I sound like a rusty gate! It is not fair!"

So Percy spread his great wings and flew up, up, up to the clouds, where Juno, the queen of the sky, sat among the stars.

"Dear Juno," said Percy, bowing low, "please give me a beautiful voice like the nightingale's. Mine is so ugly that I am ashamed to sing."

Juno smiled a kind, starry smile. "Come closer, Percy, and look at yourself in my silver mirror."

Percy looked. There was his tail — green and gold, with a hundred shining eyes.

"Now listen," said Juno gently. "The nightingale has a lovely voice — but does she have YOUR tail? Every gift is given to someone. The nightingale got the song. You got the beauty."

Percy blinked. He had never thought of it that way.

"Be happy with your own gift, Percy," said Juno. "The garden needs your colors just as much as it needs her song."

Percy flew back down to the garden — down, down, down — his heart light as a feather.

That evening, when the nightingale sang her silver song, Percy opened his great tail wide and danced in the sunset. Green! Gold! Blue!

"You sing beautifully," he called to the nightingale. "And I shall dance beautifully. The garden needs us both!"

And from that evening on, whenever the nightingale sang, Percy danced — and the whole garden glowed.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Be happy with your own gift — the world needs exactly what you have.
