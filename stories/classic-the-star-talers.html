The Star Talers

A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there was a kind little girl named Greta.

Greta had very little in the world. Her mother and father were gone, and all she owned was a small loaf of bread, a thin cap, a dress, and a shawl to keep her warm.

But Greta had something much bigger than all of those things: she had a heart full of kindness.

One cold evening, Greta was walking through the forest when she met an old man sitting by the path. His face was pale and tired, and he looked very hungry.

"Little girl," he said in a shaky voice, "I have not eaten all day. Could you spare me a bite of bread?"

Greta did not think twice. She broke her loaf in half and gave the bigger half to the old man.

"Thank you, dear child," said the old man, smiling. "May your kindness come back to you."

Greta walked on. Soon she met a little boy who was shivering in the cold wind. He had no cap, and his ears were pink with cold.

"Please," he whispered, "I am so cold."

Greta took off her own thin cap and placed it gently on the boy's head. "There," she said. "Now your ears will be warm."

The boy beamed. "Thank you!" he said, and he ran off happily down the path.

A little further on, Greta met a girl who had only a thin dress and no shawl at all. The wind blew right through her.

Greta wrapped her own shawl around the girl's shoulders. "Keep it," she said. "It will keep you cosy."

"Thank you, thank you," said the girl, hugging the shawl close.

By now, Greta had given away her bread, her cap, and her shawl. She had only her dress left, and the night was growing dark.

Then, deep in the forest, Greta saw something that made her stop. A tiny bird lay on the ground, its wing tangled in a bramble. The bird was cold and frightened.

"Oh, you poor little thing," said Greta. She knelt down, freed the bird's wing very gently, and wrapped it in the soft lining of her dress to keep it warm.

The bird chirped softly, as if to say thank you, and fluttered up into the trees.

Now Greta stood alone in the dark forest, with almost nothing left. But instead of feeling sad, she felt light and peaceful inside, like a candle glowing in the dark.

And then — oh, wonder of wonders!

The sky above her began to twinkle. One by one, the stars came loose from the heavens and floated down, soft and silvery, all around her. As each star touched the ground, it turned into a shiny silver coin — a taler!

Soon the forest floor was sparkling with silver talers. And the last star of all floated down onto Greta's shoulders and turned into the softest, warmest shawl she had ever worn.

Greta laughed with joy. She gathered up the silver talers, thanked the stars, and walked home through the forest, warm and safe, with the little bird singing above her.

From that day on, Greta never went hungry or cold again. And whenever she met someone in need, she shared what she had — because she knew that a giving heart is never empty.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A giving heart is never empty — kindness always finds its way back.
