Tenali Rama and the Painted Horse

A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the shining city of Vijayanagara, there lived a kind king named Krishnadevaraya.

King Krishnadevaraya loved horses more than anything in the whole world — their soft brown coats, their swish-swish tails, and the clip-clop of their hooves.

But one morning, the king let out a long sigh.

"My horses are lovely," he said, "but they eat and eat and eat. They grow tired on long journeys. And sometimes, when a gate is left open, they run away!"

The king called his courtiers — the people who helped him rule — to the palace hall.

"Listen, everyone!" he said. "I wish for a horse that never needs food, never gets tired, and never, ever runs away. Bring me such a horse, and I will give you a bag of gold coins!"

The courtiers scratched their heads and whispered to one another. "A horse that never eats? A horse that never gets tired? Such a horse does not exist!"

But in the corner stood Tenali Rama, the king's cleverest friend, smiling a quiet little smile.

"Your Majesty," said Tenali Rama, bowing low, "give me one day, and I will bring you just such a horse."

"Wonderful!" cried the king. "I will wait until tomorrow morning!"

That day, Tenali Rama went to a tall white wall in the palace garden and opened his pots of paint.

Brush, brush, brush went his brush. Swish, swish, swish went his hand.

He painted a horse with a flowing mane, bright eyes, and a swishing tail.

All day long he painted, and when the sun sank low and golden, he stepped back and smiled. There on the wall stood the most beautiful horse anyone had ever seen.

The next morning, the king hurried to the garden. "Where is my horse, Tenali Rama? Where is my horse?"

Tenali Rama bowed low and pointed at the wall. "Here is your horse, Your Majesty," he said. "It eats nothing, it never gets tired, and it will never run away!"

The king stared. The courtiers stared. Then the king burst out laughing — ha, ha, ha!

"Oh, Tenali Rama!" laughed the king. "You are the cleverest man in all of Vijayanagara!"

Still chuckling, the king picked up an orange carrot and held it out to the painted horse. "Here you are, my fine horse. Eat, eat!"

But the painted horse just smiled its painted smile and did not move at all.

"See, Your Majesty?" said Tenali Rama, with a twinkle in his eye. "It eats nothing, it never tires, and it will stay right here forever."

Then the king took off his gold necklace and placed it around Tenali Rama's neck. "For your cleverness, my friend," said the king. "You have given me the very horse I wished for."

That evening, under the soft moon, the king walked past the painted horse once more. "Good night, my fine horse," he whispered. "You are the best horse of all — because you are always here."

And in the quiet garden, under the silver moonlight, the painted horse stood still, watching over the sleeping city.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever idea can solve what seems impossible — wit is worth more than gold.
