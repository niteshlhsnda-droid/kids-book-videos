Nasruddin Counts His Donkeys

A timeless Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One golden morning, Nasruddin knotted his ten donkeys to one long rope — knot, knot, knot, all the way down the line. The big gray one stood first, flicking its tail. The little brown ones stood behind.

"Off to the market we go," said Nasruddin. He climbed onto the back of the big gray donkey, straightened his turban, and clapped his hands. "Yah! Walk on, my friends."

Tap-tap-tap went the donkey hooves down the dusty road.

Tap-tap-tap went the hooves a little longer, and then Nasruddin twisted all the way around in his saddle to peek back at his donkeys.

"One," he said, pointing. "Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine."

He frowned.

"NINE?" he shouted. "Only nine? I had ten!"

He counted again, slower this time.

"One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine."

Still nine.

Nasruddin's chin started to wobble with worry. "Oh no. Oh no, no, no. One of my donkeys is lost!"

He slid off the big gray donkey and ran to the back of the line. The donkeys blinked at him, calm and still. They were not worried at all.

"Don't you worry," Nasruddin told them. "I'll count again — slow and careful this time."

He pointed at each one. "One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten."

"Ten!" he cried, clapping his hands. "All ten! Nobody is lost!"

He climbed back onto the big gray donkey and rode on, humming a soft, wobbly little tune that bobbed along with the donkey's steps.

Tap-tap-tap went the hooves.

After a little while, Nasruddin looked back again.

"One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine."

His turban tilted a little sideways. "Nine again?" he said softly, scratching his beard. "Hmm... where does my tenth donkey keep wandering off to, I wonder?"

Down he climbed. He ran to the back of the line, puffing.

"One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten."

"Ten!" he said, wiping his forehead. "Phew! There you all are."

Up he climbed again. On they went.

This happened three whole times. Down and up. Down and up. The donkeys' ears wiggled — they looked like they were trying not to giggle, the way only donkeys can.

Just then, his friend Amara came walking up the road with a basket of warm bread.

"Nasruddin!" she called. "Why are you climbing on and off your donkey, up and down, up and down?"

"Amara," said Nasruddin, stroking his beard, "a funny little puzzle keeps happening. When I ride, I count nine donkeys. When I walk, I count ten. It is quite the mystery."

Amara looked at the line of donkeys. Then she looked at Nasruddin, sitting on the big gray one. "Can YOU see the tenth donkey?" And she started to laugh — a big, warm laugh.

"Nasruddin," she said, pointing, "you are sitting on the tenth!"

Nasruddin looked down. Under him stood the big gray donkey, patient as ever.

"Oh!" he said, and began to giggle — a soft giggle that bubbled up like warm milk. The giggle went on until his turban tilted sideways, and the donkeys wiggled their ears right along with him. Then the laugh softened into a sleepy little chuckle, and all was quiet again.

"Ah," he said at last, catching his breath and patting the big gray donkey's neck. "Ha! The tenth donkey was right under me the whole time!"

Amara shared her warm bread with him — soft and fresh, smelling of the morning bakery. Nasruddin shared a piece with the big gray donkey too — the poor thing had carried the silliest rider in the whole world.

And that night, Nasruddin rode home slowly under the soft, sleepy moon, patting the big gray donkey's neck and smiling. He had counted all ten this time — including the one underneath him.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Sometimes the answer is right where you're sitting — count again, and laugh.
