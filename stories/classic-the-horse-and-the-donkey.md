# The Horse and the Donkey

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a sunny village, beside a dusty road, there lived a strong brown horse named Hugo.

Hugo was tall and proud, with a shiny mane and swift, clip-clopping hooves.

Beside him walked a small grey donkey named Danny, with soft ears and a kind, tired smile.

Danny carried a heavy load of wheat on his back, tied up in a big brown sack. The sack was heavy. Very heavy.

One morning, the sun was warm, and the road was long, and Danny's legs felt wobbly.

"Please, Hugo," said Danny, in a soft, tired voice. "Could you carry a little of my load? Just a little? My legs are so tired."

Hugo tossed his shiny mane and laughed. "Ha! I am a horse, not a helper! Carry your own sack, little donkey."

Danny sighed and kept walking. Clip-clop went Hugo's hooves. Plod, plod, went Danny's tired feet.

The sun climbed higher. The road grew dustier.

"Please, Hugo," said Danny again, even softer. "Just a tiny bit? My back is aching."

"No, no, no!" said Hugo. "I will not carry your sack."

So Danny kept walking, slow and slower, until his legs trembled like little leaves.

Then, right in the middle of the road, Danny stopped. He sat down, soft and slow, in the dust.

"I am sorry," said Danny, closing his tired eyes. "I cannot walk one more step."

Hugo looked back. There was Danny, sitting in the dust, with the big sack beside him.

"Get up, Danny!" said Hugo. "We must hurry home!"

"I cannot," whispered Danny. "My legs will not move."

Then the farmer came walking down the road. He looked at Danny, and he looked at Hugo.

"Well, Hugo," said the farmer, kindly, "if Danny cannot walk, then you must carry his load all the way home."

Hugo's ears drooped. All of it? The whole big sack?

The farmer lifted the heavy sack and placed it upon Hugo's strong back.

Oh, it was heavy! Hugo's legs wobbled, and his proud head drooped low.

"Oh dear," puffed Hugo. "This is much heavier than I thought."

Step by step, Hugo walked home, carrying the whole sack, all by himself.

At last, they reached the little farm. The farmer lifted the sack off Hugo's back, and Hugo gave a great, long sigh of relief.

"Thank you, Hugo," said Danny, smiling his kind smile. "You carried it all."

Hugo looked at his friend, and his cheeks turned a little pink.

"I am sorry, Danny," said Hugo, softly. "If I had helped you carry a little, we could have carried it together, and it would have felt light for us both."

Danny nodded, and the two friends shared a sweet apple, crunch, crunch, in the shade of the old apple tree.

From that day on, whenever Danny's sack felt heavy, Hugo carried a little of it, right away, with a smile.

That evening, Hugo and Danny curled up in the soft hay and closed their eyes, dreaming of sunny roads and shared apples.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Helping early is lighter for everyone — a little help shared is a load halved.*
