The Golden Swan

A wise Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, in a tiny cottage at the edge of a quiet pond, lived a little family who had almost nothing. They were kind people, and they shared what little they had, but some days there was barely enough bread for supper. You can imagine how their tummies rumbled at night — grrroomph! — as if a tiny frog had hopped right inside.

One morning, as the sun rose over the pond, the children heard a soft swish of wings. Down from the sky glided a swan with feathers of bright gold, each one sparkling like a coin held up to the sunlight.

"Hello, little family," said the swan in a warm, gentle voice. "I love your kind hearts, and I want to help you. Whenever you need something, little family, take just one feather — only one — and sell it. One at a time, and you will always have enough."

The youngest child gasped, "But won't you get cold with a hole in your feathers?" The swan chuckled, a soft honk-honk-honk. "Only one feather, my dear. A swan can spare one feather."

The family beamed. They took one shining feather, just as the swan said, and sold it in the village. With the money they bought just what they needed most — can you guess what? Bread and milk, warm blankets, and a cozy little lamp for the dark evenings — and one feather-shaped cookie cutter, just for fun. At bedtime they snuggled under blankets as soft and heavy as warm hugs, and the little lamp painted the walls with honey-colored light. Every day they thanked the swan.

But one night, the mother lay awake in the dark, turning this way and that in her blanket while an owl hooted far away across the pond, thinking and thinking. "If one feather gives us all this," she whispered to herself, "then imagine what all the feathers would give us! We would never need anything again." And she hatched a secret plan she told to no one.

When the swan came to visit the next morning, the mother hurried out and, before the swan could speak, gathered up every single golden feather, all at once.

But something sad happened. The moment the last feather came off, the feathers turned dull and grey, and were not worth a single coin. Sad and tired, the swan spread its bare wings and floated softly away into the sky.

The family looked down at the floor, feeling so sorry. The father hugged the mother and said softly, "Hush now, little ones. The swan loved us, and we hurt it. It was giving us enough, one feather at a time. From now on, we take only what we need, and we say thank you." The children felt sad, and the mother felt sad too, because wanting too much had spoiled the beautiful gift.

Days passed, and the family learned to live simply again. They tore warm, crusty bread apart with their fingers, and were thankful. And when they squeezed each other tight before bed, they were thankful for love most of all.

Then, as the stars began to twinkle over the sleepy pond, the family sat close together and remembered the golden swan circling once above the little cottage, as if to say, "I forgive you. Be happy." The mother's heart felt light as a feather. From that day on, the family never wished for too much. They had enough, and enough was a wonderful thing.

So now, my little one, you can close your eyes and dream of a golden swan gliding over a sleepy pond. All is calm, all is forgiven, and everything is enough.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Be thankful for enough — wanting too much spoils the gift.
