# Willie Winkie and the Sleepy Town

Have you heard the old rhyme about Wee Willie Winkie? He runs through the town in his nightgown, rapping at the window, calling through the lock: "Are the children in their beds? For now it's eight o'clock!"

Well — one autumn evening, Willie Winkie could not fall asleep himself.

He lay in his little bed, watching the moon climb past his window. He counted stars. He counted the clock's ticking. But his eyes would not stay shut.

"I am the night-watcher," Willie whispered. "Perhaps everyone else is sleepy, and only I am wide awake."

So he pulled on his soft blue nightgown with tiny yellow stars, slipped into his felt slippers, and picked up his little brass lantern. Then he tiptoed down the stairs — creep, creep, creep — and out into the lane.

The town was already dreaming. Curtains were drawn. Chimneys puffed sleepy little rings of smoke. A cat dozed on a windowsill.

Willie held his lantern high. "Are the children in their beds?" he whispered at the baker's window. Two small heads lay on pillows, breathing slow and deep. Good.

At the duck pond, something rustled. Three ducklings had drifted away from their mother in the dark. They peeped and peeped, quite lost.

"Shh," said Willie, and he swung his lantern gently side to side. The ducklings followed the warm light, like a golden ribbon, right back to their mother's soft feathers.

Willie smiled — and just then, a cold gust of wind swept down the lane. Puff! His lantern flame flickered... and went out.

Oh! The lane went dark all around him.

Willie stood very still — then he looked up.

The moon shone, round and kind. A thousand stars winked like tiny lanterns. He didn't need his little lamp at all — the whole night was glowing.

"Thank you," Willie whispered to the moon.

Softer than before, he climbed the winding hill to the very top of the town. There he looked down at all the dark, quiet rooftops, and he sang the night-watching song in his softest voice:

"Are the children in their beds? For now it's eight o'clock."

Every window was dark, every chimney quiet. The whole town was tucked in — everyone except one small boy on a hilltop.

Willie hurried home, up the stairs — creep, creep, creep — and climbed into his own warm bed. His eyelids felt heavy at last — heavy as velvet curtains.

His mother peeked through the door. "My brave night-watcher," she whispered, kissing his forehead.

Willie pulled the quilt to his chin. The moon watched through the window, the stars winked — and one small, tired boy finally closed his eyes.

Sleep now, little Willie.

The end. 🌙
