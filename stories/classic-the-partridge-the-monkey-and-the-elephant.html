The Partridge, the Monkey and the Elephant

A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Near the edge of a deep green forest stood a great big banyan tree. Its branches spread wide like open arms, and its leaves whispered in the wind.

And near that tree lived three good friends.

Pippa the partridge — a small, round bird with speckled feathers and bright, curious eyes.

Milo the monkey — who loved to swing from branch to branch, chattering all day long.

And Ellie the elephant — the biggest elephant anyone had ever seen, with kind grey eyes and a trunk that could lift a whole tree trunk.

One quiet afternoon, the three friends sat under the banyan tree, and a little argument began.

"I am the biggest," said Ellie proudly, "so I must be the eldest. And the eldest should be respected. So you must both respect ME."

"Hold on, hold on!" said Milo, scratching his head. "Being the BIGGEST is not the same as being the ELDEST. The eldest is the one who has been here the LONGEST — the one who knew this tree first."

"That is fair," said Ellie, nodding her great head.

So the three friends decided to find out — who had known the banyan tree the longest?

Ellie closed her big eyes and thought very hard. "I remember," she said slowly, "when this tree was just a little bush. It only came up to my knee! I could step right over it — step, step, step. So I must be the eldest."

"No, no!" cried Milo, bouncing up and down. "I remember when this tree was even smaller — a tiny baby plant with soft green buds. I nibbled its tender buds — nibble, nibble, nibble! They were delicious. So I must be the eldest."

Then little Pippa puffed up her round chest. She took a deep breath.

"You are both wrong," she chirped in her tiny, clear voice. "I remember when there was NO tree here at all. Just an empty patch of earth. I held the tiny seed in my beak — no bigger than a grain of rice — and dropped it. Ploop! Right into the soft soil. That seed grew, and grew, and grew — into this great big banyan tree."

Milo's mouth fell open. Ellie's trunk curled up in surprise. And the banyan tree rustled its thousand leaves — as if it was nodding, nodding, nodding.

"Then YOU are the eldest," said Ellie gently, bowing her great grey head. "And we will honor you, little sister."

"Yes!" said Milo, bowing low. "We will respect you always."

And from that day on, Ellie and Milo took care of little Pippa, just as younger ones should care for their elder.

Every morning, Ellie lifted Pippa gently onto her broad head, so Pippa could see the whole forest from up high — the rivers, the hills, the birds flying far, far away.

Milo climbed the tallest trees and picked the sweetest, ripest mangoes — and he always gave the very first one to Pippa.

And every evening, Pippa sang them the loveliest bedtime song in her tiny, clear voice, and the banyan tree rustled along, sh-sh-sh, like it was singing too.

The three friends lived together happily — the younger ones respecting their elder, and their elder loving them right back.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Respect your elders, no matter how small they are.
