# The Princes and the Water-Sprite

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, a king had three brave sons. The eldest was the Star Prince, the middle one the Moon Prince, and the youngest — the littlest — the Sun Prince.

The king had promised the queen one wish — and she wished that her youngest should be king. So the two elder brothers slipped away to live in the forest for a while, so their little brother could be king. "I am coming too!" cried the Sun Prince. "We three stay together!"

Off they went into the forest, where the path was soft with fallen leaves and the tall trees hummed with bees.

At noon they grew thirsty, and found a pond that glittered in the bright sunlight. "You two drink first," said the eldest. "I'll watch the water."

But in that pond lived a water-sprite — a small, grumpy pond creature — no bigger than a teapot, with green pond-weed hair and a round, pouty face. Long ago, the King of the Fairies had told him, "You may hold anyone who cannot answer my riddle." And the riddle was this: What are the Good Fairies like? (The Fairies are the kind spirits who watch over good hearts, you know.)

The Sun Prince bent to drink — and SPLISH! — the water-sprite scooped him up and set him down gently in his underwater cave. Then the Moon Prince came to look for him — SPLISH! — and he landed in the cave too. The two brothers sat together in the cave, where the water glowed soft blue and smooth pebbles felt cool under their hands, waiting for the Star Prince.

At last the eldest prince — the Star Prince — came. The water-sprite rose from the water. "Answer my riddle, or join your brothers! What are the Good Fairies like?" Hmm… what do YOU think the Good Fairies are like? Whisper your answer…

He grinned: "Easy — like the sun! Big and bright and warm!"

The water-sprite blinked. "Hmm… no."

"Like the moon… all soft and silvery?"

"No!"

"Like a star… twinkling high above?"

The water-sprite shook his head. "No, no, no!"

The Star Prince thought harder. Then he smiled. "The Good Fairies are like someone with a kind heart — someone who does kind things, says kind words, and always helps."

The water-sprite clapped his hands softly. "Correct," he whispered, and his grumpy face melted into the biggest smile. "A kind heart. That's the answer." And he lifted all three brothers out of the cave.

The three princes hugged, with sleepy smiles on their faces — and that night they curled up together under the trees, safe and warm, and slept till morning.

Goodnight, princes. The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart is the greatest wisdom — goodness answers every riddle.*
