The Lark and the Farmer

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a wide green field, where the wheat grew tall and gold, a little lark named Lila built her nest.

She tucked it down low, soft and safe, between the wheat stems. There she laid four tiny eggs, speckled brown like little stones.

Soon the eggs cracked open — pop, pop, pop, pop! — and out came four baby larks, pink and fluffy, with beaks open wide.

"Cheep, cheep!" they cried. "Mummy, we are hungry!"

Every morning Lila flew off to find seeds for her babies. Every evening she came back to sing them a soft song and tuck them under her warm wings.

One morning, as Lila was about to fly away, she heard a sound. Stomp, stomp, stomp! It was the farmer, walking through his field.

The farmer looked at the tall wheat and rubbed his chin. "This wheat is ripe and ready," he said. "Tomorrow I will ask my neighbours to come and help me cut it."

Lila's heart went thump, thump, thump. She flew back to her nest in a hurry.

"Little ones, do not be afraid," she said. "The farmer will ask his neighbours. But neighbours are busy people. They may say yes, and then forget. We are safe for now."

The next morning the farmer came again. He looked at the wheat and shook his head.

"My neighbours did not come," he said. "Tomorrow I will ask my cousins to come and help me cut the wheat."

Lila listened from her nest. "Not yet, little ones," she said softly. "The farmer will ask his cousins. But cousins live far away. They may come, or they may not. We are safe."

Day after day, the farmer came to the field. Day after day, he had a new plan.

"Tomorrow my friends will help me," he said one day.

"Tomorrow my brother will help me," he said the next day.

Each time, Lila listened with one ear up. Each time, she said, "Not yet, little ones. He is still waiting for someone else."

But one morning the farmer came with a big, sharp sickle in his hand. He looked at the golden wheat, strong and sure.

"This wheat must be cut today," he said. "No more waiting. Tomorrow I will cut it myself, with my own two hands."

Lila's heart went thump, thump, thump — faster than ever before!

"This time it is different, little ones," she said. "When a person says, 'I will do it myself,' then it will really be done. It is time to move!"

So Lila picked up her babies, one by one, and flew them far away to a quiet meadow. There, under a big shady tree, she built a new nest — soft and safe, with soft grass and feathers.

The next morning, swish, swish, swish! The farmer cut his wheat all by himself.

And in the meadow, Lila sang her sweetest song, while her four baby larks snuggled close and drifted off to sleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: If something must be done, do it yourself — do not wait for others.
