# The Golden Mallard

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a little cottage by a silver pond, lived a kind family — a mother, a father, and their little daughter, Riya.

They were not rich. Their roof leaked when it rained, and their soup was often thin. But they shared everything, and they sang while they worked.

One golden evening, a beautiful duck landed softly on their pond. Quack!

But this was no ordinary duck. Every feather on her wings shone like pure gold in the setting sun.

"I am Sona," said the duck, in a voice like a tiny bell. "I have watched you share your little with everyone. Please — take one of my golden feathers. Sell it, and mend your roof."

Riya gently stroked one glowing feather, and — pluck! — Sona gave it gladly.

The father sold the feather in the town, and with the coins they mended the roof. The next week, Sona gave another feather, and they bought warm blankets.

Every full moon, Sona came back, and every time she left one golden feather behind.

But slowly, a greedy thought crept into the mother's heart.

"Why wait for the full moon?" she whispered one night. "If we pluck ALL her feathers at once, we will be rich forever!"

So the next time Sona landed, quack-quack, happy to see her friends, the mother caught her and plucked every golden feather — pluck, pluck, pluck!

Sona gave a soft, sad cry. And as the last feather came away, something strange happened.

The new feathers growing in were not gold at all. They were plain, ordinary white — like any pond duck's.

Sona looked at the family with sad, wise eyes. Then she spread her white wings and flew away over the silver pond — and never came back.

The cottage was quiet that night. Riya held her mother's hand.

"We had enough," Riya whispered. "We just wanted more and more."

From that day on, the family was poor again — but never greedy again. And every full moon, Riya left a bowl of grain by the pond, just in case Sona ever flew home.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Greed turns gold to grey — be thankful for enough.*
