The Thirsty Crow

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 3 minutes.

One hot, hot afternoon, the sun shone down like a big warm lamp.

A little crow flew over the fields, and his throat felt dry as dust.

"Caw!" he said. "I am so thirsty. I need a drink of water."

He flew here. He flew there. At last, near an old farmhouse, he saw a tall clay pitcher.

"Water!" cried the crow, and he flew down fast.

But when he peeked inside, his heart sank. There was only a tiny bit of water at the very bottom — far, far below his beak.

He stretched his neck. Stretch, stretch, stretch!

He tipped the pitcher. Push, push, push!

But the pitcher was too heavy, and the water was too low.

"What shall I do?" said the crow. "Think, think, think."

Then he saw the little pebbles lying on the ground. Shiny, round pebbles.

Plip! He dropped one pebble into the pitcher.

Plop! He dropped another.

Plip! Plop! Plip! Plop!

One by one, the pebbles fell in — and one by one, the water rose up. Higher... higher... higher...

At last, the water reached the top, and the crow dipped his beak in.

Glug, glug, glug! Ah, sweet, cool water!

The crow drank his fill, and then he flew home, cool and happy.

Goodnight, little crow.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Little by little, clever thinking solves big problems.
