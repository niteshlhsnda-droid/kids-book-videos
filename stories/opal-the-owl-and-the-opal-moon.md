# Opal the Owl and the Opal Moon

*A brand-new bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a tall oak tree at the edge of a whispering wood, there lived a little owl named Opal.

Opal had big round eyes, soft brown feathers, and a tiny golden beak. She loved the night — the stars, the moon, the soft hoot of the wind in the leaves.

One evening, as Opal was flying home, something caught her eye. There, in a hollow log by the stream, lay a small, round stone. It was smooth and milky-white — and it GLOWED!

It glowed soft and gentle, like a tiny moon that had fallen out of the sky.

"Oh!" whispered Opal. "A little moon!"

She picked up the stone with her feet and carried it home to her oak tree. She tucked it into her nest, and it glowed all night long — soft and milky and warm.

"This is my opal," said Opal. "My own little moon. I will keep it all to myself, forever and ever."

But the next night, Opal heard a tiny voice calling from the bushes.

"Help! Help! I am lost in the dark!"

It was Twinkle, the little firefly. Her light had gone out, and she could not find her way home.

Opal looked at her glowing opal. She looked at the dark bushes. And she thought, "My opal is mine. But Twinkle is lost."

So Opal flew down with the opal stone and held it up high. Its soft light shone over the bushes — and there was Twinkle, safe and sound!

"Thank you, Opal!" cried Twinkle. "Your little moon saved me!"

Opal smiled. "You are welcome, Twinkle."

The next night, Opal heard a splash from the stream.

"Help! Help!" cried Oscar the otter. "I dropped my favourite pebble in the deep water, and it is too dark to see!"

Opal flew down with her opal stone. Its soft light shone into the water — and there was Oscar's pebble, twinkling on the river bed!

"Thank you, Opal!" cried Oscar. "Your little moon saved my pebble!"

Night after night, Opal shared her glowing stone — with Twinkle, with Oscar, with every little animal who was lost or afraid in the dark.

And do you know what happened? The more Opal shared her opal, the brighter it glowed! It glowed soft and milky and warm — brighter than ever before!

One night, Opal looked up at the big, round moon in the sky. And she understood.

"The moon shares its light with everyone," she said. "And that is why it shines so bright. My little moon is the same. A light that is shared is a light that grows."

So Opal hung her opal stone on the lowest branch of the oak tree, where everyone in the wood could see it. And every night, the whole forest glowed — soft and gentle and safe.

And Opal the owl slept happily in her nest, knowing that the best light of all is the light you share.

The end. 🌙
