# Chiku the Monkey and the Jamun Tree

On the bank of a wide, slow river stood a tall jamun tree, and in it lived a small brown monkey named Chiku.

Every morning, Chiku climbed his tree and picked the ripest purple jamuns, soft and sweet, that stained his little fingers purple.

One morning, a big green crocodile swam up to the bank and looked up at the tree.

"Good morning," said the crocodile. "My name is Kumo. What are those purple fruits?"

"They are jamuns," said Chiku, "the sweetest fruit in the whole world. Catch!"

Chiku dropped jamuns down, and Kumo caught them with a snap, until his tummy was full.

"Thank you, little friend," said Kumo. And he came again the next day, and the next, until the two of them met by the river every day.

Each evening, Kumo carried jamuns home to his wife, Rani, across the river. But one evening, a greedy thought came into Rani's mind.

"If the jamuns are this sweet," she said, "then the monkey who eats them must have the sweetest heart of all. Bring me Chiku's heart."

Kumo shook his big head. "I cannot do that. Chiku is my friend."

But Rani would not eat or smile, and at last poor Kumo agreed. The next morning, he swam to the jamun tree.

"Chiku," he called, "Rani has made a grand supper. Climb on my back, and I will carry you across the river."

Chiku climbed onto Kumo's broad, bumpy back. Halfway across, Kumo's voice went small and sad.

"Chiku," he whispered, "I must tell you the truth. Rani wants your heart, because she thinks it will taste as sweet as jamuns."

Chiku's tummy did a somersault. But he did not scream. He thought, quick as a wink.

"Oh, Kumo!" he said. "Then we must turn back at once. I left my heart hanging on my jamun tree this morning. Monkeys always leave their hearts in their trees."

Kumo turned around and swam fast back to the bank. The moment they reached the shore, Chiku leaped off, scampered up his tree, and sat high among the branches, safe and sound.

"My heart stays right here with me," Chiku called down. "But I will still drop jamuns down to you every day, Kumo, because you are still my friend."

Kumo hung his big green head. "I am sorry, Chiku," he said.

Across the river, Rani felt sorry too. "I am sorry as well," she called.

After that, Chiku dropped jamuns down to Kumo every day, and Rani came to the bank to eat them too. And when the stars came out over the river, Chiku curled up in his tree, full of sweet jamuns and happy thoughts.

Sleep now, little Chiku.

The end. 🌙
