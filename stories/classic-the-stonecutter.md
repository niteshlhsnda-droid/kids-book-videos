# The Stonecutter

*A timeless Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High in the hills, where grey stones crunched underfoot and the air smelled of pine, there lived a stonecutter.

Every morning he walked to the mountain with his little hammer. Tap, tap, tap. All day long he chipped stones into neat squares. At night he went home to his small hut. He ate his plain dinner, and slept soundly on his straw mat. He was not rich. But he was not sad, either.

One warm day, as he carried his stones down the road, a big shiny cart pulled by horses rolled past. Inside sat a rich man in a soft, silky coat, waving a feather fan to cool himself down.

"Oh," sighed the stonecutter, wiping his forehead. "To be rich like that man! Then I could rest in the shade and never chip another stone."

And he became rich. Just like that.

His hut turned into a grand house. His plain dinner turned into a feast — warm cakes and steaming bowls on a shining table. He had a silky coat and a feather fan. For a while, he was the happiest he had ever been.

But one afternoon the summer sun beat down so hot that even his grand house felt too hot to sit in. The stonecutter fanned himself and fanned himself, and still he was tired and sticky.

"Oh," he sighed. "The sun is stronger than a rich man. I wish I were the sun."

And he became the sun.

He floated high above the world, warm and glowing. He poured golden light over the mountains, and the rivers sparkled like ribbons of glass. It was lovely — for a while.

Then a heavy, wet-smelling grey cloud drifted over and tucked him away — his light couldn't squeeze through its soggy belly.

"Oh," said the stonecutter, more quietly now. "The cloud is stronger than the sun. I wish I were the cloud."

And he became the cloud.

He floated low and soft, letting gentle rain fall over the fields — pitter, patter, pitter, patter. It was peaceful — for a while.

Then he tried to cross the valley, and there stood the mountain — a giant cold wall of grey rock, rising higher than he could ever float.

"Oh," whispered the stonecutter, very softly now. "The mountain is stronger than the cloud. I wish I were the mountain."

And he became the mountain.

He stood still, with snow on his rocky top, high up in the clouds. He could just rest. This, he thought, was the calmest wish yet.

And at the very bottom of his big rocky foot, he felt a small, familiar tapping — tap... tap... tap. Far below, a tiny stonecutter with a little hammer was chipping stones off his rocky foot to make into neat squares, just as he himself had once done.

The mountain gave a slow, rocky smile.

"Ah," he said, in a voice like a gentle rumble. "That little stonecutter is stronger than the mountain... and I miss being him. I wish to be myself again."

And he became the stonecutter once more, sitting on the mountainside, his little hammer resting beside him.

The afternoon light was soft, and the birds sang a sleepy little song. His hut was small, his dinner was plain, and his mat was only straw. But when he curled up on his crackly straw mat that night, its sweet dry-grass smell wrapping around him, he drifted into the deepest, happiest sleep he had ever known.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The happiest place is being yourself.*
