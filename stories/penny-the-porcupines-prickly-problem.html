Penny and the Prickly Problem

A cozy original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a mossy burrow under the old oak tree lived a young porcupine named Penny.

Penny had the softest brown eyes and the sweetest smile. But all over her back grew a coat of sharp quills.

One evening, Penny watched the rabbit family next door. The little bunnies snuggled into their mother's warm fur — squish, squash — all cozy together.

Penny sighed. "Nobody can cuddle me," she whispered. "My quills are too prickly."

Just then, her best friend Ruby the rabbit hopped over. "Penny! Come play with us!"

"I can't," said Penny, looking at the ground. "I'm too prickly for cozy games."

Ruby tilted her head. "Silly Penny," she said. "Watch this."

Ruby came close and gave Penny a soft side-hug, cheek to cheek, far away from the quills. "See? Warm as toast."

Then Sammy the squirrel scampered down from the oak tree. "My turn!" he said, and gave Penny the gentlest nose-nuzzle — boop! — right on her soft little nose.

Penny giggled. It tickled!

That evening, in the soft blue twilight, all Penny's friends gathered around her burrow. Ruby showed everyone the side-hug. Sammy showed off the nose-nuzzle. And one by one, each friend found their own special way to say "I love you" to Penny — without a single poke.

Penny's heart felt warm and melty, like honey in the sun.

"You know what?" she whispered, curling up in her mossy bed as the stars came out. "I don't need to change one single quill. I am loved exactly as I am."

And she fell asleep with a smile, dreaming of side-hugs and nose-nuzzles.
