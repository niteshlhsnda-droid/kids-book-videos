The Golden Bird

A Brothers Grimm fairy tale, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, behind a tall palace wall, there was a garden with a special tree. Every autumn it grew apples of shining gold — but every morning, one apple was gone.

"Someone is taking my apples in the night!" said the king.

Prince Neil kept watch the first night — but the soft night air made his eyelids heavy, and he was soon fast asleep on the grass. Prince Jay kept watch the next night — but the crickets sang their sleepy song, and soon he was snoring too.

Then the youngest prince, Vihaan, said, "Let me try, Father."

Vihaan packed bread and water in a little basket and sat down under the golden tree. When his eyes felt heavy, he counted the stars; when they felt heavy again, he hummed a soft song.

And at midnight — there! A soft golden light floated down through the branches. It was a bird with feathers of shining gold. It picked one golden apple with its beak and flew away into the dark.

Vihaan woke the king. "It is a golden bird — no thief," he said. "Let me follow it and ask it, very kindly, to stop."

"Go, my brave boy," said the king.

At sunrise Vihaan set out — and soon a reddish-brown fox stepped out from behind a bush, with a big feathery tail and kind, bright eyes.

"Hello, young prince," said the fox. "Where are you off to, walking so softly?"

"I am following a golden bird," said Vihaan. "I am hungry — and I think you are too. Will you share my bread?"

The fox's eyes went round — no one had ever shared bread with him before. He ate it and licked his lips. "Climb on my back," he said. "I know where the golden bird lives."

Off they went — over hills and past villages — to a faraway garden, where a cage of shining gold hung from a low branch, with the golden bird asleep inside.

The fox whispered, "Take the bird gently — but leave the golden cage where it hangs. And on the way home, speak to no one."

Vihaan tiptoed to the branch, opened the little door, and lifted the bird out as gently as lifting a warm biscuit from a tray.

The bird opened its bright eyes. "You woke me so kindly," it said in a voice like tiny bells. "I will come home with you — and never take an apple without asking."

On the way home, Vihaan spoke to no one, just as the fox had said.

Back at the palace, the steward — the man who looked after the king's things — had packed his bag and run away, never to be seen again. For it was HE who had been taking the golden apples — not the bird at all! The bird had only wanted one apple for its breakfast.

The king hugged Vihaan tight. "Patient when your brothers slept, and kind when you could have been angry — that is the greatest treasure of all."

Neil and Jay came to Vihaan, their cheeks pink. "We laughed at you," they said. "We were wrong. Forgive us?"

"Of course," said Vihaan, and hugged them both.

And the fox? The palace cook baked him warm honey-cakes, smelling of honey, which he ate one by one, closing his eyes with every bite.

Every spring, the golden bird flew back to sing in the king's garden, and Vihaan left honey-cakes by the garden gate, for his reddish-brown friend.

At night, the song floated soft and slow over the sleeping flowers, and the flowers swayed gently, as if they were listening too.

Sleep now, little one. The bird is singing just for you.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness and patience win the greatest treasures.
