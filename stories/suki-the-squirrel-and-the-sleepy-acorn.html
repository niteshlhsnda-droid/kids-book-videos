Suki the Squirrel and the Sleepy Acorn

A sweet original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a tall oak tree at the edge of a green forest, there lived a little red squirrel named Suki.

Suki loved acorns. She loved to crunch them. She loved to nibble them. She loved to hide them in secret places all over the forest.

All autumn long, Suki gathered acorns. She gathered big ones and little ones. She gathered round ones and pointy ones.

But when winter came, cold and white, Suki looked in her hollow and found only one acorn left.

"Oh no!" said Suki. "My very last acorn!"

Her tummy rumbled. "Eat it, eat it!" it said.

Suki picked up the acorn. It was small and brown and smooth, and it felt warm in her paws.

But then she heard a tiny sound. "Peep! Peep!"

It was a little bird, cold and hungry, sitting on a snowy branch.

"Are you hungry, little bird?" asked Suki.

"Peep," said the bird. "I have not eaten all day."

Suki looked at her last acorn. Then she looked at the little bird. Then she smiled.

"Here," she said, and she shared her acorn — half for the bird, and half for herself. "Crunch, crunch!" they nibbled together.

The little bird chirped a happy song. "Thank you, Suki! You are the kindest squirrel in the forest!"

All winter long, whenever the snow fell soft and white, the little bird came back to Suki's tree and sang her a sweet, sleepy song. And Suki shared whatever she had — a seed here, a berry there — because sharing made the cold days feel warm.

When spring came, warm and green, Suki went to plant her half of the acorn in the soft earth. She patted the soil gently.

"Grow, little acorn," she whispered. "Grow tall and strong."

And the acorn grew! First a tiny green shoot. Then a little sapling. Then, year after year, a tall, whispering oak tree.

The oak tree's leaves rustled softly in the wind — shhh, shhh, shhh — like a lullaby.

Soon, all the baby animals of the forest came to nap under Suki's oak tree. The baby rabbits. The baby mice. The baby birds. They all curled up in the soft grass and fell fast asleep to the whispering leaves.

Suki sat on a branch above them, smiling. She counted the sleepy babies below her — one little rabbit, two little mice, three little birds — and hummed the whispering song along with the leaves. "Shhh," she whispered. "Sleep well, little ones."

And that night, Suki slept the deepest, cosiest sleep of her life — under her very own whispering oak tree, dreaming of acorns and springtime.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little kindness, planted like an acorn, can grow into shade and songs for everyone.
