# Nasruddin and the Pumpkin

*A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One warm afternoon, Nasruddin was walking through his garden with his little grandson, Ali. The garden was full of tall oak trees and long, winding pumpkin vines.

Nasruddin stopped under a great oak tree and looked up. Hanging from its huge branches were tiny acorns, no bigger than Ali's little finger.

Then he looked down at the pumpkin vine creeping along the ground. Resting on the thin vine was an enormous pumpkin, round and orange and as big as a cooking pot!

Nasruddin scratched his beard and frowned. "This is very strange," he said. "Why did God put tiny acorns on a giant tree, and a giant pumpkin on a tiny vine? It is all the wrong way round! The pumpkin should grow on the oak tree, and the acorns should grow on the vine!"

Ali giggled. "Grandfather, are you sure?"

"Quite sure!" said Nasruddin, folding his arms. "If I were in charge, I would swap them at once!"

Just then, a soft breeze rustled the oak tree, and one tiny acorn came loose. Plop! It fell right onto Nasruddin's head.

"Ow!" said Nasruddin, rubbing his head and laughing. "That little acorn gave me quite a bump!"

Ali laughed too. "Imagine if a great big pumpkin had fallen from that tree instead, Grandfather! It would have squashed you flat like a pancake!"

Nasruddin's eyes went wide. Then he laughed louder than anyone. "Ha! Ha! Ha! You are right, my boy! Thank goodness the pumpkin grows on the ground, where it cannot fall on anyone's head!"

He picked up the little acorn and held it gently in his hand. "Everything grows exactly where it should," he said wisely. "Small things on big trees, and big things low on the ground. What a clever world this is!"

And from that day on, whenever Nasruddin walked through his garden, he tipped his hat to the oak tree, patted the big pumpkin, and said, "Thank you for not falling on my head!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Everything in nature grows just where it belongs — even the things that look funny at first.*
