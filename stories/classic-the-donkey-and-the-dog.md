# The Donkey and the Dog

*A gentle Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little village by a river lived a kind washerman named Raju.

Every morning, Raju loaded big bundles of clothes onto his donkey, Dudu.

Dudu carried them clip-clop, clip-clop, all the way to the river and back.

It was hard work. But Dudu never complained. He liked his master.

At home, Raju also had a little dog named Moti.

Moti did not carry anything. Moti wagged his tail, barked at strangers, and curled up at Raju's feet.

And Raju patted Moti's head and said, "Good boy, Moti! Good boy!"

He even slipped Moti little treats from his pocket.

Dudu watched all this with big, sad eyes.

"Why does Moti get all the pats and treats?" thought Dudu. "I work so hard. I carry everything. And nobody ever says, 'Good boy, Dudu!'"

One evening, Dudu made up his mind.

"I will do what Moti does," he said. "Then Raju will pat me too."

So when Raju sat down for his supper, Dudu trotted up to him.

He wagged his long ears. He tried to bark — "Hee-haw! Hee-haw!"

And then — oh no! — Dudu tried to jump onto Raju's lap, just like little Moti did.

But Dudu was not little at all!

The stool wobbled. The supper plate slid. Raju laughed and laughed as he caught the plate just in time.

"Dudu! Dudu!" cried Raju, laughing so hard his sides hurt. "What are you doing, my big silly friend?"

Dudu hung his head. "I only wanted a pat," he said softly. "Moti gets pats for doing nothing. I carry everything, and I get none."

Raju stopped laughing. He put his arms around Dudu's big, soft neck.

"Oh, Dudu," he said. "Moti guards the house. That is his job. But you — you carry my whole shop on your back! Without you, there would be no clean clothes at all."

Then Raju patted Dudu's head, long and slow, and gave him the biggest, crunchiest carrot from the kitchen.

"Good boy, Dudu!" he said. "Good boy!"

Moti wagged his tail and licked Dudu's nose, as if to say, "See? You are loved too."

From that day on, Dudu walked a little taller.

Clip-clop, clip-clop, he carried the bundles — and every evening, Raju patted his head and said, "Good boy, Dudu!"

And Dudu knew a happy secret: everyone has their own special job, and love is not the same for everyone — but it is there for all.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Do not copy someone else to win love — your own good work is already loved.*
