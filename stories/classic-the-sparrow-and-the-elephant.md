# The Sparrow and the Elephant

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a big, leafy banyan tree lived a little sparrow named Chinki and her husband, Chintu.

Together they had built a soft, round nest from twigs and cotton and dry grass.

Every morning, Chinki sang a sweet song from the top branch, and the whole tree seemed to wake up smiling.

One morning, a great big elephant named Gajraj came walking through the forest.

Thump! Thump! Thump! went his huge feet on the ground.

The banyan tree shook. The leaves trembled. And poor Chinki's little nest tipped right over!

Down fell the nest — bump! — onto a pile of soft, soft leaves below.

Luckily, the nest was empty, and nothing was broken. But Chinki's heart was thumping fast.

"Oh no!" she chirped. "Our lovely nest!"

Gajraj stopped. He looked down with his kind, big eyes and saw the tiny sparrow fluttering around her fallen nest.

"Oh dear," he rumbled in his deep, gentle voice. "Did my big feet do that? I am so sorry, little one. I did not see your nest at all."

Chinki took a deep breath. She was small, but she was brave.

"Please, Gajraj," she chirped, "when you walk past our tree, could you walk a little more slowly and softly? Then our nest will be safe."

Gajraj nodded his great head. "I will, little friend. And I will help you, too."

Very carefully, Gajraj curled his long trunk around the fallen nest and lifted it — up, up, up — back into the banyan tree.

Then he tucked it into the strongest fork of the branches, where it sat snug and safe.

"There!" he rumbled. "Now it is even stronger than before."

Chinki and Chintu hopped around their nest, chirping with joy. "Thank you, thank you, Gajraj!"

From that day on, whenever Gajraj walked past the banyan tree, he tiptoed — as much as a great big elephant can tiptoe!

Thump... thump... thump... went his feet, soft and slow.

And Chinki sang her sweetest song of all, just for her big, gentle friend.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even the biggest among us can learn to tread gently — and a small voice can teach a big lesson.*
