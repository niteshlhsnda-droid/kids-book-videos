The Little Red Hen

A beloved English folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, on a little farm with a red barn and a white fence, lived a little red hen named Ruby.

Ruby was a busy, cheerful hen. She loved to scratch in the yard, sing to her chicks, and keep her little house tidy. And she loved fresh bread more than anything — but only bread she had helped to make.

One sunny morning, Ruby was scratching in the farmyard when she found some grains of wheat lying in the dust.

"Cluck, cluck!" said Ruby. "Some wheat! If we plant this wheat, it will grow tall and golden, and we can grind it into flour and bake lovely bread."

She turned to her friends: the lazy cat, the sleepy dog, and the plump duck.

"Who will help me plant this wheat?" asked Ruby.

"Not I," said the cat, stretching in the sun.

"Not I," said the dog, yawning behind the barn.

"Not I," said the duck, waddling off to the pond.

"Then I will plant it myself," said Ruby, and she did. She scratched little holes in the soft earth and tucked each grain in gently, patting the soil over it like a blanket.

All summer long, Ruby tended the wheat. She watered it when the sun was hot, and she sang to it as it grew taller and taller, until it waved golden in the breeze.

"Who will help me cut the wheat?" asked Ruby.

"Not I," said the cat.

"Not I," said the dog.

"Not I," said the duck.

"Then I will cut it myself," said Ruby, and she did, snip-snip-snip, until all the wheat lay in neat golden bundles.

"Who will help me grind the wheat into flour?" asked Ruby.

"Not I," said the cat.

"Not I," said the dog.

"Not I," said the duck.

"Then I will grind it myself," said Ruby, and she did, round and round went the millstone, until she had a sack of soft white flour.

"Who will help me bake the bread?" asked Ruby.

"Not I," said the cat.

"Not I," said the dog.

"Not I," said the duck.

"Then I will bake it myself," said Ruby, and she did. She mixed the flour with water, kneaded the dough with her wings, and popped it into the oven.

Soon the whole farm smelled of warm, fresh bread — sweet and golden and delicious.

"Sniff, sniff," said the cat, coming out of the sun.

"Sniff, sniff," said the dog, waking from his nap.

"Sniff, sniff," said the duck, hurrying back from the pond.

"Who will help me eat this bread?" asked Ruby, smiling.

"I will!" said the cat.

"I will!" said the dog.

"I will!" said the duck.

Ruby looked at her friends. She was not angry — she was a kind hen. But she wanted them to learn something important.

"My friends," she said gently, "I planted, I cut, I ground, and I baked — all by myself. Bread tastes sweetest when everyone helps. So here is my promise: I will share this bread with you today, and next time, you will help me — and we will all enjoy it together."

The cat, the dog, and the duck hung their heads. "We are sorry, Ruby," they said. "Next time, we will help."

And they did! They shared the warm bread together, and the cat licked his lips, the dog wagged his tail, and the duck quacked happily.

From that day on, whenever Ruby found wheat, the cat helped to plant, the dog helped to cut, and the duck helped to carry — and the bread they baked together tasted sweeter than ever.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Work is lighter when everyone helps — and bread shared together tastes sweetest of all.
