Birbal and the Mangoes

A clever Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the grand palace of Fatehpur Sikri, lived the great Emperor Akbar.

One summer morning, a farmer brought the emperor a basket of the sweetest, juiciest mangoes anyone had ever seen. They were golden and glowing, and their smell filled the whole court.

"Birbal," said Emperor Akbar, stroking his beard, "here are ten perfect mangoes. Share them among my courtiers — but give the biggest mango to the wisest person in this court."

All the courtiers sat up straight. Each one thought, "Surely I am the wisest! The biggest mango will be mine!"

Birbal bowed and took the basket. He looked at the mangoes. Then he looked at the courtiers. Then he smiled his clever smile.

He walked past the generals in their shiny armor. He walked past the ministers in their fine silk turbans. He walked past the poets with their clever rhymes.

And he walked — right out of the court!

The courtiers gasped. "Where is he going with our mangoes?"

Birbal walked through the palace gardens, down the marble steps, and into the little orchard behind the palace. There, under a mango tree, sat old Gopal, the gardener, wiping the sweat from his brow.

"Gopal chacha," said Birbal warmly, "you planted this tree. You watered it every day. You waited ten long years for it to grow. Tell me — who is wiser than the one who plants sweetness for others to enjoy?"

And Birbal placed the biggest, goldenest mango in the gardener's hands.

Then Birbal went back to the court and shared the rest of the mangoes — one for each courtier, all sweet and juicy.

Emperor Akbar laughed out loud. "Well done, Birbal! You are right. The wisest person is not the one with the finest turban — it is the one whose work feeds everyone."

Old Gopal the gardener ate his big mango under his tree, and he shared every slice with the palace children who came running.

And from that day on, whenever the courtiers saw a mango, they remembered: true wisdom is quiet, kind, and grows sweet things for others.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: True wisdom is not in fine clothes or loud words — it is in the quiet work that feeds everyone.
