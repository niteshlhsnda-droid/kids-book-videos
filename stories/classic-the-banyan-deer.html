The Banyan Deer

A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green park near the city of Varanasi stood a great banyan tree, and under its wide, shady branches lived a big herd of deer. Their king was a golden deer named Sona, and all the deer loved him.

But the king of the city loved to hunt deer. Every day, the king came to hunt, and every day there were fewer deer left. The clearing felt emptier, and the deer's ears twitched at every snap of a twig.

Sona thought and thought. At last he walked up to the king and said, "Please, King, do not chase us with your arrows. Every day, we will send you one deer ourselves — every morning we will pull one name out of a bowl, so it is fair for everyone."

The king agreed. So every day, one deer walked quietly to the palace.

One morning, the name drawn was a young mother deer. Her baby had wobbly legs thin as twigs. "My baby needs me," she whispered, her eyes filling with tears.

Sona felt something warm and achy spread through him. "Go back to your baby, little mother — a king looks after his people first. That is what kings are for. Today, I will take your place."

The mother deer hurried back to her baby — who wobbled toward her on his twig-legs, tripped, and plopped onto his nose. Even Sona smiled.

Then Sona walked to the palace gate, folded his golden legs on the cool stone, and waited, breathing slow and steady. The guards went to fetch the king, and out he came, blinking in surprise.

"Why are you here, King of Deer?" he asked, his voice soft with wonder. "It was not your turn."

"So that her baby can keep its mother," said Sona. "Take me instead."

The king stood very still. "No," he said quietly. "I will not take you — nor any deer, ever again. From today, no arrow of mine will fly in this park."

The king's hard face softened — and two slow tears slid down into his beard. "You are the bravest king I have ever met," he said, "because you have the bravest, kindest heart of all."

He kept his promise. That night the deer slept close together under the banyan's leaves, safe and warm — and Sona watched over them, golden in the moonlight.

Goodnight, Sona. The end. 🌙

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: True courage is protecting others — the bravest heart is the kindest heart.
