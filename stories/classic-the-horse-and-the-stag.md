# The Horse and the Stag

*A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a wide green meadow, there lived a proud brown horse.

Every morning, he galloped through the tall, sweet grass, and the wind tickled his mane.

"This meadow is mine," he neighed happily. "All mine!"

One morning, a tall, gentle stag came to the meadow, with long, branching antlers. He bent his head and nibbled the sweet grass — munch, munch, munch.

The horse stamped his hoof. "Hey! That is my grass!" he cried. "Go away, stag! This meadow is mine!"

But the stag only smiled. "There is grass enough for two," he said, and went on munching.

The horse was very cross. He did not want to share. So he trotted off to find the hunter, a kind man who lived at the edge of the meadow.

"Please, hunter," said the horse, "chase that stag away from my meadow!"

The hunter looked at the horse. "I can help you," he said. "But you must wear this bridle, and you must let me ride upon your back."

The horse wanted the stag gone. "Very well," he said at last.

The hunter slipped the bridle over the horse's head. It felt strange and tight. Then the hunter climbed onto the horse's back, and together they galloped after the stag.

"Go on! Go away!" cried the horse. The stag leapt over the hill and disappeared into the forest.

"There!" said the horse, puffing. "Now the meadow is mine again!"

But the next morning, the hunter was there again, with the bridle in his hand. Day after day, the horse wore the bridle and the hunter rode upon his back. He could no longer gallop where he wished, or stop to smell the sweet clover, or roll in the soft, warm dust.

One evening, the horse looked across the meadow and felt sad.

"Oh, hunter," he said softly, "I asked you to chase the stag away, and you did. But now I cannot run free. Please, please set me free."

The hunter was a kind man. He looked into the horse's big brown eyes, and he smiled.

"You are right," he said. "A quarrel is not worth your freedom." And he gently took off the bridle.

The horse shook his mane. He stamped his hooves. He felt light and free again!

Then he trotted to the top of the hill and called out, "Stag! Stag! Come back!"

The stag came back, stepping softly out of the forest. "May I eat the grass?" he asked.

"Yes," said the horse. "There is grass enough for two. Let us share the meadow, and let us be friends."

From that day on, the horse and the stag shared the sweet grass and the cool stream. At dusk, they lay down side by side in the soft grass and counted the fireflies as they winked on and off — one, two, three, four, five — until their eyes grew heavy.

And under the twinkling stars, they both fell fast asleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Freedom is the sweetest grass of all — share the meadow, and everyone has room to run.*
