The Gold-Giving Cobra

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little village with mango trees all around, there lived a kind farmer named Dev.

One morning, Dev was walking through his field when he saw something shiny in the grass — a cobra, with scales that glowed like shiny gold in the sunlight.

Dev did not shout. He did not run. He bent down low and said softly, "Hello, friend. Are you hungry?"

The cobra lifted her head and looked at him with calm, round eyes.

That evening, Dev brought a small clay bowl of warm milk and set it near the cobra's hole. "For you," he whispered.

The next morning, the bowl was empty — and beside it lay a shiny gold coin!

Dev's eyes went wide. "A gift!" he laughed. "Thank you, Sona!" And from that day, he called the cobra Sona.

Every evening, Dev brought warm milk. And every morning, beside the empty bowl, there was one gold coin. One coin. Never more, never less.

With the coins, Dev bought seeds for his field, a soft blanket for cold nights, and sweet jaggery for the village children.

Dev had a young son named Chintu, who loved counting the gold coins.

"One coin every day," Chintu said one morning, tapping his chin. "But what if I take ALL the gold at once? There must be a whole mountain of it down that hole!"

That evening, Chintu did not bring milk. He brought a big empty sack.

He tiptoed to the cobra's hole and reached his hand in — grab, grab, grab!

But Sona was quicker. WHOOSH! She rose up tall, her hood spread wide like a fan, and let out a long, loud HISSSSS!

Chintu dropped the sack and ran all the way home, his heart thump-thump-thumping.

"Papa!" he cried. "Sona is angry! She hissed at me!"

Dev held his son close. "Sona gives her gold to friends, not to grabbers," he said gently. "Gold that is grabbed turns to fear. Gold that is given turns to joy."

Chintu felt small and sorry, all the way down to his toes. He filled the clay bowl with warm milk — the creamiest milk in the house — and carried it to the hole himself.

"I'm sorry, Sona," he whispered. "I was greedy. Please forgive me."

There was a soft rustle in the grass. Out slid Sona, slow and calm. She drank the milk, every last drop.

Then she looked at Chintu for a long, long moment — and dropped ONE gold coin at his feet.

Chintu picked it up and held it tight. "I will never grab again," he promised. "One gift at a time is plenty."

And from that day on, Chintu brought the milk himself, every single evening. And every morning, one gold coin waited — a gift between friends.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Gifts given with love are sweet; things grabbed in greed turn bitter.
