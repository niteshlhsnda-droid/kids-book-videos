# The Water of Life

*A Brothers Grimm fairy tale, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, there was a kind old king who ruled his land with a warm smile.

But one winter, the king fell gravely ill. His cheeks grew pale, and his smile faded away.

The palace doctor shook her head. "Only the Water of Life can heal him," she said. "It flows from a spring inside an enchanted castle, far beyond the whispering hills."

Prince Rishi, the eldest, set out at once. On the road he met a hungry little dwarf with a long white beard. "Please — might you share a bite of your bread?" asked the dwarf.

"Out of my way!" said Rishi rudely, and marched on.

Soon the road grew narrow and twisted, and Rishi wandered into a deep ravine — a narrow valley in the hills. His eyelids grew heavy, heavier — and he fell into an enchanted sleep, curled up on the soft moss like a sleepy puppy.

Then Prince Aman set out and met the same dwarf. "Please — might you share a bite of your bread?" "No! It's MY bread!" said Aman rudely, and marched on — and he too wandered into the ravine and fell into the same enchanted sleep, right beside his brother.

Then the youngest prince, Dev, said, "Let me try, Father."

Dev packed bread and cheese in his bag and set out. On the road he met the hungry dwarf.

"Please," said the dwarf, "might you share a bite of your bread?"

"Of course!" said Dev, and sat right down on a flat stone. He shared his bread AND his cheese, bite for bite.

The dwarf's eyes twinkled. "You have a kind heart, Prince Dev. Take this iron wand and these two small loaves.

"Beyond the hills stands the enchanted castle. Tap the door three times with the wand, and it will open. Go in BEFORE the clock strikes twelve. Fill your flask with the Water of Life. A princess sleeps in the castle — wake her with kindness, not with noise. And do not linger — come straight back."

The castle door was tall, covered in ivy. Dev tapped it — tap, tap, tap — with the iron wand, and it swung open without a creak.

Inside, everything was quiet and dusty, as if the whole castle was holding its breath. In a little room, a princess lay asleep on a soft bed, her hair spread out on the pillow.

Dev sat beside her and sang, very softly, the gentle song his mother used to sing to him.

Her eyelids fluttered, and she smiled. "What a lovely song," she whispered. "I have slept for such a long, long time."

Dev gave her one of the loaves and a sip from his flask. Color came back to her cheeks.

Then he found the spring of the Water of Life, sparkling like liquid starlight, and filled his flask to the top.

On the way home, Dev stopped at the ravine. His brothers still lay fast asleep, so he sprinkled a few drops of the Water of Life on them — plip, plip — and they woke up, yawning and rubbing their eyes.

"Thank you, little brother," they said, and they meant it.

Dev hurried home and gave the flask to the king. The king drank — and his cheeks grew warm, and his smile came back, bigger than ever. He rose from his bed and hugged his three sons.

Every spring after that, Princess Meera rode over the hills with baskets of strawberries, and the whole palace feasted in the garden.

And the flask of sparkling water stayed by the king's bed, shining like a tiny captured star.

Sleep now, little one. The castle is quiet, the princess is smiling, and everyone you love is safe and warm.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart finds what proud hearts miss.*
