The Ass and the Lapdog

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On a little farm, there lived a donkey named Golu.

Golu was a good, hardworking donkey. Every day, he carried heavy baskets of vegetables from the field to the farmhouse. He pulled the cart. He carried the water pots. He did all the big, heavy jobs.

In the farmhouse, there lived a tiny lapdog named Moti.

Moti was small and fluffy, with soft white fur and bright, happy eyes. She did no work at all. All day long, she sat on her master's lap, and the master stroked her soft ears and fed her little treats.

"Good girl, Moti!" the master would say. "What a sweet little dog you are!"

Golu watched from the field, and he felt a little ache in his heart.

"Why does Moti get all the cuddles?" thought Golu. "I work so hard. I carry all the heavy baskets. Surely I deserve cuddles too!"

One sunny afternoon, the master was sitting in his chair under the big tree, with Moti on his lap. Golu decided it was his turn.

He trotted over — clip, clop, clip, clop — and with one big, happy jump, he tried to climb onto the master's lap!

Crash! Bang! Clatter!

Golu's big feet knocked over the tea table. The teapot tipped. The cups rolled. The biscuits went flying! Moti jumped up with a little yelp, and the master jumped up too, laughing and shouting all at once.

"Whoa, Golu! Whoa!" cried the master.

Golu stood in the middle of the mess, his ears drooping. "Oh no," he thought. "I have made everything worse."

But the master did not scold him. He laughed a big, kind laugh and patted Golu's soft nose.

"Oh, Golu," said the master, "you are a big, strong donkey, not a tiny lapdog! You cannot fit on my lap — you would squash me flat!"

Golu's ears drooped even lower.

"But," said the master, "I love you just as much as Moti. You are my strong helper. Come here."

And the master gave Golu a big, juicy carrot — crunch! — and scratched him right behind his ears, in the spot Golu loved best.

Moti wagged her tail and licked Golu's nose. Golu's ears went up again, and he gave a happy little bray. "Hee-haw!"

From that day on, Golu did not wish to be a lapdog anymore. He knew that Moti was loved for being small and cuddly, and he was loved for being big and strong. Everyone is loved in their own way.

That evening, the master sat under the big tree with Moti on his lap and Golu resting his head on the master's knee. The stars came out, one by one. And Golu closed his eyes, happy and loved, and fell fast asleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Everyone is loved in their own way — you do not need to be someone else to be loved.
