# Birbal and the Parrot

*A timeless Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In the palace of Emperor Akbar lived a bright green parrot named Mitthu.

Mitthu lived in a golden cage in the palace garden, and Akbar loved him very much.

Every morning, Mitthu sang a sweet song, and Akbar clapped his hands with joy.

"Good morning, Mitthu!" Akbar would say, and Mitthu would bob his head and sing again.

One warm morning, a servant opened the cage door to give Mitthu fresh water.

But Mitthu saw the open sky, spread his bright green wings, and flew away!

He flew up, up, up, and landed on a tall mango tree in the garden.

"Oh no!" cried the servant. "The king's parrot has flown away!"

All the servants ran about, whispering in fear.

"Who will tell the king?" asked one.

"Not me!" said another. "The king will be very sad."

Just then, Birbal came walking through the garden, humming a soft tune.

"What is all this whispering?" he asked with a kind smile.

The servants told him everything, their voices soft and shaky.

Birbal nodded and thought for a moment.

"Do not worry," he said. "I will tell the king, gently, like a riddle."

Birbal walked to Akbar and bowed low.

"Your Majesty," he said, "I have a little riddle for you. What sings in the morning, wears a coat of green, and now sits in the mango tree, eating sweet fruit?"

Akbar thought and thought, then his face lit up.

"Mitthu!" he cried. "My little green parrot!"

"Yes, Your Majesty," said Birbal softly. "Mitthu flew out of his cage this morning. He is safe and happy in the mango tree."

Akbar's smile faded a little, but he was not angry.

"Oh, Mitthu," he said, "I miss your sweet song."

"Then let us call him home together," said Birbal.

Birbal picked the ripest, sweetest mangoes from the tree and held one up high.

"Mitthu! Mitthu!" he called in a soft, singsong voice. "Look what I have for you!"

Mitthu turned his bright head and looked down.

He saw the juicy yellow mango, and his little eyes sparkled.

"Mitthu! Mitthu!" Akbar called too, holding out his empty hand.

Mitthu fluttered his wings, hopped from branch to branch, and then — swoosh! — he flew down, right onto Akbar's hand.

"Mitthu!" laughed Akbar, his heart full of joy. "You came back to me!"

Mitthu nibbled the sweet mango and sang his sweetest song of all.

Akbar laughed and laughed, and Birbal laughed too.

"Thank you, Birbal," said Akbar. "You told me gently, and you brought my friend home."

"Kind words open every door," said Birbal with a smile, "even a parrot's heart."

That evening, Mitthu sang in the garden as the sun went down, soft and golden.

Akbar sat under the mango tree, listening, calm and happy.

The leaves rustled softly, and the birds sang their good-night songs.

Soon, Akbar yawned a small, sleepy yawn.

"Good night, Mitthu," he whispered.

"Good night, sweet friend," said Birbal.

And Mitthu tucked his head under his wing and drifted off to sleep, safe and loved.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kind, gentle words can bring a friend home.*
