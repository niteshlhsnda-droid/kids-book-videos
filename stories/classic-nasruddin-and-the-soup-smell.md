# Nasruddin and the Smell of Soup

*A Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a dusty little town full of rattling carts and hurrying people, there lived a soup-seller named Chotu. Every morning he lit a crackling fire under his big, round pot, and soon the smell of warm lentil soup — thick, golden, and bubbling — floated all down the street. It smelled of turmeric and cumin and comfort.

One sunny afternoon, a poor traveler named Rahim came walking down that street. His tummy was rumbling, but his pockets were empty. All he had for his lunch was a small piece of dry bread.

But Rahim was a clever fellow. He sat down on a low wall near Chotu's big pot and held his bread in the warm steam rising off the soup. The steam softened the bread, and the smell of the soup soaked right into it.

Rahim took a big bite. "Ahhh," he said, smiling. "That tastes wonderful."

Now Chotu the soup-seller was watching. His eyebrows went up, up, up, and his forehead got all crinkly.

"Hold on, hold on!" he cried, waving his big wooden spoon. "You ate the smell of my soup! That smell belongs to my pot! You must pay me for it!"

Poor Rahim's smile slid right off his face. He turned his pockets inside out — flap, flap. Nothing. "I have no coins," he said quietly. "I'm sorry."

Just then, clip-clop, clip-clop, down the street came Nasruddin, riding his little donkey. He saw the crinkly forehead and the empty pockets. Can you guess what he understood? Everything — in one look.

Nasruddin swung down from his donkey with a big smile. "Don't worry," he said. "I will pay the man." And he pulled out his own small purse.

Chotu rubbed his hands and leaned in close. But Nasruddin only held the purse up to the seller's ear — and jingled it. Jingle-jingle-jingle went the coins.

"There," said Nasruddin. "The sound of my coins — for the smell of your soup. Fair is fair."

For a moment, nobody made a sound. Then a giggle popped out of the crowd. Then a laugh. And soon everyone was laughing — even Chotu himself.

Chotu shook his head and grinned. "You win, Nasruddin," he said. "You always win." And he scooped out a small, steaming bowl of soup for Rahim to eat with his bread.

Rahim's eyes lit up. "Thank you," he whispered. And he ate his soup and bread in slow, happy bites, until his tummy felt warm and full.

That evening, as the sun dipped low and the busy street grew soft and quiet, Rahim curled up under his warm blanket. He smiled at the first star and drifted off to sleep, dreaming of jingling coins and the smell of soup.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Fairness means what you pay should match what you took.*
