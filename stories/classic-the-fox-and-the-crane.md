# The Fox and the Crane

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green meadow full of daisies, there lived a red fox named Foxy and a tall grey crane named Kavya.

Foxy had a bushy tail, bright eyes, and a very big smile. Kavya had long, thin legs, a long, thin beak, and a very kind heart.

One sunny morning, Foxy said, "Kavya, my friend! Come to my house for dinner tonight. I am making my very best tomato soup!"

"Oh, how lovely!" said Kavya. "I would love to come!"

That evening, Kavya flew to Foxy's house. The soup smelled wonderful — warm and red and steamy. Foxy poured the soup into two flat plates and set them on the table.

"Eat up!" said Foxy, and he lapped up his soup with his big, pink tongue. Slurp, slurp, slurp!

But poor Kavya could not eat a single drop. Her long, thin beak was made for sipping, not for lapping. Every time she tried, her beak just tapped the flat plate — tap, tap, tap — and the soup stayed right where it was.

Foxy did not notice. He finished his soup and smiled. "Was that not delicious?" he said.

Kavya smiled politely, though her tummy was still empty. "Very delicious," she said. "And now, Foxy, you must come to my house for dinner tomorrow!"

"With pleasure!" said Foxy.

The next evening, Foxy trotted to Kavya's house. The soup smelled wonderful — warm and red and steamy. Kavya poured the soup into two tall, thin jars and set them on the table.

"Eat up!" said Kavya, and she dipped her long, thin beak into her jar and sipped the soup. Sip, sip, sip!

But poor Foxy could not eat a single drop. His big, bushy face could not fit into the tall, thin jar. Every time he tried, his nose just bumped the top — bump, bump, bump — and the soup stayed right where it was.

Kavya watched him for a moment. Then she said, very quietly, "Foxy, do you remember last night, when my beak could not eat from a flat plate?"

Foxy's ears drooped. "Oh, Kavya," he said. "I am so sorry. I served the soup in a way that was easy for me — but not for you. And now you have done the same to me. We have both been silly!"

Kavya laughed. "Then let us be clever instead!" she said. She took a wide bowl and poured the soup into it. She put a little ladle beside it, and a tall, thin straw beside that.

"Now," she said, "you can lap from the bowl, and I can sip through the straw!"

Foxy lapped. Slurp, slurp, slurp! Kavya sipped. Sip, sip, sip! They both finished their soup, and their tummies were full and happy.

"From now on," said Foxy, "when I invite a friend to dinner, I will ask how they like to eat!"

"And from now on," said Kavya, "I will do the same!"

And they were the very best of friends — because they had learned the most important dinner rule of all: treat others the way you want to be treated.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Treat others the way you want to be treated — a kind host thinks of the guest, not only of himself.*
