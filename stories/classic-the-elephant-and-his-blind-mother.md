# The Elephant and His Blind Mother

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Deep in a green forest, where the trees whispered in the breeze and the river sang a soft song, there lived a young elephant named Chintu. Chintu was big and strong, with a long, gentle trunk and kind, round eyes.

Chintu lived with his mother, an old elephant who could no longer see. Her eyes were cloudy, like two soft grey clouds, but her smile was warm and her heart was full of love.

Every morning, Chintu woke up early. He walked to the sweetest mango tree and plucked the ripest mangoes with his trunk. He gathered soft bananas and juicy leaves. Then he walked to the river and filled his trunk with cool, clear water.

"Here, Mother," he would say, placing the fruit gently before her. "Eat, and drink, and rest."

"Thank you, my dear Chintu," his mother would say, touching his trunk with hers. "You take such good care of me."

One day, the king's men came riding into the forest. They had heard about the strong, gentle young elephant, and they wanted him for the palace.

"You must come with us," they said. "You will be the royal elephant. You will live in a palace of gold and eat the finest food."

Chintu did not want to leave his mother. But the king's men led him away, and soon he was standing in a beautiful palace garden, with golden gates and a soft bed of hay.

The king gave him the sweetest sugarcane and the finest fruit. But Chintu ate nothing. He stood quietly, his head bowed low, and his eyes were full of tears.

A wise old gardener whispered, "Your Majesty, the elephant misses his blind mother. He cared for her every day, and now he pines for her."

The king listened, and his heart melted. "Then bring his mother to the palace at once," he said. "No one should be kept from the one they love."

The king's men brought his mother to the garden, and when Chintu saw her, his ears flapped with joy. He rushed to her side and wrapped his trunk around hers.

"Mother! You are here!" he trumpeted softly.

"My dear Chintu," said his mother, smiling. "I am here, my child."

That very day, Chintu ate happily for the first time. He munched his mangoes and drank the cool water, and his mother ate beside him, smiling all the while.

The king watched them and smiled too. "Love like this must never be parted," he said. "You are both free to go. Live together in the forest, happy and free."

So Chintu and his mother walked back to their forest home, trunk in trunk, step by step. The birds sang, the river sparkled, and the trees swayed gently above them.

That night, under a sky full of twinkling stars, Chintu brought his mother sweet fruit and cool water, just as he always had. Then they lay down side by side, and as the moon rose, they both drifted off to sleep, safe and together.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Love for family is the greatest strength.*
