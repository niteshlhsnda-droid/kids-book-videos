# Nasruddin and the Tiger

*A gentle Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little town at the edge of a great forest, there lived a wise and funny man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin loved to tell stories. He told stories in the market, stories under the big banyan tree, and stories to his friends over cups of hot, sweet tea.

One sunny afternoon, Nasruddin sat with his two best friends, Omar and Yusuf, under the banyan tree.

"Friends," said Nasruddin, leaning forward with a very serious face, "yesterday I met a tiger in the forest. A huge tiger! With stripes as wide as my arm!"

Omar's eyes grew round. "A tiger! What did you do?"

"I chased it!" said Nasruddin, thumping his chest. "I ran after that tiger, and I scared it away!"

Yusuf nearly dropped his cup of tea. "You chased a tiger? You?"

"Yes, me!" said Nasruddin. "I ran and I ran, shouting, 'Go away, tiger! Go away!' And the tiger ran away from me!"

Omar and Yusuf looked at each other. Then they both burst out laughing.

"Nasruddin!" laughed Omar. "You are the funniest man in the whole town!"

"Wait, wait," said Yusuf, wiping his eyes. "Tell us the truth now. How did you really scare the tiger?"

Nasruddin grinned his biggest grin. "Very well, I will tell you the truth," he said. "I ran away as fast as my legs could carry me. And the tiger ran after me. And then the tiger saw how fast I was running, and it thought, 'That man runs so fast, he must be very brave!' — and so the tiger got scared and ran away!"

Omar laughed so hard he nearly fell off the bench. Yusuf laughed so hard his tea spilled.

"So the tiger was scared of your running?" asked Omar.

"Exactly!" said Nasruddin. "I ran, the tiger ran, and we both scared each other. That is what I call a fair chase!"

The three friends laughed until their bellies hurt. Then Nasruddin said, "Friends, a tall tale is fun to tell. But the truth is the best story of all. And the truth is — I am very good at running away from tigers!"

Just then, they heard a rustle in the bushes. And there, in the soft mud, they saw the paw print of a real tiger — big and round, with four little toe marks.

"Look!" whispered Yusuf. "A real tiger!"

"Let us walk home now," said Nasruddin, standing up very calmly. "Slowly, quietly, and together. A wise man enjoys a tiger in a story — and keeps a good distance from a tiger in real life!"

So the three friends walked home together, laughing softly, and Nasruddin told them three more stories on the way — each one funnier than the last.

And that night, when the moon came up over the forest, the tiger walked through the trees, all alone, and Nasruddin slept safe in his bed, dreaming of his next tall tale.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A tall tale is fun to tell — but the truth is the best story of all.*
