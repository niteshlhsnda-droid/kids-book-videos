Ollie the Otter's River Lullaby

A cozy original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a curvy corner of the Silver River lived a young otter named Ollie.

Every evening, Ollie watched the sun sink low and the water turn to gold. And every evening, he had the same wish.

"I want to help the whole river fall asleep," he told his grandmother, old Nani Otter, as they floated on their backs.

Nani Otter smiled her warm, wrinkly smile. "Then you must learn the river lullaby, little one. Listen…"

And in her soft, low voice, she sang:

*"Hush now, river, soft and slow,* *Let the sleepy ripples go…* *Moon is rising, stars are deep,* *River, river, fall asleep."*

Ollie listened three times. Then he tried it himself — very, very softly. Maybe you can sing it with him, very, very softly.

That evening, Ollie swam from bank to bank, singing the lullaby.

First he sang to the three ducklings, who were still splashing. They grew quiet… and tucked their beaks under their wings.

Then he sang to the frogs on the lily pads. Their croaking slowed — croak… croak… — until it stopped for the night.

Then he sang to the fireflies dancing over the water. They dimmed their lights to the softest glow, like tiny night-lamps.

The river itself seemed to sigh. The ripples grew smaller. The water grew still and glassy, holding the round silver moon like a mirror.

Ollie floated onto his back in the middle of the quiet river. Nani Otter floated beside him and wrapped one paw around his.

"Hush now, Ollie," she whispered, "soft and slow…"

And the river, and the ducklings, and the frogs, and the fireflies — and one very sleepy little otter — all drifted off to sleep together.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A gentle song can help the whole world fall asleep.
