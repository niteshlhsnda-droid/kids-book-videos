Birbal and the Dream

A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a grand palace with golden domes and gardens full of roses, there lived a kind emperor named Akbar.

Emperor Akbar loved his people, and he loved his clever friend Birbal most of all. Birbal could solve any puzzle, answer any riddle, and always knew the kindest thing to say.

One morning, Emperor Akbar woke up looking very sad. He sat on his golden throne with his chin in his hand, and he did not smile at anyone — not even at the palace cat, who had come to say good morning.

"Oh, Birbal," said the emperor, "I had a terrible dream last night. I dreamed that all my teeth fell out, one by one — plip, plop, plip! — until my mouth was quite empty. What can it mean?"

Birbal thought for a moment, and then he smiled. "Your Majesty, dreams are like riddles. Let me think about it, and I will tell you tomorrow."

But the other courtiers in the palace could not wait. One by one, they came to the emperor with gloomy faces.

"Your Majesty," said the first courtier, bowing low, "a dream like that is very bad news. It means all your family will leave you, one by one."

"Oh no!" cried the emperor, and he looked even sadder than before.

"Your Majesty," said the second courtier, shaking his head, "it means you will lose all your friends, one by one, like teeth falling out."

"Oh dear!" cried the emperor, and he put his chin back in his hand.

All day long, the courtiers came with sad words, and the emperor grew sadder and sadder. Even the roses in the garden seemed to droop.

The next morning, Birbal came to the palace with a bright smile on his face.

"Your Majesty," he said, bowing low, "I have thought about your dream, and I bring you good news!"

"Good news?" said the emperor, sitting up straight. "Tell me quickly, Birbal!"

"Your Majesty," said Birbal, "your dream means that you will live a very, very long life — longer than all of us! Your teeth fell out in the dream because you will still be here, smiling, long after all of us are gone. It is a dream of a long and happy life!"

The emperor's eyes went wide. Then a big smile spread across his face. "Birbal!" he laughed. "You have turned my sad dream into a happy one!"

All the courtiers looked at each other. "But Your Majesty," said one of them, "we said the very same thing!"

"No, you did not," said the emperor. "You said my family would leave me. Birbal said I would live a long, long life. It is the same dream — but Birbal said it kindly!"

The emperor clapped his hands. "Birbal, you are the cleverest friend in the whole kingdom. From today, whenever anyone brings me news, they must bring it the way Birbal does — with kindness!"

And the emperor laughed so much that the whole palace laughed with him — even the palace cat, who purred and purred.

That night, the emperor went to sleep with a smile on his face. And do you know what? He dreamed of a garden full of roses, and in his dream, everyone he loved was smiling right beside him.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The same words can be said kindly or unkindly — and kind words make everyone happier.
