The Monkey and the Crocodile

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On the bank of a wide, green river, there grew a tall jamun tree. Its branches were full of sweet, purple jamun fruit, and in that tree lived a cheerful monkey named Manny.

Every morning, Manny picked the ripest jamuns and munched them happily. Plop, plop, they were juicy and sweet.

One day, a crocodile named Cassie swam slowly up to the riverbank. She looked tired and hungry.

"Good morning!" called Manny. "Would you like some jamuns? They are the sweetest fruit on the whole river."

"Oh, yes, please!" said Cassie. She had never tasted anything so sweet. She munched the jamuns, and her eyes shone with delight.

From that day on, Manny gave Cassie jamuns every morning, and Cassie carried Manny on her back across the river to see the flowers on the far bank. They became good friends.

One evening, Cassie went home and told her husband about the sweet jamuns and her clever monkey friend. Her husband's eyes gleamed. "A monkey who eats such sweet fruit must have a very sweet heart," he said. "Bring me his heart tomorrow, and we will share it."

Cassie felt troubled. Manny was her friend. But her husband kept asking, and at last, with a heavy heart, she agreed to a trick.

The next morning, Cassie said to Manny, "My husband would love to meet you. Climb on my back, and I will carry you to our home for a feast."

Manny climbed on happily. Splash, splash, they set off across the river.

Halfway across, Cassie went quiet. Then she said in a small voice, "Manny, I must tell you the truth. My husband wants your heart, because he thinks it will be sweet like your jamuns. I am so sorry. I should never have agreed."

Manny's heart thumped. But he kept his head cool. "Oh, my heart?" he said, as calmly as he could. "Why, I left it back in the jamun tree! I always leave it there when I go visiting. Let us swim back, and I will fetch it for you."

Cassie was so relieved that she turned around at once and swam back to the bank. The moment they reached the shore, Manny leaped up into his tree, quick as a flash.

"My heart is right here, safe inside me," laughed Manny, "and it is staying here!"

Cassie hung her head. "You are right to be cross with me," she said. "I was a bad friend today. I am truly sorry."

Manny looked down at his friend. He was still a little shaken, but he could see that Cassie meant her sorry words.

"I will stay in my tree for now," said Manny kindly. "But if you are truly sorry, we can still be friends from the riverbank. You bring me pretty river stones, and I will drop you jamuns from the branches. No more tricks — only sharing."

Cassie smiled with relief. "No more tricks," she promised. "Only sharing."

And that is how it was, from that day on. Manny stayed safe in his tree, Cassie visited the riverbank every morning, and the sweet jamuns were shared, just as friends should share.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A quick mind keeps you safe, and a true sorry can mend a friendship — but real friends never trick each other.
