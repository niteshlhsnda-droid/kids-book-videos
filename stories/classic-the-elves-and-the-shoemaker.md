# The Elves and the Shoemaker

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a little town with cobbled streets, there lived a kind old shoemaker and his wife.

The shoemaker made shoes that were strong and soft. But times were hard, and one winter his shop grew very quiet. People had no coins to spare.

One evening, the shoemaker looked at his last piece of leather. It was just enough to make one pair of shoes.

"I will cut the leather tonight," he said to his wife, "and I will stitch the shoes in the morning."

So he cut the leather into neat shapes, laid them on his workbench, and went to bed.

In the morning, he walked to his workbench. And there — oh, wonder of wonders! — sat the most beautiful pair of shoes he had ever seen, stitched so neatly he could hardly believe his eyes.

"Who made these?" he whispered.

That day, a fine gentleman tried on the shoes and smiled. "These are the finest shoes I have ever worn!" he said, and paid more coins than the shoemaker had ever earned.

With the coins, he bought leather for two more pairs. He cut it that evening, and in the morning — there they were! Two pairs of beautiful shoes, already made!

Day after day, it happened again, until his shop was full of happy customers. But the shoemaker and his wife could not stop wondering.

"Who is helping us?" said his wife one evening. "Tonight, let us stay awake and watch."

So that night, they hid behind the curtain as the moon rose high. And then — there they were!

Two tiny elves, no taller than a teacup, climbed onto the bench. They hummed as they worked — stitch, stitch, stitch! — their little fingers flying. Their clothes were ragged and their little feet bare, but they sang as they sewed, and by morning the shoes were done.

"Oh, the dear little things!" whispered the shoemaker's wife. "They work so hard for us, and they have no warm clothes and no shoes of their own. We must thank them!"

The next day, the wife sewed the tiniest clothes you ever saw — two little jackets, trousers, caps, and two pairs of the smallest, softest shoes. That evening, instead of leather, they laid the little clothes on the workbench.

At midnight, the elves climbed up — and stopped. Their eyes went wide. There, waiting for them, were the tiny clothes and shoes!

They giggled with delight and tried them on at once. The jackets fit just right! The caps sat just so! And the shoes were the softest they had ever worn!

They danced around the workbench, laughing. Then they waved goodbye, hopped down, and skipped out into the moonlit night, singing as they went.

The shoemaker and his wife never saw the elves again. But the shop prospered, and they lived happily, always remembering their two tiny friends. And whenever the moon shone, they smiled, sure that somewhere, two little elves were dancing in their new shoes.

That night, the shoemaker yawned, his wife tucked the blanket around him, and they drifted off to sleep under the twinkling stars.

The end. 🌙

*Moral: Kindness is repaid with kindness.*
