# Doctor Dolittle and the Talking Parrot

In a little town called Puddleby-on-the-Marsh, in a little house with a big garden, lived a kind doctor named Doctor Dolittle. He wore a tall black hat and loved animals more than anything in the world.

In his garden lived Dab-Dab the duck, Jip the dog, Gub-Gub the pig, Too-Too the owl, and Polynesia the parrot, nearly two hundred years old and very wise. The animals followed him everywhere, and he talked to them all day — though he could not understand a word they said back.

Once, he had been a doctor for people. But his sister Sarah said the animals frightened the patients away, and one by one they stopped coming. The doctor did not mind — he liked his animals better than money. But one thing made him sad.

One morning, Dab-Dab came waddling into the kitchen, holding one wing low. "Quack, quack, quack," she said, in a worried sort of way. The doctor looked at her wing, soft and careful, but Dab-Dab only quacked again. He wished with all his heart that he knew what she was trying to say.

Polynesia ruffled her bright green feathers. "I can teach you," she said. "The talk of animals is not so hard, if you listen with your whole heart."

So every evening, after supper, Polynesia taught him — the quack of ducks, the bark of dogs, the squeak of mice. Night after night he listened, and little by little, the sounds turned into words.

Then one cold, moonlit night, a tiny tap-tap-tap came at the window. It was Nibbles, the smallest field mouse in Puddleby, shivering in the frost. "Doctor! Doctor!" she squeaked. "Old Prince, the farm horse, has a sore tooth and cannot eat a bite!"

The doctor put on his coat and his tall hat, lit his lantern, and hurried out into the frosty night. Old Prince stood in his stable with his head low. The doctor listened to him and looked gently inside his mouth. There it was — the sore tooth. With soft, careful hands, he helped it feel better, and Old Prince whinnied a happy thank-you.

Word travelled fast through the fields and the woods. By morning, animals were coming from all around — a cat with a thorn in her paw, a rabbit with a sniffly nose. The doctor listened to each one, helped each one, and every animal went home smiling.

The animals wanted to thank him. Dab-Dab brought fresh eggs, the bees brought honey, and the mice brought sweet blackberries. The little house was full of friends, and Doctor Dolittle was never lonely again.

That night, as the moon rose, the animals curled up in their warm corners, and Polynesia tucked her head beneath her wing.

Sleep now, Doctor Dolittle.

The end. 🌙
