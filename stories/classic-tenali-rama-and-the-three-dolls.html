Tenali Rama and the Three Dolls

A Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in the sunny kingdom of Vijayanagara, there lived a kind king named Krishnadevaraya.

His palace was full of music and laughter, and the cleverest, funniest friend in the whole court was a man named Tenali Rama.

One morning, a messenger arrived from a neighboring kingdom, carrying a long wooden box.

Inside the box lay three little dolls, each one dressed in a tiny silk robe.

They were exactly alike. Same painted smile. Same bright eyes. Same tiny golden crown.

The messenger bowed low and read out his king's letter.

"My king sends these three dolls to your wise court. Tell us — which of the three is the wisest?"

The court fell quiet. The courtiers leaned closer, stroking their beards and frowning at the dolls.

"This one," said one minister. "Her smile is the sweetest."

"No, no," said another. "This one's robe is a shade brighter. She must be the wisest."

They guessed this way and that, but nobody could tell the dolls apart — not by even a thread.

At last the king turned to Tenali Rama and smiled. "Well, Tenali? What do you say?"

Tenali Rama thought for a moment, twirling his mustache. Then he asked, "May I have a thin, thin straw, Your Majesty?"

The king nodded, curious, and a servant brought him a thin straw. Tenali Rama picked up the first doll.

"Watch closely," he whispered, and gently slipped the straw into the doll's ear.

In went the straw… and out it came — straight through the other ear!

The court gasped. Tenali Rama smiled. "This doll hears," he said, "but keeps nothing inside. Words go in one ear and straight out the other."

He picked up the second doll and slipped the straw into her ear.

In went the straw… and out it popped — through her mouth!

Everyone chuckled. "This one," said Tenali Rama, "tells everything she hears. Nothing stays with her either."

Then he lifted the third doll and gently slipped the straw into her ear.

In went the straw… and down, down it slid — settling quietly near her heart.

A hush fell over the court.

"This one," said Tenali Rama softly, holding her up, "listens — and keeps wisdom in her heart. She thinks before she speaks."

King Krishnadevaraya clapped his hands and laughed with delight.

"Tenali Rama! Once again, your wit has answered what all my courtiers could not!"

He rewarded Tenali Rama with a bag of gold coins, and the messenger hurried home with the answer for his king.

That night, the palace bells rang soft and low, and everyone went to sleep smiling.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The wisest person listens carefully and thinks before speaking.
