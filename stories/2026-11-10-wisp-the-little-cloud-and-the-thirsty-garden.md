# Wisp the Little Cloud and the Thirsty Garden

High above a green valley, where a little river curled like a silver ribbon, drifted a small white cloud named Wisp. Soft and round and light as a feather, he loved to float slowly across the sky, watching the world below.

Every morning, Wisp looked down at the little cottage garden at the foot of the hill, where roses glowed the colour of sunset, daisies shone white as fresh milk, and tall sunflowers turned their faces to the sun. A little girl named Ana watered them each morning with her small blue watering can, singing as she went.

But one summer, the sun shone and shone, and no rain came. The river grew thin. The well ran low. And in the little garden, the flowers began to droop, their petals curling, their leaves hanging down like tired hands.

Ana watered them with the last of the water in her can. "Please hold on, little flowers," she whispered. "Rain will come soon, I hope."

Wisp felt heavy. Inside him was rain — soft, silver rain, waiting to fall. But Wisp was afraid. "What if I rain too hard?" he whispered. "What if I frighten the flowers? What if the garden does not want my rain?"

Just then, a great grey cloud rumbled past and poured a loud, splashing rain all over the far hills. Thunder grumbled. Wisp trembled. "I do not want to be loud and scary like that," he said. "I will keep my rain inside."

But the flowers drooped lower. Ana sat on the garden step and looked up at the sky with sad eyes. A little sparrow landed on the fence and sang a sweet, hopeful song. Wisp listened, and something warm fluttered inside him — braver than fear.

That evening, the old oak tree rustled its leaves. "Little cloud," it whispered, "the garden does not need a storm. It only needs a gentle drink. Rain softly, a little at a time, and that will be enough."

Wisp took a deep, soft breath. And then — drip, drip, drip — the tiniest silver drops began to fall, light as a lullaby, over the little garden.

Ana laughed and held out her hands. The roses lifted their heads. The daisies opened wide. The sunflowers stood tall again, and the whole garden drank, cool and happy. Wisp rained gently until every flower was smiling again, then stopped, light as a feather once more.

As the last drops fell, a rainbow arched across the sky, pink and gold and soft blue. Ana waved up at Wisp. "Thank you, little cloud!" she called. Wisp smiled a small, proud smile and drifted up to rest beside the moon.

Sleep now, little Wisp.

The end. 🌙
