# Mabel the Mouse and the Midnight Bakery

*A brand-new bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little house at the end of a quiet lane, there lived a tiny grey mouse named Mabel.

Mabel lived in a little hole behind the kitchen, with a bed made of soft cotton and a blanket made of a yellow duster.

Every night, when the house went dark and quiet, Mabel smelled something wonderful drifting down from the bakery next door. Warm bread! Sweet buns! Cinnamon rolls!

"Oh, how lovely," Mabel would whisper, sniffing the air. "I wish I could bake bread like that."

One night, Mabel tiptoed out of her hole and peeped through the bakery window. Inside, the baker was fast asleep in his chair, snoring softly — snore… snore… snore.

On the table lay a big bowl of dough, ready to be shaped into buns. But the baker was too sleepy to shape them.

"Poor baker," whispered Mabel. "He is so tired. I will help him."

Mabel climbed up onto the table — tip, tap, tip, tap — and began to pat the dough with her tiny paws. Pat, pat, pat! She rolled it into little round buns, just the way she had watched the baker do it. And as she worked, she hummed a soft little baking song, so quiet that only the flour sacks could hear it.

Then she found a tiny brush and brushed the buns with warm honey. Then she sprinkled them with cinnamon — sniff, sniff, sniff!

When the baker woke up, he rubbed his eyes. "What is this?" he cried. "The buns are all shaped! And they smell wonderful!"

He put the buns in the oven, and soon the whole bakery smelled of warm bread and honey.

Mabel peeped out from behind a flour sack and smiled. But just then, the baker saw her little pink nose!

"Hello, little mouse," said the baker kindly. "Did you help me with my buns?"

Mabel nodded, just a tiny bit. She was a little bit scared.

But the baker smiled. "Thank you, little helper," he said. "You have the softest paws in the whole village. Will you come and help me every night?"

So every night after that, when the moon was high and the village was quiet, Mabel tiptoed into the bakery. She patted the dough — pat, pat, pat! — and brushed on the honey, and sprinkled the cinnamon.

And every morning, the baker left a tiny warm bun on the windowsill, just for Mabel. It was the best bun in the whole bakery, because it was made with love and a little mouse's helping paws.

And Mabel would carry her bun home, curl up in her cotton bed, and fall asleep with a happy, full little tummy, dreaming of cinnamon and warm bread.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even the smallest helper can make something wonderful.*
