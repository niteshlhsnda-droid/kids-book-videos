Nasruddin and the Mirror

A timeless Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One sunny market day, Mullah Nasruddin was wandering through the bazaar, looking at pots and pans and pretty scarves, when something shiny caught his eye. It was a small, round mirror, sparkling in a shopkeeper's stall.

Nasruddin had never seen a mirror before. He picked it up and looked inside — and what did he see? A little man with a round face and a funny white beard, staring right back at him!

"Why, hello there!" said Nasruddin, waving at the little man. The little man waved back!

Nasruddin grinned. The little man grinned too. Nasruddin stuck out his tongue. The little man stuck out his tongue as well!

"What a friendly fellow!" laughed Nasruddin. "He copies everything I do! I must take him home and be his friend."

So Nasruddin bought the mirror and hurried home. At supper time, he set the mirror on the table and put a plate of warm bread beside it.

"Eat, my new friend!" said Nasruddin. "You must be hungry after a long day in the shop."

But the little man did not eat. He just sat there, holding his own plate of bread, waiting.

Nasruddin's wife came in and saw her husband talking to the mirror. She burst out laughing!

"Oh, Nasruddin, you silly man!" she chuckled. "That is not a little man at all. That is a mirror — it shows your own face back to you!"

Nasruddin looked again. He waved — the man waved. He laughed — the man laughed. And suddenly Nasruddin understood, and he laughed louder than anyone!

"So the friendly fellow was me all along!" he cried. "Well, I am very glad to meet myself! I am a fine fellow indeed!"

From that day on, Nasruddin hung the mirror by his door. Every morning he waved to himself and said, "Good morning, my friend! Let us have a happy day!"

And the man in the mirror always waved right back.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The friendliest face you meet each morning is your very own.
