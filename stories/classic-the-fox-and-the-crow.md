# The Fox and the Crow

*A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One bright morning, a crow named Cora found the most beautiful piece of cheese in the whole wood.

It was golden and creamy, and it smelled wonderful. Cora picked it up carefully in her beak and flew up to a high branch of the old oak tree to enjoy it in peace.

"At last," thought Cora. "A quiet breakfast, all to myself."

Just then, a fox named Fable came trotting along the path below. He sniffed the air. Sniff, sniff, sniff.

"Cheese!" said Fable, licking his lips. "Oh, how I love cheese!"

He looked up and saw Cora on her branch, holding the cheese in her beak.

"Good morning, beautiful Cora!" called Fable, smiling his widest smile. "What lovely black feathers you have! They shine like silk in the sun!"

Cora sat up a little straighter. She did like her feathers.

"And what a fine bird you are!" went on Fable. "So tall, so grand! Why, with a voice to match those feathers, you would be the queen of all the birds!"

Cora's heart swelled with pride. "The queen of all the birds?" she thought. "Oh, I would love to show him my beautiful voice!"

So Cora opened her beak wide and sang, "CAW! CAW! CAW!"

And down fell the cheese — plop! — right at Fable's feet.

Fable snapped it up in one happy gulp. "Mmm! Thank you, Cora!" he said. "That was a very fine song — and a very fine cheese!"

But when Fable looked up, he saw something he did not expect. Cora was not angry. She looked sad. Her head drooped, and two little tears shone in her eyes.

"Oh dear," said Fable. His happy gulp suddenly felt not so happy. "Cora, are you all right?"

"I am just sad," said Cora quietly. "I was proud, and my pride made me drop my breakfast. And now I am hungry."

Fable shuffled his paws. He had been clever, but clever did not feel kind. He looked at the cheese — well, at the last little bit of it, still on the ground.

"You know what?" said Fable. "A clever trick that makes a friend sad is not a good trick at all. Here — let us share what is left. Half for you, and half for me."

So Fable pushed half the cheese back toward Cora, and Cora flew down and nibbled it happily.

"Thank you, Fable," said Cora. "That was kind."

"And thank you, Cora," said Fable. "You taught me something today. Pretty words are nice to hear, but they should never trick a friend."

From that day on, whenever Fable met Cora, he still said, "Good morning, beautiful Cora!" — because her feathers really did shine like silk. But he never, ever asked her to sing for his supper again.

And Cora, whenever she found a tasty treat, always remembered to keep her beak closed until she had finished her breakfast!

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Beware of flattery — and a kind heart is cleverer than a clever trick.*
