# Credits — Willie Winkie and the Sleepy Town

- **Original title:** "Wee Willie Winkie"
- **Author:** William Miller (Scottish poet, 1810–1872); the rhyme has circulated in Mother Goose collections and nursery books ever since
- **Source:** "Wee Willie Winkie," a traditional nursery rhyme — first printed in *Whistle-binkie: Stories for the Fireside* (1841); a standard Mother Goose / nursery-rhyme collection item (Roud Folk Song Index 13711)
- **Source URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wee_Willie_Winkie
- **License:** Public domain (first published 1841; copyright long expired)
- **Illustrations:** All ten illustrations are freshly generated originals (not reused source art).
- **How it was adapted:** The four-line rhyme was expanded into a completely original ~450-word bedtime story for ages 4–7, in new wording and a gentle bedtime register. The night-watcher became a small boy named Willie in a starry blue nightgown with a brass lantern; the "tapping at windows" motif became a tender tour of a sleepy town (checking on sleeping children, guiding lost ducklings home); a lantern-out setback under moonlight supplies the story's turning point; and the rhyme's "Are the children in their beds? For now it's eight o'clock" is sung as a lullaby on the hilltop. The lesson — that a calm, kind nighttime round settles the heart into sleep — is shown through the story and never stated.
