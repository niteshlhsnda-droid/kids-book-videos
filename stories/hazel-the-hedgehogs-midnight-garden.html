Hazel the Hedgehog's Midnight Garden

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a soft green corner of the meadow, under a rosebush that smelled of honey, lived a small hedgehog named Hazel.

Hazel had a round tummy, a pointy nose, and a coat of spines as soft as pinfeathers — she was a very well-brushed hedgehog.

One cool spring evening, Hazel found a tiny packet of seeds on her doorstep. It was tied with a silver ribbon, and the label said, in curly letters:

*Moonflower seeds. Plant at dusk. They only bloom at midnight.*

"Ooh!" whispered Hazel. "A midnight garden!"

So at dusk, when the sky turned pink and gold, Hazel dug twelve little holes with her little paws. *Pat, pat, pat.* She tucked a seed into each hole. She sprinkled them with cool water from her acorn cup. *Drip, drip, drip.*

"Goodnight, seeds," she whispered. "Sleep tight. I'll be back at midnight."

Then Hazel went inside, curled up in her cozy moss bed, and set her flower-clock alarm — *tick, tick, tick* — for midnight.

But oh, waiting was hard! Hazel's eyelids felt heavy, heavy as two little stones. She yawned a tiny hedgehog yawn.

"Must... stay... awake," she mumbled. "Must see... the midnight garden..."

She nibbled a crumb of oatcake to stay awake. *Nibble, nibble.* She counted stars through her window. One... two... three... forty-seven... She hummed a sleepy little song. *Hum-hum-hum.*

Just then — *tap-tap-tap* — someone knocked at her door. It was her friend Wren, the tiny brown bird, all fluffed up in her night feathers.

"Aren't you asleep yet, Hazel?" chirped Wren.

"I can't sleep," said Hazel. "My moonflowers are blooming at midnight, and I want to see."

Wren smiled. "Then I will stay awake with you," she said. "Tell me about your garden."

So Hazel told Wren all about the silver ribbon and the curly letters, and Wren sang her a soft waiting song. *Tweedle-dee, tweedle-doo, midnight's coming soon for you.*

And just as Hazel's eyes were closing — *BRRRING!* — the flower-clock rang!

"Midnight!" cried Hazel, popping up. She wrapped herself in her thistledown shawl, Wren fluttered onto her shoulder, and together they tiptoed out into the garden.

And oh! Oh, my!

The garden was glowing.

Twelve moonflowers had opened, wide as teacups, and each one shone a soft silver-blue, like little moons floating on the dark earth. The whole garden glimmered and shimmered, and tiny sparkles drifted in the air like fairy dust.

Hazel gasped. "It's beautiful," she breathed.

Just then, something fluttered down from the sky. It was Milo the moth, with dusty wings like powdered sugar.

"Hazel! Hazel!" he whispered. "Your garden is brighter than the moon!"

Then came Ollie the owl, gliding in on silent wings. *Whoosh.* "What a lovely light for my night flying," he hooted softly.

Then came Pip the mouse, hurrying along with his long tail. *Pitter-patter, pitter-patter.* "It's so dark by the river," he squeaked. "This light feels like a warm hug."

Hazel looked at her glowing garden. Then she looked at her night friends. And she had a wonderful idea.

"Moonflowers are for sharing," she said. "Each of you may take one petal of light home."

So Milo took a petal, and it glowed like a tiny lantern all the way to his tree.

Ollie took a petal, and it lit his path through the night sky.

Pip took a petal, and it glowed warmly beside his little river nest.

And Hazel? Hazel kept the whole garden, all twelve moonflowers, shining just for her.

She curled up in the soft grass under the moonflowers, pulled her thistledown shawl up to her chin, and listened to the night.

*Shimmer,* went the petals. *Hush,* went the grass. *Twinkle,* went the stars.

"Thank you, midnight garden," Hazel whispered. "You were worth waiting for."

And under the silver-blue glow, with the sweet smell of moonflowers all around her, Hazel the hedgehog closed her eyes — and fell fast asleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The loveliest things in life are worth waiting for — and even lovelier when we share them.
