# The Singing, Springing Lark

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, there lived a merchant with three daughters. The youngest was called Tara, and she was as kind as she was lovely.

One day, the merchant was going on a long journey. "What shall I bring you back, my daughters?" he asked.

"Beautiful dresses!" said the eldest. "Fine jewels!" said the middle one.

But Tara said, "Father, bring me only a singing, springing lark — a little bird that sings as it springs up into the sky."

The merchant travelled far and wide. He found dresses and jewels easily. But a singing, springing lark was nowhere to be found — until, on his way home, he walked through a deep, dark wood, and there, in the tallest tree, sat the lark!

As the merchant reached up to take the little bird, a great lion stepped out of the trees. His eyes were golden, and his mane was like the sun.

"That lark is mine!" roared the lion. "But I will let you take it — if you promise me the first thing that greets you when you reach home."

The merchant thought of his dog, who always ran to meet him. "Very well," he said. "I promise."

But when he reached home, it was not the dog who greeted him first — it was Tara, his youngest daughter, running out with a happy cry: "Father!"

The merchant's heart sank. He had promised his daughter to the lion!

Tara heard the whole story, and she was not afraid. "A promise is a promise, Father," she said. "I will go to the lion."

So Tara travelled to the deep, dark wood, carrying the little lark in a golden cage.

The lion's castle was grand and bright. And there, Tara found something wonderful — by day, he was a lion, but by night, he became a kind young prince named Veer!

"An old spell made me a lion," said Veer. "Only true love can break it."

Tara and Veer were very happy together. They sang with the lark every morning, and they walked in the garden every evening.

But one day, Tara went home to visit her family. Her sisters whispered, "Do not go back to that lion! Stay with us!"

Tara felt torn — and when she returned to the wood, the castle was empty. Veer was gone!

A little white dove flew past her, and Tara knew at once: it was Veer, turned into a dove for seven long years, flying far, far away.

Tara did not give up. She followed the trail of white feathers — over hills, through valleys, across rivers — singing the lark's song as she went.

At last, on the seventh year, she came to a little cottage in a sunny meadow. And there, on the doorstep, stood Veer — a prince again, smiling at her!

"Tara!" he cried. "You never stopped looking for me! Your love has broken the spell!"

And so Tara and Veer went home to the castle, where the singing, springing lark sang its sweetest song of all — a song of love that never gives up.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: True love never gives up — it keeps looking, keeps hoping, and keeps singing.*
