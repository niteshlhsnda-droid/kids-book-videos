# The Little Plover and the Big Sea

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

By the edge of the big blue sea, where the sand was warm and soft, there lived a tiny plover bird named Titu.

Titu was no bigger than a puff of cloud. She had quick little feet, a bright round tummy, and a beak like a tiny triangle. Every evening she ran along the shoreline, chasing the foam as it went *whoosh* and giggling as it tickled her toes.

One spring morning, Titu made a cozy little nest in the sand, lined it with soft feathers, and laid four perfect little eggs inside.

"There," she whispered. "Sleep tight, my little ones. Mama is right here."

For three days, Titu sat beside her eggs, singing tiny songs to them. *Loo-lee-loo, sleep and grow, sleep and grow.*

But on the fourth morning, the wind came dancing from far away, and the waves rose up — *whoosh, whoosh, whoosh* — taller than Titu had ever seen.

"Oh no!" cried Titu. "My eggs!"

The great waves rushed up the sand and swept the nest away. Titu watched her four little eggs floating away out to sea, carried off by the foamy blue water.

"Come back!" she cried. "Come back!"

But the sea only went *shush... shush... shush*, as if it hadn't heard her at all.

Titu sat on the wet sand and cried tiny tears that went *plop, plop, plop* into the sand.

Then she stopped, puffed up her small chest, and lifted her tiny beak high.

"I may be little," she said, "but I am not giving up. I will empty the whole sea, one beakful at a time, and I will find my eggs."

So she flew to the water's edge, dipped in her beak, picked up one drop of water, and carried it behind the dunes.

One drop. Then another. Then another.

*Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.*

A sandpiper came hurrying along, her legs going *pat-pat-pat* on the sand.

"Titu, what are you doing?" she asked.

"The sea took my eggs," said Titu. "I am carrying it away, one beakful at a time."

The sandpiper blinked. Then she puffed up her chest too.

"Then I will help too!" she said. *Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.*

A big white gull sailed down from the sky.

"Titu, what are you doing?" he asked.

"The sea took my eggs," said Titu. "We are carrying it away, one beakful at a time."

The gull blinked. Then he puffed up his chest too.

"Then count me in," he said. *Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.*

A wise old heron, tall and gray, stepped slowly out of the reeds.

"Titu, what are you doing?" he asked.

"The sea took my eggs," said Titu. "We are carrying it away, one beakful at a time."

The heron nodded his long neck. "Courage comes in small feathers," he said. "I will help." *Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.*

The sea watched all this and began to chuckle. *Hoo-hoo-hoo,* went the waves, rolling in and out. "Little birds! Busy birds! You could sip for a hundred years and never empty me!"

But Titu did not stop. *Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.* Her friends did not stop either. *Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.*

High above the clouds, the great Garuda, the king of all birds, looked down and saw four small birds trying to carry away the whole sea.

"How brave they are!" said Garuda. "How full of love!"

Down he flew on great wide wings — imagine his great shadow gliding over the waves — until he stood before the sea.

"O Sea," said Garuda, in his deep and gentle voice. "You are big and strong. But you have taken something precious from a very small mother. Please give her eggs back."

The sea went quiet. *Shush... shush...* The sea went very still, as if it were holding its breath. It had only been playing — the waves loved to splash and splosh — and it had not meant to hurt anyone.

"Very well," murmured the sea. "I am sorry."

And slowly, the waves rolled the four little eggs back to the shore, one by one. Then they laid them gently in Titu's sandy nest.

"Mama's here!" Titu sang, dancing round and round. *Loo-lee-loo, you're safe now, safe now!*

The sandpiper, the gull, and the wise old heron smiled. Garuda folded his great wings and flew back up into the clouds.

That evening, Titu sat beside her eggs and told them the story of the day.

"You see, little ones," she whispered, "even the smallest bird can move the biggest sea — when friends stand together."

*Shush... shush...* went the waves, soft and sleepy now.

*Sip. Drop. Sip. Drop.* The birds went home to rest.

And the moon rose over the calm, calm water, and everybody drifted off to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even the smallest bird can move the biggest sea — when friends stand together, never give up.*
