The Bamboo Princess

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a green bamboo grove in Japan, there lived an old bamboo cutter named Takumi and his kind wife, Hana.

Every morning, Takumi walked into the bamboo grove with his sharp knife. Tap, tap, tap — he cut the tall bamboo, and Hana wove it into baskets.

One morning, Takumi saw something strange. One bamboo stalk was glowing — soft and golden, like moonlight!

He cut it open very carefully. And inside — oh, wonder of wonders! — there was a tiny little girl, no bigger than his thumb, with shining black hair and a smile like the morning sun!

"Look, Hana!" cried Takumi, carrying her home. "A little princess from the bamboo!"

Hana wrapped the tiny girl in a soft cloth. And do you know what happened? The little girl began to grow — and grow — and grow! In a few weeks, she was as big as any child in the village. And in a few months, she was a beautiful young girl.

They named her Kaguya — the Shining Princess.

Kaguya was gentle and kind, and everyone in the village loved her. She helped Hana weave baskets. She sang songs that made the bamboo sway. And every night, she sat by the window, looking up at the moon with sad, thoughtful eyes.

When Kaguya grew up, princes came from far and wide to ask for her hand. They brought her golden bowls and jewelled branches and robes of shining silk.

But Kaguya smiled and shook her head. "Thank you, kind princes," she said. "But my home is not here."

One night, the moon shone round and bright, and a soft silver light filled the little house. Kaguya put on a robe of moonlight, and her face shone like the moon itself.

"Dear Takumi, dear Hana," she said, "I was never really from this world. I am a child of the Moon, and tonight the Moon has come to take me home. But do not be sad. I will always be close by."

She gave Hana a letter and a little bottle of moonlight. "Keep this letter," she said. "And whenever you miss me, look up at the moon — and I will be looking down at you, smiling."

Then, with a wave of her hand, Kaguya floated up, up, up into the silver moonlight — and was gone.

Takumi and Hana cried a little that night. But the next night, they looked up at the moon — and it shone softer and sweeter than ever before, as if Kaguya were smiling down at them.

And every night after that, whenever the moon was full and bright, the whole village would look up and whisper, "There is our Kaguya — the Bamboo Princess — watching over us all."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Love stays close even when someone must go far away — just look up at the moon.
