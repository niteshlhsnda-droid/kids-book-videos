# Nasruddin and the Two Coins

*A timeless Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little village, there lived a kind man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin had a friendly face and a soft white beard.

Every morning, he walked down the lane to the baker's shop.

Oh, how lovely it smelled there! Warm bread, fresh from the oven, with a golden crust.

The baker was a kind man named Hassan, with flour on his apron.

Every morning, Hassan held out his hand and showed Nasruddin two coins. It was their little morning game.

"Choose, my friend," said Hassan. "One big silver coin — or one small gold coin?"

The silver coin was big and round and shiny, like a little moon.

The gold coin was small, no bigger than a button.

But gold is precious, even when it is small. A tiny gold coin is worth much more than a big silver one.

Every morning, Nasruddin smiled and took the small gold coin.

"Thank you, Hassan," he said. "How is your family today?"

"My family is well," said Hassan, smiling back.

Then Nasruddin tucked the little gold coin into his pocket and walked home, humming a happy tune.

Next door lived Rahim, a grumpy neighbour who loved money. Every morning, he watched from his window and shook his head.

"Look at that silly Nasruddin!" he muttered. "The silver coin is so big! Why does he always take the little one?"

One morning, Rahim hurried out and stopped Nasruddin in the lane.

"Nasruddin!" he called. "Take the big coin tomorrow, and you will be rich!"

Nasruddin smiled gently. "Come and sit with me, Rahim, and I will tell you a secret."

So they sat on a low wall by the baker's shop.

"Rahim," said Nasruddin, "if I take the big silver coin, what will happen tomorrow?"

Rahim frowned. "You will have the big coin. That is all."

"Exactly," said Nasruddin. "The game would be over. Hassan would have nothing left to offer me."

"But if I take the small gold coin," said Nasruddin, "Hassan smiles at me every morning, and we ask about each other's families."

Nasruddin patted his pocket. "A small coin comes with a smile and a warm hello every morning," he said. "And a little kindness every day is worth more than one big gift."

Rahim scratched his head. "Hmm," he said at last. "Maybe you are right."

The next morning, Rahim walked to the baker's shop, and when Hassan held out the two coins, Rahim smiled and took the small gold coin.

"Good morning, Hassan," he said. "How is your family today?"

Hassan's eyes opened wide. Then he laughed a happy laugh.

"Good morning, Rahim!" he said. "My family is well!"

Rahim felt warm and happy inside, like fresh bread rising in the oven.

From that day on, Rahim came every morning too, and took the small gold coin with a smile and a warm hello, just like Nasruddin. And Hassan smiled more than ever, because now he had two friends to greet instead of one.

That evening, as the moon rose over the rooftops, Nasruddin walked home slowly, humming his tune, and the gold coin jingled in his pocket.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A little kindness every day is worth more than one big gift.*
