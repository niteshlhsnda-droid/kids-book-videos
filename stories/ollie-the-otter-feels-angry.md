# Ollie the Otter Feels Angry

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Ollie was a little otter who loved building towers with smooth pebbles. One sunny morning, he built his tallest tower ever.

One pebble. Two pebbles. Three pebbles. Four pebbles. Five!

Then a big wave came.

SPLASH!

The tower fell down.

Ollie felt angry. His face felt hot. His paws felt tight.

"NO!" he shouted. He wanted to throw a pebble. He wanted to stomp his tail.

But Mama Otter swam over. "I see you feel angry," she said.

Ollie nodded. His whiskers shook.

"Let us do the slow breath together," Mama said. "Breathe in slow, blow out slow."

Ollie breathed in slowly. He smelled the fresh river air.

"Breathe in slow, blow out slow."

Ollie blew out slowly, like blowing out birthday candles.

"Breathe in slow, blow out slow."

Ollie felt his paws get soft. He felt his face get cool. The angry feeling got smaller.

"I felt angry," Ollie said.

"It is okay to feel angry," Mama said. "We just do not throw or stomp. Talking helps the angry out," she added.

Ollie nodded. He told Mama about his tower. "I worked so hard," he said. "It was my tallest one ever."

Mama listened and hugged him. She did not rush him — she just stayed close.

Then Ollie had an idea. "Can we build it together?" he asked.

"Yes," said Mama.

They stacked the pebbles again. One pebble. Two pebbles. Three pebbles. Ollie smiled. The tower was not as tall, but building it felt good.

That evening, the river was calm. Ollie floated on his back, holding Mama's paw. He breathed in slow. He blew out slow. The stars came out one by one.

Ollie closed his eyes. He was calm. He was loved.

Goodnight, Ollie.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When anger comes, slow breaths help it drift away.*
