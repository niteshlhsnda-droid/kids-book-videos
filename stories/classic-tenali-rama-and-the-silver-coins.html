Tenali Rama and the Silver Coins

A witty Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a green village in South India, there lived a merchant named Mr. Rao. He was kind, but he worried a lot about his money.

Mr. Rao had saved many bright silver coins. He did not trust the bank, so one night he put them in a clay pot, tiptoed into his big garden, and buried the pot under a tree.

"There!" he whispered. "No one will ever find my coins. Not even my best friend will know."

But Mr. Rao had a problem. He told no one — not his wife, not his son, not even his dog! And after a few weeks, he forgot which tree he had chosen.

There were so many trees in his garden! Mr. Rao walked round and round, scratching his head.

"Was it this tree?" he said. "Or that tree? Oh dear, oh dear! My silver coins are lost!"

He dug here and there, but all he found was mud and worms. He sat down and sighed a big, sad sigh.

Just then, his clever friend Tenali Rama came walking by, with a happy smile on his face.

"Hello, Mr. Rao!" said Tenali Rama. "Why do you look so sad?"

Mr. Rao looked round. No one else was there. At last, he whispered the whole secret — the clay pot, the silver coins, and the tree he could not remember.

Tenali Rama listened with care. He did not laugh. He just nodded and said, "Do not worry, my friend. What do you remember about the tree?"

Mr. Rao closed his eyes and thought hard. "I remember… there was a little bird's nest in the tree, high up in the branches."

"Good!" said Tenali Rama. "What else?"

"I remember… there was a smooth grey stone at the foot of the tree, shaped like a round moon."

"Even better!" said Tenali Rama. "Let us walk through the garden together, slowly, and look for a tree with a bird's nest and a moon-shaped stone."

So the two friends walked together, tree by tree, looking up for nests and down for stones.

Then, near the corner of the garden, Tenali Rama stopped. He pointed up. "Look! A little bird's nest!"

And Mr. Rao looked down. "And look! A smooth grey stone, shaped like a round moon!"

It was the very tree! Mr. Rao dug with his hands, quick, quick, quick — and there was his clay pot, full of bright silver coins!

"My coins! My coins!" cried Mr. Rao, hugging the pot. "Thank you, Tenali Rama!"

Tenali Rama smiled. "You are welcome, my friend. But remember — a worry shared with a true friend is half a worry. Next time, do not hide your troubles. Tell a friend you trust."

Mr. Rao nodded. From that day on, he kept his coins safe in a strong box — and he told his wife where it was! And that night, he slept soundly, with a happy heart and no worries at all.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Share your worries with a friend you trust — a problem shared is a problem halved.
