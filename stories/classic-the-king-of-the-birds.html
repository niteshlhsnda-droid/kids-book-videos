The King of the Birds

A world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, all the birds of the forest gathered under the tallest tree to choose a king. The parrots, the sparrows, the pigeons, the crows, and the mighty eagle all fluttered in, chattering and squawking.

"Who shall be our king?" asked the wise old owl, blinking her big eyes.

"Let us have a contest!" cried the eagle, spreading his great wings. "Whoever flies the highest shall be king!"

All the birds agreed. At sunrise, they lined up on a high branch. "Ready... steady... FLY!"

Up they all went! The sparrows fluttered up, the pigeons flapped up, the crows cawed their way up. But the eagle soared higher than them all, up, up, up into the blue sky, until the other birds looked like tiny dots below.

"I am the highest!" cried the eagle proudly. "I shall be king!"

But wait — what was that tiny sound from just above him? A little voice chirped, "Peep! I am higher still!"

It was Wren, the tiniest bird in the whole forest! While the eagle had been flying, clever little Wren had tucked herself softly among the eagle's wing feathers and ridden all the way up, without flying a single flap!

The eagle burst out laughing — a great, kind, rumbling laugh. "Well, little one! You are the cleverest bird I ever met! You flew higher than the eagle himself!"

Down they all flew to the tall tree, where the birds were waiting. The owl blinked wisely. "Then we have two winners," she said. "Eagle is the strongest, and Wren is the cleverest."

The birds cheered and cheered. And this is what they decided: Eagle would be the King of the Birds, strong and fair, and little Wren would be the Royal Singer, whose sweet song would wake the forest every morning.

Wren sang her happiest song, the eagle spread his wings in a royal bow, and all the birds sang together until the stars came out.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Being small is no bar to being clever — and the best kings share their crown.
