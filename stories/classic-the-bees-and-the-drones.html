The Bees and the Drones

A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a sunny garden, behind a tall sunflower, there was a happy little hive.

Inside the hive lived Diya the bee and her busy friends. Every morning, Diya woke up early, stretched her tiny wings, and flew out into the sunshine.

Diya visited pink flowers and purple flowers.

Back at the hive, the bees worked together, side by side. They turned the nectar into honey and built neat little rooms of golden wax, called honeycomb, where they kept the honey safe.

"One cell for today, and one for tomorrow," said Diya, smiling.

But not everyone in the garden liked to work.

High up on a warm branch lived Dax and his drone friends. They had big, buzzy wings and loud, buzzy voices, but they did not like to fly to flowers or carry nectar.

One sunny afternoon, Dax flew down to the hive and puffed out his chest.

"That honey is ours!" he buzzed loudly. "We are big and strong, so the honey must belong to us!"

Diya peeked out of the hive. "But we made the honey," she said softly. "We flew to the flowers every single day."

Just then, a wise old wasp named Wilma flew down from the old oak tree.

"Let us settle this kindly," said Wilma. "I have a fair test. Whoever can build a honeycomb shall keep the honey."

"Ready, set, build!" said Wilma.

Diya and her friends got straight to work. They shaped soft wax with their tiny feet and made neat little rooms, six sides each, all in a row. Soon the honeycomb shone like gold in the sunshine.

Dax and his friends tried too. They pushed the wax and patted the wax. They buzzed and buzzed and buzzed. But their rooms wobbled, their walls fell down, and their honeycomb looked like a lumpy pile of playdough.

Wilma looked at the two honeycombs and smiled her kind, wise smile.

"The bees built beautiful golden cells," she said. "The drones built only buzzes and boasts. The honey belongs to the makers."

Dax looked down at his wobbly wax. "You are right," he said quietly. "We did not make the honey. We are sorry, Diya."

Diya smiled a warm, sunny smile. "That is all right," she said. "Would you like to learn? Tomorrow you can fly with us to the flowers."

Dax's eyes lit up like sunshine. "Yes, please!" he said. "We would love to help!"

The very next morning, Dax and his friends woke up early. They stretched their wings and flew with Diya to the pink flowers and the purple flowers. They sipped nectar, carried pollen, and helped make honey. It was hard work, but it felt good. And when they were done, they shared the sweet honey together.

From that day on, the drones were not lazy anymore. They were helpers. And every evening, as the sun went down, the whole hive sang a soft, happy song together.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: What we make with kind, steady work is truly ours — and sharing it makes it even sweeter.
