Rory the Raccoon and the Riverside Lanterns

A brand-new bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a cosy little hollow by the river, there lived a raccoon named Rory.

Rory had a stripy tail, clever little paws, and a big, kind heart. Every evening, just as the sun went down, Rory loved to sit on the riverbank and watch the fireflies dance over the water.

One evening, as Rory was walking along the river, he found a wooden box floating gently by the bank. It had washed up on the soft mud.

Rory opened the box — creak! — and peeked inside.

Paper lanterns! Ten beautiful paper lanterns, all folded up neatly, red and gold and blue and green.

"Oh, how lovely!" said Rory. "But what are they for?"

Just then, his friend Fern the little frog hopped over. "Rory," she said in a small, shy voice, "I am afraid of the dark. When the sun goes down, the river is so dark, and I cannot see where to hop."

Rory looked at the lanterns. Then he looked at Fern. Then he had a wonderful idea.

"Watch this, Fern!" he said.

Rory unfolded one lantern, lit a tiny candle inside it, and set it floating on the river. It bobbed along, glowing soft and gold, like a little floating star.

Fern's eyes went wide. "Oh! It is beautiful!"

Every evening after that, Rory lit one lantern and set it floating on the river. The fireflies danced around the lanterns. The ducks quacked happily. And Fern the little frog was not afraid anymore, because the river glowed with soft, warm light.

Soon, all of Rory's friends came to help. Dodo the duckling brought leaves to make little lantern boats. Basil the beaver brought twigs to hold the candles steady. And Fern, the littlest of all, carried the matches — very, very carefully — in her tiny froggy paws.

One evening, there was only one lantern left in the box. Just one.

Rory held it gently. "This is the last one," he said. "Who should have it?"

All his friends looked at Fern. She was still the shyest, and sometimes the dark still made her feel a little bit scared.

"Fern," said Rory, "this last lantern is for you. You can light it whenever the dark feels big."

Fern took the lantern with her tiny paws. Her eyes shone like the lantern itself. "Thank you, Rory," she whispered. "Now I will never be afraid of the dark."

That night, the last lantern floated down the river, glowing soft and gold. The fireflies danced around it. The river sang a quiet song. And Rory sat on the riverbank with all his friends, watching the little light float away into the starry night, lighting the way home for everyone.

"Good night, river," whispered Rory. "Good night, lanterns. Good night, friends."

And the little lantern floated on, soft and gold, until it was just a tiny star on the water.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little light, shared with friends, can make the dark feel small.
