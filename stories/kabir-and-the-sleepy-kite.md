# Kabir and the Sleepy Kite

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little house with a blue door, at the end of a sunny lane, there lived a boy named Kabir.

Kabir had curly black hair, bright brown eyes, and a big, happy smile. He loved to run, to jump, and to play in the park with his kite.

His kite was his very favourite toy. It was bright red with a long, yellow tail, and it danced in the sky like a happy bird.

One breezy afternoon, Kabir took his kite to the park. The wind was just right — soft and gentle, like a friendly push.

"Up, up, up!" laughed Kabir, as his kite soared high into the blue sky. "Look how high you can fly!"

The kite twirled and swooped and danced among the soft white clouds. Kabir ran across the grass, laughing, holding the string tight.

Then, all of a sudden, the wind changed. A soft gust blew the kite towards a big, leafy tree. The string tangled gently around a branch, and the kite came to rest among the leaves.

"Oh no," said Kabir, looking up. "My kite is stuck!"

Kabir tugged the string very gently. The kite wiggled a little, but it stayed right where it was, nestled among the green leaves.

Kabir sat down under the tree and looked up at his kite. It did not look stuck at all. It looked peaceful and cosy, tucked into the branches like a bird in a nest.

"Maybe my kite is tired," said Kabir softly. "Maybe it just wants a little rest."

So Kabir decided to sing his kite a lullaby, the same one his mother sang to him every night.

"Hush, little kite, rest in the tree," sang Kabir in a soft, sleepy voice. "The wind will rock you gently, just like the sea. Dream of the clouds, dream of the stars, and when you wake, you will fly far, far."

As Kabir sang, the leaves rustled softly, and the kite swayed gently in the breeze, as if it were listening. The sun began to set, painting the sky pink and gold, and the tree looked like a cosy, green blanket.

Kabir closed his eyes and leaned back against the trunk of the tree. He felt warm and sleepy, and he began to dream.

In his dream, Kabir was flying! He was holding his red kite, and together they soared up, up, up, past the clouds and into the night sky. They flew among the twinkling stars, and the moon smiled down at them. The stars sang a soft, sleepy song, and the kite danced happily in the starlight.

"Look, kite," said Kabir in his dream. "We are flying among the stars!"

When Kabir opened his eyes, the sky was soft and dusky, and the first stars were twinkling. His mother was walking towards him, smiling.

"Time to go home, Kabir," she said gently.

Kabir looked up at his kite, still resting in the tree. "Goodnight, little kite," he whispered. "Sleep well in your leafy bed. I will come back for you in the morning, and we will fly again."

Kabir walked home holding his mother's hand, feeling calm and happy. That night, he climbed into his cosy bed, pulled his blanket up to his chin, and closed his eyes.

He dreamed of his red kite, dancing among the stars, and he smiled in his sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A gentle song and a little patience can turn a worry into a wonderful dream.*
