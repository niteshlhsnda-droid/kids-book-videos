The Three Little Pigs

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there were three little pigs — Pip, Poppy, and Percy. They were brothers and sister, and they loved each other very much.

One sunny spring morning, their mother said, "My little ones, you are big enough now to build houses of your own. Build them strong, and always look after one another."

So the three little pigs set off down the lane, singing as they went.

Pip, who loved to play, built his house quickly out of straw. "Done!" he cried. "Now I can play all day!"

Poppy, who loved pretty things, built her house out of sticks. "Done!" she cried. "Now I can dance all day!"

But Percy, the eldest, worked slowly and carefully. He built his house out of strong red bricks, with a firm door and a snug chimney. "Done!" he cried at last. "Now we are safe, whatever comes."

One windy evening, along came a big, grey wolf named Wendel. Wendel was not a bad wolf at heart, but he was grumpy, and he loved to huff and puff.

He knocked on Pip's straw house. "Little pig, little pig, let me come in!"

"Not by the hair on my chinny-chin-chin!" squeaked Pip.

"Then I'll huff, and I'll puff, and I'll blow your house down!" cried Wendel.

And he huffed, and he puffed — WHOOSH! — and the straw house blew right away! Pip squealed and ran as fast as his little legs could carry him to Poppy's stick house.

Wendel trotted after him and knocked on the stick door. "Little pigs, little pigs, let me come in!"

"Not by the hair on our chinny-chin-chins!" squeaked Pip and Poppy.

"Then I'll huff, and I'll puff, and I'll blow your house down!" cried Wendel.

And he huffed, and he puffed — WHOOSH! — and the stick house tumbled down! Pip and Poppy squealed and ran to Percy's strong brick house.

Wendel knocked on the brick door. "Little pigs, little pigs, let me come in!"

"Not by the hair on our chinny-chin-chins!" squeaked all three pigs together.

"Then I'll huff, and I'll puff, and I'll blow your house down!" cried Wendel.

And he huffed, and he puffed, and he huffed, and he puffed — but the brick house did not move one little bit. Not even a brick wiggled.

Wendel was so cross that he climbed up onto the roof and slid down the chimney. But Percy had thought of that too — a big pot of cold water was waiting at the bottom!

SPLASH! Down went Wendel, into the cold water. "Brrr!" he cried, leaping out of the chimney and running away down the lane, dripping and shivering. "Those pigs are too clever for me!"

The three little pigs laughed and hugged each other. Then Percy opened his door wide.

"Come back for tea tomorrow, Wendel," called Percy kindly, "if you promise not to huff and puff at our houses any more."

And from that day on, whenever the wind blew, the three little pigs were safe and snug in their brick house — all together, just as their mother had told them.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Work done carefully lasts longest — and looking after one another keeps everyone safe.
