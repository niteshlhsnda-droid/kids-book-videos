Aarav and the Moonlight Kite

A gentle original bedtime tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little house with a flat rooftop and a sky full of stars, there lived a boy named Aarav.

Aarav loved kites. With his grandmother's help, he had made the prettiest kite in the whole lane: bright red with a long yellow tail that fluttered like a ribbon.

One evening, just as the sun was setting, Aarav took his kite up to the rooftop, with his grandmother watching from a chair nearby, as she always did.

"Fly, my kite, fly!" cried Aarav, running across the rooftop.

The wind caught the kite, and up it soared — higher and higher, past the rooftops, past the tallest tree, right up towards the moon!

"Oh!" gasped Aarav. "My kite is flying to the moon!"

And indeed, the kite danced in the moonlight, and the moon seemed to smile down at it. As the kite brushed past the moon's silver glow, tiny sparkles of stardust drifted down and settled on its tail, twinkling like fireflies.

"Come back, little kite," called Aarav softly. "It is nearly bedtime."

His grandmother smiled from her chair. "What a brave kite," she said. "It touched the moon and came safely home — just like a good dream."

Aarav whispered a sleepy wish to his kite: "Thank you for flying so high. Tomorrow we will fly again, and I will tell you all about my dreams."

Down floated the kite, gentle as a feather, right into Aarav's hands. Its tail still sparkled with stardust.

That night, Aarav hung the kite beside his bed. As he closed his eyes, the stardust twinkled softly, and Aarav dreamed that he was flying with his kite, up among the stars, while the moon whispered, "Goodnight, little flyer. Goodnight."

And in the morning, when Aarav woke up, the stardust was still there, twinkling — a tiny piece of the moon, keeping his dreams bright.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Dreams fly highest when someone you love is watching nearby.
