# Clever Gretel

*A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little house with a red roof, there lived a cook named Gretel.

Gretel was the best cook in the whole village. Her bread was golden. Her soup was splendid. And her roast chicken — oh! Her roast chicken was the most delicious thing anyone had ever tasted.

One morning, her master called her into the kitchen.

"Gretel," he said, "a very important guest is coming for dinner tonight. Roast two fat chickens — the finest you have ever made!"

"Yes, master!" said Gretel, and she set to work.

All afternoon, the kitchen smelled wonderful. The chickens turned golden in the oven. Gretel brushed them with butter. She sprinkled them with herbs. At last, they were ready — two beautiful, golden, crispy chickens, sitting on a platter.

Gretel looked at them. She sniffed them. And her tummy rumbled — RUMBLE!

"Oh dear," said Gretel. "I have been cooking all day, and I am so very hungry. Surely the master would not mind if I tasted just one little bit?"

She nibbled a wing. Mmm! So delicious!

She nibbled a leg. Mmm! So crispy!

And before she knew it — nibble, nibble, nibble — she had eaten the whole first chicken!

"Oh no!" cried Gretel. "What shall I do? The guest is coming, and there is only one chicken left!"

She looked at the second chicken. It smelled so good. Her tummy rumbled again — RUMBLE, RUMBLE!

"Well," said Gretel, "one chicken is not enough for a guest and a master. And I am still so hungry..."

Nibble, nibble, nibble — and the second chicken was gone too!

Just then — KNOCK, KNOCK! — the guest arrived!

"Oh dear, oh dear!" whispered Gretel. "What shall I say?"

But Gretel was clever. Very clever. She ran to the door with a big, worried face.

"Dear guest," she whispered, "you must run away quickly! My master has gone quite mad! He is waving his arms about and shouting that he wants to cut off both your ears!"

The guest turned pale. "My ears!" he cried. And he ran away down the lane as fast as his legs could carry him!

Then Gretel ran to her master with an even bigger, worried face.

"Master, master!" she cried. "The guest has run off with both your chickens! Quick — run after him!"

The master grabbed his hat and ran out of the door, shouting, "Stop! Stop! My chickens!"

The guest heard the shouting and saw the master waving his arms. "He wants my ears!" he cried, and he ran faster than ever!

And Gretel? She sat down in the kitchen, wiped the chicken crumbs from her chin, and laughed until her sides ached.

When her master came back, puffing and panting, Gretel said, very sweetly, "Master, I am sorry about the chickens. I was so hungry, and they smelled so good. Next time, please let me have a chicken of my own — and I promise I will never, ever eat the guest's dinner again."

Her master looked at her. He looked at the empty platter. And then — to Gretel's great surprise — he laughed too!

"Gretel, you are too clever for your own good!" he said. "Very well. From now on, there will always be a chicken for you as well as for our guests."

And from that day on, Gretel never went hungry — and she never had to be quite so clever again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A quick mind can solve a tricky spot — but an honest word and a full tummy are better still.*
