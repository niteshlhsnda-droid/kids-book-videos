The Bharunda Birds

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Far, far away, beside a sparkling lake, there lived a bird shaped like a plump, soft-feathered dove. But on its one neck sat TWO heads — each with its own little beak and blinking eyes. And the two heads never, ever agreed.

The bird was called Bharunda. Sunny was the head on the left, and Grumpy was the head on the right.

One morning, Sunny spotted a bunch of sweet, juicy berries hanging from a branch over the lake. "Yum!" said Sunny, and gobble-gobble, he ate them all up. "Delicious! You should have tasted them, Grumpy!"

"I didn't get even ONE berry," grumbled Grumpy. "You never share!"

The next day, Grumpy saw something hanging from a branch — a bunch of yucky, bitter berries. A sneaky little thought wriggled into his head. His eyes narrowed into sneaky little slits, and a little smile curled around his beak. Can YOU guess what it was? He didn't tell Sunny.

And gobble-gobble — Grumpy ate the bitter berries, every single one.

Now, remember — Sunny and Grumpy had two heads but only ONE tummy. So the bitter berries went into Grumpy's mouth, but BOTH of them tasted them.

"Ugh! Bitter! Horrible!" cried Sunny, making a face. Can you make a bitter face too? "My tummy hurts!"

"My tummy hurts too!" sighed Grumpy.

The two heads looked at each other, their bright eyes gone droopy and sad.

"I'm sorry I didn't share," whispered Sunny.

"I'm sorry I ate the bitter berries," whispered Grumpy.

From that day on, whenever Sunny found sweet berries, he nudged half toward Grumpy's beak with his own, and the two beaks munched side by side, purple juice dribbling down their soft feathers. And whenever Grumpy found something yummy, he'd nudge Sunny softly and whisper, "Sunny… come taste this."

And their one tummy was happy ever after.

That night, the two heads leaned softly against each other beside the quiet lake, and all four eyes drifted shut.

Goodnight, Sunny. Goodnight, Grumpy. The end. 🌙

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When we share one life, we must share with love — what hurts one, hurts all.
