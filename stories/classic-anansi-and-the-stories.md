# Anansi and the Stories

*A beloved African folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, all the stories in the world belonged to Nyame, the great Sky God.

Nyame kept the stories in a shining box, high up in the sky, and no one else could tell them.

But down on the earth there lived a clever little spider named Anansi.

Anansi loved stories more than anything — funny stories, kind stories, and stories that make you think.

"Oh, how I wish I could tell stories to all the children of the world," said Anansi one sunny morning. "I will climb up to the sky and ask Nyame for them!"

So Anansi spun a long, silvery thread and climbed up, up, up to the great home of Nyame.

"Great Nyame," said Anansi, bowing low. "Please, may I have the stories, so I can share them with all the children of the world?"

Nyame smiled. "Little spider, the stories are very precious. If you want them, you must bring me three things. First, a gourd full of hornets. Second, a long python. Third, a leopard."

Anansi's eight legs wobbled a little — but he was clever, and he was kind, and he did not give up.

He climbed back down to think.

First, he went to the hornets' nest with a gourd and a little pot of honey.

"Dear hornets," he called. "It is going to rain! Come into my gourd and taste this sweet honey."

Buzz, buzz, buzz! The hornets flew in to taste the honey, and Anansi popped the lid on. Then he opened the lid and let them all fly home again.

Next, Anansi went to the python, who lay curled in the grass.

"Dear python," said Anansi, "I have a long bamboo stick, and I wonder — are you longer than this stick, or is the stick longer than you?"

The python stretched out proudly beside the bamboo to be measured. "I am longer, of course!"

"Let us measure you properly," said Anansi, and he tied the python gently to the stick with a soft vine. Then he untied the vine, and the python slithered away, free and happy.

Then Anansi went to the leopard, who sat by a deep pit in the forest.

"Dear leopard," said Anansi, "a shiny ball has rolled down into this pit, and I cannot reach it. You are so strong and brave — will you jump in and fetch it for me?"

The leopard jumped down into the pit — and then he could not climb out! The sides were too steep.

"Help!" cried the leopard.

Anansi lowered a long vine into the pit. "Hold on tight," he said, and he pulled until the leopard was safe on the grass again.

"Thank you, little spider," said the leopard, and he bounded away.

Anansi climbed back to the sky.

"Great Nyame," he said, "I met the hornets, the python, and the leopard — but I was kind to them all, and they are all safe and free."

Nyame laughed a great warm laugh. "Oh, clever little Anansi! You have done it with kindness, not with tricks. The stories are yours!"

And Nyame gave Anansi the shining box of stories, and Anansi shared them with every child in the world.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Stories grow sweeter when they are shared.*
