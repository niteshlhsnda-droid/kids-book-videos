# The Merchant of Seri

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, two merchants walked from village to village, pots and pans clanking on their backs. One was Daya. He smiled at every customer, and his prices were always fair. The other was Lobhi — greedy, greedy, greedy! He always wanted more and more.

"Let's go our separate ways," said Daya, "so we don't fight over customers."

"Fine!" said Lobhi. "You take the morning. I'll take the afternoon!"

In one village, in a tiny mud hut with a saggy thatched roof, lived a poor old grandmother and her little granddaughter, Pari. They had one treasure — an old bowl, black with age.

One afternoon, Lobhi came to their door. "Pots! Pans! Buy my shiny pots and pans!"

"Grandmother," said Pari, "can we trade our old bowl for a bright little painted cup?"

Lobhi looked at the bowl — and his eyes grew wide. Under the black dirt… what do you think he saw shining there? GOLD! But he said, smooth as honey, "It's worth nothing. I'll give you two copper coins."

Pari shook her head. "Then keep your coins, sir."

Lobhi tossed the bowl aside and walked away — but as he went, he kept looking BACK at the little hut.

The next morning, Daya came to the same door. "Pots! Pans! Buy my pots and pans!"

"Grandmother," said Pari, "let's ask this kind merchant."

Daya picked up the bowl. He tapped it — ting! — and his eyes grew round as full moons. "Little one… this is a GOLDEN bowl! It is worth more than everything I carry!"

"Then pay us fairly," said the grandmother, "and it's yours."

Daya did better than fair. He filled their little hut till it overflowed — clanking copper pots, heavy sacks of rice, and bright coins that chimed. Can you hear them chiming?

Lobhi came hurrying back, calling, "Wait! I'll buy that bowl!" — but Daya was already walking away, and greedy Lobhi slipped — splash! — into the soft mud by the road.

The sun was sinking low, and the little hut glowed warm and quiet in the evening light.

Their hut was full of warm, jingling treasure now. But what Pari remembered most was Daya's kind smile — warmer than all the gold in the bowl.

Goodnight, little one. The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Honesty is the best wealth — a fair heart shines brighter than gold.*
