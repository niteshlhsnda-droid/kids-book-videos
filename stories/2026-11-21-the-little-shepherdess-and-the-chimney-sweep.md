# The Little Shepherdess and the Chimney Sweep

In a cozy little house at the end of a quiet lane, a fire crackled in the sitting room every evening. Above the fire, on the wooden mantelpiece, stood two small porcelain friends: a little shepherdess with rosy cheeks, a blue ribbon in her golden hair, and a tiny golden crook; and a little chimney sweep with a sooty cap and a small ladder.

Every evening, when the house grew quiet and the moon peeped through the window, the two friends talked about their day. She told him about her painted meadow. He told her about the clouds above the chimney tops. They were the very best of friends.

But one evening, the big old wooden Mandarin on the shelf above them cleared his throat. "Little shepherdess," he said in his creaky old voice, "it is time for you to marry the grand Goat-Legs and move away to live in the dark cupboard."

The little shepherdess looked at the grand Goat-Legs, with his stiff wooden legs and his stiff wooden smile, and her heart sank to her little porcelain toes. She did not want to leave her friend. That night, when the moon was high, the little chimney sweep whispered, "Don't be afraid. We will climb up and see the wide world together."

So when the clock struck twelve, the two little friends climbed down from the mantelpiece, hand in hand. They tiptoed across the rug, past the sleeping cat, and up to the fireplace. The chimney sweep went first, because he knew the way.

Up and up they climbed, through the dark, sooty chimney, until suddenly — oh! — there was the sky. The rooftop was washed in moonlight, and the whole sleeping town lay below them, all rooftops and twinkling windows.

"Look," said the chimney sweep, pointing with his ladder. "There's the river, shining like a ribbon. And there's the church tower, and the baker's shop, and the little park." The little shepherdess laughed, and her laugh sounded like a tiny bell.

But then a cold wind blew across the rooftops, and the little shepherdess shivered. The world was so big, and so wide, and so far from the warm fire. "I miss our mantelpiece," she whispered. "I miss the crackling fire and you beside me." The chimney sweep took her hand. "Then home is where we belong," he said.

So down they climbed, through the chimney, across the rug, past the sleeping cat, and back up onto the mantelpiece, just as the first pink light of morning touched the window. The big old Mandarin looked at them — sooty, sleepy, and side by side — and nodded, slowly, and smiled. "Very well. Stay together, little friends."

And so the two friends stayed side by side, just as they had always wanted. Every evening the fire crackled, the moon peeped in, and they talked about their day, happy to be home.

Sleep now, little shepherdess, safe beside your friend.

The end. 🌙
