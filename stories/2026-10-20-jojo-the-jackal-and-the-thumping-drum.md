# Jojo the Jackal and the Thumping Drum

Under the roots of an old banyan tree, in a den lined with soft dry leaves, lived a young jackal named Jojo.

Every night, Jojo curled up tight in his leaves, tucked his long ears flat, and fell asleep to the gentle hush of the wind in the branches.

But one night, just as his eyes were fluttering shut, he heard it.

THUMP. Thump. Thump.

Jojo's ears shot straight up. His heart went thump, thump, thump too, keeping time with the sound.

"What is THAT?" he whispered.

The sound was coming from the clearing by the stream, slow and heavy. It sounded like giant footsteps. Or — Jojo gulped — like a great drum being beaten by something enormous.

Jojo pulled his leaf blanket right over his head. "If I am very quiet and very still," he thought, "maybe it will go away."

But the thumping did not go away. THUMP. Thump. Thump.

Jojo squeezed his eyes shut. He thought of his mother, and the lullaby she hummed when thunder rolled over the hills — low and soft, until his trembling melted away like morning mist.

Thinking of her song, Jojo felt a tiny warm spark inside his chest. He peeked out from under his leaves.

Moonlight spilled silver over the grass. The thumping was louder now, and it was definitely coming from the clearing by the stream.

Jojo's paws trembled. Part of him wanted to hide. But a bigger part of him wanted to know. Slowly, one soft paw after another, he crept out of his den and tiptoed toward the clearing, his fluffy tail low behind him.

In the middle of the clearing stood an old war drum, left behind long ago by travellers, leaning against a grey rock. And above it hung one long, dry branch of the peepal tree.

The night breeze rocked the branch gently back and forth, back and forth. And every time the wind swung it, the tip of the branch knocked softly against the drum.

THUMP. Thump. Thump.

Jojo stared. His whiskers twitched. And then — he giggled. A small, surprised giggle that bounced off the rock.

"No monster!" he said. "No giant! It is only the wind, playing the drum like a musician!"

Jojo trotted up to the branch and gently pushed it aside with his nose, tucking it behind a stone where the wind could not reach it. The clearing fell silent, except for the soft shush of the stream.

Jojo trotted home feeling tall and warm inside. He curled up in his dry leaves, tucked his long ears flat, and listened to the quiet forest.

The scary sound had only been the wind. And now he knew.

Sleep now, little Jojo.

The end. 🌙
