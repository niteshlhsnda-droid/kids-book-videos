# The Heron and the Fish

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, beside a wide blue river, there lived a tall grey heron named Hari.

Hari had long, thin legs like sticks, a long yellow beak, and a very, very picky tummy.

Every morning, Hari stood on one leg at the edge of the river, watching the fish swim by.

"Breakfast!" he would say, and dip his beak into the water.

One morning, a school of little silver fish swam past, wiggling and giggling.

"Good morning, Mr. Heron!" they called. "Would you like a nibble?"

Hari looked down his long beak at them. "Too small," he sniffed. "I am a grand heron. I only eat BIG fish. Shoo, shoo!"

The little fish swam away, laughing and splashing.

A little later, a round, rosy fish swam by, slow and fat.

"Good morning, Mr. Heron!" she called. "I am fresh and tasty!"

Hari looked down his long beak at her. "Too fat," he sniffed. "I only eat SLIM fish. Shoo, shoo!"

The rosy fish swam away, wiggling her tail.

Then a long, stripy fish swam by, quick as a flash.

"Good morning, Mr. Heron!" he called. "Catch me if you can!"

Hari looked down his long beak at him. "Too fast," he sniffed. "I only eat SLOW fish. Shoo, shoo!"

The stripy fish darted away with a splash.

All morning, Hari stood on one leg, turning away fish after fish. Too small, too fat, too fast, too slow, too spotty, too plain.

By noon, the sun was high and hot. Hari's tummy began to rumble. Rumble, rumble, grumble.

"Oh dear," said Hari. "I am so hungry. Where have all the fish gone?"

But the fish had all swum away to the cool, deep water. The river was quiet and still.

Hari looked left. Hari looked right. Not a single fish was left.

Then, at the muddy edge of the river, Hari saw something small and slow. It was a little brown snail, gliding along on her tiny foot.

"Oh no," said Hari with a sigh. "Is this all that is left? A little snail?"

Hari's tummy rumbled again, louder this time. Rumble, GRUMBLE, grumble.

So Hari bent down his long neck and ate the little snail. It was not a grand heron's breakfast. It was not big or slim or slow. But it was better than nothing at all.

As the sun went down, Hari flew slowly home to his nest in the tall reeds. His wings felt heavy. His tummy still felt empty.

"Tomorrow," he said to himself, yawning a big, wide yawn, "tomorrow I will not be so picky. A little fish is better than no fish at all."

And the very next morning, when the first little silver fish swam by and said, "Good morning, Mr. Heron!" — Hari smiled and said, "Good morning, little friend. Thank you. That is just right."

He ate his breakfast happily, tucked his long beak under his wing, and took a long, peaceful nap in the warm morning sun.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Do not turn away good things while waiting for perfect ones — a little is better than none at all.*
