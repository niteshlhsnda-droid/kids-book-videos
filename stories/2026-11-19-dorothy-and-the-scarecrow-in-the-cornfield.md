# Dorothy and the Scarecrow in the Cornfield

One bright morning, Dorothy skipped along the yellow brick road, her little dog Toto trotting beside her. Poppies nodded in the sunshine, and her silver shoes went tap, tap, tap, all the way to the Emerald City.

Soon they came to a wide cornfield, where tall green stalks rustled in the breeze. In the middle stood a scarecrow on a tall pole, with a pointed hat and a friendly painted smile. He waved one straw arm. "Look, Toto!" said Dorothy. "He is waving hello!"

Toto barked once, twice — and the scarecrow's head turned. "Oh, please!" he called in a soft, rustly voice. "Would you lift me down? I have been stuck up here all morning." Dorothy's eyes went wide. A talking scarecrow! She hurried into the cornfield and lifted him gently off his pole.

The Scarecrow wobbled on his straw legs and nearly toppled over, so Dorothy caught his arm and steadied him. "Thank you," he said, bowing his pointed hat. "I have a straw head, and I would love to have brains. Will you take me with you to see the Wizard?"

"Of course," said Dorothy, smiling. "Friends walk together." She shared her bread and cheese, though the Scarecrow only nibbled a crumb and laughed. Then she tied a blue ribbon around his hat. "Now you look splendid," she said, and the Scarecrow stood a little taller.

But soon the yellow brick road split into two, and Dorothy did not know which way to go. A gust of wind blew the Scarecrow's hat clean off into the corn. "Oh dear," sighed the Scarecrow. "If only I had brains, I would know the way."

Then the Scarecrow tilted his head and listened — to the wind, the river, and the poppies. "Wait," he said slowly. "The river runs beside the road to the Emerald City, and I can hear it to the left!" Dorothy clapped. "You found the way yourself!" The Scarecrow grinned. "Perhaps my straw head is cleverer than I thought."

They took the left-hand road, and soon they came to a little stream that sang over smooth stones. Toto did not like the splashing water one bit. So the Scarecrow lifted him onto his shoulder, stepped carefully from stone to stone, and set him down safe and dry.

As the sun began to sink, painting the sky pink and gold, they came to a snug haystack beside the road. Dorothy spread her shawl, and the Scarecrow piled soft hay into a cosy nest. "You keep watch, and I will sleep," said Dorothy. "Tomorrow we will walk to the Emerald City together."

Dorothy curled up in the hay with Toto warm against her side. The Scarecrow sat nearby, humming a soft, rustly song as the first stars blinked awake. "Goodnight, Scarecrow," whispered Dorothy. "Goodnight, dear Dorothy," rustled the Scarecrow. "Sweet dreams."

Sleep now, little Dorothy.

The end. 🌙
