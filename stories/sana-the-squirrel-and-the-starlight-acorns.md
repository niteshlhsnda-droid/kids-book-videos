# Sana the Squirrel and the Starlight Acorns

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a tall oak tree at the edge of a sleepy village lived a little squirrel named Sana. Sana loved collecting acorns. She had acorns for breakfast, acorns for lunch, and a whole pile of acorns saved for winter.

But Sana had one special wish. Every night, she looked up at the sky and whispered, "Oh, how I wish I could collect a star, just one tiny star, to light up my little home."

One clear autumn night, when the moon was round and bright, something wonderful happened. As Sana scampered along her branch, she saw tiny golden sparkles falling softly from the sky — like little stars, drifting down, down, down into the forest.

"What are those?" gasped Sana. She scampered down the trunk and found them resting on the moss: five acorns, glowing gently with warm golden light. Starlight acorns!

Sana gathered them carefully in her paws. They were warm and soft, and they glowed like little lanterns. "Oh, how beautiful!" she whispered. "Now my home will shine all night long!"

But on her way home, Sana met her friend Titu the mouse, who was shivering in the dark. "I am afraid of the dark path," squeaked Titu. "I cannot find my way home."

Sana smiled and gave him one starlight acorn. "Hold this, Titu. It will light your way."

Titu's path glowed golden, and he scampered home happily. "Thank you, Sana!"

Next, Sana met old Kaki the owl, who could not read her bedtime book because her tree hole was too dark. Sana gave her another starlight acorn. Kaki's hole glowed warm and cosy. "Thank you, Sana!" hooted Kaki.

Then Sana met the baby rabbits, who were too scared to sleep in their dark burrow. Sana gave them another starlight acorn, and their burrow glowed like a night-light. "Thank you, Sana!" squeaked the bunnies.

When Sana finally reached her own tree, she had only two starlight acorns left. She placed one beside her bed, and it glowed softly, like a tiny star come down to keep her company.

And the last one? She planted it under her oak tree, where it would grow into a tree that glowed a little, every single night, for everyone in the forest.

That night, Sana fell asleep smiling. She had wished for a star of her own — and instead, she had shared her stars with all her friends. And somehow, that made her home shine even brighter.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing something wonderful makes it shine even brighter.*
