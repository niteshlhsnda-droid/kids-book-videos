# The Wolf and the Lamb

*A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High up in the green hills, a clear little stream ran down through the meadow.

Every morning, a big grey wolf named Rowan came to the top of the stream to drink the cold, fresh water.

Every morning, a small white lamb named Lily came to the bottom of the stream to drink the very same water.

One sunny morning, Rowan the wolf looked down the stream and saw Lily sipping gently from the water below.

"Hey, little lamb!" growled Rowan, puffing out his chest. "You are muddying my water! Stop drinking at once!"

Lily looked up with her soft brown eyes and spoke in a quiet, gentle voice.

"Dear Mr. Wolf," she said sweetly, "how can I muddy your water? I am drinking far below you, and the water flows down from you to me."

Rowan blinked. He had not thought of that. But he wanted to sound important, so he tried again.

"Well, last spring you said rude things about me!" he growled, a little less loudly.

Lily shook her little head and smiled. "Last spring I was not even born yet," she said. "I was still a tiny lamb in my mother's soft woolly arms."

Rowan felt his ears grow warm. He shuffled his big paws in the grass.

"Well, then, it must have been your cousin!" he said, but his voice was much softer now.

Lily giggled. "My cousin lives far away, over the hills," she said. "He has never even seen this stream."

Rowan sat down with a bump. All his big, growly excuses had floated away, just like leaves on the stream.

Just then, his tummy gave a very loud rumble. "GRRRR!" it said. "GRUMBLE, GRUMBLE!"

Lily did not run away. She trotted over to a patch of sweet green clover and nibbled a big bunch.

"Here," she said kindly, holding it out. "You must be hungry. Sweet clover always makes my tummy feel happy."

Rowan took the clover very gently between his teeth. He chewed slowly. "Mmm," he said. "That is the sweetest clover I have ever tasted."

"Would you like to drink with me?" asked Lily. "The water is cool, and the sun is warm, and there is plenty for us both."

Rowan nodded, and he felt a funny, warm feeling in his chest. It was much nicer than growling.

So the big grey wolf and the little white lamb lay down side by side in the soft grass.

They sipped the cool water together. They shared the sweet clover. They watched the fluffy clouds drift slowly across the blue sky.

"I am sorry I growled at you," said Rowan quietly. "You were kind, even when I was not."

"That is all right," said Lily, smiling. "Everyone feels grumpy sometimes. A kind word helps it float away."

From that day on, Rowan and Lily met at the stream every morning.

Rowan never growled again, and Lily never felt afraid again.

And the little stream kept singing as it ran down through the meadow, happy that the wolf and the lamb were friends at last.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A gentle answer and a kind heart can turn a grumpy stranger into a friend.*
