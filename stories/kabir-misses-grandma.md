# Kabir Misses Grandma

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Kabir was a small boy, five years old.

His grandma lived far, far away.

Across the big sea.

He used to see her every day.

Now he saw her on a little screen.

One evening, Kabir felt sad.

He missed Grandma's hugs.

He missed her stories.

He missed her warm lap.

"I miss Grandma," he said.

Tears filled his eyes.

Mama sat beside him.

She said, "I see you feel sad."

Kabir nodded.

"It is okay to feel sad," Mama said.

"Missing someone means you love them."

She held him close.

She said, "Love can travel farther than any road."

"Love can travel farther than any road," she said again.

"Love can travel farther than any road," she said one more time.

Kabir thought about that.

Farther than any road.

"What can I do?" he asked.

"We can send her love," Mama said.

They made a card together.

Kabir drew Grandma's house.

He drew himself waving.

He drew a big red heart.

"I love you, Grandma," he wrote.

Then they called her.

Grandma's face appeared on the screen.

She smiled so big.

"Kabir!" she said.

"I miss you too, my darling."

Kabir showed her the card.

Grandma clapped.

"It is beautiful," she said.

"I will keep it by my bed."

They talked and laughed.

Kabir felt the sad feeling shrink.

The love feeling grew.

Love really could travel far.

That night, Kabir put the card by the door.

To post in the morning.

He felt sad earlier.

Now he felt close to Grandma.

Even across the sea.

Mama kissed his forehead.

"Grandma loves you very much," she said.

The night was soft.

Kabir closed his eyes.

He was loved.

Near and far.

Goodnight, Kabir.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Missing someone is love with nowhere to sit — so we send the love to them.*
