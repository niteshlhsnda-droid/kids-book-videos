The Mole and His Mother

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Deep under the green meadow, where the earth was soft and cool and smelled of rain, there lived a mole family in a cozy tunnel home.

There was Mother Mole, with her velvet-soft fur, and her little son, Monty.

Now, moles are not famous for their eyesight. They live in the dark, you see, and they find their way by sniffing and feeling. But Monty the little mole did not like being told what he could not do.

"I can see perfectly well!" Monty announced one morning, puffing out his tiny chest. "I can see better than ANY mole in this whole meadow!"

Mother Mole smiled. "Can you, my love?"

"Oh yes!" said Monty. "I can see the worms. I can see the roots. I can even see... um... things. Lots of things!"

Now, Mother Mole was very wise. She did not scold Monty for boasting. Scolding never helps a heart that's busy bragging. Instead, she had an idea.

"Well then," she said, "let's play a little game. Close your eyes — no, wait, keep them open! Use your wonderful eyes and tell me: what is this?"

She held something under Monty's nose.

Monty squinted. He stared very, very hard. His little nose twitched and twitched.

It was a lump of frankincense — sweet-smelling sap from a faraway tree. It smelled of pine trees and honey.

But Monty was so busy trying to SEE it that he forgot to smell it.

"Um..." said Monty. "It is... a pebble! Yes, a small gray pebble!"

Mother Mole gave a soft little giggle. "Oh, my darling boy," she said, nuzzling him. "That is not a pebble at all. It is frankincense — the sweetest-smelling lump in the whole tunnel. You didn't see what it was... and you didn't even smell it!"

Monty went pink right to the tip of his nose. "Oh," he said in a very small voice.

Mother Mole wrapped him in a warm hug. "It's all right, my love. There's no shame in not seeing well — we moles were made for sniffing and feeling, and we're wonderful at that! The only silly thing was pretending. Boasting is like wearing someone else's big shoes — they just make you trip."

Monty thought about this. Then he took a big, honest sniff.

"Frankincense!" he cried. "It smells like... like the pine forest after rain! Like honey! Like warm bread from the oven!"

"There you go," said Mother Mole proudly. "That's MY boy. The best sniffer in the whole meadow."

From that day on, Monty never boasted again. When he didn't know something, he said so — "I don't know, can you show me?" — and little by little, he learned more than ever before.

And whenever a boasty thought popped into his head, he would take a deep sniff of something sweet and remember: telling the truth about what you can do is the smartest thing of all.

So tonight, little one, sniff the sweet night air. Be proud of what you do best — and it's always okay to say, "I don't know yet."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Don't boast — be honest with yourself, and proud of what you do best.
