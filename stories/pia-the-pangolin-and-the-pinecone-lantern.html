Pia the Pangolin and the Pinecone Lantern

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a deep green forest, lived a little pangolin named Pia.

Pia had soft brown scales that shone like polished wood, a long curly tail, and a very curious nose. But Pia had one big worry: she was afraid of the dark.

Every evening, when the sun went down and the forest grew dark, Pia would curl into a tight little ball and hide under the leaves.

"I wish I had a light of my own," whispered Pia. "Then the dark would not feel so big."

One evening, as Pia peeked out from under the leaves, she saw a tiny glow moving through the trees. It was Twinkle the firefly!

"Hello, Pia!" said Twinkle, glowing bright and golden. "Why are you hiding?"

"I am afraid of the dark," said Pia shyly. "Do you ever get afraid?"

"Never!" said Twinkle. "My light goes wherever I go. Would you like me to show you how to make a light of your own?"

"Oh yes, please!" said Pia, uncurling a little.

So Twinkle led Pia to the tallest pine tree, where the biggest pinecones grew. Together, they gathered soft moss, dry leaves, and one big open pinecone.

"Now," said Twinkle, "put the moss inside the pinecone, like a little nest. Then I will sit inside and glow!"

Pia placed the moss carefully. Twinkle fluttered in and glowed — and the pinecone became a beautiful lantern, warm and golden!

"Oh!" cried Pia. "It is the prettiest light I have ever seen!"

Pia carried her pinecone lantern through the forest. Its warm light showed her the path, the mushrooms, and the friendly faces of the night animals.

"Hello, Pia!" called Olga the owl from her branch. "What a lovely light!"

"Would you like to walk with us?" asked Pia. And Olga swooped down and walked beside her, softly and slowly.

Soon, all the night animals were walking together behind Pia's lantern — Olga the owl, the field mice, and even a sleepy hedgehog — all sharing the warm golden light.

When they reached Pia's home under the leaves, Pia hung the lantern on a branch.

"Thank you, Twinkle," said Pia. "Now the dark is not scary at all. It is just the time when friends share their light."

And Pia fell asleep in the warm glow of her pinecone lantern, dreaming of fireflies and friends.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little light, shared with friends, makes the dark feel small.
