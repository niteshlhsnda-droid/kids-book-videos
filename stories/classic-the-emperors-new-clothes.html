The Emperor's New Clothes

A timeless tale by Hans Christian Andersen for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there was an emperor named Otto who loved new clothes more than anything in the world.

He had a coat for Mondays and a coat for Tuesdays and a coat for every day of the week — and then some. He spent all his gold on fine robes, and he changed his clothes ten times a day, just for fun.

One day, two tailors came to the palace. They were tricky tailors, and they loved tricks more than sewing.

"Your Majesty," they said, bowing low, "we can weave the most wonderful cloth in the world. It is light as air and bright as a rainbow. And it has a magic secret: it is invisible to anyone who is silly or not good at their job."

"Wonderful!" cried Emperor Otto. "Weave me a suit from this magic cloth at once!"

The tailors set up two big looms and pretended to weave, day and night. Clickety-clack, clickety-clack went the empty looms. They asked for gold thread and silver thread, and they tucked it all away in their pockets.

Soon, the emperor sent his wisest minister to see how the cloth was coming along.

The minister looked at the empty looms. He saw nothing at all. "Oh, no," he thought. "If I say I see nothing, everyone will think I am silly! I must pretend."

"Beautiful cloth!" said the minister. "So light! So bright!"

Then the emperor himself came to look. He stared at the empty looms. He saw nothing at all. "Oh, no," he thought. "If I say I see nothing, everyone will think I am silly! I must pretend."

"Magnificent!" said the emperor. "Weave faster! I will wear this suit in the grand parade!"

On parade day, the tailors pretended to dress the emperor in his new suit. They fussed and fiddled and said, "Turn a little this way, Your Majesty. Perfect! You look splendid!"

The emperor marched out into the street, wearing his crown, his shoes — and not a stitch of anything else.

All the people lining the street looked at each other. They saw an emperor wearing only his crown and his shoes. But nobody wanted to look silly, so everyone clapped and cheered.

"How splendid!" they cried. "How magnificent!"

Then a little girl named Mia, sitting on her father's shoulders, looked at the emperor and giggled.

"But he isn't wearing any clothes at all!" she cried, in her clear little voice.

For a moment, there was silence. Then a chuckle ran through the crowd. Then a laugh. Then everyone was laughing, kindly and happily — and the emperor laughed loudest of all.

"Ha, ha, ha!" laughed Emperor Otto. "The little girl is right! I have been very silly. Thank you, Mia, for telling the truth when everyone else was afraid to."

Emperor Otto went straight back to the palace, put on his favourite real robe — a soft blue one with golden buttons — and invited the whole town to a party. Mia sat in the place of honour, and everyone had cake.

And from that day on, Emperor Otto still loved clothes, but he loved the truth even more. And whenever anyone in the palace was afraid to speak up, they remembered little Mia and her brave, honest voice.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Telling the truth takes courage — and a good leader laughs at his own mistakes.
