Tenali Rama and the Cat

A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the town of Vijayanagara, there lived a clever man named Tenali Rama.

Tenali Rama was funny and kind, and everyone in the town loved his clever ideas.

One day, Tenali Rama's wife, Sharda, said, "Tenali, little mice keep nibbling our rice in the kitchen. Let us keep a cat."

So Tenali Rama brought home a small grey cat named Moti.

Moti had round green eyes, soft paws, and a very loud purr.

The first night, Moti chased the mice away. Tap, tap, tap went her little paws across the kitchen floor.

But the next morning, Sharda found the milk pot empty.

"Moti!" she cried. "Who drank all the milk?"

Moti sat beside the empty pot, licking her lips, looking very pleased with herself.

Every morning it was the same. Moti chased the mice at night, and in the morning the milk was gone.

Sharda was not happy at all. "Tenali," she said, "this cat drinks more milk than the mice ever ate rice. We must do something!"

Tenali Rama stroked his chin and thought.

"Do not worry, Sharda," he said. "I have a kind idea."

That evening, Tenali Rama took a tiny saucer and filled it with warm milk.

He set the saucer in a sunny corner by the door, right beside Moti's soft basket.

"This saucer is just for you, Moti," he said. "Your very own milk, every morning."

Then Tenali Rama lifted the family's big milk pot and hung it high on a hook, far above the floor.

The next morning, Moti woke up and stretched. She sniffed the air and smelled milk.

She trotted to her sunny corner and found her own little saucer, full to the brim.

Moti lapped up the milk, purring louder than ever. Slurp, slurp, slurp.

Then she looked up at the big milk pot, hanging high above her head.

She tried to jump for it. Boing! She jumped, but the pot was far too high.

Moti looked at her saucer. She looked at the pot. Then she curled up in her basket and purred, happy as could be.

"She has her own milk," said Tenali Rama, smiling. "Why would she reach for ours?"

From that day on, everything was just right.

Every night, Moti chased the mice away. Tap, tap, tap went her little paws.

Every morning, she drank her own milk from her own little saucer in the sunny corner.

And the family's milk pot stayed full, hanging high and safe on its hook.

Sharda laughed and patted Moti's soft head. "You clever Tenali," she said. "You did not scold the cat. You just gave her what she wanted."

Tenali Rama smiled. "When someone keeps taking what is not theirs," he said, "first ask what they really need. Often, a little kindness is all it takes."

That night, Moti curled up in her basket and purred herself to sleep.

And Tenali Rama and Sharda slept soundly too, knowing their rice was safe, their milk was safe, and their little cat was happy.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little kindness can solve what scolding never can.
