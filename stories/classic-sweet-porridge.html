Sweet Porridge

A beloved Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once there was a poor little girl named Naina, who lived with her mother in a tiny cottage at the edge of a green forest.

They loved each other more than anything. But they were very poor, and some days there was nothing at all to eat. On those nights, Naina's mother would hold her close and whisper, "One day, my love, our pot will be full again."

One sunny morning, Naina went into the forest to gather sticks. Deep among the tall trees, she met a kind old woman with silver hair and a smile as warm as fresh bread.

"Why do you look so hungry, little one?" asked the old woman.

Naina told her everything. The old woman nodded, reached into her basket, and gave Naina a small round pot.

"It is a magic pot," she said. "When you are hungry, just say, 'Cook, little pot!' And when you have had enough, say, 'Stop, little pot!' But remember the stopping words, little one. They are the most important of all."

If you had a magic pot, what would YOU cook first?

"Thank you, thank you!" cried Naina, and she ran all the way home.

"Cook, little pot!" said Naina. And — bubble, bubble, bubble! — the little pot began to cook sweet porridge, creamy and golden, smelling of honey and warm milk.

Naina and her mother ate till their tummies were round. "Mmm," sighed Naina's mother, "this is the sweetest porridge in the whole wide world!"

From that day on, they were never hungry again. Whenever their tummies rumbled, Naina said the magic words, and the little pot bubbled away.

One morning, Naina went to the forest to gather sticks, and her mother stayed home. Soon her mother's tummy began to rumble. "Cook, little pot!" she said.

Bubble, bubble, bubble! The pot cooked porridge. The mother ate a big bowl. "Mmm, delicious!" Then she ate another bowl. And then another.

But oh no — she had forgotten the stopping words!

The porridge kept bubbling. It rose higher and higher — plop! — right over the edge of the pot. It flowed across the table — glug! — and onto the floor. It poured out of the door — splash! — and down the lane. It filled the whole village!

Porridge in the street! Porridge on the steps! Porridge up to the windows!

"Help!" cried the mother. "Somebody stop the pot!"

Just then, Naina came running home. She saw the rivers of porridge and clapped her hands to her cheeks. "Oh no!" she laughed. Then she took a deep breath and cried, "Stop, little pot!"

And the pot stopped — at once. Not one more bubble.

The whole village came out of their houses. And when they saw the rivers of sweet porridge, they laughed and laughed. Everyone brought their bowls and spoons, and they all sat together in the warm sunshine and ate sweet porridge till the sun went down.

And from that day on, Naina's mother never, ever forgot the stopping words.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness is the sweetest magic — and little words matter.
