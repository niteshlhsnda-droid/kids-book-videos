# The Deer King

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green forest full of tall trees and soft grass lived a herd of deer.

Their king was a beautiful deer with a golden coat and gentle eyes. His name was Nyan, and all the deer loved him.

Every morning, Nyan led his herd to the sweetest meadows to graze, and every evening he counted them all to make sure everyone was safe.

But one day, the king of the country came hunting in the forest, and he made a new rule: "Every day, one deer must come to my palace, or my hunters will chase the whole herd!"

The deer were frightened. Who would go first?

"I will go," said Nyan calmly, "when it is my turn. A king must look after his herd."

Day after day, one deer at a time walked to the palace. Nyan made sure the oldest and weakest never had to go alone — he walked beside them and comforted them.

Then came a sad day. It was the turn of a gentle mother deer, and her little fawn was still very small.

"Please," whispered the mother deer, "my baby still needs me. Who will feed him if I go?"

Nyan stepped forward at once. "I will go in your place," he said. "Stay with your little one. I will take your turn."

So Nyan, the golden deer king, walked proudly to the palace and stood before the human king.

The human king was amazed. "You are the deer king!" he said. "Why have you come instead of the mother deer?"

"Because," said Nyan gently, "a king's job is to protect his people — even if it means giving his own turn. Her little fawn needs her more than I need my safety."

The human king's heart melted. He had never seen such kindness.

"From this day on," he announced, "no deer in this forest shall ever be hunted again! This forest is a safe home for all deer, forever!"

The mother deer nuzzled her little fawn. The whole herd danced with joy.

And Nyan, the golden deer king, led his happy herd back to their green forest, where they lived safely ever after.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A true king puts his people first — kindness is stronger than any crown.*
