# The Three Spinners

*A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a little village with red roofs and a winding lane, there lived a girl named Mia and her mother.

Mia's mother was the best spinner in the village. All day long, her spinning wheel went whirr, whirr, whirr, turning soft flax into fine golden thread.

Mia tried to help, but spinning was hard. Her thread came out lumpy and bumpy, and it kept snapping.

"Do not worry, my love," said her mother, patting her hand. "Little by little, your hands will learn."

One morning, the kind Queen came riding through the village. She stopped to watch Mia's mother spin.

"What beautiful thread!" said the Queen. "Who spins so well?"

"My mother does," said Mia proudly.

The Queen smiled at Mia. "And can you spin too, little one?"

Mia blushed. "Only a little," she said. "My thread is still learning."

The Queen laughed kindly. "Then here is a happy task for you. Spin me three big bundles of flax, and I will invite you to a feast at my castle, with cakes and songs and dancing!"

Mia's eyes went wide. A feast! But three big bundles — that was so much spinning! Her hands felt tired just thinking about it.

That evening, as Mia sat by the window wondering how she would ever finish, there came a gentle knock, knock, knock at the door.

Outside stood three old women, smiling warmly. One had a very big foot, one had a very big thumb, and one had a very big lip — and they all looked as kind as grandmothers.

"We heard about your spinning," said the first. "May we help you?"

"Oh, please do!" said Mia.

So the three old women sat down at the spinning wheels, and whirr, whirr, whirr went the wheels, faster than Mia had ever seen. The first woman treadled with her big foot. The second woman drew the thread with her big thumb. The third woman smoothed it with her big lip. And before Mia could believe it, all three bundles were spun into the finest golden thread in the world.

"Thank you, thank you!" cried Mia. "But how can I ever repay you?"

"Just one thing," said the three women together, smiling. "Invite us to your feast, and let us dance and laugh with you."

"Of course!" said Mia. "You are my dearest guests!"

On the day of the feast, the Queen saw the beautiful thread and clapped her hands. "Wonderful spinning, Mia!" she said.

Then the three old women arrived, and the music began. They danced and twirled and laughed, and Mia danced with them, round and round, until everyone was giggling.

And from that day on, Mia kept practising her spinning, little by little, and she never forgot the three kind women who had helped her — or the feast where everyone danced together.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kind help deserves a kind thank-you — and a dance together.*
