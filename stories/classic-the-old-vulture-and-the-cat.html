The Old Vulture and the Cat

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On the tallest branch of the oldest peepal tree lived Vera, a kind old vulture.

Her wings were too tired for long flights now, so she stayed home and did what she loved best — telling stories to the little birds in the nests below.

Every evening, as the sun turned gold, the baby sparrows and mynahs would chirp, "Vera Dadi! Vera Dadi! One more story!"

And Vera would smile her wrinkled smile and begin.

One bright morning, a sleek cat named Cleo padded to the foot of the peepal tree. She had heard about the plump little birds in the nests above.

"Little birds! Oh, little birds!" Cleo called up in her honey-sweet voice. "Your songs are the sweetest songs in the whole forest! Come down and sing just for me — I have brought honey-cakes to share!"

The baby birds peeked over the edge of their nests. Honey-cakes! Their beaks watered.

Vera opened one wise old eye. She knew that voice. She knew those smooth, smooth words.

"Little ones," she said softly, "sweet words can hide sharp plans. A friend who truly loves your song is happy to hear it from right where you are."

"Sing from here, kind cat!" chirped the bravest sparrow. "We will sing, and you can listen!"

Cleo's smile stretched thin. "Oh, but the music sounds so much better down here," she purred. "Just hop down for one little minute."

The smallest bird, Pip, hopped to the very edge of the nest. His tiny heart went thump-thump-thump.

Just then Vera spread her great wide wings — whoosh! — like a soft umbrella over the nests. "Time for our evening song, little ones," she said. "All together now."

And she sang in her deep, warm, creaky voice, and every little bird sang along, safe and snug under her wings.

Cleo waited and waited. But no bird came down. At last she flicked her tail and slunk away through the tall grass, her honey-cakes uneaten.

That night, Pip snuggled close to Vera's warm feathers. "Vera Dadi," he whispered, "how did you know?"

"Sweet talk tickles the ears, little one," Vera said, tucking him in, "but love keeps you safe. Now sleep."

And under the silver moon, with Vera's wing like a blanket over them all, every little bird drifted into the softest of dreams.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Sweet talk can hide a trick — trust the kind voices that keep you safe.
