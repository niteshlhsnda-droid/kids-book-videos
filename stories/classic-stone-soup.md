# Stone Soup

*A beloved world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once, a hungry traveller came walking to a little village. He had walked all day, and his tummy was rumbling.

"Perhaps the kind people of this village will share a little food with me," he thought.

But in the village square, every door was shut tight. Not a single pot bubbled on a fire. The village was very quiet.

The traveller smiled. He knew what to do.

He set his big pot in the square, built a small fire under it, and poured in clear water. Then, from his pocket, he took a smooth, round stone and dropped it in with a gentle plop.

"I am making stone soup!" he announced in a friendly voice. "It is the finest soup in all the world. It only needs a little carrot, a little potato, and a pinch of salt."

A window creaked open, and a little girl peeked out. "Stone soup?" she whispered. "Can a stone make soup?"

"Oh yes!" said the traveller, stirring the pot. "But a carrot would make it even better."

The little girl ran inside and came back with a bright orange carrot. "Here!" she said, dropping it in. Plop!

The traveller tasted the soup with his wooden spoon and smacked his lips. "Mmm! Lovely! But you know, a potato would make it truly wonderful."

A door opened, and an old man shuffled out with a potato. "Well," he said, "I suppose one potato will not be missed." Plop!

"Mmm!" said the traveller. "Now it needs just a pinch of salt."

A mother came out with a little bag of salt. "Here you are," she said. "A pinch for your soup." She sprinkled it in, and the steam rose up, smelling warm and delicious.

Word spread through the village. One by one, doors opened. A farmer brought onions. A grandmother brought beans. A little boy brought peas. A baker brought crusty bread. Someone else brought fresh green herbs.

Each person added a little something, and the traveller stirred, and the soup bubbled and smelled more and more wonderful.

At last, the pot was full of rich, golden soup, thick with carrots, potatoes, beans and peas.

"Soup is ready!" cried the traveller.

The villagers brought out bowls and spoons and a long table under the big oak tree. Everyone sat together, and the traveller served them all, one by one.

They ate and ate, and they laughed and talked under the stars. The soup was the best anyone had ever tasted — not because of the stone, but because everyone had shared a little something to make it.

When the last bowl was empty, the traveller patted his tummy and smiled. "Thank you, friends," he said. "You have shown me that the finest soup is made together."

He picked up his smooth stone, washed it clean, and put it in his pocket. "I will keep it," he said, "to remind me of this village."

Then the villagers waved goodbye, and the traveller walked on, humming. Behind him, the village windows glowed warm and golden. And that night, everyone slept soundly, with full tummies and happy hearts, dreaming of the soup they had made together.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing makes a feast.*
