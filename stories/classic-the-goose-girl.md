# The Goose Girl

*A gentle Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a kingdom far away, lived a princess named Ana.

Ana had a kind smile, a golden crown, and a beautiful white horse named Falada.

One day, Ana had to travel to a faraway kingdom. Her maid, Leela, went with her.

On the way, they stopped by a stream to rest. Leela looked at Ana's golden crown. She looked at Ana's fine dress. And a greedy thought crept into her heart.

"Give me your crown and your dress," said Leela, "or I will leave you here all alone!"

Ana was frightened. She gave Leela her crown and her dress, and put on Leela's plain clothes instead.

From then on, Leela rode the white horse like a princess, and poor Ana walked behind, like a maid.

When they reached the faraway kingdom, Leela said to the king, "I am Princess Ana!"

And Ana was sent to the fields to look after the king's geese.

So every morning, Ana walked to the meadow with the geese — waddle, waddle, waddle.

But Ana did not cry. She sang.

She sang about the sun, and the stream, and the soft green grass. Her voice was so sweet that the geese stopped to listen, and the flowers seemed to lean closer.

One evening, the old king was walking in his garden when he heard the song.

"Who sings so beautifully?" he wondered.

He followed the song to the meadow — and there was Ana, in plain clothes, singing to the geese, with a golden light in her eyes that no plain dress could hide.

"Little goose girl," said the king kindly, "your song sounds like a princess's song. Tell me truly — who are you?"

Ana took a deep breath and told him everything: the stream, the crown, the dress, and Leela's greedy trick.

The king listened, and his face grew serious. Then he called for Leela.

Leela came, wearing the golden crown. But when she saw Ana, her face turned red, and her eyes filled with tears.

"I am sorry," whispered Leela. "I wanted to be a princess. But a crown does not make a princess. A kind heart does — and Ana has the kind heart."

The king nodded. "You spoke the truth at last," he said. "Go home to your village, Leela, and be kind there. That is your fresh start."

Leela went home, wiser and kinder.

And Ana? Ana put on her golden crown again — and she asked the king for one special thing: that her geese could live in the palace garden forever.

So every morning, the geese waddled through the palace garden — waddle, waddle, waddle — and Ana sang to them, just as before.

Because a true princess sings the same song, crown or no crown.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A crown does not make a princess — a kind heart does.*
