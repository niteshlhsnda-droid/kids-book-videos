# Nasruddin and the Ring

*A gentle Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a little town, beside a busy market square, lived a kind and funny man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin wore a big turban, a long coat, and a smile that made everyone smile too.

One sunny morning, Nasruddin walked through the market to buy bread. The market was full of colours and smells — red tomatoes, yellow bananas, fresh bread, and sweet-smelling flowers.

As Nasruddin walked past the flower stall — glint! — something shiny caught his eye.

He looked down. There, on the dusty ground, lay a beautiful gold ring!

It was round and bright, and inside it was carved a tiny, tiny star.

"Oh my!" said Nasruddin. "Someone has lost their ring. They must be very sad."

Nasruddin picked up the ring and held it high. "Has anyone lost a ring?" he called out. "A gold ring with something special inside it?"

A crowd gathered around him.

"I lost a ring!" cried a tall man with a loud voice. "It is mine! Give it to me!"

Nasruddin looked at the man. "Tell me, friend — what is carved inside your ring?"

The tall man scratched his head. "Um… a… a lion?" he guessed. "A big roaring lion?"

Nasruddin shook his head. "No. This ring does not have a lion inside it."

"I lost a ring!" cried a rich lady with jingling bangles. "It is mine! Give it to me!"

Nasruddin smiled. "Tell me, friend — what is carved inside your ring?"

The rich lady waved her hand. "A… a ruby? A big red ruby?"

Nasruddin shook his head. "No. This ring does not have a ruby inside it."

Then, from the edge of the crowd, came a small voice. "Excuse me… I think… I think it might be mine."

It was a little boy named Arif. His eyes were red from crying. "My grandmother gave me that ring," he said softly. "I was buying flowers for her, and it slipped off my finger."

Nasruddin knelt down beside Arif. "Tell me, little one — what is carved inside your ring?"

Arif thought hard. "A… a tiny star," he said. "Grandmother said the star means: someone is always watching over you."

Nasruddin's smile grew wide. "That is exactly right!" he said, and he placed the ring gently in Arif's hand.

Arif's face lit up like the sun. "Thank you! Thank you!" he cried, and he ran home to show his grandmother.

The tall man and the rich lady shuffled their feet. "Nasruddin," they said, "we are sorry. The ring was not ours. We only wanted a gold ring."

Nasruddin nodded. "Wanting is easy," he said. "But an honest word is worth more than all the gold in the market. Today, a little boy told the truth — and the truth brought his ring home."

That evening, Nasruddin walked home with his bread, humming a happy tune. And in the market square, the flower-seller smiled and said to everyone, "If you lose something in this market, bring it to Nasruddin. He always finds the right owner."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Honesty is worth more than gold — the truth always finds its way home.*
