Mira the Mouse and the Moonlight Picnic

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Mira was a tiny mouse who loved the moon more than anything in the world.

Every full-moon night, she did the same happy thing. She packed her picnic basket: crumbs of cornbread, a thimble of warm milk, and three blackberries. Then she climbed the old oak stump, spread her leaf-cloth, and had a picnic in the moonlight.

"Dear moon," she would whisper, "thank you for coming to dinner with me."

Tonight the moon was big and round and creamy, like a pancake in the sky. Mira climbed the stump and set out her basket. In the moonlight, the three blackberries shone like tiny purple jewels. She was just about to take a crumb of cornbread when she heard a small *rustle-rustle-rustle* in the grass.

Out popped Prickles the hedgehog, shy and round, with leaves stuck to his spines.

"Oh!" said Mira. "Hello, Prickles."

"Hello," said Prickles softly. "I smelled cornbread. May I… watch the moon with you?"

"Of course," said Mira. "There is always room at a moonlight picnic."

So Prickles climbed up and sat very still, looking at the moon with shining eyes.

Just then, a soft *whoosh-whoosh* fluttered down through the branches. It was Hooti, a baby owl with fluffy feathers and very big eyes.

"Oh!" said Hooti in a tiny voice. "I… I got lost. The dark is so dark tonight."

Mira patted the leaf-cloth beside her. "Come and sit, Hooti. The dark is not empty, you know. Look — it is full of us."

Hooti blinked and sat down. Prickles smiled at her, and the moon poured silver light over her soft feathers. Hooti smiled a little.

Then came a slow *thump-thump-yawn* up the side of the stump. Nini the dormouse pulled herself over the top, wobbled twice, and curled into a ball.

"I want to stay awake," Nini murmured, her eyes already half-closed. "Tell me a moon story."

Mira poured her thimble of warm milk into four tiny cups made of acorn shells. Then she shared the cornbread crumbs, and gave one blackberry to Prickles, one to Hooti, and one to Nini.

And look — there was exactly enough. Crumbs and milk and berries for everyone.

"That is the magic of a picnic," said Mira. "When you share, small things become big."

They told moon stories while they nibbled. Prickles told a story about a moon made of cheese that mice only dreamed about. Hooti's story was about an owl who carried the moon home in her pocket to keep her grandmother warm. Nini started one about a sleepy star, but she yawned in the middle and fell asleep with a smile.

"Shhh," giggled Hooti. "Nini is dreaming already."

Hooti looked up at the dark sky. It did not feel so dark anymore. The stars were twinkling like tiny night-lights. Her new friends were warm beside her. The moon was watching over them all, round and kind.

"You were right, Mira," Hooti whispered. "The dark is full of friends."

One by one, their eyelids grew heavy. Prickles curled his spines into a soft ball. Hooti tucked her beak into her fluffy chest. Mira snuggled close to Nini, who was already snoring the tiniest snore: *peep… peep… peep.*

The old oak stump was warm from the day's sunshine. The moonlight wrapped around them like a silver blanket. The basket sat empty beside them, because the best picnics end with empty baskets and full hearts.

"Goodnight, moon," whispered Mira. "Thank you for our picnic."

And the moon shone a little brighter, as if it were smiling back.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Sharing makes small things big — and friends make the dark feel bright.
