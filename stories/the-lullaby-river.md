# The Lullaby River

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Bobby the beaver had too many thoughts. They swirled in her head like leaves in the wind — what if it rains tomorrow? What if she forgot something? Her whole body felt jittery and buzzy.

Her grandfather took her paw. "Come," he murmured. "Let us visit the Lullaby River by Bluebell Pond."

They walked through the moonlit woods of Willowmere Wood, and soon they heard it — a gentle river flowing between the trees, humming as it moved. La-loo, la-loo, la-loo.

"This river carries worries away," Grandfather said, picking up a broad green leaf. "Put a worry on this leaf. The river will take it."

Bobby thought of the rain worry. She placed it on the leaf and set the leaf on the water. It floated away, spinning slowly, while the river hummed its song. "La-loo, la-loo, drift away," sang Grandfather.

Bobby felt the jittery buzz soften a little. She picked up another leaf and laid the forgotten-something worry on it. The leaf bobbed away downstream, and the moon shone on the ripples. "La-loo, la-loo, drift away," sang Grandfather again.

She picked up a third leaf. This time she could not think of a worry at all — her head felt quiet, and the buzzy thoughts were gone. So she placed the empty leaf on the water just for fun. "La-loo, la-loo, drift away," sang Grandfather one last time.

They sat together on the soft riverbank while the water hummed its lullaby and fireflies danced above the ripples. Bobby leaned against her grandfather, calm and empty in the best possible way.

Her eyelids grew heavy. The river's song was so gentle — la-loo, la-loo — and Bobby's head began to nod. Grandfather carried her home wrapped in his warm coat and tucked her into her bark bed. The river's hum seemed to follow them home, singing through the open window all night long.

Sleep now, little Bobby.

The end. 🌙
