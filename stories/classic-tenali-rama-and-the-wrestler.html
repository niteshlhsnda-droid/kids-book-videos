Tenali Rama and the Wrestler

A clever Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago in the kingdom of Vijayanagara, there lived a wise king named Krishna Deva Raya.

In the king's court sat his cleverest minister, Tenali Rama. Tenali Rama was funny and kind, with twinkling eyes and a quick wit. He could solve any problem — not with fighting, but with thinking.

One sunny morning, the court doors flew open — *bang!* — and in marched a huge, boastful wrestler.

His name was Bheem, and oh, was he big! His arms were like tree trunks. His chest was like a barrel. When he walked, the floor went *thump, thump, thump.*

Bheem flexed his giant muscles. *Rrrrip!* went his shirt sleeve. "I am the strongest man in all the land!" he bellowed. "Is there anyone here brave enough to fight me?"

The courtiers hid behind their cushions. *Eep!*

The king stroked his beard. "Hmm," he said. "We do not want a fight in my court."

But Tenali Rama stood up, smiling his warm smile. "No fighting needed, Your Majesty," he said. "I propose a different contest — a gentle one. Whoever can make the palace kitten purr first, wins!"

Just then, the palace kitten tiptoed in. Her name was Mimi. She was tiny and soft, with round green eyes and a tail that went *swish, swish.*

"A kitten?" roared Bheem. "Ha! This is too easy!"

Bheem stomped over to Mimi, grabbed her up in his giant hands, and squeezed her tight. "Purr, little cat! Purr!"

But Mimi did not purr. *Hisssss!* went Mimi. She wriggled free, darted across the marble floor — *skitter, skitter* — and hid under the king's throne.

"Come back here!" bellowed Bheem, chasing her around a pillar. *Thump, thump, thump.*

The courtiers giggled. *Hee hee.*

"My turn," said Tenali Rama calmly.

He walked slowly over to the throne and sat down on the cool marble steps. He did not grab. He did not shout. He simply held out one soft hand and waited.

*Patience... patience...*

Mimi peeked out. She sniffed Tenali Rama's hand. Then she tiptoed closer — *pitter, patter* — and climbed right into his lap.

Tenali Rama began to stroke her softly, from her ears to her tail. *Strokey, strokey, gentle and slow.* And he hummed a tiny little tune. *Hmm-hmm-hmm.*

And then... *purrrr... purrrr...* Mimi began to purr! *Purrrr-purrrr,* soft as a little motor, happy as honey.

"Purrrr!" laughed the king, clapping his hands. "Tenali Rama wins!"

Bheem's big shoulders drooped. He shuffled his giant feet. *Scuff, scuff.* "I am the strongest man in the land," he mumbled, "but I could not make one tiny kitten purr."

Tenali Rama smiled kindly. "Dear Bheem," he said, "strength is not only in muscles. Gentleness is a strength too. Mimi did not run from your arms — she ran from your squeezing. A soft touch wins hearts that force cannot."

Bheem thought about this. Then his round face broke into a grin. "You are right, clever one!" he boomed, much more softly this time. "Teach me to be gentle!"

So that very evening, Tenali Rama taught the big wrestler how to stroke a kitten. *Strokey, strokey, gentle and slow.*

And — *purrrr!* — Mimi purred in Bheem's great big hand too.

The king was so delighted that he ordered warm milk for everyone — for Tenali Rama, for Bheem, and a little golden saucer for Mimi. *Lap, lap, lap,* went Mimi, drinking her milk.

Bheem and Tenali Rama drank their warm milk together and became the best of friends.

That night, Mimi curled up in a soft round ball on a velvet cushion, purring herself to sleep. *Purrrr... purrrr... purrrr...*

And the whole palace drifted off to sleep — the king, the courtiers, the clever minister, and the strong, gentle wrestler.

*Purrrr... purrrr...* Goodnight, Mimi.

Goodnight, sleepy you.
