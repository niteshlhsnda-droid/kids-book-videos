# Lily the Lamb and the Lullaby Meadow

*A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High on a green hill, in a little stone barn, lived a small white lamb named Lily.

Lily loved the daytime. She loved chasing butterflies, nibbling sweet clover, and tumbling in the soft grass with her friends.

But when the sun went down and the stars came out, Lily could not fall asleep. She tossed. She turned. She counted the stars. She counted the fence posts. Still, her eyes stayed wide open.

She even tried counting sheep jumping over a fence, but the sheep jumped too fast, and counting them only woke her up more.

"Mama," she whispered one evening, "my eyes will not close. My head is full of buzzing bees."

Mama Sheep smiled and nuzzled Lily's woolly white head. "Then come, little one," she said. "Let us take a slow walk through the meadow. The meadow knows a secret about sleep."

Out they went, into the purple dusk. The air was cool and sweet, and the meadow was quiet — except it was not quiet at all.

"Listen," said Mama Sheep. "The breeze is humming."

Sure enough, the evening breeze moved through the tall grass — shhh, shhh — like a soft song with no words.

"The breeze hums a lullaby," said Mama Sheep. "Shhh, shhh."

Lily listened, and her ears drooped a little.

Then the grass began to sway — sway, sway — back and forth, back and forth, like a cradle rocking in the dark.

"The grass rocks a lullaby," said Mama Sheep. "Sway, sway."

Lily swayed too, and her eyes drooped a little.

Then the crickets began to sing — chirp, chirp, chirp — a tiny song in the dark, soft and steady, soft and steady.

"The crickets sing a lullaby," said Mama Sheep. "Chirp, chirp, chirp."

Lily hummed along, and her legs felt heavy, like warm wool.

A firefly drifted past, blinking its tiny lamp — blink, blink — like a small star that had come down to say good night.

Then, one by one, the flowers closed their petals. The daisies folded up. The buttercups tucked themselves in. The violets yawned a tiny purple yawn.

"Even the flowers go to sleep," whispered Mama Sheep. "Close, close, close."

Lily yawned a big lamb yawn. "Mama," she said, "I think I have gathered a lullaby."

"Yes," said Mama Sheep. "The breeze hums it, the grass rocks it, the crickets sing it, and the flowers dream it. Now let us carry it home."

So they walked home, slow and sleepy, under the round silver moon. The moon followed them all the way, watching over the meadow like a kind night-light. Mama Sheep walked right beside her, humming the breeze's song.

Lily sang her lullaby, low and sleepy: "Shhh, shhh. Sway, sway. Chirp, chirp, chirp."

Back in the barn, Mama Sheep tucked Lily into her warm hay bed and kissed her woolly white head.

"Good night, little lamb," she whispered.

"Good night, Mama," murmured Lily — and before Mama Sheep had even reached the door, Lily was fast asleep, dreaming of the lullaby meadow.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When sleep will not come, listen for the soft sounds around you — they will sing you home.*
