# The Dog Who Went Abroad

*A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a small green village, there lived a happy brown dog named Bingo.

Bingo had a soft bed of straw by the farmer's door. Every morning, the farmer's wife gave him warm milk and a crust of bread. Every evening, the village children scratched his ears and played tug-of-war with an old rope.

But one day, Bingo sighed a big, heavy sigh.

"Every day is the same," he said to himself. "The same milk. The same bread. The same old rope. Surely life is better somewhere else!"

So the very next morning, Bingo packed nothing at all — dogs do not pack suitcases — and trotted off down the road to see the world.

First, he came to a busy town. The streets were full of carts and shouting people.

"Hello!" barked Bingo to a big grey dog by the market. "I have come to live here!"

But the big grey dog bared his teeth. "Grrr! This is MY street! Go away!"

Bingo ran off as fast as his legs could carry him, his tail tucked tight between his legs.

Next, he came to a grand city. The houses were tall, the roads were wide, and the smells were wonderful — roast chicken! Fresh bread! Fish!

Bingo sniffed happily. But when he peeked into a shop doorway, a broom came whizzing out.

"Shoo! Shoo!" cried the shopkeeper. "No dogs here!"

Bingo trotted on, his tummy rumbling. He found a bone in the gutter, but it was old and dry, and no one smiled at him. Not once. Not all day.

At last, Bingo came to the seaside. The waves went shhh, shhh, and the sand was soft and warm.

"This is lovely!" said Bingo. He dug a hole. He chased a crab. He found a big fish bone.

But when the sun went down, the beach grew cold and empty. There was no warm straw bed. There was no kind hand to scratch his ears. There was no one to say, "Good boy, Bingo."

Bingo curled up all alone under a fishing boat, and a cold little wind blew over his nose.

"I have seen the town, and the city, and the sea," he whispered. "And nowhere is there a warm bed, or warm milk, or friends who love me. I was a silly dog to leave home."

So the very next morning, Bingo ran all the way home — over the hill, past the river, and down the lane to the little green village.

The farmer's wife was at the door. "Bingo!" she cried. "You are home!"

The children ran out, laughing. "Bingo is back! Bingo is back!"

They scratched his ears. They gave him warm milk and a crust of bread. And Bingo curled up on his soft straw bed, happier than he had ever been.

"Home is not the same milk and the same bread," said Bingo, with a sleepy smile. "Home is where somebody loves you."

And he slept the deepest, cosiest sleep of his whole life.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The finest place in all the world is home — where somebody loves you.*
