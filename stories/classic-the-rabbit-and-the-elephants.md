# The Rabbit and the Elephants

*A wise Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

At the edge of a green meadow lived a family of soft, round rabbits.

They had dug the coziest burrows under the old banyan tree, with round doors and soft leaf beds.

One hot summer, a great herd of elephants came marching through the meadow to drink at the lake.

Stomp, stomp, stomp went their huge feet — and CRUNCH! Many rabbit burrows were squashed flat.

"Oh no!" cried the little rabbits. "Our homes are broken! What shall we do?"

The oldest rabbit, Grandpa Longears, stroked his long white whiskers and thought very hard.

"Elephants are big and strong," he said, "but we are small and clever. Follow me — and be very brave."

That night, when the round moon rose over the lake, Grandpa Longears hopped up to the great Elephant King.

"O mighty Elephant King," he said in his tiniest, politest voice, "the Moon has sent me with a message. She is angry that your herd has trampled our homes."

The Elephant King frowned. "The Moon? Angry with ME?"

"See for yourself," said Grandpa Longears, and he led the Elephant King to the still, silver lake.

The Elephant King lowered his great trunk to the water — and the Moon's face trembled and shook on the ripples!

"Oh!" gasped the Elephant King. "She IS trembling with anger!"

"She says," whispered Grandpa Longears, "that if your herd drinks from another lake, she will smile again."

The Elephant King bowed his great head low. "Tell the Moon I am sorry," he said. "We will never trouble the rabbits again."

And he led his herd away to a faraway lake, where they splashed and trumpeted happily ever after.

The little rabbits peeked out of their burrows and cheered, "Hooray for Grandpa Longears!"

The little rabbits rebuilt their burrows, and every full-moon night they danced in the silver moonlight, thanking the Moon for her help.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Clever kindness can solve what strength cannot — and even the mighty should listen to the small.*
