Birbal's Khichdi

An Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago in India, a kind emperor named Akbar ruled a great palace. His dear friend Birbal could solve the trickiest riddle with a smile.

In a little village near the palace lived a poor woodcutter named Mohan. He had a warm heart and a small family he loved more than all the gold in the world — his wife Meera and their little daughter Leela.

One chilly evening, Ravi — a proud courtier in the king's service — stopped by Mohan's hut. Ravi was rich, and he loved to boast.

"Ha!" said Ravi, puffing out his chest. "No man on earth could stand in the cold river all night!"

Mohan looked up from his chopping. "For my family," he said softly, "I could."

"Then do it!" Ravi declared. "Stand in the river from sunset to sunrise, and I will give you a whole bag of gold!"

Mohan's heart beat fast. A bag of gold would buy warm blankets for Meera and Leela for the whole winter. So he nodded.

That night, the stars came out like tiny silver bells. Mohan stepped into the river, and the water was icy cold.

So he stood there, thinking warm thoughts all night long — Meera's sweet smile when she served dinner, little Leela clapping her hands when he came home. Then he hummed the lullaby he sang to Leela every night, soft and low, so the moon could listen.

By sunrise his teeth were chattering — but he had stood the whole night through.

"You cannot have the gold," said Ravi quickly. "The palace lamp was shining across the river all night. It kept you warm! That is cheating."

Mohan's shoulders drooped. "But the lamp is so far away..."

"Nonsense!" said Ravi, and he marched off without paying.

So Mohan went to the palace, bowed low before Emperor Akbar, and told his story.

Akbar turned to Birbal. "My friend," he said, "you judge this fairly."

Birbal smiled. "Bring me a pot of rice and lentils — the makings of khichdi," he said.

The next morning, in the middle of the palace courtyard, everyone saw a funny sight: a pot of rice and lentils on the cold ground, and far, far above it — tied to the top of a flagpole — a little burning lamp.

"Cook!" Birbal said to the pot, in his gentlest voice.

What do you think happened next? Nothing! Not one bubble, not one bit of steam. The little lamp swayed in the breeze, far too high to warm even a pebble — while Birbal nodded at it very seriously.

The whole court burst out laughing.

But Birbal raised his hand, and his eyes twinkled. "If the palace lamp across the river could warm Mohan through a cold winter night," he said, "then surely this little lamp, high above the pot, can cook this khichdi."

The courtyard went very quiet.

Then Akbar smiled, and his whole face lit up. Even Ravi laughed — at himself! "Birbal, you have caught me," he said. "I was wrong, and I am sorry, Mohan."

Ravi gave Mohan the bag of gold, just as he had promised.

That evening, Mohan bought the warmest blankets for Meera and Leela, and a paper bag of sweet jalebis for everyone.

And as they curled up under the new blankets, warm and snug, Mohan hummed Leela's lullaby once more — and the whole little family drifted off to sleep, smiling.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Be kind, and judge other people's struggles fairly.
