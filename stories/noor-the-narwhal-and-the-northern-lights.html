Noor the Narwhal and the Northern Lights

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Far, far away, in the cold blue sea near the top of the world, there lived a little narwhal named Noor.

Noor had smooth grey skin, a bright little smile, and a long, twisty tusk that sparkled like a unicorn's horn.

Every night, Noor loved to swim to the top of the sea and watch the Northern Lights dance across the sky. Green ribbons, pink ribbons, purple ribbons — they twirled and swirled above the water, like a giant sky-dance.

"Oh, how beautiful!" Noor would say, floating on her back. "One day, I will swim right up to the lights and dance with them!"

One frosty night, as Noor swam beneath the lights, she heard a tiny voice calling.

"Help! Help! I am lost!"

It was Lulu, a little white beluga whale, no bigger than a boat. She was swimming in circles, round and round, looking very scared.

"What is wrong, Lulu?" asked Noor, swimming close.

"I was following my mother," sniffled Lulu, "but the sea is so big and dark, and I cannot find my way home."

"Do not worry," said Noor gently. "I will help you find your mother. I know just the way."

"But which way is home?" asked Lulu, wiping her eyes with her little fin.

Noor looked up at the sky. The Northern Lights were dancing — green and pink and purple — and their colours shimmered on the water like a shining path.

"See the lights?" said Noor. "They are dancing over your home. All we have to do is follow their colours on the water."

So Noor and Lulu swam together, side by side, following the ribbons of light. Noor sang a soft, sweet song to keep Lulu brave. "Swim, swim, little friend, the lights will show the way."

They swam past the sleepy seals. They swam past the drifting jellyfish, glowing like little lanterns. They swam past a school of silver fish, who twinkled like stars.

And then — oh! — in the distance, they heard a happy, familiar voice.

"Lulu! Lulu, where are you?"

"It is my mother!" cried Lulu, and she swam as fast as her little fins could carry her.

Lulu's mother wrapped her in a big, warm fin-hug. "Thank you, Noor," she said, smiling. "You brought my little one home."

"You are welcome," said Noor, and her heart felt warm all over, like a cup of hot cocoa.

That night, the Northern Lights danced brighter than ever — green and pink and purple — as if they were dancing just for Noor.

Noor floated on her back and watched them twirl. "Thank you, lights," she whispered. "You showed us the way home."

And as the lights swirled softly above her, Noor closed her eyes, smiled her bright little smile, and drifted off to sleep, dreaming of ribbons of light.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When someone is lost, a kind friend and a little light can show the way home.
