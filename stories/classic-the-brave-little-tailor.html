The Brave Little Tailor

A Brothers Grimm fairy tale, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a cozy village, in a cottage with blue shutters, lived a young tailor named Neel.

Neel was small and quick-fingered, and he could stitch a button faster than you could blink. One warm afternoon, he sat by his window eating bread piled thick with jam — when seven buzzing flies landed right on his jam!

"How rude!" cried Neel. He grabbed a cloth and — SWAT! — caught all seven flies at one blow.

Neel looked at the seven little bumps under the cloth, and his chest puffed up with pride.

"Seven at one blow!" he declared. "Why, the whole world should know about this!"

So he stitched the words SEVEN AT ONE BLOW in big golden letters on his belt, packed a small bundle, slipped a tiny pebble and a little pet bird into his pocket, and set off to seek his fortune.

On the road, he met a giant — a very big fellow named Bholu, with hands like dinner plates and a voice like distant thunder.

"Hello, little one," rumbled Bholu. "What does your belt say?"

"Seven at one blow!" said Neel proudly.

Bholu's eyebrows shot up. He thought Neel meant seven GIANTS, not seven flies! "Hmm," he said. "If you are so strong, squeeze water from this stone!"

He handed Neel a grey rock. What trick do you think Neel had hiding in his bundle? Neel smiled, slipped the rock into his pocket, and pulled out a soft ball of fresh cheese from his bundle instead. He squeezed it — and drops of watery milk dripped out.

"See?" said Neel. "Water from a stone!"

Bholu stared. Then he picked up a stone and threw it so high it vanished into the clouds. "Beat THAT, little one!"

Neel picked up a tiny pebble… and slipped something else from his pocket. What was it? It was his little pet bird! Up it fluttered, carrying the pebble into the sky — and it did not come back.

"My stone flew to the moon!" said Neel.

Bholu scratched his huge head. "You are stronger than you look, little tailor. Will you be my friend?"

And from that day, the great big giant and the brave little tailor were the best of friends. Bholu carried Neel's bundle when it got heavy, and Neel stitched Bholu's torn trousers with the strongest thread he had.

When they reached the king's city, the king heard of the tailor who had done "seven at one blow." He was a king with a warm, booming laugh, and he laughed with delight at Neel's clever tricks.

"Stay in my kingdom," said the king. "We need clever, kind people more than we need strong ones."

So Neel opened a tailor's shop with a golden sign, and Bholu became a guard who helped lost children find their way home and carried heavy baskets for old grandmothers. And whenever anyone asked about his belt, Neel would wink and say, "Seven at one blow — but I never tell whether they were flies!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever mind and a kind heart are stronger than the biggest muscles.
