# Rosie the Robin and the Raindrop Lullaby

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a garden full of roses and sleepy sunflowers, there lived a little robin named Rosie.

Rosie had a rust-red tummy, soft brown wings, and the sweetest singing voice in the whole garden.

Every morning, Rosie sang a morning song: "Tweet-tweet-tweet! Good morning, world!"

Every evening, Rosie sang an evening song: "Tweet-tweet-tweet! Goodnight, world!"

But one evening, something strange happened. The sky turned grey. The wind began to whisper. And then — pitter, patter, plip, plop — the rain began to fall.

Pitter, patter, plip, plop. Pitter, patter, plip, plop.

The rain fell on the roses. The rain fell on the sunflowers. The rain fell on Rosie's little nest.

"Oh no!" chirped Rosie. "My nest is getting wet! And the baby birds in the garden cannot sleep with all this noise!"

Rosie looked around. The little sparrows were shivering in their nest. The baby bunnies were hiding under the lettuce leaves. Even the ladybirds were huddled under a leaf, saying, "Brrr!"

Rosie took a deep breath. She had an idea.

"If the rain will not stop," she said, "then I will sing the rain a lullaby!"

So Rosie lifted her little head, opened her beak, and sang — soft and slow and sweet:

"Raindrop, raindrop, soft and slow,
Sing a song and let it go.
Pitter, patter, hush-a-bye,
Rain is tired — time to lie."

And something wonderful happened.

The raindrops began to slow down. Pitter… patter… plip… plop…

The rain listened to Rosie's song, and it began to dance — gently, gently — like tiny dancers twirling down from the sky.

Rosie sang on:

"Raindrop, raindrop, on the leaf,
Every drop is soft relief.
Pitter, patter, hush-a-bye,
Rain is tired — time to lie."

And one by one, the raindrops settled onto the leaves. They sparkled like tiny stars. The roses shook their petals dry. The sunflowers lifted their sleepy heads.

Then — peep! — the clouds drifted apart, and the moon peeped out, round and silver.

"Thank you, Rosie," whispered the little sparrows, snuggling into their nest.

"Thank you, Rosie," whispered the baby bunnies, curling up under the lettuce leaves.

And the ladybirds whispered, "Thank you, Rosie," and closed their tiny eyes.

Rosie flew back to her own nest, fluffed up her soft brown feathers, and tucked her head under her wing.

"Goodnight, roses," she whispered. "Goodnight, rain. Goodnight, garden."

And the whole garden — roses and sunflowers, sparrows and bunnies, ladybirds and raindrops — slept soundly, all night long, under the silver moon.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind song can calm any storm — and help everyone sleep.*
