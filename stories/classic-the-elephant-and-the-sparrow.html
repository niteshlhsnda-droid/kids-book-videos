The Elephant and the Sparrow

A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green forest beside a small, sparkling pond lived a tiny sparrow named Chiki.

Chiki had built a soft, round nest in the low branches of a neem tree. Inside the nest lay three little spotty eggs.

Every morning, Chiki sang a sweet song over her eggs. "Soon, soon, my babies will come," she chirped. "And I will teach them to sing too."

One hot afternoon, a big elephant named Ellie came stomping down the forest path. Ellie was not mean. She was just big — very, very big — and she did not always watch where her feet were going.

Thump! Thump! Thump! went her feet.

Then — crunch! One of Ellie's big feet stepped on Chiki's low branch, and the nest tumbled down.

Chiki flew down and gasped. Her nest was broken, and her three little eggs had rolled onto the soft grass, safe and sound.

"Oh no! Oh no!" cried Chiki. "My nest! My poor little eggs!"

Ellie looked down and felt her heart sink. "I am so sorry," she rumbled softly. "I did not see your nest at all."

But Chiki was too upset to listen. She flew off to find her friends.

First she found Pip, the woodpecker, tapping on a hollow log. Tap-tap-tap!

"Pip!" cried Chiki. "Ellie broke my nest! She never looks where she walks!"

Then she found Fifi, the frog, sitting on a lily pad in the pond. "Fifi! Ellie broke my nest!"

And then she found Zuzu, the little fly, humming in the sunshine. "Zuzu! Ellie broke my nest!"

Pip, Fifi, and Zuzu looked at one another. "We must help Ellie learn to walk carefully," said Pip. "But how?"

Zuzu buzzed in a happy circle. "I have an idea! Ellie loves music. Let us lead her with a sweet sound, all the way to the pond, where the ground is soft and open."

So Pip tapped a gentle rhythm on the hollow log. Tap-tap, tap-tap, like a little drum.

Fifi sang a soft, round song from her lily pad. "Ribbit, ribbit," she crooned.

And Zuzu hummed a sweet, floaty tune, leading the way through the trees.

Ellie lifted her big ears. "What a lovely sound!" she said. "I must follow it!"

Step by step, Ellie followed the music. And because she was listening so hard, she walked slowly. She lifted each foot high and set it down softly. She looked left, and she looked right. For the very first time, she noticed the tiny flowers, the little mushrooms, and the ants marching in a line.

At last she reached the pond, where the ground was soft and wide. There stood Chiki, with Pip, Fifi, and Zuzu beside her.

Ellie lowered her great head. "Chiki, I am truly sorry I broke your nest," she said. "I was walking too fast and not looking. I will walk slowly and carefully from now on."

Chiki's heart melted. "Thank you, Ellie," she chirped. "And I am sorry I flew off without listening."

Then Ellie did something wonderful. With her long, gentle trunk, she gathered soft twigs and dry grass, and she helped Chiki build a new nest — this time high up in the neem tree, where big feet could never reach it.

Chiki sang her sweetest song of all. "Thank you, Ellie! Thank you, friends!"

From that day on, Ellie walked slowly through the forest, looking left and right. And whenever she passed the neem tree, Chiki sang her a song.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When we slow down and look carefully, we can keep our small friends safe.
