# The Tortoise and the Geese

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green valley beside a shining lake lived a little tortoise named Tunnu. He had a round, bumpy shell and a big, big wish.

Every morning, Tunnu watched two kind geese, Naina and Tara, swoop down from the sky. Their white wings went whoosh, whoosh through the air.

"Oh, I wish I could fly," said Tunnu with a sigh. "Just once. I want to see the clouds up close and the whole valley down below."

Naina and Tara loved their slow, steady friend very much.

"We can help you fly," said Naina, "if you promise to do one important thing."

"Anything!" said Tunnu. "Anything at all!"

"Hold this stick in your mouth," said Tara, "and do not let go. We will each hold one end in our beaks and carry you up into the sky. But keep your mouth shut tight. Not one word."

"I promise," said Tunnu. "Mouth shut tight. Not one word."

So Tunnu bit the stick, gently but firmly. Naina took the left end in her beak. Tara took the right end in her beak. They flapped their great white wings — whoosh, whoosh, whoosh — and up they went!

Up went Tunnu, higher and higher. Oh, it was wonderful! The lake sparkled below like a big blue mirror. The clouds puffed past like soft white pillows.

Tunnu wanted to shout, "Look at me! I am flying!" But he remembered his promise. Mouth shut tight.

On they flew, over the lake and over a little village with red rooftops. Down below, the children in the village looked up and pointed.

"Look!" cried a boy. "A tortoise is flying!"

"Look!" cried a girl. "A tortoise in the sky!"

Tunnu heard them, and his heart went thump, thump, thump with pride. He wanted to call down, "It is me! I am flying!"

He forgot his promise. He opened his mouth to call out —

And down, down, down he tumbled!

"Oh no!" cried Naina.

"Tunnu!" cried Tara.

But Tunnu was falling fast through the soft, warm air. Down, down, down —

POOF!

He landed with a big, soft POOF in the middle of a tall, golden haystack. Tunnu blinked. He wiggled his feet. He tapped his shell. Nothing hurt at all. Not even a little bit.

"I am all right!" he called up. "I landed in the soft hay!"

Naina and Tara swooped down and landed beside the haystack, their feathers fluttering.

"Are you hurt?" asked Naina.

"Not one bit," said Tunnu, smiling. "The hay caught me like a soft, golden hug."

"I opened my mouth when I should have kept it shut," said Tunnu. "I am sorry. Thank you for carrying me, and thank you, haystack, for catching me."

The three friends walked slowly home together as the sun sank low and gold.

That night, Tunnu curled up in his shell and thought about the clouds, and the lake like a mirror, and the soft golden hay. He had flown, and he had landed safe, and he had learned a very good thing: when kind friends give you good advice, it is wise to listen.

And above him, the stars came out, one by one, twinkling softly over the quiet valley.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Listen to kind advice — it keeps you safe, even when you are excited.*
