# The Ass and His Shadow

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, on a hot, sunny summer day, two travellers set out on a long journey across the dusty hills.

One traveller was named Yash. He was tall, and he carried a big water bottle and a basket of bread.

The other traveller was named Naman. He was short, and he carried a little bag of sweet mangoes and a soft blanket.

The sun was hot, the road was long, and soon their feet felt tired.

"We need a little donkey to carry our bags," said Yash, wiping his brow. "Yes," agreed Naman, "and a donkey will help us walk faster too."

So they went to a little farm where a kind farmer lived with his small grey donkey named Chotu. Chotu was gentle, with soft ears and a sweet smile.

"Please, may we hire your little donkey for the day?" asked Yash. "Of course," said the farmer with a smile. "Chotu is strong and kind. He will carry your bags all the way to the next village."

Yash and Naman were so happy. They put their bags on Chotu's back, and off they went, with Chotu trotting happily in the middle.

At last, the sun climbed high. It was noon, and the day was very, very hot. "Oh, I am so hot!" cried Yash. "Me too!" cried Naman. "I need a rest in the shade!"

Just then, they looked at Chotu. His body made a small patch of cool shade on the ground. "Look!" said Yash. "Chotu's shade! I will rest in it!" "No, I will rest in it!" said Naman.

"I paid more money!" said Yash, stamping his foot. "No, I paid more!" said Naman. The two friends began to quarrel, louder and louder. "Mine!" shouted Yash. "Mine!" shouted Naman.

Poor little Chotu stood in the middle, looking from Yash to Naman and back again. He did not understand why his friends were shouting.

So Chotu did something very clever. He lifted his head, wiggled his soft ears, and let out a long, gentle bray — "Hee-haw! Hee-haw!"

It was such a funny, soft sound that Yash and Naman stopped shouting. They looked at Chotu, and then — they both began to laugh!

"Ha, ha, ha!" laughed Yash. "What silly friends we are, quarrelling over a little patch of shade!" "Ha, ha, ha!" laughed Naman. "Chotu is right to laugh at us!"

"Let us take turns," said Yash, smiling. "I will rest first, and you can rest next." "And I will share my sweet mangoes with you," said Naman. "And I will share my bread and water," said Yash.

So Yash rested in the cool shade first, while Naman fanned him with his hat. Then they swapped.

After their rest, they sat together and shared their lunch — Yash's bread and Naman's mangoes, with cool water to drink.

When they had finished, they felt fresh and strong again. They thanked Chotu, and walked on together to the next village.

And from that day on, they never quarrelled over little things again. They always shared and took turns, because sharing makes every journey happier.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Sharing and taking turns makes every journey happier.*
