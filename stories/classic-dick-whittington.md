# Dick Whittington and His Cat

*A classic English folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little village far from London, there lived a poor boy named Dick Whittington.

Dick had no mummy or daddy and no warm bed. He had only one friend — a small ginger cat named Ginger.

Ginger had soft orange fur and bright green eyes.

"One day," said Dick, "we will go to London. They say the streets there are paved with gold!"

"Meow!" said Ginger, as if to say, "Yes, let's go!"

So one sunny morning, Dick packed a tiny bundle, put on his old hat, and set off for London with Ginger trotting beside him.

They walked and walked until at last they saw the tall towers of London.

But London was not paved with gold. The streets were grey and muddy.

"Do not worry, Ginger," said Dick, bravely. "We will find a kind friend here."

A kind merchant named Mr. Fitzwarren saw the hungry boy and the little ginger cat.

"Come and work for me," said Mr. Fitzwarren, kindly. "You can sweep my shop, and your cat can sleep by the warm fire."

Dick was so happy! He swept the shop every morning.

But at night, the shop was full of squeaky mice, nibbling the sacks and scampering over the floors!

"Do not worry," said Dick. "Ginger will help us."

And Ginger did! She was the cleverest cat in London. Pounce! She caught mouse after mouse.

Soon there was not one mouse left in the shop.

"What a clever cat!" said Mr. Fitzwarren. "Dick, my ship is sailing far away tomorrow. Take Ginger with you. She can catch the mice on the ship, and I will pay you well."

"We are going on a big adventure!" whispered Dick, hugging Ginger tight.

The next morning, Dick and Ginger climbed aboard the tall ship, and the white sails flapped in the wind.

Out on the sea, the ship was full of mice!

But Ginger was ready. Pounce! Pounce! Pounce! She caught every mouse, one by one.

When the ship reached a faraway land, the king was so glad to be rid of the mice that he gave Dick a chest of gold coins.

"Thank you, clever Ginger!" laughed Dick. "Now we can go home!"

Back in London, Dick was rich at last. But he did not forget how it felt to be poor and hungry.

He built a warm house for children with no mummy or daddy, and he shared bread with everyone who was hungry.

And the people of London loved kind Dick so much that they chose him to be their mayor!

Dick wore a fine red robe and a shiny gold chain, and Ginger sat beside him, purring softly.

Every evening, Dick and Ginger walked home together and shared their supper by the warm fire.

And Dick smiled and said, "The best gold of all is a kind heart and a true friend."

The moon rose over London, soft and silver, and Dick and Ginger curled up together, safe, warm, and happy.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart and a true friend are worth more than gold — and sharing makes everyone rich.*
