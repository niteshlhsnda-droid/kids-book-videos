# The Grateful Parrot

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In the middle of a whispering forest stood a grand old fig tree.

Its branches were wide, its leaves were thick and green, and every year it grew the sweetest purple figs you ever tasted.

In a cozy hollow of that tree lived a young parrot named Mithu, with emerald feathers and a bright orange beak.

When Mithu was just a fluffy baby, the fig tree had sheltered him from the rain. When he was hungry, it dropped ripe figs right at his door. When the cold wind blew, its thick leaves wrapped around him like a blanket.

"You are the kindest tree in the whole forest," Mithu chirped every night before sleep.

But trees grow old, just like everything else. Year by year, the grand old fig tree grew tired. Its leaves turned yellow and fell. Its branches grew bare. No more sweet figs ripened on its twigs.

One by one, the other birds flew away to greener trees.

"Come with us, Mithu!" called the sparrows. "This old tree has nothing left to give!"

But Mithu shook his little green head. "This tree gave me everything when I was small and helpless. I will not leave it now that it is old."

So Mithu stayed. Every morning, he flew down to the stream, filled his beak with cool water — drip by drip — and poured it gently over the tree's thirsty roots.

It was slow work. A beak holds very little water. But Mithu did not mind. He sang while he worked, and his song was full of love.

Now, the king of the gods — the mightiest god of all — was watching from his throne of clouds, and his eyes grew round and bright with wonder.

He disguised himself as a bent old traveler and hobbled up to the dried-up old tree.

"Little parrot," he said, "why do you waste your days on this dead tree? Fly away and find a green one!"

Mithu bowed politely. "Kind sir, this tree fed me and kept me warm when I was a tiny baby. I'm not leaving it now that it's old."

The old traveler smiled — and suddenly he was not old at all! Light shone from his face, and flowers bloomed where his feet touched the ground.

"Mithu," said the king of the gods, "your gratitude is the most beautiful thing in this forest. Let me thank the tree that raised such a good heart."

He laid his shining hand on the old trunk — and, oh, wonder! What do YOU think happened next? Green buds burst open all along the bare branches. Fresh leaves unfurled like little flags. And among the leaves, small purple figs began to swell, sweeter than ever before.

Mithu chirped with happiness and snuggled into the soft new leaves.

From that day on, the fig tree stood green and grand again — and every evening, the two old friends whispered a slow goodnight song together under the quiet stars.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A grateful heart never forgets kindness — and kindness always finds its way back.*
