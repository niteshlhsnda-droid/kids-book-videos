How the Camel Got His Hump

A playful Just So story by Rudyard Kipling, retold for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, at the very beginning of the world, there was a camel named Sammy.

Sammy lived in the middle of a hot, sandy desert. He had a long neck, long legs, and a very lazy way of looking at the world.

Every morning, the Horse came to Sammy and said, "Camel, come and run with me! There is work to do!"

And Sammy said, "Humph!"

Every morning, the Dog came to Sammy and said, "Camel, come and fetch with me! There is work to do!"

And Sammy said, "Humph!"

Every morning, the Ox came to Sammy and said, "Camel, come and plough with me! There is work to do!"

And Sammy said, "Humph!"

The Horse, the Dog, and the Ox were very tired. They were doing all the work, and Sammy was doing none of it.

"This is not fair," said the Horse. "Sammy must help!"

So the three friends went to see the Djinn of All Deserts — the kind, clever spirit who looks after everything in the desert.

"Dear Djinn," said the Horse, "the Camel will not work. He only says 'Humph!'"

The Djinn nodded his wise old head. "I will talk to the Camel," he said.

The Djinn found Sammy sitting in the sand, looking at the sky.

"Camel," said the Djinn, "why will you not work?"

"Humph!" said Sammy.

"Then I have a gift for you," said the Djinn, with a twinkle in his eye. "Since you will not work for your food, you shall carry your food with you!"

And POOF! — a great big hump grew on Sammy's back! It was soft and round, like a little hill.

"What is this?" cried Sammy, looking over his shoulder.

"It is your hump," said the Djinn. "It will hold your food and water for many days. Now you can walk across the desert without stopping. And now that you can carry your own food, you can help your friends carry theirs too!"

Sammy looked at his new hump. He wiggled it. He wobbled it. And then he smiled a big camel smile.

"You know," said Sammy, "this hump is rather useful!"

The next morning, the Horse, the Dog, and the Ox came to Sammy again.

"Camel," said the Horse, "will you help us carry the heavy baskets across the desert?"

This time, Sammy did not say "Humph!" He stood up tall, and he said, "Yes! I will help! My hump can carry food for all of us!"

And Sammy walked across the desert with his friends, carrying baskets on his hump, and singing a happy camel song.

From that day on, Sammy the Camel always helped his friends. And whenever anyone asked him why he had a hump, he would smile and say, "My hump helps me carry food for everyone — because a little help makes the work light!"

And that is how the camel got his hump.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little help makes the work light — and a kind heart carries more than a hump.
