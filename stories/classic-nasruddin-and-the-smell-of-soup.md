# Nasruddin and the Smell of Soup

*A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a small town long ago, there lived a kind and clever man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin loved to walk through the town in the morning. He had a big warm laugh.

One sunny morning, he was walking past a little cook-shop. The shop was small, but the smell coming out of it was wonderful. Inside, a big pot of soup was bubbling away, and its lovely smell danced along the street.

Outside the shop stood a poor traveller. He had walked a very long way, and his tummy was rumbling. He had no money at all.

The traveller closed his eyes and breathed in the lovely smell. "Mmm," he said softly. "That soup smells so good. Just the smell makes me feel a little less hungry."

"Hey, you!" shouted the cook, running out of his shop. "You are eating my soup with your nose! That smell is mine, and you must pay for it!"

The poor traveller shook his head. "But sir, I did not eat anything," he said. "I only stood here and smelled."

"You smelled, so you must pay!" cried the cook. He grabbed the traveller by the arm and would not let go. "Give me money, or I will take you to the judge!"

The traveller looked very sad. He had no money to give.

Just then, Nasruddin came walking down the street, humming a happy tune. "What is all this noise about?" he asked with a smile.

The cook pointed at the traveller. "This man stole the smell of my soup! He must pay me!"

Nasruddin's eyes twinkled. "Hmm," he said. "That sounds fair. A smell is a fine thing, and fine things should be paid for."

"Of course," said Nasruddin. "Now, my friend, do you have any coins in your pocket?" The traveller shook his head. "Not one coin, sir."

"No matter," said Nasruddin. He reached into his own pocket and took out three shiny coins. Then he shook them in his hand, and they jingled — jingle, jingle, jingle!

"There," said Nasruddin, putting the coins back in his pocket. "You have your payment, my good cook."

The cook stared at him. "What? But you did not give me the coins! You only shook them!"

Nasruddin smiled. "And the traveller only smelled your soup. He did not eat it. The payment for the smell of the soup is the sound of the coins."

For a moment, nobody said a word. Then the whole crowd burst out laughing — ha, ha, ha! The cook laughed too. He let go of the traveller's arm. "Oh, Nasruddin," he said, "you are too clever for me!"

"Come, my friend," said Nasruddin. "Let us sit under that big tree. I have some bread in my bag, and bread always tastes better when it is shared."

So they sat under the tree and shared the bread. The traveller smiled and smiled, and the smell of the soup floated sweetly over them both.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever and kind heart can solve a problem without hurting anyone.*
