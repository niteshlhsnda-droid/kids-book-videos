# Tenali Rama and the Goddess Kali

*A timeless Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a small village, there lived a little boy named Tenali.

Every evening, when the sun sank low and the sky turned soft and pink, Tenali would walk to the tiny temple at the edge of the village. Its stone steps were still warm under his bare feet, and inside, tiny oil lamps glowed like fireflies while the air smelled of sweet crushed marigolds. There he would sit on the cool floor, fold his hands, and whisper his prayers — small and soft as a sleepy sigh.

The villagers would smile when they passed by. "That Tenali has such a kind heart," they would say.

One warm evening, Tenali closed his eyes and prayed as he always did. But this time, something wonderful happened.

A warm, golden glow filled the little temple — bright as morning. And there, standing before him, was the goddess Kali. She did not look scary at all. Her skin was deep and dark as the night sky, her eyes warm as lamplight, and her smile curved up her cheeks like the edges of a full moon — the smile of a loving mother.

In her hands, she held two shining bowls.

One bowl was full of gold coins that sparkled like little suns. The other bowl held a soft golden light, swirling slow like honey, humming a quiet lullaby.

"Dear child," the goddess said in a voice like rustling leaves, "your kind heart makes me smile. Now choose your gift — the gold, or the wisdom?" And what would you choose, little one?

Tenali bowed his head. He thought for a moment. His heart beat softly, like a little drum.

Then he looked up and said, "Oh Mother, give me the wisdom first! If I am wise, I can find gold anywhere. But no gold in the world can buy me wisdom!"

The goddess's eyes shone with joy, and she laughed — sweet and happy, like a stream bubbling over smooth stones.

"What a clever boy!" she said. "Then I will give you both."

And she touched Tenali's forehead — soft as a falling petal. A warm feeling melted through him, like honey swirling into warm milk.

From that day on, Tenali grew wiser every single year. He solved every puzzle as easily as untying a ribbon, answered every riddle before the village lamp burned low, and his happy laughter made everyone smile.

When he was grown, the great king of the land asked him to be his wisest helper — the one everyone came to when they had a problem. And he always remembered the little temple, the warm golden glow, and the goddess's gentle smile.

And on quiet evenings, when the sky turned soft and pink, Tenali would sit beneath the stars with the smell of night jasmine in the air — and he would remember the glowing night in the little temple.

On very still nights, the villagers said they could see a faint golden glow near the little temple — soft and warm, like a nightlight left on just for them. And the crickets chirped their sleepy songs, and the whole village would drift into peaceful dreams, safe under the goddess's gentle smile.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Wisdom is the greatest treasure of all.*
