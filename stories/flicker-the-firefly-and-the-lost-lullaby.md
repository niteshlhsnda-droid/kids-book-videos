# Flicker the Firefly and the Lost Lullaby

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Flicker was a tiny young firefly with a round, warm tummy.

Every evening, when the sun went to sleep and the moon woke up, Flicker would hum her grandmother's lullaby. And as she hummed, her tummy would glow.

Not just a little glow. The brightest, softest, most golden glow in the whole meadow.

Hush-a-bye, glow-a-bye, little light of mine,
Shine-a-little, shine-a-soft, till the morning time.

One evening, Flicker flew up over the meadow, ready to hum. She opened her mouth and —

Nothing came out.

She tried again. She hummed and hummed. But the tune was gone — lost somewhere between the stars and the grass.

And as the tune slipped away, her glow flickered . . . and faded . . . and went out.

"Oh," whispered Flicker. She felt small and dim.

Just then, Barnaby the bullfrog came hopping by with a great big boing.

"Why so dim, little one?" asked Barnaby.

"I lost my lullaby," said Flicker. "Can you help me hum it?"

"Of course!" said Barnaby. He puffed out his cheeks and croaked a tune:

*Croak-a-croak-a, ribbit-ribbit, hop-hop-hop!*

Flicker tried to hum along. But Barnaby's tune was too bumpy. Her glow blinked . . . blink . . . blink . . . and stayed dark.

"Thank you, Barnaby," said Flicker. "But that is not my lullaby."

Next, Misty the moth came fluttering by on soft, dusty wings.

"Why so dim, little one?" asked Misty.

"I lost my lullaby," said Flicker. "Can you help me hum it?"

"Of course!" said Misty. She flapped her wings and sang a tune:

*Flutter-flutter, flutter-flee, wee-wee-wee!*

Flicker tried to hum along. But Misty's tune was too fluttery. Her glow shivered . . . shiver . . . shiver . . . and stayed dark.

"Thank you, Misty," said Flicker. "But that is not my lullaby."

Then Old Tully the turtle came slowly, slowly, slowly across the grass.

"Why so dim, little one?" asked Old Tully.

"I lost my lullaby," said Flicker. "Can you help me hum it?"

"Of course," yawned Old Tully. And he began to hum, low and slow:

*Huuuuuum . . . huuuuuum . . . huuuuu —*

And Old Tully fell fast asleep, right in the middle of the tune, with a tiny turtle snore.

*Snrrrk. Snrrrk.*

Flicker giggled. But her glow was still dark.

She felt smaller and dimmer than ever. So she flew to the tallest moonflower and sat down on its soft white petal. She wrapped her little wings around herself and sat very, very still.

And then she listened.

The crickets sang, chirr-chirr, chirr-chirr.

The breeze whispered, hushhhh, hushhhh.

The pond said lap, lap, lap against the stones.

Flicker listened . . . and listened . . . and smiled.

The night itself was humming her grandmother's lullaby.

Hush-a-bye, glow-a-bye, little light of mine,
Shine-a-little, shine-a-soft, till the morning time.

Flicker hummed along. And as she hummed, her tummy glowed.

First a tiny spark. Then a warm shimmer. Then a glow brighter than ever before — bright and soft and golden, all at once.

"I remember!" said Flicker, glowing brighter. "Thank you, night!"

She flew home, humming all the way, lighting the path for all the sleepy meadow babies — the baby bunnies hopping home, the baby ducks waddling to the pond, the baby mice running home to their burrows.

And there, at the lantern-flower, was Grandmother Firefly, glowing warm and waiting.

"You found it," said Grandmother Firefly, smiling.

"I never really lost it," said Flicker. "It was humming all around me."

And together, they hummed the lullaby — soft and slow and sweet — until the whole meadow was glowing, and every little creature was fast asleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When you feel lost, be still and listen — the answer is often humming nearby.*
