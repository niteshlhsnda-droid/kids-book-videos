# Dara the Dormouse and the Dreamy Drum

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a hollow oak tree at the edge of a meadow, lived a little dormouse named Dara.

Dara loved to sleep. She slept in the morning, she slept in the afternoon, and she slept most of all in the winter. But there was one thing Dara loved even more than sleeping: music.

One day, while tidying her hollow tree, Dara found a tiny drum tucked behind a pile of acorns. It was round and golden, with two little sticks beside it.

"Oh!" said Dara. "A drum! I wonder what it sounds like."

She tapped it softly: tum-tum. Then a little louder: tum-tum-tum. The sound rolled out of the tree like warm honey.

Dara tapped a happy rhythm, and soon Sasha the squirrel came scampering over.

"What a lovely sound!" said Sasha. "Can you play it again?"

So Dara played again — tum-tum, tum-tum-tum — and Sasha danced a twirly dance around the oak tree.

Then Ruby the rabbit hopped over. "What a dreamy sound!" said Ruby. "It makes my ears feel sleepy and happy at the same time!"

So Dara played a slow, dreamy rhythm — tum... tum... tum — and Ruby swayed gently from side to side.

Soon, all the meadow animals had gathered: squirrels, rabbits, field mice, and even a very old tortoise. Dara played, and they all swayed and danced under the evening sky.

But as the stars came out, Dara noticed something. The little ones were yawning. The rabbits' eyes were drooping. Even Sasha's twirls were getting slower and slower.

"My drum is making everyone sleepy!" giggled Dara. "That is perfect. It is bedtime!"

So Dara played her slowest, softest rhythm of all: tum... tum... tum. One by one, the animals curled up in the soft grass, tucked their paws under their chins, and drifted off to sleep.

Dara smiled, put down her little sticks, and curled up in her hollow tree.

"Good night, meadow," whispered Dara. "Good night, drum."

And the last soft tum... floated away on the night air, like a lullaby.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Gentle music can turn a busy day into a peaceful good night.*
