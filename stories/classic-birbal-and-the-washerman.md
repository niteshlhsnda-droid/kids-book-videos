# Birbal and the Washerman

*A timeless Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One warm morning, Emperor Akbar sat in his grand court, listening to the people of his kingdom.

Then a kind washerman named Mohan came forward, carrying a big basket of clean white clothes.

He bowed low.

"Your Majesty," said Mohan, "I have washed clothes by the river for twenty years, and I have never lost a single sock."

Akbar smiled. "Then why do you look so worried, Mohan?"

"Because today I have a puzzle I cannot solve," said Mohan.

He lifted two white kurtas from his basket and held them up high.

"These two kurtas look just the same," he said. "One belongs to Arjun the tailor, and one belongs to Kabir the farmer. Both men came to me this morning, and both said the same thing."

"What did they say?" asked Akbar.

"They both said, 'That soft kurta with the blue button is mine,'" said Mohan. "I washed them both, and now I cannot tell which is which. I do not want to give the wrong kurta to the wrong man."

"Birbal," said Akbar, "can you help poor Mohan?"

Birbal stood up and smiled his kind, clever smile.

"Of course, Your Majesty," he said. "Let us ask Arjun and Kabir to come to the court."

Soon Arjun the tailor and Kabir the farmer stood before the emperor.

"That one is mine!" said Arjun.

"No, that one is mine!" said Kabir.

Birbal held up his hand, and the court grew quiet.

"Do not worry," he said gently. "We will find the truth with kindness, not with shouting."

"Arjun," he said, "tell me something small about your kurta. Something only its owner would know."

"My kurta has a tiny blue stitch inside the collar," he said at last. "I sewed it there myself, so I would know it was mine."

"And you, Kabir?" he asked. "Tell me something small about your kurta."

Kabir smiled a broad, happy smile.

"My kurta smells of fresh wheat," he said. "I wear it in my fields all day, and the wheat dust stays in the cloth."

Birbal picked up the soft kurta.

He looked inside the collar, and there it was — a tiny blue stitch, neat and small.

The kurta smelled of soap and sunshine, but not of wheat at all.

Birbal turned to the two men and smiled.

"Arjun," he said, "this kurta is yours. The blue stitch is your own little secret, and it tells the truth."

Arjun beamed. "Thank you, Birbal!"

Then Birbal picked up the other kurta and held it out to Kabir.

"Kabir," he said, "smell this one."

"Wheat!" he cried. "Fresh wheat! This one is mine!"

Everyone in the court laughed a soft, happy laugh.

"Birbal," said Akbar, "you have solved the puzzle with kindness and cleverness. No one was punished, and everyone goes home happy."

"Thank you, Birbal," he said. "Now I know a clever trick for my washing basket."

"That is a very good idea," said Birbal, patting him on the shoulder.

Mohan walked home by the river, humming a happy tune.

That night, Mohan slept soundly, dreaming of sunshine and kind, clever Birbal.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A small, true detail can solve a big puzzle — and kindness makes everyone happy.*
