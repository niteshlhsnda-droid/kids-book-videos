# Ollie the Otter and the Moonlit Pebbles

At the edge of Willow River, where the water whispered over smooth stones, lived a small otter named Ollie. He had soft brown fur, a cream-coloured tummy, and round dark eyes. Every evening, as the sky turned peach and gold, Ollie slipped into the river and swam in circles until the first stars came out.

Ollie loved pebbles more than anything. Beside his burrow door he kept a neat pile of smooth pebbles. Tonight he wanted to finish a pebble path from the riverbank to his burrow door, so he could follow the stones home in the dark.

"I will carry them all at once," Ollie said, piling pebbles into his arms. "One trip, and the path will be done before the moon is high." He stacked them high: grey ones, speckled ones, and one smooth white pebble that glowed in the evening light.

But pebbles are slippery, and Ollie's arms were small. Three steps along the bank, plop! A grey pebble rolled away into the reeds. Five steps more, plip-plop! Two more tumbled down and splashed into the river. Ollie huffed and puffed, but the faster he hurried, the more pebbles slipped away.

A green frog on a lily pad chuckled kindly. "You are carrying the whole river, Ollie," she said. "Would you like a little help?" Ollie shook his head. "I can do it myself," he said, though his arms were wobbling.

Then Ollie tripped over a root, and all his pebbles went tumbling, plop, plip, splash, down the bank into the shallow water. The smooth white pebble rolled furthest and stopped beside a tall reed, glowing like a tiny moon.

Ollie sat down on the bank with a sigh. His path was not done, his arms were empty, and the moon was climbing into the sky. The frog hopped closer. "Even rivers ask the banks for help holding their water," she said. "Shall we gather them together?"

This time Ollie nodded. "Yes, please," he whispered. So the frog gathered pebbles from the reeds, a sleepy dragonfly showed him where two more had rolled, and Ollie gathered the rest from the shallow water, one careful pebble at a time.

Together they laid the pebbles in a curving line from the riverbank to Ollie's burrow door. The smooth white pebble went last, beside the door, glowing like a tiny lantern. Ollie looked at the finished path and smiled.

"Thank you," Ollie said to the frog and the dragonfly. "The path is lovelier than I ever could have made alone." His friends waved goodnight, and Ollie followed the pebble path home under the stars.

He curled up in his burrow and listened to the river whispering outside.

Sleep now, little Ollie.
