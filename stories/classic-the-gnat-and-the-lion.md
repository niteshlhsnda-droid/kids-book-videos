# The Gnat and the Lion

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In the middle of a warm, green forest lived Leo the Lion. Leo was big and strong, with a golden mane that shone in the sun.

"ROAR!" went Leo every morning. "I am the king of the forest! No creature is stronger than me!"

All the animals nodded, because Leo really was very strong.

One sunny morning, a tiny gnat named Gina came buzzing by. Gina was no bigger than a crumb, but her buzz was loud and brave.

"Bzzz!" said Gina. "Excuse me, King Leo. You say no creature is stronger than you. I think I could beat you in a fair contest."

Leo laughed a great big lion laugh. "HA! HA! HA! You? A tiny little buzzing speck? Very well, little one. Let us see what you can do."

Gina zoomed up into the air. She buzzed around Leo's nose — bzzz! She zipped past his ear — bzzz! She tickled his tail — bzzz!

Leo swiped with his great paw. SWISH! He missed. Gina was too quick.

Gina buzzed around his mane — bzzz! She danced around his paws — bzzz! Poor Leo spun round and round, swiping and snapping, until he was quite dizzy.

"Stop! Stop!" cried Leo at last, sitting down with a thump. "You win, little gnat. You are quicker than I am, and I cannot catch you at all."

Gina puffed out her tiny chest with pride. "Bzzz! I beat the king of the forest! I am the strongest creature in the whole wide world!"

She buzzed away, singing a proud little song. "Bzzz, bzzz, I beat the lion! Bzzz, bzzz, I am the best!"

But Gina was so busy singing that she did not watch where she was flying. Straight ahead hung a soft, silvery spider's web, sparkling between two branches.

Bump! Gina flew right into the web and got stuck.

"Oh no!" squeaked Gina. "Help! I am stuck!"

The web wobbled, but it held her gently. Gina wriggled and wiggled, and at last she popped free and fluttered down onto a leaf, feeling very small and very silly.

Leo padded over, shaking his great mane. "Little friend," he said kindly, "you beat a lion today, and that is something to be proud of. But even the quickest flyer must watch where she flies."

Gina nodded. "You are right, King Leo," she said. "Being quick is a gift. Being careful is a gift too. I will remember both."

And from that day on, Gina still buzzed her brave little buzz — but she always looked where she was flying first.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even small strengths are worth celebrating — but pride that forgets care can trip anyone up.*
