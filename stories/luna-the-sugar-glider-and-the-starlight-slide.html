Luna the Sugar Glider and the Starlight Slide

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

High in a tall gum tree in Australia lived a sugar glider named Luna.

Luna was small and soft and grey, with big eyes and a special flap of skin between her arms and legs. When she spread her arms wide, she could glide through the air like a little kite!

Every night, Luna glided from tree to tree, visiting her friends. She glided to see Wattle the wombat. She glided to see Banksia the bandicoot. And she always came home before dawn, sleepy and happy.

But Luna had one secret wish. "Oh," she whispered to the stars one night, "I wish I could glide higher than the tallest tree. I wish I could touch the sky!"

One starry night, as Luna stood on the very top branch of her gum tree, something magical happened. A shooting star streaked across the sky — and as it passed, it left behind a shimmering slide made of pure starlight, curving down from the sky to Luna's branch!

"Wow!" gasped Luna. "A starlight slide!"

Luna took a deep breath, spread her arms wide, and — WHOOSH! — she glided onto the slide!

Round and round she went, sliding on the starlight, giggling all the way. The slide carried her up, up, up — past the treetops, past the clouds, and right up to the Moon!

"Hello, little glider!" said the Moon, smiling. "Welcome to the sky!"

Luna glided around the Moon, waving at the stars. She saw the whole world below — the forests, the rivers, and the twinkling lights of the sleeping towns.

"This is the best glide ever!" laughed Luna.

But then she looked down and saw something that made her stop laughing. Far below, her friend Wattle the wombat was sitting outside his burrow, looking up at the sky with worried eyes.

"Where is Luna?" Wattle was saying. "It is nearly dawn, and she has not come home!"

"Oh no!" said Luna. "My friends will be worried! I must go home!"

The Moon smiled kindly. "Then slide back down, little one," it said. "The starlight slide goes both ways."

So Luna spread her arms wide and slid all the way back down — past the clouds, past the treetops — and landed softly on her gum tree branch, just as the first pink light of dawn touched the sky.

"Luna!" cried Wattle, Banksia, and all her friends, running to hug her. "We were so worried!"

"I am sorry," said Luna, hugging them back. "I glided all the way to the Moon! It was wonderful — but coming home to you is even better."

From that night on, whenever a shooting star left a starlight slide in the sky, Luna took one happy glide up to the Moon — and always, always glided home before dawn, to her friends who loved her.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The grandest adventures are sweetest when you come home to the friends who love you.
