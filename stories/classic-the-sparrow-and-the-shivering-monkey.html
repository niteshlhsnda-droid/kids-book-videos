The Sparrow and the Shivering Monkey

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Have you ever seen a monkey shiver in the rain? Montu was shivering so hard that his teeth went chatter-chatter-chatter, like two tiny drums.

It was a cold, cold evening. Rain was falling — pit-pat, pit-pat — on the big green leaves of the peepal tree.

Montu sat huddled under the tree, hugging his own arms tight. "Brrr," he said. "Cold, cold, cold."

Up above, tucked in a little nest, sat Chinni the sparrow. Her nest was small and round and cozy, lined with soft feathers. Not a single drop of rain could reach her.

Chinni looked down at Montu. Her heart went thump-thump with kindness.

"Dear monkey," she called down softly, "why don't you build yourself a little shelter, the way I built my nest? I used twigs and leaves, and now I am warm and dry. If you build one, the rain will not make you shiver so."

Montu was cold and tired and very, very grumpy. The rain dripped off his nose — drip, drip, drip. "Mind your own beak!" he snapped.

And then — oh no! Montu lost his temper. He grabbed Chinni's little nest and pulled it apart. Rip! Rip! Twigs tumbled. Soft feathers floated everywhere, like tiny gray snowflakes.

"Oh!" cried Chinni. She fluttered up into the cold rain, and now SHE was shivering too. Her little wings went tremble-tremble-tremble.

But Chinni was clever. And Chinni was brave.

She flew to another tree — a safer tree — and gathered dry leaves and strong twigs. Close your eyes and picture her weaving: twig over twig, leaf over leaf, until — at last — the nest was round and warm and snug.

The next morning, the sun peeked out. Montu looked up and saw the empty branches where Chinni's little nest used to be. He saw the scattered twigs on the wet ground.

And his heart felt heavy — like a stone in his tummy.

"Chinni," he said in a very small voice, "I am so, so sorry. You were only being kind, and I was rude. Please forgive me."

Chinni flew down and sat near him. "I forgive you," she said gently.

"I promise," said Montu, "that from today I will listen kindly — even when I am grumpy."

So Chinni taught Montu to weave a little leaf-roof of his own. And Montu shared his sweetest bananas with her. They became the very best of friends.

That evening, the sun sank slowly, painting the sky soft gold and rose. The air smelled of rain and green leaves.

Chinni snuggled into her warm nest. Montu curled up under his little leaf-roof. Both of them were dry. Both of them were safe. Both of them were warm.

Chinni began to hum a tiny, sleepy song. Montu gave a big, slow yawn.

The wind rocked the branches gently — rock, rock, rock.

Chinni closed her eyes… and Montu closed his… and up in the sky, the stars came out, twinkling softly.

Warm hearts, warm nests, and a warm goodnight.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Listen kindly to good advice — and when grumpiness hurts someone, say a heartfelt sorry.
