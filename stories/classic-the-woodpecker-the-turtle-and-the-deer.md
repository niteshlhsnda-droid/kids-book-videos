# The Woodpecker, the Turtle and the Deer

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Deep in a green forest, beside a giggling stream, lived three best friends.

Woody was a woodpecker with a ruby-red cap, who tapped tap-tap-tap on the tall trees every morning.

Tara was a turtle with a smooth brown shell, who moved slowly along the bank and never, ever hurried.

And Dara was a young deer with soft eyes and springy legs, who could leap over the stream in a single bound.

Every evening, the three friends met under the big banyan tree. Woody told jokes. His favorite was: "What is a woodpecker's favorite kind of music?" — "TAP music!" — and he would tap-tap-tap the punchline on the banyan bark. Tara told slow stories full of rustling leaves and faraway rivers. Dara shared the sweetest grass he had found that day.

"We are the best of friends," Dara would say, "and friends look after each other."

One misty morning, a hunter came into the forest. Quietly, he tied a rope trap between two bushes on the path where Dara loved to run — and hid behind a tree to wait.

Along came Dara, humming a little tune. SNAP! The rope tightened around his leg!

"Help!" cried Dara. "Woody! Tara! Help me!"

Woody heard the cry and came swooping down. "Oh no! Hold on, Dara — I am coming!"

Tara heard the cry too. She tucked her head in, tucked her legs in, and ROLLED herself down the slope as fast as she could — which was not fast, but she did not stop.

"Woody —" Tara panted, catching her breath. "The rope is tough and thick. Your beak can't cut it. But MY jaws can chew through it — just give me time."

"Then I will give you time!" said Woody.

Woody flew to the bush where the hunter was hiding and began tapping on a hollow branch right above his head — TAP-TAP-TAP-TAP!

The hunter looked up, annoyed. "Shoo, bird!"

Woody flew to another tree — TAP-TAP-TAP! Then to another — TAP-TAP-TAP!

The hunter stomped after him, waving his arms. "That noisy bird! I will catch YOU instead!"

And while the hunter chased Woody this way and that, Tara chewed and chewed at the rope. Her jaws were slow, but they were strong. Bit by bit, the rope got thinner… and thinner… and — SNAP! — broke apart!

"Quick, Dara!" whispered Tara. "Run!"

Dara leaped up and bounded away into the trees. Tara flipped herself over and scuttled into the stream. Woody gave one last cheeky TAP and flew up, up into the sky.

The hunter came back to find an empty trap. He scratched his head, picked up his rope, and walked all the way home.

That evening, under the banyan tree, Dara rubbed his soft nose against his friends. "You were so clever, Woody! And Tara — you never, ever gave up. Thank you both!"

The corners of Tara's old mouth lifted into a slow, wrinkly smile. "That is what friends are for."

And the three friends watched the stars come out, side by side, safe and together.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: True friends stand by each other — each in their own special way.*
