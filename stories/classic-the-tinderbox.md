# The Tinderbox

*A Hans Christian Andersen tale, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One sunny morning, a cheerful soldier named Veer came marching home through the forest. Clickety-clack went his boots on the path. He was whistling a happy tune, and he did not have a single worry in the world.

Under a big tree with a hollow trunk — empty inside, like a secret room — sat a strange old woman with a crooked smile.

"Good morning, soldier!" she called in a squeaky voice. "Will you do a small favor for an old grandmother?"

Veer tipped his cap politely. "If I can, grandmother."

"Climb down into this hollow tree," she said. "At the bottom you will find three little rooms. In the first room sits a funny dog with eyes as big as teacups, guarding a chest of copper. In the second sits a funnier dog with eyes as big as mill-wheels — great big round wheels — guarding a chest of silver. In the third sits the funniest dog of all, with eyes as big as round towers, guarding a chest of gold. The dogs will not hurt you — they are only playing guard. Fill your pockets with whatever you like, and bring me back my grandmother's old tinderbox — her little box for making sparks."

Down climbed Veer. And sure enough — there were the three dogs, each one sillier-looking than the last, each wagging its tail like a friendly flag.

Veer patted the first dog. "Hello, teacup-eyes!" He filled his pockets with copper. He patted the second dog. "Hello, mill-wheel-eyes!" He filled his pockets with silver. He patted the third dog, who licked his hand with a great sloppy tongue. "Hello, tower-eyes!" He filled his pockets with gold — and tucked the old tinderbox under his arm.

Up he climbed, and handed the tinderbox to the old woman.

She grabbed it and cackled, "Now I will make those dogs do all MY tricks, and you will do as I say, soldier!"

Veer stood very straight. "No more tricks, grandmother," he said, firmly but kindly. "Off you go — and be kind from today!"

The old woman's crooked smile melted away. She looked down at her shoes, gave a small ashamed squeak, and hurried off down the path — and was never seen again.

Back in town, Veer was rich — but a sharing kind of rich. He shared his gold with poor families, bought warm blankets for shivering grandmothers, and still had plenty left for laddoos.

And whenever he struck the old tinderbox — click, click! — the funniest dog appeared in a puff of warm smoke, wagging its tail, ready to help. "Carry my parcels, tower-eyes!" Veer would laugh. Or, "Find my lost cap, teacup-eyes!"

One day in the market, Veer met a lonely little princess named Pari. She sat by the fountain with no one to play with, and her smile was hiding.

"I know a hundred stories," said Veer. "Would you like to hear one?"

Pari's smile peeked out. Then it came out all the way. They became the best of friends, and Veer told her a new story every single day.

When the king and queen saw how happy their little girl was, they invited everyone — EVERYONE — to a grand wedding feast for Veer and Princess Pari. There were mountains of laddoos and rivers of jalebis, and the three funny dogs sat in a row, catching crumbs in their big happy mouths.

And that night, when the music had faded and the lamps burned low, Veer clicked the tinderbox shut — click — and the dogs curled up by the fire, round and warm, snoring the tiniest dog-snores.

Pari yawned a small, sleepy yawn. Veer yawned too.

Sleep now, little one. The dogs are dreaming of biscuits, and everyone is full, and everyone is loved.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Share your good fortune, and clever kindness beats greed.*
