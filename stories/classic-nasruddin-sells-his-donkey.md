# Nasruddin Sells His Donkey

*A funny Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a sunny little town with white houses and twisty lanes, there lived a funny, clever man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin had a round tummy, a twinkly beard, and a donkey named Golu.

Golu was a very good donkey. He was gray and soft, with long ears that wiggled when he was happy. Every morning, Nasruddin rode Golu to the market — clip-clop, clip-clop — and every evening they rode home together, Golu humming his happy donkey hum: "Hee-haw, hee-haw."

But one morning, Nasruddin looked at his empty pocket and sighed.

"Golu, my friend," he said, scratching the donkey's long ear, "we need some money for rice and lentils. I think... I think I must sell you at the market today."

Golu stopped humming. His long ears drooped. "Hee-haw?" he said sadly.

"I know, I know," said Nasruddin, patting him. "Don't worry. I'll find you the kindest owner in the whole town."

So off they went to the market — clip-clop, clip-clop — with a little sign around Golu's neck that said: DONKEY FOR SALE.

Soon, a crowd gathered. Everyone wanted to see Nasruddin's famous donkey.

And Nasruddin began to praise him.

"Friends! Neighbors! Come and see the finest donkey in all the land!" he announced. "This donkey, Golu, is strong! He can carry sacks heavier than himself and never once complain!"

"True," nodded the crowd.

"He is gentle!" Nasruddin went on. "So gentle that little children can ride him, and he walks as carefully as a grandmother crossing the street!"

"Very true," said the crowd.

"He is clever!" cried Nasruddin. "He knows the way home all by himself! He never gets lost! And listen to this — he hums! A donkey that hums happy songs! Where will you find another like him?"

Golu, hearing all this praise, lifted his head proudly. His ears stood up straight. He gave his happiest hum: "Hee-haw! Hee-haw!"

The buyers pushed forward. "I'll take him!" "No, I'll take him!" "Name your price, Nasruddin!"

But Nasruddin stood very still. He looked at Golu — at his soft gray coat, his bright eyes, his wiggling ears.

He thought about their morning rides — clip-clop, clip-clop. He thought about Golu's happy hums. He thought about how Golu always waited patiently while Nasruddin chatted with friends.

And slowly, a big smile spread across Nasruddin's face.

"Friends," he said, climbing up onto Golu's back, "I have changed my mind!"

The crowd gasped. "What? Why?"

"Because," said Nasruddin, laughing his big, round laugh, "while I was telling you how wonderful my donkey is, I realized something. A donkey this strong, this gentle, this clever — a donkey that hums happy songs — why would I sell HIM? I'd be the silliest man in town!"

And with that, Nasruddin turned Golu around and rode him all the way home — clip-clop, clip-clop — with Golu humming his happiest hum of all: "Hee-haw! Hee-haw! Hee-haw!"

That evening, they shared their dinner — rice and lentils for Nasruddin, and the sweetest, crunchiest carrots for Golu.

"You know what, Golu?" Nasruddin said, scratching that favorite ear. "Sometimes we don't see how precious something is... until we try to give it away."

Golu nodded wisely, munching his carrot. He had known it all along.

---

*Moral: Sometimes we don't see how precious something is until we almost give it away.*
