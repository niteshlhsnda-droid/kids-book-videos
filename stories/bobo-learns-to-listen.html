Bobo Learns to Listen

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Bobo was a baby bat with very big ears — but he did not use them well. He talked and talked, and hardly ever listened.

One evening, Mama said, "Listen, Bobo," and told him about the night sounds. But Bobo was too busy chattering. He missed the owl's soft hoot. He missed the stream's gentle song.

"I see you love to talk," said Mama.

Bobo nodded proudly.

"But listening is a superpower too," Mama said. She taught him the listening game.

"Quiet ears hear the world sing."

"Quiet ears hear the world sing," she said again.

"Quiet ears hear the world sing," she said one more time.

Bobo wanted to try. He closed his mouth and opened his big ears wide.

At first, he heard nothing. Then, very softly, he heard it. Hoot, hoot.

"The owl!" he whispered.

Then he heard more. Drip, drop, went the stream. Rustle, rustle, went the leaves. Buzz, buzz, went a sleepy bee.

Bobo was amazed. His ears tingled, and his heart felt happy. "I can hear everything!" he said.

Mama smiled. "You listened carefully," she said. "Listening shows we care."

That night, they flew together. Bobo listened to the wind — whoosh, whoosh. He listened to the crickets — chirp, chirp. He listened to Mama's wings — flap, flap, beside him.

He felt close to everything. Listening made the night bigger and more beautiful.

Back in the cave, Bobo snuggled upside down and listened to Mama's lullaby, soft and low. He felt happy and calm, and the cave was quiet.

Bobo closed his eyes. He was a good listener. He was loved.

Goodnight, Bobo.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Listening carefully helps us hear the whole world — and shows we care.
