The Oak and the Reeds

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

By the side of a wide, singing river, there grew a mighty oak tree.

The oak was the biggest tree anyone had ever seen. His trunk was thick as a house door. His branches reached up, up, up into the sky, and his green leaves rustled like little flags in the breeze.

At his feet, along the riverbank, grew a family of slender reeds. They were thin and green as blades of grass, and they swayed softly whenever the breeze came by, humming their quiet river song.

Every day, the oak looked down at the reeds and chuckled in his deep tree voice. "Look at you!" he boomed. "You tremble and bend at every little breeze! A leaf falls, and you shiver! Watch me — I stand tall and straight, and I never, ever bow!"

The little birds who nested in his branches chirped, "How strong you are, Oak!" And the oak stood a little straighter, feeling very proud indeed.

The reeds listened quietly. Then the oldest reed answered in her softest whisper-voice, "Dear Oak, we bend so that we do not break."

The oak laughed a big leafy laugh. "Break? Ha! Nothing in the whole world can break ME!"

Days went by, soft and slow. The sun shone warm on the river. Little silver fish went flip-flop in the water. The reeds swayed and hummed their gentle song, and the dragonflies danced above them like tiny blue jewels. The oak stood stiff and proud, just as always.

Then one evening, the sky turned dark. Thick clouds rolled over the hills like giant grey sheep. The wind began to howl — "Whoooo!" — and the first drops of rain went pitter-patter on the leaves.

A terrible storm was coming.

Close your eyes — can you hear the wind coming? Whoooo...

The reeds did what they always did. They bent low, low, low, till their green heads nearly touched the water. "Bend, little ones, bend," whispered the old reed. And the wind rushed over them like a wild river in the sky.

But the oak would not bend. "I never bow!" he cried, and he stood stiff and straight against the storm.

The wind pushed. The wind pulled. The branches creaked — creeeak! The trunk groaned — groooan!

Then — CRACK! With a great crash, the mighty oak was torn up by his roots and fell into the river with a great big SPLASH!

The storm raged all night long. The little birds huddled close in the reeds, safe and dry among the bending stems.

But when the morning sun peeked over the hills, warm and golden, the storm was gone. The sky was soft and blue again.

And there, along the riverbank, stood the reeds — straight and green again, whispering softly in the sunshine.

The old reed looked at her fallen friend, resting quietly in the shallow water. "Rest now, dear Oak," she whispered gently. "If only you had bent a little, you would still be standing tall beside us."

The reeds swayed softly in the morning breeze. The river sang its low, sleepy song. And all was calm and quiet once more.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Gentleness and flexibility are stronger than stiff pride.
