# The Rabbits and the Elephants

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, at the edge of a wide green meadow, there was a soft, sandy hill.

On that hill lived a big, happy family of rabbits. They hopped and nibbled clover all day, and at night they slept in cozy burrows under the moon.

The rabbits' best friend was a round, silver lake at the bottom of the hill. Its water was cool and sweet, and on clear nights the moon sailed across it like a boat.

But one hot summer, a herd of elephants came marching through the meadow — tromp, tromp, tromp!

The elephants were thirsty. Every day they splashed down to the little lake and drank and drank and bathed and trumpeted.

SPLISH! SPLOSH! Their big feet churned the water into mud. Their trunks sucked up almost all of it.

Soon the lake was nearly empty. The rabbits' burrows near the shore crumbled. The poor rabbits had no water to drink.

"What shall we do?" squeaked little Ruby Rabbit, her nose trembling.

The oldest rabbit, Grandpa Bramble, twitched his long ears. "We cannot push the elephants," he said. "But perhaps we can make them think."

That night, when the moon was round and bright, Grandpa Bramble hopped to the lake. The elephants were coming down the hill — tromp, tromp, tromp!

Grandpa Bramble sat up very tall on a stone and called out in his deepest, most serious voice, "HALT! Who dares to trouble the lake of the Moon?"

The elephant king stopped. "The lake of the Moon?" he rumbled. "We only want a drink."

"Look!" said Grandpa Bramble, pointing one paw at the water.

The elephant king bent his great head. There, in the rippling lake, floated the round, glowing face of the moon.

"The Moon lives in this lake," said Grandpa Bramble solemnly. "And the Moon is very, very angry. Every day you shake his house and turn his water to mud. See how his face trembles!"

Just then a breeze ruffled the water, and the moon's reflection wobbled and shook.

The elephant king gasped. "Oh no! We did not know! We are so sorry, great Moon!"

All the elephants bowed their big heads to the water. "Forgive us!" they trumpeted softly.

Grandpa Bramble nodded slowly. "The Moon will forgive you — if you drink from the big river beyond the meadow from now on, and let this little lake rest."

"We promise!" said the elephant king. "We promise, promise, promise!"

And from that day on, the elephants marched past the little lake to the big, wide river. The lake filled up again, cool and silver. The rabbits drank and splashed and danced in the moonlight.

And if you look at the moon on a clear night, you might see it smiling — for it knows that even the smallest rabbit can solve the biggest problem with a clever thought.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: You don't need to be big to solve a big problem — a clever, kind idea is stronger than stomping feet.*
