Birbal and the Half Reward

A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the kingdom of the kind King Akbar, there lived a poor farmer named Mohan.

Mohan had very little money, but he had a big, kind heart and one small mango tree behind his little house.

Every summer, that little tree gave him the sweetest mangoes in the whole village, golden and soft, smelling of sunshine and honey.

One bright morning, Mohan picked the ripest mangoes and placed them gently in a basket.

"I will take these to the king," said Mohan, with a smile. "King Akbar loves mangoes, and I want to share my best ones with him."

So Mohan set off down the road, the basket balanced on his head.

At the palace gates stood a guard named Raghu, with a big moustache and a bigger frown.

"Stop!" said Raghu, holding up his hand. "What is in that basket?"

"Sweet mangoes for the king," said Mohan, politely.

Raghu leaned close and whispered, "Listen, farmer. Whatever reward the king gives you, half is mine! Promise me, or you cannot go in."

That did not seem fair at all. But the gate was the only way in.

"Very well," said Mohan, softly. "I promise."

Raghu grinned and opened the gates.

Inside, King Akbar sat on his throne, and beside him stood his clever friend Birbal, smiling quietly.

Mohan bowed low and held out his basket. "Your Majesty, I bring you my sweetest mangoes," he said.

King Akbar took one mango and bit into it. "Mmm!" he said. "So sweet! So juicy! Ask me for any reward you wish, and it shall be yours."

Mohan looked at Birbal, who gave him the tiniest wink.

Mohan said, "Your Majesty, I would like a hundred tickles."

The whole court gasped. King Akbar blinked. "Tickles?" he asked. "A hundred tickles? Are you sure?"

"Quite sure, Your Majesty," said Mohan. "And the guard at the gate must share half of my reward."

Raghu was called in, and his face turned pale.

"Fifty tickles for Raghu!" said the king, laughing. "Bring the softest peacock feathers!"

Two servants came forward with long feathers and tickled Raghu's arms and toes.

"Hee-hee-hee!" giggled Raghu, wiggling this way and that. "Ha-ha-ha! Stop! Please, stop!"

Everyone in the court giggled.

At last Raghu sat down, puffing and laughing. "Enough, enough!" he cried. "I have learned my lesson! I will never ask for half of someone else's reward again. I am sorry, Mohan."

Birbal stepped forward and patted Mohan on the shoulder. "Mohan, you are clever and kind," he said. "You did not shout or fight. You used a gentle joke to teach a big lesson. That is true wisdom."

King Akbar smiled and nodded. "Birbal is right," he said. "Mohan, you may skip your fifty tickles, and you shall have a bag of gold coins and a new mango tree beside your house. And Raghu, you shall help him plant it."

"Gladly, Your Majesty!" said Raghu, and he meant it.

That evening, Mohan walked home under the silver moon, his basket light and his heart lighter still, while far behind him the king's happy laughter floated on.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A gentle joke can teach a big lesson — and kindness is always the cleverest reward.
