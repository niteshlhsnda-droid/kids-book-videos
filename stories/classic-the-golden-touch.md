# The Golden Touch

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, there lived a kind king named Midas in a castle with a sunny garden.

King Midas loved gold more than anything — gold coins, gold cups, golden crowns.

One morning, a kind old stranger with a long white beard came to the castle gate.

"King Midas," said the stranger, "you have been kind to me, so I will give you one wish. What would you like most of all?"

King Midas did not think for even a moment. "I wish that everything I touch would turn to gold!"

"Are you sure?" asked the stranger. "Gold cannot be eaten, and it cannot be hugged."

"I am sure!" laughed the king. "Gold is the best thing in the world!"

"Then your wish is granted," said the stranger, and he walked away.

King Midas ran into his garden. He touched a red rose — and oh! It turned to shiny gold!

He touched a leaf — gold! A stone — gold! His chair — gold!

"Wonderful!" cried the king. "Everything I touch turns to gold!"

Soon it was lunch time. A servant brought warm bread and a cup of cool milk.

But when he picked up the bread — oh no! It turned hard and gold in his hand!

He lifted the cup of milk — and it turned to gold before he could take a sip!

King Midas felt a little knot in his tummy.

Just then, his little daughter Tara came running in with her arms open wide.

"Papa! Papa!" she cried. "Look at the pretty gold rose!"

Tara gave her father a big hug — and her little hand turned to hard, cold gold!

"Oh no!" cried King Midas, tears running down his cheeks. "What have I done? My Tara!"

Tara stood very still, her golden hand sparkling in the sun. She looked up at her papa.

"Papa, do not cry," she said softly. "Please find a way to make me soft and warm again."

King Midas held his daughter close. "I do not want the gold!" he cried. "I want my Tara and her warm little hand!"

Just then, he heard a soft voice behind him. It was the old stranger.

"Go to the magic river," said the stranger. "Wash your hands there, and sprinkle the water on all the gold. Then your wish will be undone."

King Midas ran to the magic river and washed his hands in the cool water.

Then he hurried home with cool river water and sprinkled it on the golden rose, and it turned soft and red again!

He sprinkled the bread, and it turned warm and soft again!

And then he sprinkled the water on Tara's golden hand.

And oh! Her hand turned soft and warm again! Tara wiggled her fingers and laughed.

From that day on, King Midas loved his daughter, his garden, and warm bread more than all the gold in the world.

And every morning, he picked a red rose for Tara, just because it smelled so sweet.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Love, warm bread and a soft hug are worth more than all the gold in the world.*
