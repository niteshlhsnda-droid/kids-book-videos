Danny the Dormouse and the Drowsy Daisy

A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Danny the dormouse was the sleepiest little dormouse in the whole meadow.

He could sleep in a leaf. He could sleep in a log. He could sleep curled up in his soft, round nest like a little ball of fur. His friends said he could fall asleep anywhere at all.

But tonight, Danny could not sleep.

He tossed. He turned. He wiggled his little toes. He twitched his little whiskers.

"Why am I not sleepy?" he yawned. "I am usually the sleepiest dormouse of all! Something is wrong with my sleep."

He tried counting sheep. One sheep, two sheep, three sheep. But the sheep just yawned back at him.

Outside his nest, the meadow was quiet and silver in the moonlight. All the flowers were closed up tight, fast asleep. The grass swayed softly. The crickets sang their quiet night song.

All the flowers, that is, except one.

One tall daisy was still open. Her white petals were bright in the moonlight, swaying gently in the night breeze.

"Hello, Danny," said the daisy, in a soft, sleepy voice. "Why are you awake?"

"I cannot sleep," said Danny, climbing out of his nest. "I have tried everything. I counted sheep. I hummed a song. Nothing works."

"Come and sit with me," said the daisy. "Tell me a story. A soft story. Then we can both be sleepy together."

So Danny sat down beside the tall daisy. He took a big breath. And he told her a story.

"Once upon a time," he said, "there was a moon. And the moon was very, very tired. The moon yawned a big, wide yawn. Aaaaaah."

The daisy giggled. One of her petals closed, soft and slow.

"The moon said goodnight to the stars," said Danny. "One star, two stars, three stars. They all closed their twinkly eyes."

Another petal closed. Then another.

"The moon tucked the clouds into bed," whispered Danny. "He sang them a soft, soft song. La, la, la. The clouds yawned, and they drifted off to sleep."

Another petal closed. The daisy was nearly asleep now. Her stem swayed slowly, slow as a rocking chair.

"And then," whispered Danny, "the moon went to sleep. And the whole sky went to sleep too. The stars twinkled one last twinkle, and then everything was quiet and soft."

The last petal closed. The daisy was fast asleep, swaying gently in the moonlight.

And Danny? Danny's eyes were closing too. His head drooped. His whiskers drooped. He curled up beside the daisy, warm and safe, tucked into the soft grass.

"Goodnight, daisy," he murmured. "Thank you for listening."

And he fell fast asleep, dreaming of a moon that yawned, and stars that closed their twinkly eyes.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A story shared is the softest lullaby.
