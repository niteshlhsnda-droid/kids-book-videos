The Camel and the Jackal

A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

By the side of a wide, slow river lived Chintu the camel. You could hear the water going *shhh-shhh* through the soft grass. Chintu was tall and kind, with long, long legs and a soft, round hump. His very best friend was Jhumki the jackal — small, quick, and clever, with a pointy nose and a fluffy tail.

Chintu and Jhumki did everything together. They watched the clouds drift by and played hide-and-seek among the tall grass. And on warm evenings, they sat side by side on the riverbank, watching the moon rise over the water, laying a silver road on the ripples.

Across the river grew a field of sweet melons — row upon row of round green ones, ripe and ready. I wonder which melon Chintu would pick first. Jhumki loved melons more than anything in the world. But there was one problem — Jhumki could not swim.

"Oh, those melons!" Jhumki would sigh, staring across the river. "If only I could reach them!"

"Then I will carry you," said kind Chintu. "Hop on my back, and hold on tight."

So Jhumki climbed up between Chintu's hump and his neck, and Chintu waded into the river. *Splish, splash, splish, splash.* The water swirled around his long legs, cool and gentle. Jhumki held on tight and giggled all the way across.

In the melon field, they ate and ate. Crunch, munch — sweet juice ran down their chins. Jhumki rolled a little melon back and forth. Chintu munched one melon, then another. *Mmm, mmm!*

But as the moon rose round and bright, Jhumki began to wiggle and fidget. She looked up at the moon and felt fluttery and giggly all over — because Jhumki loved to howl at the moon more than almost anything.

"Jhumki," whispered Chintu. "Not now, friend. The farmer will hear!"

But Jhumki could not help herself. She lifted her pointy nose to the sky and sang out, "HOOO! HOOO!"

Jhumki's *HOOO!* echoed back across the water: *hooo… hooo…*

Sure enough, the farmer came running from his little hut, waving his stick and shouting, "Who is stealing my melons? Shoo! Shoo!"

Chintu did not wait. He dashed to the river with Jhumki holding on tight to his back. But the farmer's stick swung once — *bonk!* — and gave Chintu a bump right on his hump. It was only a little bump — more surprise than ouch.

Chintu splashed into the river, safe and sound, and waded across with his heart thumping. *Splish, splash, splish, splash.* At last they reached their own bank, wet and shivery in the moonlight.

Chintu sat down with a sigh. "Ouch, my hump," he said softly. "You didn't think of me, friend."

Jhumki's ears drooped low. "I'm sorry, Chintu," she whispered. "The moon made me forget."

They sat quietly for a while, watching the silver ripples. Then Chintu said, very quietly, "Jhumki… I love to roll in the water."

Jhumki gulped. "Not now, friend! Please, not now!"

But Chintu was already wibble-wobbling. Sideways he tipped — *SPLASH!* — and rolled right over in the shallow water.

"Whoa! Wheee!" cried Jhumki as she tumbled in. *Splash, sputter, glug!* Up popped her head, dripping and squeaking. She sputtered, she shook her ears, and then — she started to laugh. "Ha-ha! You got me!"

Chintu laughed too, a big rumbling camel laugh. They splashed and giggled together in the moonlit shallows until the water was calm again.

Dripping wet on the bank, Jhumki shook her fluffy tail and looked at her friend with big, sorry eyes. "I am sorry with all my heart, Chintu," she said. "I only thought of my howl. I did not think of you."

Chintu touched her gently with his soft nose. "And I only thought of my roll," he said. "I did not think of you either. So I forgive you, friend — and please forgive me too."

"I forgive you," said Jhumki, smiling.

From that night on, they did things differently. When Chintu carried Jhumki to the melons, Jhumki whispered her howl very, very softly — *hooo* — just for the moon, and no louder. And when they crossed back, Chintu saved his rolling for his own bank, where Jhumki watched and laughed from the dry grass.

And every evening, the two friends shared their melons — carried across quietly this time — and sat side by side on the riverbank, watching the moon rise over the water, thinking of each other first.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Good friends think of each other first.
