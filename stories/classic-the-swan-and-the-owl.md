# The Swan and the Owl

*A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One summer, the birds of the great blue lake decided they needed a king.

"A king will keep us safe," said the ducks. "A king will stop our little fights," said the sparrows.

So all the birds gathered on the sandy shore — thousands of wings, thousands of beaks, all chattering at once.

"Who should be our king?" they asked.

The peacock spread his glittering tail. "Me! I am the most beautiful!"

The parrot puffed up his chest. "Me! I can talk the sweetest!"

Then the heron pointed with his long beak to a tree stump, where a round owl sat blinking his enormous eyes.

"Him!" said the heron. "Look at those big, wise eyes! He must be very, very clever."

All the birds looked at Golu the owl. His eyes were round as moons. His feathers were soft and grey.

"Yes! Yes!" chirped the birds. "The owl looks so wise! Let him be our king!"

Just then, Kaga the crow came flapping in, late as usual. "Wait! Wait!" he cawed. "What is happening?"

"We are choosing our king," said the ducks. "The owl — because he looks so wise!"

Kaga tilted his black head. "Hmm," he said. "You are choosing a king because of his LOOKS?"

"His eyes are so big and round," said the sparrows.

"Big eyes are lovely," said Kaga, "but eyes are not wisdom. Tell me — has anyone asked him a single question?"

The birds went quiet. Shhh.

Kaga flew to the stump. "Golu," he said kindly, "what will you do if a storm comes at noon, when the sun is high?"

Golu blinked his big round eyes. "Er… um… I… like the night," he mumbled.

"What will you do if the baby birds are hungry?" asked Kaga.

Golu blinked again. "Um… er… I like the night," he mumbled again.

All the birds looked at each other. The ducks shuffled their feet. The sparrows rustled their wings.

"Maybe," whispered the peacock, "we should not choose a king just by looking."

Hans the swan glided forward, white and graceful. "A king should be chosen by what he DOES," she said softly, "not by how he looks. Let us take our time, watch, and learn — and then decide."

"Yes," said all the birds together. "Let us take our time."

And Golu the owl? He was not sad at all. He opened his beak in a big, soft yawn and said, "Good idea. Choosing is hard work. I think I will go back to sleep."

And he slept, cozy and content, while the lake grew soft and sleepy around him.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: What someone does matters more than how they look.*
