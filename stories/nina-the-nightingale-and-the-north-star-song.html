Nina the Nightingale and the North-Star Song

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a tall pine tree at the edge of a quiet wood lived a little nightingale named Nina.

Nina had soft brown feathers, a round tummy, and a voice as sweet as honey in warm milk. But Nina was shy. Very shy.

Every morning, the other birds sang their songs loud and bright. Robin sang from the top of the oak. Blackbird sang from the garden gate. Finch sang and sang until the whole wood rang with music.

Nina listened from her branch. She opened her beak — and closed it again.

"What if my song is not loud enough?" she whispered. "What if my song is not pretty enough?"

So Nina kept her song tucked inside her, like a secret in a little box. All day long, she hummed so softly that nobody could hear her at all.

But in the evenings, when the wood grew quiet and the last light faded from the sky, Nina would practise all alone. She sang to the rustling leaves. She sang to the sleepy stream. She sang to the moon as it rose, round and kind, above the trees. Her voice was small and sweet, and the evening air carried it gently away.

One evening, the wood grew quiet. The sun sank low and painted the sky pink and gold. One by one, the birds tucked their heads under their wings and went to sleep.

But Nina was still awake. She looked up at the night sky, and there it was — the North Star, bright and steady, twinkling above the tallest pine.

"Hello, North Star," whispered Nina. "Everyone is asleep. Would you listen to my song? Just you?"

The North Star twinkled, as if to say yes.

So Nina took a deep breath. And then, in the quiet dark, she sang.

Her song was not loud. It was soft as a feather falling. It floated up through the pine branches like a ribbon of moonlight — la, la, la, loo. A gentle tune about sleepy leaves and silver stars and dreams that drift like soft feathers.

Down below, in the mossy hollow, Benny the bunny was tossing and turning. He could not get to sleep. But when Nina's song floated down to him, he snuggled into the moss, gave a sleepy sigh, and smiled.

In the bramble bush, Molly the mouse was wide awake, wiggling her whiskers. But when Nina's song floated down to her, she curled up in her nest of soft leaves, and her eyelids grew heavy.

In the old oak tree, Oliver the owl blinked his big round eyes. "What a lovely song," he hooted softly. Then he tucked his head under his wing and drifted off too.

By the sleepy stream, Hazel the hedgehog curled into a round, prickly ball. When Nina's song floated down to her, she gave a tiny happy sigh and closed her eyes.

Nina sang on, soft and slow. Her voice rose and fell like a boat rocking on a calm sea. The stars seemed to lean in closer to listen. The pine branches swayed gently, as if they were rocking the whole wood to sleep.

At last, Nina sang her very last note — a long, soft, sleepy loo-oo-oo — and the wood was quiet and still.

"Thank you, Nina," whispered the North Star, twinkling its brightest twinkle. "Your song was just right."

Nina smiled. Her heart felt warm and light, like a lantern glowing in the dark. She tucked her head under her wing, snuggled into her nest, and closed her eyes.

And all through the quiet wood, beneath the watchful North Star, every sleepy animal dreamed sweet dreams, smiling in their sleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A soft, gentle song can be the most beautiful song of all.
