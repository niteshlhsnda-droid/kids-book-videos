# Nasruddin Sells His House

*A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One sunny morning, Nasruddin decided to sell his house. Soon a kind neighbour named Omar came knocking.

"I will buy your house," said Omar with a smile. "It is a lovely house, with a sunny garden and a strong roof."

"Wonderful!" said Nasruddin. "But there is one small thing. You see that little nail on the wall? That nail is still mine. I am selling you the house — but not the nail."

Omar laughed. "A nail? Of course, my friend. Keep your nail."

So Omar moved in happily, and Nasruddin moved into a new little house down the road. All was well — or so Omar thought.

The very next morning, there was a knock at the door. Tap, tap, tap!

"Hello, Omar!" said Nasruddin, beaming. "I have come to check on my nail."

Omar smiled and let him in. Nasruddin walked over to the wall, tapped the little nail, and said, "Ah, still here. Good, good." Then he sniffed the air. "Is that fresh bread I smell?"

Omar laughed and shared his breakfast. Nasruddin ate and went home.

The next day, there was another knock. Tap, tap, tap!

"Hello, Omar! I have come to check on my nail. And — oh! — I see it is a little dusty. Let me hang my coat on it to keep it warm."

Omar chuckled. "Your coat? On the nail? Well, it is your nail, after all."

Nasruddin hung his coat on the nail and stayed for lunch. And the next day, he came again to check the nail, and he stayed for lunch again. And the day after that, too!

Every day, Nasruddin popped in to "check his nail." He hung his coat on it, then his hat, and once even a little basket of apples, "to keep the nail company."

Omar was a kind man, and he liked Nasruddin very much. But one evening, sitting in his garden, he had a bright idea, and he smiled.

The next morning, when Nasruddin came knocking, Omar opened the door with a big grin.

"Good morning, Nasruddin! I have a gift for you," he said. He held out a brand-new, shiny nail. "This nail is all yours — keep it on your own wall, and visit it whenever you like!"

Nasruddin looked at the shiny nail. Then he looked at Omar. Then he burst out laughing — a great big belly laugh that made the birds in the garden sing.

"Oh, Omar!" he said, wiping tears from his eyes. "You are cleverer than I am! You are right. A nail is a very silly thing to keep."

From that day on, the nail on Omar's wall belonged to Omar, and Nasruddin hung his shiny nail on his own wall.

And best of all, the two friends did not need a nail to visit each other anymore. They shared meals and stories and laughter, because they were true friends.

That night, Nasruddin sat in his garden under the moon, his new nail glinting softly on the wall. He yawned a big, happy yawn, and drifted off to sleep, dreaming of friendship and fresh bread.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever trick is only funny once — be fair to your friends.*
