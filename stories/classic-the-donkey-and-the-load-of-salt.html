The Donkey and the Load of Salt

A classic Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Every morning, in a little village by the river, a merchant named Sohan loaded two heavy sacks of salt onto his donkey, Duggu. Sohan was a kind merchant, and Duggu was a good donkey — but oh, those sacks were heavy!

"Off to the market, Duggu!" Sohan would say, patting Duggu's soft grey neck.

The road to the market went over a stream. The water was shallow and cool, and little stones shone at the bottom like coins.

Duggu hated the heavy sacks. They dug into his back. "Huff… puff…" he grunted with every step.

One morning, on the way back from the market, Duggu's hoof slipped on a wet stone. Splash! Down he went — right into the stream, sacks and all.

Sohan jumped back. "Oh no, Duggu! Are you all right?"

Duggu stood up, dripping. He shook himself — shiver, shiver, shake! — and drops of water flew everywhere. And then he noticed something wonderful.

The sacks felt… light. So light!

You see, the salt had melted away into the stream. What was left was nothing but two empty sacks.

Duggu trotted home that evening, light and happy, his ears bouncing. "What a clever donkey I am," he thought, and he smiled a wide, toothy donkey grin.

The next morning, the sacks were full of salt again. "Huff… puff…" grunted Duggu at the stream.

This time, Duggu did not slip by accident. He looked left. He looked right. And then — plop! — he sat right down in the water on purpose.

Splash, splosh! The salt melted away into the stream, and the sacks went wonderfully light. "Hee-haw!" laughed Duggu.

But Sohan was watching. And Sohan was no fool.

That evening, Sohan patted Duggu's head and said, "You clever, clever donkey. Tomorrow, my friend, you'll carry something else."

What do you think Sohan put in the sacks? Something soft… something very, very thirsty…

The next morning, Sohan loaded the sacks with soft, fluffy sponges instead of salt.

"Huff… puff…" grunted Duggu, and at the stream he sat down in the water with a happy SPLASH.

But oh no! The sponges drank and drank the stream water, swelling up fat and heavy like big soggy pillows — heavier than the salt had ever been!

Duggu stood up with a long, tired groan. "Hee… haw…" he sighed. Every step home was slow, and the wet sponges went squish, squish, squish.

That night, Duggu lay down in the soft straw of his stable. The moon shone through the window, round and kind.

"No more tricks," Duggu whispered to himself. "From now on, I will just walk. One step, then another step. Honest work is lighter work."

And with that, Duggu closed his eyes and drifted off to sleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Tricks can backfire — honest work feels lighter.
