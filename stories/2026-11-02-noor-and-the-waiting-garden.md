# Noor and the Waiting Garden

In a little cottage at the end of a lane lived Noor, a five-year-old girl with curly dark hair and a yellow dress covered in tiny white daisies. Behind her cottage lay a small garden, and in it one sunny patch of soil waited empty, just for her.

One spring morning, Noor's mother pressed a single striped seed into her palm. "This is a sunflower seed," she said. "Plant it in the sunny patch, and give it water and time." Noor dug a little hole with her spoon, tucked the seed inside, patted the soil soft, and poured water over it in a slow, silver stream.

"Grow tall by tomorrow," Noor whispered to the soil before she went to bed. But when morning came, the sunny patch looked exactly the same. No green shoot. No leaf. Just brown soil, quiet in the sun.

Noor watered the patch again and waited all afternoon. Still nothing pushed through the soil. "Maybe I planted it upside down," she said, and her fingers itched to dig the seed back up and look.

Just then a tiny snail slid across a stone beside her. "Hello, Shelly," said Noor. The snail moved so slowly that Noor giggled. "You are slower than my seed!" Shelly stretched her little feelers toward the sun and kept on sliding, steady and unhurried, as if slow was a perfectly fine way to travel.

The next morning, Noor did not dig. She watered the patch, sang it a soft little song, and went to play. Day after day she did the same: water, a song, and play, without peeking under the soil.

Then, one dewy morning, Noor gasped. A tiny green shoot, no bigger than her thumbnail, had curled up out of the soil. "You came!" she cried, clapping her hands. Shelly slid by again, as if to say hello to the new green neighbour.

The shoot grew and grew. First came two round leaves, then a tall green stem, then leaves as big as Noor's hands. Soon the stem stood taller than Noor herself, and at its very top a round bud opened, petal by petal, into a great golden sunflower that turned its face to follow the sun.

That evening, Noor sat beneath her sunflower while the sky turned pink and gold. Bees hummed around the flower, and Shelly rested on a leaf in the shade.

As the first stars blinked on, Noor yawned and waved to her tall golden flower. "Goodnight, sunflower," she whispered. "Thank you for growing in your own good time."

Sleep now, little Noor.
