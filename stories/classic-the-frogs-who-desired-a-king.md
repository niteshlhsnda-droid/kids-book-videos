# The Frogs Who Desired a King

*A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green valley, tucked between soft hills, there was a quiet little pond. The water was clear as glass, and lily pads floated on it like green plates. At night the stars winked down at their own reflections.

In the pond lived a family of frogs. There was Pippa, who loved to hum while she swam. There was Bolo, who could jump higher than anyone. And there was little Miri, who liked to sit on the biggest lily pad and watch the clouds drift by.

The frogs splashed and sang and played hide-and-seek among the reeds, and every evening they floated on the water and watched the moon rise.

"It is a lovely pond," said Pippa one morning, stretching her legs in the sun.

"Yes," said Bolo, with a big splash. "But don't you think something is missing? Every fine pond should have a king!"

Pippa blinked. "But we already know what to do," she said. "We splash, we sing, we nap."

"Still," said Bolo, "a king would make us important. Let us wish for a king!"

So the frogs all wished together. "Dear sky," they croaked, "please send us a king!"

And one soft, rainy afternoon, a big old log came loose from the bank of the stream, high up the hill. It rolled, it tumbled, it floated down the stream — and with a great SPLASH, it landed right in the middle of the pond!

The frogs all jumped. Water sprayed everywhere.

"A king!" cried Bolo. "Look! The sky has sent us a king!"

The log bobbed gently on the water. It was long and brown, with moss growing on one end like a green crown. It did not speak. It just floated, quiet and still.

"He is a very quiet king," said little Miri, hopping up onto the log. "I like him."

So the log became their king. The frogs sang their songs from it, napped beside it, and floated beside it as the moon rose. The log never gave a single order. It never hurried anyone. It just floated, calm and kind, and the pond stayed peaceful and happy.

But one morning, something new happened.

Down from the pale blue sky came a tall stork with long legs and wide white wings. Her name was Stella, and she loved busy mornings.

"Good morning, pond!" called Stella, landing with a soft rustle on the bank. "What a lovely pond! And look — so many frogs! I will be your new queen, and I will make this pond the busiest, brightest pond in the whole valley!"

At once, Stella began to give orders.

"Line up, everybody! Tidy the lily pads! Sing the morning song — all together now, one, two, three!"

The frogs lined up, tidied the lily pads, and sang the morning song — all together, one, two, three.

"Now," said Stella, "let us count the reeds! Let us polish the pebbles! Let us march around the pond!"

So the frogs counted the reeds, polished the pebbles, and marched around the pond until their little legs were quite tired.

"Now," said Stella, "let us do it all again!"

Pippa's voice was small. "But we are tired," she said.

"And we are hungry," said Bolo.

"And we miss our quiet pond," said little Miri.

Stella stopped. She looked at the frogs. Their eyes were droopy. Their smiles were gone. Even the little birds had flown away to find a quieter place.

"Oh," said Stella softly. "I thought busy mornings would make you happy. But I only made you tired."

"You are not a bad stork," said Pippa kindly. "You just like busy mornings. And we like quiet ones."

Stella nodded slowly. "Then this is not my pond," she said. "Somewhere out there is a pond that loves busy mornings. I will go and find it."

"Will you come back and visit?" asked little Miri.

"Of course," said Stella, spreading her wide white wings. "I will fly over every evening and wave."

And with a soft whoosh, she rose into the sky and flew away.

The pond grew quiet again.

That evening, the frogs floated beside their log king and watched the moon rise.

"Our pond was lovely all along," said Pippa. "We just did not know it."

And as the stars came out, one by one, the frogs closed their eyes and drifted off to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Be happy with what you have — the quiet good things around you are treasures too.*
