# Daisy the Dormouse and the Dreamy Dandelion

*A gentle bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a hollow under an old hazel tree lived a dormouse named Daisy — a tiny mouse who loved to sleep. Her nest was a round cup of soft moss, lined with one grey feather, and it smelled faintly of hazelnuts.

Daisy was a champion sleeper. She could nap through rain pattering on leaves and wind humming in branches. But one evening, something was wrong. She curled up tight, then stretched out long. She counted the cracks in the hazel bark, one by one. Still, sleep would not come.

"My dreams are too small," Daisy whispered to the feather. "Other animals dream of mountains and oceans. I dream of crumbs."

The moon was up, round and kind. Daisy slipped out of her nest and padded across the meadow, where the grass was cool under her feet and the air smelled of clover.

At the top of the meadow stood a tall dandelion named Dilly. Her yellow head nodded in the night breeze, and all around her floated the soft white parachutes of her seeds.

"Why are you awake, little one?" asked Dilly, swaying gently.

"I cannot sleep," said Daisy. "My dreams feel too small. Your seeds fly far on the wind and dream big dreams. Mine stay right here."

Dilly laughed a soft, rustly laugh. "Oh, Daisy," she said. "My seeds fly far because the wind carries them. But every seed begins exactly where you are — small, and waiting, and full of hope. Watch."

A warm night breeze drifted over the meadow. One by one, Dilly's seeds let go and floated up — up, up — like tiny lanterns drifting over the silver grass.

But one little seed did not fly. It slipped off its parachute and plopped onto the hard, dusty path, where the earth was too packed for roots to grow.

"Oh no," said Daisy. "That one will never find a home."

She cupped the tiny seed in her paws, soft as holding a snowflake, and carried it to a patch of dark, crumbly earth beside the hazel tree. She pressed it in with the tip of her nose, tucked a leaf over it like a blanket, and patted the soil smooth.

"There," she whispered. "A soft bed for a small dream."

Dilly swayed above her. "You see, Daisy?" she said. "You helped a dream find its home. That is a very big thing."

Daisy yawned a long, sleepy yawn. She climbed back into her nest, curled around the grey feather, and closed her eyes.

That night, Daisy dreamed. She dreamed she was light as a seed, floating over moonlit meadows on a warm wind. She drifted over a silver river, over sleeping fields, over treetops that shimmered in the moonlight. And all the while, far below, her little seed slept snug in its soft bed of earth.

When morning came, Daisy woke with a smile. Her dreams had never felt small again. They had been big all along — because a dream that helps something grow is the biggest dream there is.

The end. 🌙
