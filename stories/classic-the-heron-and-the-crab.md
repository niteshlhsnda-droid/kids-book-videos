# The Heron and the Crab

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Beside a quiet green pond lived a tall grey heron named Hina. She had long, skinny legs and a pointy beak.

In the pond lived a group of tiny silver fish: Mina and Rina and ever so many more. All day they darted and splashed among the reeds.

One morning, Hina stood at the edge of the pond and sighed a big, heavy sigh.

"Oh dear, oh dear," she said. "This little pond is drying up. Soon there will be no water left at all."

The little fish popped their heads up. "No water?" squeaked Mina. "But we need water to swim in!"

"Do not worry," said Hina, with a sneaky little smile. "I know a big, deep pond on the other side of the hill. It is cool and safe. I can carry you there, one by one, in my beak."

So Hina picked up Mina in her beak and flew off over the hill. But she did not fly to the big pond at all. She flew to a hollow log and tucked Mina inside it, out of sight.

Then she did the same with Rina.

One by one, Hina carried the fish away and hid them in the log. She planned to keep them tucked away as snacks for later. The fish were not hurt at all, but they were squished together in the dark log, feeling frightened and far from home.

At last, only one friend was left in the pond: Chiku the crab. Chiku was small and round, with clever eyes. He had been watching Hina, and something did not feel right.

"Hina," said Chiku, "it is my turn now. May I hold on to your neck while you fly, so I do not slip?"

"Of course," said Hina, thinking only of her snack. "Climb aboard!"

So Chiku climbed up and held on gently to Hina's long neck. Up they flew, over the reeds and over the hill.

But Chiku was looking all around with his clever eyes. He did not see a big, deep pond anywhere. He saw only the hollow log — and inside it, all his frightened friends, wiggling and whispering, "Help! Help!"

"Oh no!" said Chiku. "Hina, you did not take us to the big pond at all! You tricked us! Fly back and let everyone go."

But Hina shook her head and swooped down toward the log, to tuck Chiku in with the rest.

So Chiku gave her neck the tiniest, gentlest pinch — just a little squeeze, like a tickle, to startle her. "Let us go, please!" he said.

"Oh! Oh my!" cried Hina, startled. Her beak popped open. Down tumbled Chiku — plop! — right into their own pond below.

Hina flew back to the hollow log and carried every fish safely home to their own pond. Mina, Rina, and all their friends splashed back into the cool water with happy cries.

"Thank you, Chiku!" they sang. "You saved us!"

Hina landed softly on the bank. Her face was red with shame.

"I am so sorry," she said. "I tricked you, and that was wrong. I will never, ever trick anyone again. Will you forgive me?"

"We forgive you," said the fish, "if you promise to be honest from now on."

"I promise," said Hina.

That night, the pond was quiet and kind again.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever, kind friend can stop a trick — and saying sorry can make things right again.*
