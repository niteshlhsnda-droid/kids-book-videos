# Suri the Squirrel Shares One Acorn

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Suri was a red squirrel who lived in a tall oak tree in Willowmere Wood. She loved acorns — she collected them every day, hid them in hollows, and counted them every night. One, two, three, all the way to twenty. Her acorns were her treasure.

One chilly morning, her friend Chip the chipmunk knocked. Knock knock knock.

"Hello, Suri!" said Chip. "I am so hungry. My store is empty. May I have just one acorn?"

Suri felt nervous. Her tummy twisted. What if she ran out? What if winter was long?

"I need them all," she whispered.

Chip's smile faded. He turned to go.

Suri felt sad. She did not like seeing Chip hungry.

Suri's grandmother had told her something. "Sharing makes the happy grow," Grandma used to say.

"Sharing makes the happy grow." Suri said it out loud.

"Sharing makes the happy grow." It became her brave thought.

"Sharing makes the happy grow."

Suri looked at her pile of acorns — twenty acorns. She picked the biggest, shiniest one.

"Wait, Chip!" she called. "Here is an acorn for you."

Chip's eyes went wide. "Really? For me?"

Suri nodded. She felt scared, but she smiled.

Chip took a big crunchy bite. "Mmm! Delicious!" he said.

Suri felt warm. She felt happy. Sharing felt better than keeping.

Can you count acorns with Suri? One shiny acorn for Chip. One for you, one for me. Count them: one, two, three!

Chip shared his blackberry with Suri. They ate together on the branch. Crunch, munch, giggle.

Suri gave Chip one more acorn. She still had eighteen left — plenty for winter.

"Sharing makes the happy grow," they sang. And it was true. Their happy grew and grew.

That evening, the sky turned pink and gold. Suri curled in her leafy nest while Chip slept in the hollow below, tummy full.

"Thank you for sharing," he whispered up.

"You are welcome," yawned Suri.

She counted her acorns one last time. Eighteen, safe and sound. Her heart felt fuller than her store.

And Suri drifted into a cozy, happy sleep.

Sleep now, little Suri.

The end. 🌙
