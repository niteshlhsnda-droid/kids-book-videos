The Fox and the Grapes

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One hot summer afternoon, a clever little fox named Fern was trotting through the meadow, feeling very hungry.

Her tummy rumbled. "Rumble, rumble," it said. "I would love something sweet and juicy right now."

Just then, Fern looked up — and what did she see?

High above her head, a grapevine curled around an old wooden trellis. Hanging from the vine were the biggest, roundest, purplest grapes Fern had ever seen. They glowed in the sunshine like little jewels.

"Oh, how lovely!" whispered Fern. "Those grapes look perfect for a hungry fox."

Fern stood on her tiptoes and stretched up her nose. Sniff, sniff. The grapes smelled sweet and summery. But they were far too high to reach.

"Never mind," said Fern. "I am a very good jumper."

She took three steps back, wiggled her tail, and — BOING! — she jumped as high as she could.

She missed by a whisker.

Fern tried again. She ran a little faster this time and jumped even higher. BOING!

Still too short. The grapes swung gently in the breeze, just out of reach, as if they were teasing her.

"One more try," puffed Fern. She climbed onto a tree stump, balanced very carefully, and leaped with all her might.

Plop! She landed back on the grass, and the grapes were still up there, smiling in the sun.

Fern sat down and fanned herself with her tail. Her legs were tired, and her tummy was still rumbling.

"Well," she said with a sniff, "those grapes are probably sour anyway. I bet they would make my mouth pucker. I do not want sour grapes."

And with her nose in the air, Fern trotted away down the path.

But as she turned the corner, she heard a tiny voice. "Peep! Peep!"

A small brown bird was sitting on a low bush, pecking at something dark and shiny.

"What have you found there, little bird?" asked Fern.

"Blackberries!" chirped the bird. "Sweet, ripe blackberries. There are plenty for two."

Fern's eyes lit up. The blackberries were low and easy to reach. She picked one with her teeth and popped it into her mouth.

"Mmm!" she said. "Sweet and juicy!"

The little bird pecked a berry, and Fern nibbled a berry, and together they munched happily in the shade until their tummies were full.

"Thank you, little bird," said Fern, licking her lips. "These berries are just right."

As the sun began to set, Fern trotted home with a happy heart. She had not reached the grapes, it was true. But she had found something sweet after all — and a new friend to share it with.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: It is easy to call sour what we cannot reach — better to enjoy the good things close at hand.
