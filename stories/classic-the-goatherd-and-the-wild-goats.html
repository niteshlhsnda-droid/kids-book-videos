The Goatherd and the Wild Goats

A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, on a green hillside, there lived a kind goatherd named Arun.

Every morning, Arun climbed the hill with his ten little goats. "Baa! Baa!" they sang, skipping after him with their bells going tinkle-tinkle-tinkle.

Arun knew every one of his goats by name. He knew which one liked the sweetest grass, and which one liked to nap in the sunniest spot.

Every morning, Arun counted his goats before they set off. "One, two, three…" he would say, tapping each little head. And the smallest goat would climb right into his lap and bleat, "Baa!" — because she loved Arun best of all.

One cold afternoon, dark clouds rolled over the hill. The wind began to howl. "Whooo! Whooo!"

"Rain is coming!" cried Arun. "Quick, little goats! Into the cave!"

Arun and his goats hurried into a big, dry cave. Just then, a herd of wild goats came trotting down the hill. They were wet and shivering.

"Please," said the biggest wild goat, "may we shelter in your cave too?"

"Of course," said Arun. "There is room for everyone."

That evening, Arun shared his supper. He gave the wild goats the soft hay he had brought for his own goats. He gave them the sweetest apples too.

"Eat well, friends," he said. "Tomorrow the sun will shine again."

The wild goats ate and ate. "What a kind goatherd!" they whispered. "We would love to stay here forever!"

But Arun's own little goats went to bed with empty tummies. They looked at Arun with sad, sleepy eyes.

"I'm sorry, my dears," whispered Arun. "Tomorrow I will feed you first, I promise."

The next morning, the sun came out, warm and golden. The wild goats shook the rain off their coats.

"Thank you for the shelter and the supper!" they said. And off they trotted, up over the hill, without even a goodbye.

Arun looked at his own little goats. Their tummies rumbled. Their bells tinkled softly, like a sigh.

"Oh, my little ones," said Arun. "I gave your supper to new friends, and forgot the friends who follow me every day."

He hurried down the hill to the meadow and picked the softest, greenest grass he could find. Then he gave each of his goats a warm mash of oats and a crunchy apple.

"Munch, munch, munch!" went the goats, and their bells went tinkle-tinkle-tinkle, happy again.

From that day on, Arun always shared what he had — but he fed his own dear goats first. And whenever the wild goats passed by, he waved and smiled.

"Hello, friends!" he called. "There is always a little hay to share — after my own goats have had their supper!"

And his goats knew they were loved best of all.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Share kindly with new friends — but never forget the old friends who love you every day.
