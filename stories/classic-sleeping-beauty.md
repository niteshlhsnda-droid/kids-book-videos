# Sleeping Beauty

*A beloved fairy tale, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a kingdom of rolling hills, there lived a king and queen who wished for one thing more than anything in the world — a child.

And at last, their wish came true. A baby princess was born, with rosy cheeks and wide blue eyes. They named her Aanya, which means "grace."

The whole kingdom rejoiced! Bells rang, and the king and queen held a grand feast. They invited all the good fairies of the land, who came in shimmering gowns and blessed the baby with gifts — kindness, laughter, and a singing voice sweet as a flute.

But there was one fairy they had forgotten to invite — an old, grumpy fairy who lived alone on a misty hill. When she heard about the feast, her feelings were hurt, and hurt feelings can turn even a fairy grumpy.

She swept into the hall uninvited, and in a crackly voice she said, "When this princess grows up, she shall prick her finger on a spindle — a sharp little stick for spinning thread — and fall into a sleep of a hundred years!"

Everyone gasped. But the youngest fairy, who had not yet given her gift, stepped forward gently.

"I cannot undo what has been spoken," she said softly, "but I can soften it. The princess will not be alone in her sleep. And when a hundred years have passed, she will wake — as if from the loveliest dream."

The king ordered every spindle in the kingdom to be put away. And for sixteen years, Princess Aanya grew up climbing trees, singing to the birds, and sharing her laughter with everyone she met.

But on her sixteenth birthday, while wandering through the castle, Aanya found a tiny forgotten room at the top of a winding stair. What do YOU think was waiting inside? Inside sat an old woman, humming as she spun thread on a spindle.

"What is that?" asked Aanya, curious.

Before the old woman could answer, Aanya reached out — and pricked her finger on the sharp little spindle.

At once, her eyelids grew heavy. She sank onto a soft velvet bed — and fell into the deepest, sweetest sleep.

And the youngest fairy's gift came true: all over the castle, everyone fell asleep too — the king and queen on their thrones, the cook with her spoon in the air, the horses in the stable, even the little birds on the windowsills.

Around the castle grew a great hedge of roses and thorns, tall and thick, to keep the sleepers safe. And there the castle slept… for a hundred years.

Many princes heard the tale and tried to push through the thorns, but the hedge would not let them pass.

Then, exactly a hundred years later, came a young prince with a gentle smile, who had heard the tale of the sleeping princess. As he approached, the roses leaned aside — and the thorns parted like a curtain, as if the hedge itself had been waiting for him.

He climbed the winding stair and found the little room. There lay Princess Aanya, peaceful as moonlight, not a day older.

Very gently, the prince whispered, "Aanya, time to wake up," and kissed her forehead as softly as a goodnight kiss.

Aanya's eyes fluttered open. "What a lovely dream I have had," she whispered.

And all through the castle, everyone woke up — the king stretched, the cook's spoon clattered, and the birds burst into song, as if no time had passed at all.

The prince and Aanya became the dearest of friends, and that very night the whole castle fell into the softest, sweetest sleep, dreaming the loveliest of dreams.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kindness and patience can wake even the deepest sleep.*
