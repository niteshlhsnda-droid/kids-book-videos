Ziggy the Zebra Counts His Stripes

A cozy original bedtime tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On the soft grass, the silver moon was rising.

Little Ziggy the zebra lay beside his mama, tucked into the tall warm grass.

But Ziggy's legs kept on wiggling.

"Wiggle, wiggle, wiggle," went his legs, all on their own.

"I can't sleep, Mama," Ziggy whispered. "My legs won't be still."

Mama Zebra blinked slowly, her ears flopping softly against her head, and smiled. She hummed a low lullaby that rustled like dry grass bending in the breeze.

"Then let's play the stripe game," she said, low and warm. "We'll count your stripes, one by one, until your eyes feel sleepy."

Ziggy giggled. Counting his own stripes! What a funny idea.

Stripe one, on his nose. Sniff, sniff. That smelled like the morning grass, cool and a little bit wet.

Stripe two, on his ear. Flap, flap. That sounded like the morning wind, singing through the tall leafy trees.

Stripe three, on his neck. That was the thumping race with his best friend Pia, the baby giraffe. Her long spotted legs flashed beside him as they ran.

They ran so fast their hooves went clip-clop, clip-clop, all the way down the hill. Pia won, and Ziggy cheered for her anyway.

Stripe four, on his shoulder. Splash! That was the rain puddle he jumped into after lunch — plip, plop, plip! — and Mama laughed in big, happy giggles.

Stripe five, on his tummy. Rumble. That was the crunchy lunch of leaves, shared with all his friends under the shady tree.

Stripe six, on his other shoulder. That was the butterfly that landed there. Tickly, tickly.

Stripe seven, on his back. That was the afternoon nap, when the sun soaked toasty-warm into his stripes and his breath went slow and soft.

Stripe eight, on his ribs. That was the cool mud squishing soft between his hooves at the water hole.

Stripe nine, on his hip. That was the soft, sweet smell of rain on warm dust.

Stripe ten, near his tail. That was the dry grass whispering shhh-shhh as the herd wandered home.

Stripe eleven — the last stripe he could count, on his swishy tail. That was the evening, this very moment, snuggling beside Mama in the tall grass.

Each stripe was a little picture from his happy day, and Ziggy's wiggling legs were starting to feel still and sleepy.

Ziggy's mouth opened in a big, stretchy yawn. "Mama… I'm running out of stripes."

"Then look up," Mama whispered, snuggling close, "and count the stars winking in the night sky."

One star, twinkling like a tiny lantern.

Two stars, blinking hello.

Three stars… scattered like sugar crystals across the dark blue sky.

Ziggy's eyes grew heavier and heavier. His breathing slowed — slow… slow… slow.

Four stars… five…

And before he ever reached six, little Ziggy was fast asleep, breathing slow and deep beside his mama.

The moon poured its light over them, and the grass hummed a lullaby.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A cozy counting game can turn wiggly legs into sleepy legs.
