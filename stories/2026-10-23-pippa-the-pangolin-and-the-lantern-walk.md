# Pippa the Pangolin and the Lantern Walk

On the edge of the sal forest, where the river bent like silver ribbon, lived a little pangolin named Pippa. Pippa had warm copper scales that shone when the sun touched them, big soft eyes, and a very shy heart.

Whenever another animal looked at her, Pippa would curl into a tight round ball, tucking her nose under her tail. She was not being rude — she just felt safest that way, like a secret.

Tonight was the Lantern Walk. Once a year, when the moon grew round, every little animal in the forest carried a paper lantern down to the firefly glade. Pippa watched the others from behind a fern and wished, deep inside, that she could go too.

"Would you like to come?" squeaked Chirpy the cricket, who had stopped right beside her. Pippa curled a little smaller. "They will all look at me," she whispered.

Chirpy nudged a tiny lantern toward her with one long leg. "Then let them look," he chirped softly. "Your scales are the prettiest thing in the forest."

Pippa's mama walked with her as far as the old banyan tree, humming a low, sleepy song. Under the lantern light, Pippa's scales glowed like copper pennies. Mama smiled and turned back home, and Pippa walked on alone — small, brave, and still a little bit shy.

At the mossy bridge, the whole forest seemed to be waiting. Rabbits and mice and owls held lanterns of every colour. Pippa froze. All those eyes! She curled into a ball beneath the bridge and would not come out.

Something tiny and warm sat down beside her. It was Glow, the little glow-worm, who was even smaller than Pippa. "I like it here under the bridge," said Glow. "Would you like me to sit with you?"

Pippa peeked one eye out. Glow did not stare. Glow did not laugh. Glow just glowed, soft and green, beside her. Slowly — very slowly — Pippa uncurled.

When she reached the glade, the fireflies rose all at once, thousands of them, like a second sky come down to the grass. "Hold up your lanterns!" called the old owl. Pippa lifted hers high. The light danced on her copper scales until she shone like a lantern herself.

"Look at Pippa!" whispered the mice. "She's beautiful."

Pippa felt the looking — and this time it did not hurt. It felt like warm honey. She danced a slow, careful dance in the firefly light, her little feet pattering on the grass.

When the walk ended, Pippa curled up cozy in her burrow, her lantern beside her, still warm. She had been seen — and the forest was kind.

Sleep now, little Pippa.

The end. 🌙
