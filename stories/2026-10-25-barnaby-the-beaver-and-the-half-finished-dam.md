# Barnaby the Beaver and the Half-Finished Dam

Down by the slow, silver river lived a small beaver named Barnaby. He had soft brown fur, a flat tail that went pat-pat-pat on the mud, and two front teeth that were just right for nibbling sticks.

All day long, Barnaby had been building a dam. He stacked sticks across the river, one after another, patting each one snug with his paws. The dam was nearly finished — only a little gap was left in the middle, just wide enough for a leaf to float through.

"I will finish it before bedtime," Barnaby said to himself, and he hurried off to fetch one more stick.

But the evening was getting golden, and then pink, and then a soft, sleepy blue. The moon peeked over the tall reeds. Barnaby's paws felt heavy. His eyes blinked slowly, like sleepy butterflies.

A tiny firefly floated down and twinkled beside him. "Hello, Barnaby," she whispered. "The stars are coming out. Are you still working?"

"Just one more stick," Barnaby yawned. "Then one more after that."

He picked up a long stick and carried it to the dam. But his paws were so tired that the stick slipped — splop! — right into the water. It floated away down the river, spinning slowly like a little boat.

Barnaby sat down on the riverbank with a big sigh. His friend Olive the otter slid up beside him, her whiskers twinkling with river drops.

"You look sleepy, Barnaby," Olive said kindly. "The dam will still be here in the morning."

"But it's not finished," Barnaby whispered.

Olive smiled and pointed at the river. "Look," she said. "The river isn't in a hurry. It just flows, slow and steady, all night long."

Barnaby watched the water. It curled around the sticks of his dam, gentle and patient, making a soft shhhh sound. The moon made a silver path on the water. The fireflies twinkled like tiny lanterns. Everything was quiet and unhurried.

"You know what?" Barnaby said, rubbing his sleepy eyes. "I think my paws are telling me something. They are telling me to rest."

So Barnaby waddled home to his cozy lodge. He tucked himself into his bed of soft leaves, pulled a leaf blanket up to his chin, and listened to the river singing outside.

The dam waited quietly in the moonlight, half-finished and perfectly fine. The fireflies kept watch, twinkling over the water. And somewhere in the dark, an owl hooted a soft, sleepy goodnight.

Sleep now, little Barnaby.
