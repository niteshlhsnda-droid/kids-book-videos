Nasruddin and the Lamp

A funny Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little town with twisty lanes lived a funny, clever man named Nasruddin.

Everyone in the town loved Nasruddin — because he always made them laugh, and his laughter always hid a little lesson.

One evening, Nasruddin's neighbor, young Sameer, came running to his house.

"Nasruddin chacha! Nasruddin chacha! I have lost my silver ring! It fell somewhere in my courtyard. Please help me find it!"

"Of course, my boy," said Nasruddin. "Let us look together."

They went out into the dark lane. And what did Sameer see?

Nasruddin was crawling on his hands and knees — under the tall street lamp!

"Chacha," said Sameer, puzzled, "my ring fell in my courtyard. Why are you searching under the street lamp?"

Nasruddin looked up and smiled his big, silly smile. "Because, my boy, the light is much better here!"

Sameer giggled. Then he stopped. Then he thought.

"Wait..." said Sameer slowly. "Even if the light is better here, my ring is not here. It is in my dark courtyard. We will never find it under this lamp!"

"Exactly!" cried Nasruddin, jumping up and clapping his hands. "You are a clever boy, Sameer! We always look where it is easy — not where the thing truly is."

So they took the lamp down from its post — well, Nasruddin carried it carefully — and they went to Sameer's dark courtyard together.

They searched behind the flower pots. They searched under the wooden bench. They searched near the little neem tree.

And there — glinting in the lamplight — was the silver ring, hiding under a fallen leaf!

"Found it!" cheered Sameer, slipping the ring back on his finger.

That night, Sameer told everyone in the town, "If you lose something, don't just look where the light is bright. Look where you lost it!"

And Nasruddin winked at the children and said, "Remember — easy looking is not always true looking."

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Look for things where they truly are — not just where it is easy to look.
