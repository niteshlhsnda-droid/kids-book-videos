# The Lion's Whisker

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green valley in Ethiopia, there lived a kind woman named Almaz.

Almaz loved her husband, Bekele, very much. But lately Bekele had grown cross and quiet. He hardly smiled at all.

"I wish my husband would smile again," said Almaz to her old friend Abba, the wise man of the village. "Can you make a potion to bring back his smile?"

Abba stroked his long white beard. "I can," he said. "But first you must bring me one thing — a single whisker from a living lion."

Almaz's eyes went wide. "A whisker from a LIVING lion? Oh, Abba! How can I do that?"

"You are brave and patient," said Abba. "Go and try."

The next morning, Almaz walked to the edge of the valley, where the great lion lived in a cave among the rocks.

She was very frightened. But she did not go close. She put down a bowl of meat, far away from the cave, and she went home.

The next day, she came back with another bowl of meat, and she put it down a little bit closer.

Day after day, Almaz came back. Each day she put the bowl a little bit closer to the cave. And each day the great lion came out, sniffed the meat, and ate it, while Almaz sat quietly and sang a soft little song.

After many days, the lion did not run away when he saw her. He sat and ate his meat and looked at her with his big golden eyes.

One golden morning, the lion walked right up to Almaz and let her scratch behind his ear — very, very gently.

At last, one golden evening, as the lion lay beside her in the grass, Almaz reached out with two fingers — plip! — and pulled out one single whisker.

The lion blinked. Then he yawned a great big yawn and went to sleep in the sun.

Almaz ran all the way back to the village and gave the whisker to Abba. "Here it is!" she cried. "Now make the potion!"

But Abba smiled, and he dropped the whisker into the fire. It went up in a little puff of smoke.

"Oh!" cried Almaz. "Why did you burn it?"

"Because there is no magic in a whisker," said Abba. "The magic was in you. You were patient with a great wild lion, day after day, until he trusted you."

"Now go home," said Abba, "and be just as patient with your husband as you were with the lion. Give him time. Give him kindness. And his smile will come back, all by itself."

Almaz went home. She was patient with Bekele. She listened to him. She sang her soft little song as she cooked his dinner.

And slowly, day by day, Bekele began to smile again. First a little smile. Then a bigger smile. Then one evening, he laughed — a big, happy laugh that filled the whole house!

Almaz smiled too. Abba was right. Patience is the very best magic of all.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Patience and kindness can tame even the wildest heart.*
