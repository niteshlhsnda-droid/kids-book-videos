# The Donkey and the Lapdog

*Aesop's fable, retold gently for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

On a sunny little farm lived a kind farmer, his donkey Dusty — who trotted to market every day with baskets piled high — and a tiny lapdog named Pip.

Dusty was big and grey, with long ears and a steady trot. Every day he carried baskets of vegetables to the market, clip-clop, clip-clop, never complaining.

Pip was no bigger than a slipper, with soft white fur and eyes like shiny buttons. He did no work at all. His job was simply to be cuddled.

Every evening, the farmer would sit on his porch, and Pip would leap into his lap. The farmer would scratch behind Pip's ears and say, "Who's my good little fellow?"

And everyone in the house would coo, "Oh, Pip! You darling little dog!"

Dusty watched from the field, his ears drooping lower and lower as a hot grumble tightened in his chest.

"I work hard all day long," he thought. "I carry heavy baskets in the hot sun. And does anyone scratch behind MY ears? No! It is not fair. I want cuddles too!"

The next evening, Dusty made a plan. He waited until the farmer sat on the porch. Then he trotted up — clip-clop, clip-clop — wagged his long tail the way Pip wagged his little one, and tried to leap into the farmer's lap.

Now, a slipper-sized dog can leap into a lap. A donkey cannot.

CRASH! went the stool. BANG! went the basket of mangoes, rolling everywhere. The farmer tumbled backward into the hay. Dusty's big grey head rubbed against his chest, and his long ears flopped in his face.

For a moment, everyone stared.

Then the farmer started to laugh — and his wife laughed too, and Pip went yap-yap-yap with excitement, chasing a rolling mango.

"Oh, Dusty!" chuckled the farmer, sitting up in the hay and wrapping his arms around the donkey's sturdy neck. "You dear, silly fellow! Did you want cuddles too?"

Dusty nodded his big head, and his long ears wobbled.

The farmer scratched behind Dusty's ears — first one, then the other. "Listen, my strong friend. Pip is loved for being small and cuddly. And YOU are loved for being big and strong and steady. The farm could not run without you. I love you just as you are."

Dusty felt warm all the way down to his hooves. The grumbly feeling melted away like morning mist.

From that evening on, the farmer always saved a special ear-scratch for Dusty after the baskets were unloaded. And Dusty never tried to sit on anyone's lap again — though sometimes, just sometimes, he rested his big soft nose on the farmer's shoulder, and that was cuddle enough.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: You are loved just as you are — there is no need to be someone else.*
