# Zola and the Moon's Night Visit

On the edge of a still blue lake there was a little village of round huts with thatched roofs. At dusk, when the sky turned the color of a ripe mango, the children would run down to the water, laughing and splashing, while the grown-ups lit the cooking fires.

Little Zola lived in the smallest hut, with her grandmother. Zola loved the lake, and the fireflies, and the smell of rain on warm earth. But when the children sang their evening songs, Zola only moved her lips. Her voice was small and shy, and it hid inside her like a seed in a pod.

Every night, when the songs faded, the village would look up at the moon. The moon hung round and silver above the lake, watching silently. "She is listening," her grandmother would whisper, "so sing her something true."

One night, as the drums went quiet and the last song drifted away, something wonderful happened. The moon began to grow bigger and brighter. She slipped down from the sky, slow as a falling feather, until her silver glow touched the tops of the palm trees. The whole village gasped.

The moon spoke in a voice like cool water over stones. "I have watched you laugh and dance and tell stories," she said. "Tonight, I have come to greet you."

The children danced. The elders told tales of old heroes. Drums beat like happy hearts. Everyone laid mangoes and woven mats at the moon's feet as a gift, and the moon smiled, and her smile made the lake shine like a mirror.

Zola stood at the very edge of the crowd, holding her grandmother's hand. She wanted to give the moon something too, but her voice was still hiding.

Then the moon leaned closer — closer than anyone had ever seen her — and looked right at Zola. "I hear you," said the moon softly. "Every night, after the songs, you hum me your own little tune. It is the sweetest sound in my whole sky."

Very quietly, she began to hum — the soft, sleepy tune she hummed to the fireflies. One by one, the village fell silent and listened. Then the drums picked up the tune, and the children clapped, and the grandmothers swayed, until Zola's small hum had become the happiest song the lake had ever heard.

The moon closed her shining eyes and listened, learning the song by heart. Then she rose slowly back into the sky, carrying Zola's tune with her, and everyone waved goodbye.

From that night on, whenever Zola hummed her little tune, the moon hummed it back, silver and soft across the water. Zola was never afraid of her small voice again. Even the tiniest hum can be a gift big enough to light up the sky.

Sleep now, little Zola.
