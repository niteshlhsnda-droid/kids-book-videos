Birbal and the Honest Merchant

A clever Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the great city of Agra, there lived a kind merchant named Arjun.

Arjun sold rice in the market, and everyone loved him, because his rice was always clean and his measures were always true.

One morning, Arjun went to the palace to sell rice to the royal kitchen. He put his rice on the palace scales, and the scales said ten kilos.

But when Arjun took the same rice home and weighed it on his own scales, it said only eight kilos!

"Oh!" said Arjun. "Something is wrong. The palace scales must be rigged!"

Arjun was an honest man, and he did not want to be quiet about a wrong thing. So he went to the court of King Akbar and bowed low.

"Your Majesty," he said, "the palace scales are not true. My rice weighed ten kilos there, but only eight kilos at home."

The king frowned. "That is a serious matter," he said. "Birbal, my clever minister, will you find out the truth?"

"I will, Your Majesty," said Birbal, with a smile.

Birbal took a bag of rice and went to the palace scales. He weighed the rice, and the scales said ten kilos.

Then Birbal took the same bag of rice to Arjun's shop and weighed it again on Arjun's scales. The scales said eight kilos!

Birbal smiled, and he tapped the palace scales with his finger. "Ah, I see," he said. "These palace scales are rigged. A little weight has been hidden under one side, so everything weighs more than it truly does."

The king called for the man who kept the scales. He was a sly fellow, and he had been rigging the scales so that the palace paid for more rice than it got, and he kept the extra money for himself.

"Is this true?" asked the king sternly.

The sly fellow hung his head. "Yes, Your Majesty," he whispered. "I am sorry. I was greedy, and I cheated the honest merchants."

"You must say sorry to Arjun," said the king, "and you must pay back double everything you took."

"I will, Your Majesty," said the fellow. "I will pay back every grain, twice over."

And he did. He paid back double to Arjun, and double to every merchant he had cheated.

Then the king turned to Arjun and smiled. "Thank you, honest merchant," he said. "Because you spoke the truth, no one will be cheated again."

Birbal patted Arjun on the shoulder. "An honest heart," he said, "is worth more than all the gold in the palace."

Arjun went home that evening with a happy heart. The market was fair again, the scales were true, and his name was known all over Agra as the honest merchant who helped the king.

That night, Arjun sat with his family under the stars, and he told them the whole story, and they all smiled, for truth had won the day.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: An honest heart is worth more than gold.
