# The Talking Pot

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Deep in a green forest lived a kind old woodcutter named Sam.

Sam was not rich. His hut was small, his supper was simple — but his heart was big, and he always shared what he had.

One rainy evening, on his way home, Sam heard a tiny voice from behind a bush.

"Please... please take me home. I am so lonely."

Sam pushed the leaves aside — and there sat an old clay pot! It was chipped and dusty, but its little voice was as clear as a bell.

"A talking pot!" laughed Sam. "Well, no one should be lonely. Come home with me."

Sam tucked the pot under his arm and carried it through the rain — pitter-patter, pitter-patter — all the way to his little hut.

That night, Sam shared his simple supper with the pot. "I only have a little bread and soup," he said, "but half of it is yours."

"Thank you, Sam," said the pot warmly. "No one has ever shared with me before."

From that evening on, the pot talked to Sam every night — funny stories, little songs, and all the forest news. Sam was never lonely again.

One dark night, while Sam slept — snore, snore, snore — a sly fox crept into the hut. Sniff, sniff. It wanted to steal Sam's bread!

But the pot saw everything. It banged its lid — CLANG, CLANG, CLANG! — and shouted in its biggest voice, "THIEF! THIEF! WAKE UP, SAM!"

The fox jumped a mile high and ran, ran, ran into the forest, its tail between its legs!

Sam woke up and hugged the pot tight. "You saved my bread, little friend!"

And the pot glowed with happiness. "You were kind to a lonely pot," it said. "Kindness always comes back — like an echo in the forest."

So Sam and the talking pot lived happily ever after — sharing suppers, singing songs, and never, ever lonely.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Be kind to the lonely — kindness always comes back, like an echo.*
