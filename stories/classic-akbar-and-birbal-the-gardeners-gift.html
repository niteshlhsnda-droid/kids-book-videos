Akbar and Birbal: The Gardener's Gift

A merry Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in the great city of Agra, there lived a poor gardener named Chotu.

Chotu's garden was very small — just a little patch of green behind his mud house. But he loved his garden the way you love your favorite toy. Every morning he watered his plants and sang to them softly.

One spring, Chotu planted melon seeds. He watered them, shaded them from the hot sun, and whispered, "Grow big and sweet, my little ones."

And oh! One melon grew bigger and rounder and sweeter than any melon Chotu had ever seen. It was as round as the full moon. When Chotu tapped it, it sang, "Tuk-tuk-tuk!" And it smelled like honey and sunshine.

Chotu smiled a big smile. "This melon is too good for me," he said. "I will give it to Emperor Akbar!"

So early the next morning, Chotu lifted the big melon onto his head and walked all the way to the palace. The road was long and dusty. His feet grew tired. But Chotu held the melon steady and sang his little garden song all the way.

At the tall, golden palace gate, some of the courtiers saw him — a poor gardener with a dusty melon on his head. "Ha-ha!" a few of them snickered. "What a silly gift for a great emperor!"

But Chotu did not mind. He bowed low before the Emperor and said, "Your Majesty, I grew this melon in my little garden with all my love. It is for you."

Emperor Akbar smiled a warm smile. He took the melon in both hands. He cut it open — and oh, the inside was pink and juicy and sweet as honey!

Akbar ate one piece, then another. "Delicious!" he said. "Chotu, this is the sweetest melon I have ever tasted."

Then Akbar clapped his hands. "Bring me a bag of gold coins!" he said. And he gave the whole bag to Chotu. "I reward the love, not the gift," said the Emperor.

Chotu's eyes shone like stars. He bowed and bowed, and went home singing.

Now, one of the courtiers had watched all this, and a jealous grumble grew in his chest. "A poor gardener got a bag of gold for a melon?" he thought. "If I give the Emperor something COSTLY, he will give me much, much more!"

So the very next day, the courtier brought a shining jewel — red as a rose, bright as the sun. He laid it before the Emperor and waited, rubbing his hands and smiling greedily.

Emperor Akbar looked at the jewel. Then he looked at the courtier. Then he picked up the gardener's melon — there was still some left — and gave it to the courtier!

The courtier's smile melted away. "But, Your Majesty… my jewel…"

Birbal, who stood beside the throne, smiled his wise, gentle smile. "The Emperor rewards love, not price," he said softly.

The courtier hung his head. But Akbar was kind. He patted the courtier's shoulder. "Bring me a gift of love tomorrow," said the Emperor, "and we will all share this sweet melon together."

And so, in the cool of the evening, they all sat together and shared the sweet, pink melon. And everyone agreed it was the sweetest melon in all the land — because it had been grown with love.

That night, as the stars came out over Agra, Chotu the gardener fell asleep in his little house, smiling about his moon-round melon… and dreaming sweet, honeyed dreams.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: It is the love behind a gift that makes it precious.
