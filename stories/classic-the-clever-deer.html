The Clever Deer

A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green forest, beside a cool, clear stream, there lived a clever deer named Chanda.

Chanda had soft brown fur and bright eyes. She could run very fast, but she liked to think even faster.

Every morning, Chanda came to the stream to drink the cool water and nibble the sweet, soft, green grass.

One morning, a hunter named Arjun walked into the forest. He carried a big net and a basket.

"I will catch the clever deer today," he said to himself. "I will lay my net by the stream and wait."

He spread his big net on the soft grass near the water and hid behind a big tree.

Soon, Chanda came hopping down to the stream. She was thirsty, and her mouth was dry.

But before she took one sip, she stopped.

"What is this?" she thought. "The grass looks flat and lumpy, like something is hiding underneath."

Chanda sniffed the grass, then smiled a small, clever smile.

"A net," she whispered. "Someone has laid a net for me."

But Chanda did not run away. She had an idea.

She walked up to the net and said, "Oh, what a lovely soft bed! I think I will lie down and rest."

Then she lay down on the safe edge of the net and closed her eyes.

The hunter behind the tree frowned. "Why is she lying there?" he whispered. "She is not in the middle of my net!"

So Arjun waited. And waited. And waited.

Chanda opened one eye a tiny bit and peeked at the tree. She could see the hunter's foot tapping on the ground.

"Poor hunter," she thought. "He is tired of waiting. Let me help him."

Chanda stood up, stretched, and walked to the middle of the net and lay down again.

"Now!" cried the hunter, jumping out. He pulled the rope with all his might.

But Chanda was quicker. The moment the rope moved, she leaped high over the net and landed softly.

The net fell flat on the empty grass.

Arjun stood there, holding the empty rope, his mouth wide open.

Chanda laughed. "Good morning, friend," she said. "Your net is strong, but my eyes are quicker than your rope."

The hunter looked at the deer. Then he looked at his net. Then he started to laugh too.

"You are the cleverest deer I have ever met," he said. "I chased you all morning, and twice you were too clever for me."

Chanda stepped closer. "Then let us be friends," she said. "I will show you where the sweetest grass grows, and where the stream is coolest. You need never lay a net again."

Arjun smiled. "I like that plan," he said. He folded up his net and put it in his basket.

From that day on, they were the best of friends. Every morning, Arjun brought a basket of sweet apples, and Chanda shared the coolest, greenest grass.

And in the evenings, they sat by the stream and watched the fireflies dance, soft and gold.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Cleverness and kindness together can turn even a hunter into a friend.
