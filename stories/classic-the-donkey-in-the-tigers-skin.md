# The Donkey in the Tiger's Skin

*A wise old Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a small village, there lived a washerman and his donkey, Dabbu.

Every morning, the washerman piled heavy bundles of clothes onto Dabbu's back and led him clip-clop down to the river. Every evening, he led him home again at dusk. But the washerman fed Dabbu very, very little — just a handful of dry grass.

"Eat less, work more," the washerman would say.

So poor Dabbu's tummy was always rumbling. Grrr-growl. "Oh," sighed Dabbu, "I wish I could eat sweet green grass till my tummy is round and full."

One evening, Dabbu was trotting past a hunter's hut when he saw something lying in the dust — a big, stripy tiger skin! The hunter had left it out in the sun to dry.

Dabbu looked at it. He looked left, he looked right. And then — an idea popped into his head like a soap bubble! He wriggled and wiggled until the tiger skin sat snug upon his back.

"Now I look just like a tiger!" he giggled.

That night, the moon poured silver light over the fields, and the crickets sang their night songs. Dabbu clip-clopped quietly into the farmers' green fields, all dressed in the tiger skin.

A farmer sitting outside his hut saw the stripy shape in the moonlight and gasped, "A TIGER! Run, everybody, run!"

Close your eyes — can you see the stripy shadow moving in the moonlight?

The farmers ran all the way home, fast as the wind. And Dabbu smiled his biggest donkey smile and munched and munched the sweet green grass. Munch, munch, munch!

Night after night, he came back. Night after night, the farmers ran away. And Dabbu grew fat and round and happy, and his tummy never rumbled at all.

One night, the moon was big and round like a silver plate. Dabbu was munching happily when — from across the field — he heard a lovely sound. A lady donkey was braying softly to her friend.

Dabbu's long ears shot straight up. His heart went pitter-patter. Oh, how he missed his donkey friends! Before he could stop himself, he lifted his head high and brayed — "HEE-HAW! HEE-HAW!"

The farmers stopped running. They looked at each other. "Did that tiger just say... hee-haw?" said one farmer, scratching his head.

They picked up their walking sticks and crept closer. Dabbu saw them coming — and ZOOM! Off flew the tiger skin, and Dabbu ran home as fast as his little legs could carry him. Not one stick touched him.

The farmers looked at the empty tiger skin lying in the grass, and they laughed and laughed. "It was only Dabbu the donkey, all along!"

Dabbu ran all the way home and hid behind his master's haystack. His heart was thumping like a little drum. "Oh," he panted, "pretending to be a tiger was fun, but it could not last. I am just Dabbu — and just Dabbu is quite enough."

From that night on, Dabbu never wore the tiger skin again. The washerman saw how hard his donkey worked, and from then on, he gave Dabbu a little more sweet green grass every single day.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Pretending to be someone else never lasts — just be yourself.*
