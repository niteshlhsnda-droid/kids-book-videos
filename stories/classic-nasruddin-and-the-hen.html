Nasruddin and the Hen

A funny Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little village with whitewashed walls and a crooked old fig tree, there lived a man named Nasruddin. He was clever, he was kind, and he was sometimes — just sometimes — a little bit silly.

Nasruddin had a small hen with bright orange feathers and a comb like a tiny red crown. Her name was Chutki.

Every single morning, as the sun peeped over the fig tree, Chutki would lay one warm brown egg in her nest of straw.

One egg. Every morning. Without fail.

Nasruddin loved those eggs. He would pick up the warm egg, smile wide, and say, "Good morning, Chutki! You are the finest hen in the whole wide world."

He fried the egg for breakfast. *Sizzle, sizzle.* He shared it with his wife. He was a happy man.

Then one morning, Nasruddin sat under the fig tree, thinking.

"One egg a day is good," he said to himself. "But what if... what if Chutki laid TWO eggs a day? Two eggs! That would be wonderful!"

He scratched his head. He twirled his mustache. He had a plan.

"If I feed her double grain," he said, "she will lay double eggs! That is how it must work!"

So that evening, Nasruddin filled Chutki's bowl to the very top with golden grain. Heaps of it. A mountain of grain!

"Eat up, Chutki," he said, beaming. "Eat, eat! Tomorrow there will be TWO eggs!"

Chutki blinked at the mountain of grain. *Cluck,* she said, politely.

And because Chutki was a good hen, she ate. And ate. And ate. She pecked and pecked until her little tummy was round as a melon. *Peck, peck, peck.*

Then she waddled — oh, how she waddled! — into her straw nest, flopped down, and fell fast asleep. *Snore, snore, little hen snore.*

All night, Nasruddin dreamed of eggs. Two eggs! Maybe three! Maybe a whole basket!

The next morning, he rushed to the nest, rubbing his hands.

"Good morning, Chutki! Show me my TWO eggs!"

He looked in the straw. He looked behind the straw. He looked under the straw.

There were... no eggs at all.

Not one. Not two. Not even half an egg.

"Oh," said Nasruddin, very quietly.

Chutki opened one sleepy eye. *Cluck?* she said, as if to say, "I told you so."

Just then, the neighbors' children came skipping past the fig tree on their way to the well.

"Nasruddin! Nasruddin!" they giggled. "Did your hen eat all the grain?"

"Did she lay TWO eggs?" they teased. "Or did she eat them before they were born?"

The children laughed and laughed, and poor Nasruddin's cheeks turned as red as Chutki's comb.

He bent down to Chutki and gave her a gentle pat.

"Dear Chutki," he said, "I am sorry. I was greedy, and I stuffed you like a festival dumpling. One egg a day is already perfect. You are already perfect."

Then he filled her bowl — not too much, just the right amount — with her favorite sweet corn.

Chutki pecked the corn happily. *Peck, peck.* She fluffed her feathers. *Fluff, fluff.* And she settled into her straw nest, light and easy and happy.

That night, Nasruddin dreamed of one egg. Just one. And that was plenty.

The next morning, the sun peeped over the fig tree. Nasruddin walked to the nest, soft as a whisper.

And there it was — one warm brown egg, perfectly round, waiting in the straw.

Nasruddin picked it up and laughed out loud. "Chutki! You are back!"

Chutki clucked proudly. *Cluck-cluck!*

And every morning after that, there was one egg — one perfect egg — right on time. Nasruddin never fed her double grain again.

From that day on, whenever Nasruddin wanted more of something, he would smile and say, "Careful! Remember Chutki and the two eggs!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Be happy with what you have — being greedy can take away even the good things.
