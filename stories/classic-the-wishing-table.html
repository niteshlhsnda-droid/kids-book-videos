The Wishing Table

A classic Grimm fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, in a little village beside a green wood, lived three kind brothers named Nikhil, Vikram, and Aryan.

Nikhil was the eldest, Vikram the middle, and Aryan the youngest. They were poor, but kind, and they shared everything they had.

One day, the three brothers set out into the wide world to seek their fortune.

Nikhil came to a little cottage in the wood, where a kind old man lived.

"Please, sir," said Nikhil, "may I work for you?"

"You may, kind boy," said the old man. "Work for me for a year, and I will give you a wonderful gift."

Nikhil worked hard for a whole year, and the old man gave him a little wooden table.

"Thank you, sir," said Nikhil, "but how will a table help me?"

"This is no ordinary table," said the old man. "Just say, 'Table, set yourself!' and it will fill with the finest food!"

Nikhil tried it at once. "Table, set yourself!" he cried — and pop, pop, pop! The table filled with bread and butter, soup and cake, and sweet milk!

Meanwhile, Vikram had worked on a farm for a whole year, and the farmer gave him a little grey donkey.

"Thank you, sir," said Vikram, "but how will a donkey help me?"

"This is no ordinary donkey," said the farmer. "Just say, 'Donkey, give me gold!' and it will give you shiny gold coins!"

Vikram tried it at once. "Donkey, give me gold!" he cried — and clink, clink, clink! Gold coins tumbled from the donkey, bright as the sun!

And Aryan, the youngest, had worked for a miller for a whole year, and the miller gave him a little wooden cudgel in a sack.

"Thank you, sir," said Aryan, "but how will a cudgel help me?"

"This is no ordinary cudgel," said the miller. "If ever a thief troubles you, just say, 'Cudgel, out of the sack!' and it will tap the thief ever so gently until he says sorry."

The three brothers met again on the road home, so happy to show each other their gifts!

But that night, they stopped at an inn, and the greedy innkeeper stole the magic table and the gold donkey while the brothers slept, and hid them in his cellar!

In the morning, Nikhil and Vikram were very sad. "Our gifts are gone!" they cried.

But Aryan smiled. "Do not worry, brothers," he said. "I still have my cudgel."

Aryan went to the innkeeper and said, "Cudgel, out of the sack!"

Out jumped the little cudgel, and it tapped the innkeeper ever so gently — tap, tap, tap!

"Ow! Stop, please stop!" cried the innkeeper. "I am sorry! I stole your table and your donkey!"

He ran to his cellar and brought back the table and the donkey, and gave them to the brothers with a deep bow.

"I will never steal again," he promised.

The three brothers forgave him, and they went home together. That evening, Nikhil said, "Table, set yourself!" and the table filled with food, and the whole family sat down to a grand feast, sharing food and laughter under the stars.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness is the truest magic of all.
