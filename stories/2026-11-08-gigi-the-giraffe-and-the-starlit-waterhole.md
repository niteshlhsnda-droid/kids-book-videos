# Gigi the Giraffe and the Starlit Waterhole

On the wide, golden savanna, where the acacia trees stood tall, lived a young giraffe named Gigi, with soft brown spots and small, fuzzy horns that wiggled when she laughed.

Every evening, as the sun sank low, Gigi looked up at the stars. They twinkled high above her, and Gigi wished with all her heart that she could see them up close.

"If only I were taller," she sighed. "Then I could almost touch them."

Gigi was the shortest giraffe in her herd. Her mother reached the top leaves, and her grandfather could see for miles. But Gigi could only reach the middle branches, and the stars stayed far, far away.

One evening, Gigi had an idea. She climbed onto a smooth, flat rock and stretched her long neck up, up, up, wiggling her fuzzy horns. But the stars were still far away.

"Still too far," she said, and climbed down with a little sigh.

Just then, Zuri the oxpecker fluttered down and landed on Gigi's back. "What are you reaching for?" she chirped.

"The stars," said Gigi. "I want to see them close. But even on the rock, I am too short."

"Grandpa Giraffe is the tallest of all," said Zuri. "Ask him. He knows everything about the sky."

So Gigi trotted to the biggest acacia tree, where Grandpa Giraffe stood. "Grandpa," she asked, "can you touch the stars?"

Grandpa Giraffe chuckled, a deep, warm sound. "Oh, little one," he said, "the stars are far away for everyone — even for me. But I will tell you a secret. They come closer than you think. You only have to look in a different place."

"A different place?" said Gigi. "Where?"

Grandpa Giraffe smiled and nodded toward the waterhole. "Go and see. Lie down low, and look."

Gigi padded to the waterhole, where the water lay still as glass. She folded her long legs and lay down on the soft grass at the edge. And then she looked down.

There, in the water, the stars were shining — hundreds of them, twinkling right beside her. The moon floated on the water like a silver boat. Gigi gasped. The whole night sky was in the waterhole!

"Oh!" she whispered. "They came down to me!"

Zuri fluttered down beside her. "Look, Gigi! You found them!"

Soon the whole herd came and lay down beside her, watching the stars swim in the water, close and bright and beautiful.

"You were right, Grandpa," said Gigi, snuggling against his long, warm neck. "I didn't need to be taller. I only needed to look down low."

And as the stars twinkled above and below, Gigi closed her eyes and drifted off to sleep, dreaming of a whole sky in a waterhole.

Sleep now, little Gigi.

The end. 🌙
