# The Frogs Who Rode the Snake

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green pond full of lotus flowers lived Mendhak Raja, the round, clever king of the frogs.

One day, a snake came sliding to the pond. His shiny scales had gone dull, and he slid along slow, slow, slow — too slow to catch mice — and his tummy rumbled.

"I am your friend," said the snake to the frogs. "Friends don't hunt friends! Hop onto my back, little ones — I will give you rides around the pond!"

Hmm… did that smile look a little *too* sweet to you?

The little frogs cheered, "WHEEE!" as the snake slid around the pond. Can you cheer along with them? WHEEE! One by one, they climbed onto his long, shiny back and rode around — and one by one, they all hopped safely home again.

But Mendhak Raja watched carefully. He looked at the old snake's smile — stretched a little too wide to be real. And under a big lotus leaf, little Tiddly was watching too. "I don't like his smile," she whispered. "It's too sweet."

Mendhak Raja was clever. He understood the snake's sneaky plan — before a single frog got hurt.

He gathered all the frogs together. "Listen, my friends! This snake is no friend of ours. He smiles sweetly — but a sweet smile can hide a not-so-sweet plan! From today, no more rides. Stay in the deep water, where he cannot reach!"

The frogs plip-plopped down, down into the cool dark water, where the lotus roots tangled thick as curtains. The old snake waited… and waited… but not a single frog came for a ride again. All he got for his waiting was the rumbliest tummy in the whole pond. It gave one last sleepy grumble… and then was quiet.

And that night, all the frogs snuggled into the soft green pondweed — safe, and sleepy, and snug. Every single one of them.

Goodnight, Mendhak Raja. Goodnight, little Tiddly. The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A sweet smile can hide a not-so-sweet plan — trust actions, not just words.*
