# Momotaro, the Peach Boy

*A Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green valley in Japan, there lived a kind old man and a kind old woman. They lived in a little house beside a clear, chuckling river.

One sunny morning, the old woman went to the river to wash clothes. The water sparkled, and the birds sang.

Splash! Splish! Down the river came a peach — but not a small peach. It was the biggest, roundest, pinkest peach anyone had ever seen! It bobbed along like a rosy boat.

"Oh my!" said the old woman. "What a wonderful peach!"

She carried it home. That evening, she and the old man were about to cut it open for supper — when suddenly, crack! The peach split open all by itself!

And inside, fast asleep, was a tiny baby boy, round and rosy as the peach itself.

They gasped, then laughed with joy. "A baby boy!" they cried. "A gift from the river!"

They named him Momotaro, which means "Peach Boy," and they loved him with all their hearts.

Momotaro grew up strong and kind. He helped carry firewood and water. He shared his rice with the hungry, and he always said please and thank you.

One day, Momotaro heard that far across the sea, on Ogre Island, some big ogres had taken pots, rice, and toys from the villages nearby.

"Grandmother, Grandfather," said Momotaro, "I will sail to Ogre Island and kindly ask the ogres to give everything back."

The old woman packed her best rice dumplings — soft, round, and sweet. "Share these," she said. "Kindness opens every door."

So Momotaro set off and met a dog, wagging his tail.

"Woof! Where are you going?" asked the dog.

"To Ogre Island, to ask the ogres to be kind," said Momotaro. "Would you like a rice dumpling?"

"Yes, please!" said the dog, munching happily. "I will come with you!"

Next, they met a monkey, swinging from a branch. "Ooh! Ooh! Where are you going?"

"To Ogre Island," said Momotaro, sharing a dumpling. The monkey nibbled it and grinned. "I will come too!"

Then down flew a pheasant — a bright wild bird — with feathers bright as a rainbow. "Where are you going?" she asked.

"To Ogre Island," said Momotaro, sharing a dumpling. "I will come too!" chirped the pheasant.

So the four friends sailed across the sea to Ogre Island.

There stood the ogres — big and tall, with frowny faces. "GRRR!" they rumbled. "Who comes to our island?"

Momotaro smiled and held out his bag. "Hello!" he said. "My name is Momotaro. I brought rice dumplings for everyone. May we sit and talk?"

The ogres sniffed the dumplings. They had never been offered anything so kindly. Slowly, their frowny faces softened. They sat down and tasted one dumpling, then another. "Mmm!" they rumbled. "These are delicious!"

Momotaro said gently, "The villages miss their pots, their rice, and their toys. Will you give them back, and be friends instead?"

The ogres looked at one another. Their hearts felt warm, as a heart feels after a kind word and a good meal. "Yes," said the biggest ogre softly. "We will give everything back. And we will be kind from now on."

So the ogres carried back every pot, every bag of rice, every toy. And then everyone sat down for a great big feast on the beach, sharing dumplings and laughing as the sun went down.

Momotaro sailed home to hugs from his grandmother and grandfather. And from that day on, the ogres and the villagers were the best of friends.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Kindness and sharing can turn even grumpy hearts into friends.*
