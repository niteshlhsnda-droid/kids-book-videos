# Mira and the Starlit Coins

In a small cottage at the edge of a great forest lived a little girl named Mira, an orphan who owned very little — a piece of bread for supper, a warm cap, a pretty bodice, a soft skirt, and a plain linen shirt. That was all, but she had a big, warm heart.

One evening, as the sun dipped low and the sky turned apricot, Mira wrapped her shawl around herself and set out into the forest. She loved the quiet there, and the way the fireflies blinked hello.

She had not gone far when she met an old man sitting by the path. His cheeks were pale, and his stomach rumbled loudly in the hush of the trees. Mira broke her bread in half and pressed it into his trembling hands. The old man smiled, and his eyes crinkled at the corners like a happy paper fan.

Farther along, a little boy crouched beneath the ferns, shivering in the evening chill. His head was bare, and the wind tugged at his ears. Mira took off her warm cap and tucked it snugly over his head. He giggled and hugged her knees, a sound sweeter than any songbird's.

Deeper in the forest the path grew hard to see. There Mira found a small girl in a thin, torn frock, hugging her arms and trying not to cry. Mira slipped off her pretty bodice and helped her into it, buttoning each button with gentle fingers. The little girl twirled once, twice, and her tears turned into laughter.

By now the forest was very dark. Mira had given away her skirt too, to a boy who had none, and kept only her plain linen shirt. The night air prickled her arms, but she kept walking, humming her mother's lullaby.

At the very heart of the forest, she heard a small, sad sniffle. Behind a twisted oak sat the tiniest child of all, shaking with cold in only a scrap of cloth. Without a moment's pause, Mira pulled off her last shirt and wrapped it around the little one, who sighed and leaned against her, warm at last.

Mira stood in the dark with nothing left but her shawl and her smile. Above her, the deep blue sky was thick with stars. Then the most wonderful thing happened. One star twinkled, wobbled, and fell — and as it touched the ground, it turned into a small golden coin!

Then another, and another! Stars rained down all around Mira, each one turning into a bright, gleaming coin as it landed. She spread her shawl wide and caught as many as she could, laughing in wonder.

When morning came, Mira walked home with her shawl heavy with star-coins — riches beyond dreaming. And every day after that, she shared them with everyone who needed anything, just as she always had.

Sleep now, little Mira.

The end. 🌙
