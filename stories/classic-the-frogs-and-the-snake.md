# The Frogs and the Snake

*A gentle Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a soft green pond, under a tall mango tree, there lived a happy family of frogs.

The littlest frog was Pip, green as a new leaf, with bright eyes and a big, bouncy hop.

Every morning, Pip and his friends sang their happy song together. "Ribbit, ribbit! Croak, croak!" they sang, and the pond rippled with their giggles.

They played hide-and-seek among the round lily pads.

One warm afternoon, the pond went very quiet.

A long, shiny snake named Sam slid through the tall grass toward the pond.

"Oh no!" whispered Pip, hiding behind a big green leaf. "A snake is coming!"

Sam the snake was hungry, and his tummy rumbled like a little drum. "Rumble, rumble," went his tummy.

"I smell frogs," said Sam, in a low, soft voice. "Little green frogs, hiding in the pond."

Pip peeked out from behind his leaf. He saw Sam's long shadow in the water, and an idea popped into his head, bright as a bubble.

"Friends," Pip whispered, "do not hop away. Let us play a clever trick."

Pip hopped up onto a flat stone and called out in his bravest voice, "Hello, Mr. Snake! Look down at the pond! There are hundreds of frogs down there!"

Sam looked down. The pond was still and smooth, like a silver mirror.

And in the mirror of the pond, Sam saw his own long, shiny face looking back at him.

"Where are the frogs?" asked Sam, blinking his sleepy eyes.

"Down there!" cried the frogs together. "So many frogs, swimming round and round!"

Sam dipped his nose into the cool water. Splash!

"Why are they all so long and shiny?" Sam wondered aloud.

"They are the biggest frogs in the whole wide world!" said Pip, trying very hard not to giggle. "Quick, catch them before they swim away!"

Sam slid round and round the pond, chasing his own face in the water. He swam this way, then that way, then round again, in a big, dizzy circle.

His tummy still rumbled, but his head was spinning. "This is too hard," Sam said, with a great big sigh. "These frogs are far too quick for me."

Then Pip called out, in his kindest voice, "Mr. Snake, why not eat the sweet mangoes that fall from the tree?"

Sam looked up. A ripe mango plopped down into the grass, right beside him. Plop!

"Oh," said Sam, smiling. "That does smell sweet."

He picked up the mango and slithered away, slow and sleepy, under the tall grass.

The frogs peeked out from behind their leaves. Then they peeked out a little more.

"He is gone!" cried Pip, and he gave a happy hop, high into the air.

"Ribbit, ribbit! Hooray!" sang all the frogs together, splashing and dancing in the pond.

That evening, the moon rose over the quiet pond. Pip tucked himself under a soft lily pad and closed his bright eyes.

"We did not run away," he whispered, "and we did not hurt anyone. We were just clever."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever, kind idea can solve a big problem — without anyone getting hurt.*
