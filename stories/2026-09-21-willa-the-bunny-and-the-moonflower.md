# Willa the Bunny and the Moonflower

*A bedtime story for ages 4–7 · about 2 minutes to read aloud*

Willa was a small brown bunny with long, wiggly ears, and she lived in a cozy burrow under the old apple tree. In her garden grew one tiny moonflower bud — her very favorite thing in the whole world. Every morning she whispered, "Please open today?" And every morning the bud stayed shut, snug as a button. Willa huffed, Willa puffed, and her ears went all sideways with impatience.

"Be patient, little one," said Mama Rabbit, pouring Willa a cup of warm chamomile tea. "Some wonderful things take their own sweet time." So Willa tried very hard to wait. She counted every snail in the garden (twelve!), sang a song about carrots, and taught a ladybug to do somersaults.

That evening the sky turned the color of blackberry jam, and the first stars peeked out one by one. Willa tiptoed to the flower bed in her polka-dot pajamas, letting out the biggest yawn. And there it was — the moonflower was opening, petal by petal, glowing soft and silvery in the dark!

"Ohhh," whispered Willa, too amazed to feel sleepy anymore. The flower smelled like honey and moonbeams, and it seemed to wink right at her. She curled up beside it and smiled the sleepiest smile. Mama was right — waiting had made the surprise even sweeter.
