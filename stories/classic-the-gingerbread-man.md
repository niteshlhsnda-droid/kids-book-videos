# The Gingerbread Man

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a little cottage at the edge of a village, lived a kind old grandmother named Granny Rose.

One morning, Granny Rose mixed flour and honey and ginger and rolled out a delicious gingerbread man. She gave him two currant eyes, a cherry smile, and three shiny buttons.

"Now, little gingerbread man," she said, "bake nicely in the oven."

But when Granny Rose opened the oven door — POP! Out jumped the gingerbread man! He was warm and golden and full of beans.

"Run, run, as fast as you can! You can't catch me, I'm the gingerbread man!" he sang, and off he ran out of the cottage door.

Granny Rose chased him, waving her wooden spoon. "Come back, little man!" she laughed. But the gingerbread man was too quick.

Soon he met a cow in a meadow. "Stop, little gingerbread man! You look delicious!" mooed the cow.

"Run, run, as fast as you can! You can't catch me, I'm the gingerbread man!" sang the gingerbread man, and he ran right past her.

Then he met a horse in a field. "Stop, little gingerbread man! You look delicious!" neighed the horse.

"Run, run, as fast as you can! You can't catch me, I'm the gingerbread man!" sang the gingerbread man, and he ran right past him too.

Then he met a farmer, a pig, and a little hen. Each one cried, "Stop! You look delicious!" And each time the gingerbread man sang his song and ran on by.

At last the gingerbread man came to a wide, sparkling river. He could not swim, and there was no bridge. "Oh no!" he said. "How shall I ever get across?"

Just then, a sly old fox named Felix trotted up. "Good morning, little man," said Felix with a smile. "Would you like a ride across the river? Hop onto my back, and I will carry you safely over."

The gingerbread man thought for a moment. The fox did look friendly. "Very well," he said, and hopped onto the fox's back.

Splash, splash went the fox through the river. Soon the water grew deeper. "The water is rising, little man," said Felix. "Hop onto my nose, so you stay dry."

The gingerbread man hopped onto the fox's nose. But as they reached the far bank, Felix gave a great big SNEEZE — "Aaa-choo!"

And WHOOSH! The gingerbread man flew off the fox's nose, over the grass, and landed — plop! — right in a soft pile of hay beside the riverbank.

"Thank you for the ride, Mr Fox!" laughed the gingerbread man, brushing off the hay. "But I think I will walk from here!"

And before the fox could say another word, the gingerbread man sang his song one last time — "Run, run, as fast as you can! You can't catch me, I'm the gingerbread man!" — and ran all the way home to Granny Rose.

Granny Rose was waiting with a glass of cold milk. "There you are, you naughty, speedy little man," she said, hugging him gently. "Now sit down and tell me all about your adventure."

And the gingerbread man did, crumb by golden crumb, while the evening sun went down.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Quick feet are fun, but a clever head — and knowing when to say no to a stranger's ride — keeps you safest of all.*
