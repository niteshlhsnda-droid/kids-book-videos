# The Hound and the Hare

*A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, on a farm at the edge of a green meadow, there lived an old hound named Bruno.

Bruno had been a fine, fast hound when he was young. He could run like the wind! His paws went thump-thump-thump across the meadow, and no hare could ever run away from him.

But now Bruno was old. His muzzle was grey. His legs were slow. And his favourite place in the whole world was a warm blanket by the fire.

One morning, his young master said, "Come on, Bruno! Let's chase the hare that nibbles my lettuces!"

Bruno wagged his tail and trotted out into the meadow. There was the hare — a quick, brown hare with long ears and twitchy whiskers!

"There it is!" cried the master. "After it, Bruno!"

Bruno ran as fast as he could. His old legs went thump… thump… thump. But the hare was quick as a flash — hop, hop, hop! — and it darted away over the hill.

Bruno stopped, puffing and panting. "Huff… huff… huff."

His master frowned. "Bruno! You used to catch every hare in the meadow! What happened to you?"

Bruno sat down in the soft grass. He looked up at his master with his big, brown, honest eyes.

"I remember when I was a pup," he said softly. "I could run so fast that the hares would call me the wind! I chased them all day, and at night I slept by your fire, tired and proud."

"Dear master," he said, "when I was young, I ran like the wind, and I caught every hare. But now my legs are old and slow. My heart is still brave — but my legs cannot run as fast as they used to."

The master's face went soft. "Oh, Bruno," he said. "I forgot. You gave me your fastest years. You chased a hundred hares for me. And now I scolded you for being old."

He knelt down and wrapped his arms around Bruno. "I'm sorry, old friend," he said. "You did your very best — and your best was wonderful."

Then the master led Bruno back to the farmhouse. He gave him a big bowl of warm supper. He gave him a soft, warm blanket by the fire.

And as for the hare? It came back the next day and nibbled one lettuce — just one — and the master laughed.

"Let him have it," he said, patting Bruno's head. "We have had a hundred lettuces, and one old friend is worth more than all of them."

That night, Bruno slept by the fire, warm and loved, dreaming of the days when he ran like the wind.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Love your old friends — they gave you their very best years.*
