The Two Fishes and the Frog

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, there was a round, green pond full of soft reeds and sleepy water lilies.

In that pond lived two little fishes — Tinku and Rinku — and their very best friend, a little green frog named Fifi.

Every morning, the three friends played together. They raced around the lily pads. They blew bubbles that went pop, pop, pop. And when the sun went down, they sang a croaky, splashy good-night song.

One evening, Fifi was sitting on a lily pad, listening to the quiet pond, when she heard voices from the bank. Two fishermen were standing there with their big nets.

"Tomorrow morning," said one fisherman, "we will come back and catch all the fish in this pond!"

Fifi's heart went thump, thump, thump. She hopped down into the water as fast as she could.

"Tinku! Rinku!" she cried. "The fishermen are coming tomorrow! We must swim away to the big river tonight, while the moon is still up!"

Tinku's eyes went wide. "Oh, thank you, Fifi! Let us go right now!"

But Rinku laughed. "Ha! Do not be silly, Fifi. This pond is our home. The fishermen will never catch me. I am too quick! I will stay right here."

Fifi tried again. "Please, Rinku. The nets are big, and the fishermen are clever. Come with us!"

"No, no," said Rinku, flicking his tail. "I am staying. Good night!"

So Tinku swam off with Fifi, down the little stream and all the way to the big, wide river, where the water was deep and safe.

The next morning, the fishermen came back with their nets. Splash! Splash! They cast the nets over the pond. Poor Rinku swam this way and that way — but the net was too big, and he was scooped up into a basket.

"Oh no!" cried Rinku. "Fifi was right! I should have listened!"

Fifi had been watching from the reeds, her little heart thumping. She hopped, hop, hop, hop, all the way to the fisherman's little house, where his kind daughter was playing in the garden.

"Croak! Croak!" said Fifi. "Little girl, little girl! Your father has caught my friend Rinku in his basket. Please, please help him!"

The little girl looked at the frog, and then she looked at the basket. Inside it, poor Rinku was flopping and gasping.

"Oh, Papa!" she said. "This little fish is too small and too pretty to keep. May I put him back in the pond?"

The fisherman smiled. "You have a kind heart," he said. "Yes, you may."

So the little girl carried the basket to the pond and tipped it gently. Splash! Rinku swam out, happy and free.

"Thank you!" he cried. "Thank you, little girl! And thank you, Fifi!"

That very evening, Rinku swam down the stream to the big river, where Tinku and Fifi were waiting.

"I am sorry I did not listen," he said. "You were right, Fifi. A wise friend's warning is a gift."

"And a friend who says sorry is a friend indeed," said Fifi, and the three friends played together until the stars came out.

That night, they sang their croaky, splashy good-night song — louder and happier than ever before.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When a wise friend warns you, listen — and if you forget to listen, it is never too late to say sorry.
