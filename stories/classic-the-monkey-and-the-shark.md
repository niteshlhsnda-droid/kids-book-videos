# The Monkey and the Shark

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, on a warm island where the sea was as blue as the sky, there lived a little monkey named Miko.

Miko lived in a tall mango tree, right beside the sea. Every morning, he picked the ripest, sweetest mangoes, and sat on a branch to eat them, swinging his long tail.

One morning, a big grey shark named Sahil swam up to the shore.

"Good morning, little monkey!" called Sahil, poking his nose out of the water. "What are you eating? It smells so sweet!"

"Good morning, Mr. Shark!" called Miko. "These are mangoes! They are the sweetest fruit in the whole world!"

"May I try one?" asked Sahil, with a hopeful smile.

Miko thought for a moment. Then he plucked the biggest, ripest mango and tossed it — plop! — into the water.

"Yum!" said Sahil, munching happily. "That is the best thing I have ever tasted! You are a kind monkey, Miko. Will you be my friend?"

"Yes!" said Miko, clapping his hands. "Let us be friends!"

And so, every morning, Miko came to the shore with a mango for Sahil, and they talked and laughed together. Miko told stories about the trees. Sahil told stories about the deep blue sea.

They were the very best of friends.

Then one morning, Sahil said, "Miko, my friend, would you like to come and visit my home? The Sultan of the Sea is having a big feast, and I would love for you to meet him!"

"Oh, I would love to!" said Miko. "But I cannot swim!"

"Do not worry," said Sahil. "Climb onto my back, and I will carry you. Hold on tight!"

Miko climbed onto Sahil's broad grey back, and off they swam — splash, splash — across the blue, blue sea.

Halfway across, Sahil stopped. He looked very, very sad.

"Miko," he said quietly, "I have something to tell you. The Sultan of the Sea is not well. He says only a monkey's heart can make him feel better. And — oh, I am so sorry, my friend — he has asked me to bring him yours."

Miko's heart thumped. Thump, thump, thump. He was far from shore, in the middle of the sea, on a shark's back!

But Miko was a clever little monkey. He took a deep breath and smiled.

"Oh, Sahil, my friend!" he said. "Why did you not tell me before we left? You see, monkeys do not carry their hearts inside them all day. I left my heart back home, hanging in my mango tree to keep it safe!"

"Oh!" said Sahil, looking worried. "Then what shall we do?"

"Why, take me back to my tree, of course!" said Miko. "I will fetch my heart, and then we can swim to the Sultan together!"

Sahil turned around at once and swam all the way back to the shore.

The moment they reached the shallow water, Miko jumped off Sahil's back and scampered up his mango tree — up, up, up — to the very highest branch.

"Thank you for the ride, Sahil!" he called down, laughing. "But my heart stays right here, inside me, where it belongs! And from now on, I will bring your mangoes to the shore, just as before — but I will never, ever climb onto a shark's back again!"

Sahil looked up at Miko and smiled a small, sorry smile. "You are a clever monkey, Miko. And a kind one, too. I am sorry I tricked you. Let us still be friends — from the shore."

"From the shore," agreed Miko, and he tossed Sahil the ripest mango of all.

And so they stayed friends — Miko in his tree and Sahil in the sea — sharing mangoes and stories across the sand.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A quick mind can save the day — and a true friend never asks you to give away your heart.*
