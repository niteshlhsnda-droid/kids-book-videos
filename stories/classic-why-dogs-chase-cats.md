# Why Dogs Chase Cats

*A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a little village with red roofs and a round green hill, there lived a dog named Bruno and a cat named Cleo.

Bruno was big and brown, with floppy ears and a waggy tail. Cleo was small and silver-grey, with soft paws and a curly tail.

Bruno and Cleo were best friends. They shared biscuits on the doorstep. And every evening, they sat side by side and watched the sun go down behind the hill.

One sunny morning, Bruno had a lovely idea. He took a clean piece of paper and drew two little paw prints on it — one big, one small. Underneath, he wrote: "Best friends forever. We share everything."

"Here, Cleo," said Bruno, giving her the paper. "This is our promise-paper. Will you keep it safe for us?"

"Oh, Bruno!" said Cleo, her eyes shining. "It is beautiful. I will keep it safe as safe can be."

Cleo carried the promise-paper to her favourite spot by the garden fence. She laid it on a flat stone so the sunshine could fall on it. Then she skipped off to chase a butterfly.

Now, along came Gigi the goat, munching her way along the fence. Gigi loved nibbling green leaves. She nibbled this leaf, and that leaf, and then — flutter, flutter — she saw something white waving in the breeze.

"Oh! A leaf!" said Gigi. "A pale, papery leaf!"

And before she could look twice — nibble, nibble, nibble — Gigi nibbled the promise-paper right up.

Just then, Bruno came trotting back. "Cleo!" he called. "May I see our promise-paper? I want to show it to Gigi."

Cleo ran to the flat stone. The stone was empty. The promise-paper was gone.

"It was here!" said Cleo. "I left it right here on the stone!"

Bruno looked at the empty stone. His floppy ears drooped. His tail stopped wagging. "You lost it!" he said. "You lost our promise-paper! You did not care about it at all!"

"I did care!" said Cleo. "I did, I did!"

But Bruno was too upset to listen. "Come back here!" he cried, and off he ran after Cleo — round the garden, past the fence, and up the little hill.

Cleo ran and ran, her soft paws pattering. Bruno ran and ran, his big paws thumping. At last Cleo stopped at the top of the hill, out of breath.

"Bruno, please!" she panted. "Please stop chasing me and listen!"

Bruno stopped too, panting hard. He sat down with a thump.

"I am listening," he said.

"I did not lose your paper on purpose," said Cleo. "I left it on the stone, and when I came back, it was gone. I do not know where it went. But I am sorry it is gone. I loved it too."

Bruno looked at Cleo's sad face. His heart felt sad and heavy. "I am sorry I chased you," he said quietly. "I was upset, and I did not stop to ask what happened."

Just then, Gigi came hurrying up the hill, clip-clop, clip-clop. "Wait!" she called. "I know where the paper went!"

"You do?" said Bruno and Cleo together.

Gigi hung her head. "I nibbled it," she said. "I thought it was a pale leaf fluttering on the fence. I am very sorry. It was my mistake, not Cleo's."

Bruno's tail gave a slow, happy wag.

"Thank you for telling us the truth, Gigi," said Bruno.

"And thank you for stopping to listen, Bruno," said Cleo.

Then the three friends had a new idea. They found a fresh piece of paper, and together they made a brand-new promise-paper. Bruno drew a big paw print. Cleo drew a small paw print. Gigi drew a little hoof print. And underneath, Bruno wrote: "Best friends forever. We talk things over."

From that day on, whenever something went wrong, they did not chase and they did not blame. They sat down together, and they talked it over.

That evening, as the sun went down behind the hill, Bruno, Cleo, and Gigi sat side by side on the doorstep, quiet and happy. And one by one, they yawned big sleepy yawns and drifted off to sleep.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When something goes wrong, talk things over instead of chasing and blaming.*
