Yara the Yak and the Yawning Moon

A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

High, high up in the mountains, where the air is crisp and the snow sparkles like sugar, lived a young yak named Yara.

Yara was round and fluffy, with chocolate-brown wool so thick and soft that the mountain birds lined their nests with the tufts she left behind. Around her neck hung a little brass bell that went ting-ting whenever she trotted.

Every night, Yara climbed to her favorite flat rock, curled up in her own warm fluff, and fell asleep under the watchful moon.

But one night, something was different.

The moon looked pale and droopy. And as Yara watched, the moon opened its great round mouth and — YAAAAWN — let out the biggest yawn in the sky.

"Dear moon!" cried Yara. "Are you all right?"

"I am SO sleepy," sighed the moon, in a voice like a faraway lullaby. "But I cannot sleep. The mountain wind keeps whistling and whooshing past my ears. Whooo! Whooo! How is anyone supposed to rest?"

Sure enough, the wind came whistling down the valley — whooo-whooo! — rattling the little colored flags strung across the valley and swirling the snow.

Yara thought hard. Her wool was the warmest thing on the mountain. Her humming was the softest sound she knew. And her bell — well, her bell went ting-ting, which was cheerful, but not exactly sleepy.

Then she had an idea.

She climbed a little higher, to the very top rock — the closest spot to the moon. She fluffed up her wool until she looked like a big brown cloud. And she began to hum.

It was a low yak lullaby, the one her mother had hummed to her: "Hummm-hummm, hummm-hummm…"

The wind heard the humming and slowed down to listen. Whooo… became whoo… became a soft shhhh.

"Come closer, moon," whispered Yara. "Rest your tired face on my wool-cloud. It is the softest pillow in all the mountains."

The moon drifted a little lower — just a little — and settled its cheek against Yara's warm, fluffy back.

"Hummm-hummm," sang Yara.

"Shhhh," sighed the wind.

"Ting," went Yara's bell, very softly, like a tiny goodnight.

And the moon — the great round moon — closed its eyes and fell fast asleep, glowing soft and silver over the snowy peaks.

Yara grinned till her whole fluffy face crinkled, curled herself into the fluffiest ball she could, and fell asleep too — right there on the top rock, under the sleeping moon.

And if you look up at the mountains on a very quiet night, you might still see them: a fluffy yak and a sleepy moon, dreaming the same soft dream.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little kindness — and a soft song — can help anyone rest.
