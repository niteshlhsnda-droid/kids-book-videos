The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a quiet green field, under the roots of an old oak tree, lived a little country mouse named Maisie.

Maisie's home was small and cosy. She had a soft nest of dried grass, a cupboard full of wheat and barley, and a garden where sweet peas grew. Every evening she sat outside and watched the sun go down, and every night she slept safe and sound.

One day, Maisie invited her cousin Tilly, who lived in the big town, to come and visit.

Tilly arrived in a tiny coat with shiny buttons. She looked around Maisie's little home and wrinkled her nose.

"Dear cousin," said Tilly, "you live so plainly! Wheat and barley every day? In town, we eat cake crumbs and cheese and sweet jam! You must come and visit me, and see how fine life can be."

Maisie packed a little bag and set off with Tilly to the town. What a busy place it was! Carriages rumbled past, shops glittered with lights, and the streets smelled of fresh bread.

Tilly led Maisie into a grand house, up the stairs, and into a dining room. There, on a table covered with a white cloth, was the most wonderful feast Maisie had ever seen: crumbs of cake, bits of cheese, jelly, and even a dish of cream!

"Help yourself, cousin!" said Tilly, nibbling a cake crumb. "Isn't this splendid?"

Maisie had just taken a nibble of cheese when — WOOF! WOOF! — a big dog barked outside the door, and the whole table shook.

"Quick! Hide!" squeaked Tilly, and both mice darted behind a curtain, their hearts thump-thump-thumping.

When the dog had gone, they crept back to the table. Maisie had just reached for a jelly crumb when the door flew open and in came the cook with a broom!

"Scat! Scat!" cried the cook, and the two mice tumbled down the table leg and ran for their lives, squeaking all the way.

At last, safe behind a skirting board, Maisie caught her breath. "Tilly," she whispered, "your food is very grand. But I would rather eat my plain wheat in my quiet field, where no dog barks and no broom chases me."

The next morning, Tilly came to visit Maisie in the country. They sat outside under the oak tree and shared a simple supper of wheat, barley, and sweet peas, while the evening sun turned the field gold.

"This is peaceful," said Tilly softly, munching a pea. "I think I like it here, too."

And the two cousins laughed together as the stars came out, one by one, over the quiet field.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A simple meal in safety is better than a grand feast full of fear.
