Why the Sea Is Salt

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a land where the snow lay soft and white on the mountains, and the water below was cold and deep and blue, lived two brothers.

Ramu was rich and grumpy — his barns were full of grain, and his pockets full of coins. Shamu was poor and kind — but his heart was full of songs.

One winter, Shamu had no food left at all. So he walked to his brother's big house and knocked on the door.

"Please, brother," said Shamu, "may I have a little grain for my children?"

Ramu grumbled and complained, but at last he handed Shamu a small sack of barley.

On the way home, Shamu met an old traveller with kind eyes. "Where are you going, friend?" asked the traveller.

"Home to my hungry children," said Shamu, and he shared half his barley with the traveller, right then and there.

The traveller smiled. "You have a kind heart," he said. "Take this little hand-mill. It is magic. Say, 'Grind, little mill,' and tell it what you wish for. To make it stop, say, 'Stop, little mill.'"

Shamu hurried home. "Grind, little mill — grind warm rice!" he said.

Can you hear it? Whirr, whirr, whirr — warm, fluffy rice.

Every day the mill ground what they needed — wheat, lentils, sweet porridge. Soon the little house was full and happy.

When Ramu heard about the magic mill, his eyes grew wide with greed. "Sell it to me!" he demanded, and he paid Shamu a bag of gold for it.

But Ramu was in such a hurry that he forgot to ask the most important thing — how to make the mill STOP.

Ramu took the mill onto his boat. "Grind, little mill — grind salt! Salt sells for good money!"

Whirr, whirr, whirr! The mill ground white, sparkling salt, higher and higher — and no matter how Ramu shoveled, the little mill ground more.

"Stop!" shouted Ramu. "I said STOP!" But the little mill did not listen — it only listened to magic words. Whirr, whirr, whirr!

The boat grew so heavy that it tipped to one side — splash! — and the little mill slipped over the edge and sank, down, down, to the bottom of the sea.

Ramu rowed safely to shore. But the mill was gone.

Down at the bottom of the sea, in the soft dark sand, the little mill rests. It did its grinding long ago — and the salt it left behind is why, even today, the sea tastes salty.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Before you begin, learn how to end.
