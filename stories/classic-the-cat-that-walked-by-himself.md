# The Cat That Walked by Himself

*A playful Just So story by Rudyard Kipling, retold for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, when the world was new and wild, the Man and the Woman lived in a cave, and all the animals were wild too.

One day, the Woman sat by the fire and said, "I am tired of the wild. I want the animals to come and live with us, and be our friends."

So the Dog came first. He wagged his tail and said, "I will be your friend, and I will help you hunt."

"Then come," said the Woman, "and sit by our fire."

Then the Horse came. He tossed his mane and said, "I will be your friend, and I will carry you far and fast."

"Then come," said the Woman, "and eat from our hay."

Then the Cow came. She mooed softly and said, "I will be your friend, and I will give you warm, sweet milk."

"Then come," said the Woman, "and rest in our field."

The Dog, the Horse, and the Cow all came to live with the Man and the Woman. They sat by the fire. They ate the hay. They gave their milk. They were happy, happy friends.

But the Cat — oh, the Cat stayed out in the wild Wet Wild Woods, walking by himself, all places alike.

The Cat watched the cave from far away. He saw the warm fire. He saw the soft skins. He saw the bowl of warm milk.

"I like the look of that fire," said the Cat to himself. "And I like the look of that milk. But I will not be tamed like the Dog and the Horse and the Cow. I will come and go as I please, for I am the Cat that walks by himself."

So the Cat waited until night, when the moon was round and white. Then he padded — pad, pad, pad — on soft, silent paws, right up to the mouth of the cave.

"Mew," said the Cat, in his softest voice.

The Woman looked up. "Who is there?" she asked.

"It is I, the Cat," said the Cat. "May I come and sit by your fire? I am cold, and I am lonely, and I am very, very polite."

The Woman laughed. "You are a handsome cat," she said. "And you have such soft paws. Come in, Cat. You may sit by the fire — but only if you are kind to the little baby in the cradle."

"I will be kind," said the Cat, and he curled up by the fire, purring a deep, happy purr.

The baby in the cradle woke up and cried. The Cat jumped up softly and patted the baby with his gentle paw — pat, pat, pat — and sang a little purring song, until the baby smiled and fell fast asleep.

"Oh, thank you, Cat!" said the Woman. "You are a good friend after all. You may have a bowl of warm milk, morning and night, for ever and ever."

The Cat drank the warm milk, licked his whiskers, and curled up by the fire once more.

"Thank you," said the Cat. "But remember — I am still the Cat that walks by himself. I will come when I please, and I will go when I please. And all places will be alike to me."

And that is how it is, even to this very day. The Dog follows the Man. The Horse carries the Man. The Cow gives her milk. But the Cat — the Cat walks by himself, in and out, just as he pleases, and purrs by the fire when he chooses to stay.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A true friend keeps their own spirit — you can belong and still be yourself.*
