# The Brave Tin Soldier

*A timeless Hans Christian Andersen tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Once upon a time, in a little toy shop at the end of a quiet street, there lived twenty-five tin soldiers, all brothers, all shiny and brave.

They stood in a neat row in their red and blue uniforms, with tall black hats and tiny tin swords at their sides.

But one soldier was different from all the rest. He had only one leg.

When the tin was poured, there was just a little bit left for the very last soldier, so he was made with a single strong leg. Yet he stood just as straight and just as tall as all his brothers, and his heart was the bravest of all.

One sunny morning, a kind little boy named Ishaan carried them home and set them on a shelf in his room.

On that shelf stood a beautiful paper dancer. She wore a soft pink dress, and a tiny silver star sparkled in her hair. She twirled so gently in the breeze that she looked as if she were dancing just for him.

The little tin soldier looked at her and smiled. "She is the loveliest dancer in the whole wide world," he whispered. "I will stand here and watch over her, brave and steady, for ever and ever."

Then one windy afternoon, the window flew open with a great WHOOSH! A gust of wind swept across the shelf, and the little tin soldier tumbled right out of the window!

"Oh no!" cried the dancer, waving her paper arms. "Come back, brave soldier!"

Down, down he fell, into the garden below, and plip-plop into a little stream that ran past the house.

Two kind children found him and set him gently in a little paper boat.

"Off you go, brave sailor!" they laughed. "Sail safely home!"

Away sailed the little boat, bobbing along the stream, while the soldier stood straight and tall.

Soon the stream grew wider, and the little boat sailed into a deep, dark pond.

Suddenly — GULP! A big, gentle fish swam up and swallowed the boat, soldier and all!

Inside the fish it was dark and quiet. But the brave tin soldier did not cry. "I am a soldier," he said to himself. "I will stand steady and wait. Someone kind will find me."

The fish swam back toward the little house, where a fisherman caught it and carried it to the kitchen!

The kind cook opened the fish, and there, safe and sound, was the little tin soldier, still standing tall on his one leg!

"Well, look at you, brave little fellow!" said the cook with a smile. "You have had quite an adventure. Let us take you home."

She dried him gently, polished him until he shone, and carried him back to the shelf.

And there was the paper dancer, still twirling softly in the breeze, waiting for him.

"Welcome home, brave soldier," she whispered, and her silver star sparkled brighter than ever.

From that day on, the tin soldier stood beside his dancer, brave and steady, and whenever the wind blew, he held on tight and smiled.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A brave heart and a kind friend can always find the way home.*
