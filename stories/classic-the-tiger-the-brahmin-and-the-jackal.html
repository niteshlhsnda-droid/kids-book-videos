The Tiger, the Brahmin and the Jackal

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, a kind Brahmin named Dev was walking home through the forest. The evening light was soft and golden, and birds were singing their sleepy songs.

Suddenly, Dev heard a deep, rumbling voice. "Help! Please, help me!"

Behind some tall bushes stood a great iron cage. Inside the cage sat a big tiger with amber eyes. The tiger had been caught by hunters long ago, and the cage door had rusted shut.

"Kind traveller," said the tiger in his deepest, politest voice, "please open this cage. I have been locked inside for so long. I only wish to stretch my legs and walk in the forest again."

Dev felt sorry for the tiger. He lifted the heavy latch — CREAK! — and the cage door swung open.

Out bounded the tiger with a mighty ROAR! "At last, I am free!" he cried. "And now, kind Brahmin, I am so hungry that I shall eat you up!"

Dev stepped back in surprise. "But that is not fair!" he said. "I set you free, and now you want to eat me?"

"Fair or not, I am hungry," growled the tiger. "That is the way of the world."

"Let us ask three friends," said Dev bravely. "If they all say you are right, I will not argue. But if they say you are wrong, you must let me go."

The tiger flicked his tail. "Very well. Three friends. No more."

First they met a great banyan tree. "Wise tree," said Dev, "I freed this tiger from his cage. Now he wants to eat me. Is that fair?"

The banyan tree sighed through its leaves. "I give shade to travellers all day long," it said, "and travellers break my branches for firewood. So yes, good deeds are often repaid with unkindness."

"One for me," grinned the tiger.

Next they met a gentle old cow grazing in a meadow. "Kind cow," said Dev, "I freed this tiger. Now he wants to eat me. Is that fair?"

The cow mooed softly. "I give sweet milk every morning," she said, "and when I grow old, people forget my kindness. So yes, good deeds are often repaid with unkindness."

"Two for me," purred the tiger.

Dev's heart sank. But then, trotting down the path, came a small, clever jackal named Jai.

"Clever jackal," said Dev, "please hear our story. I freed this tiger from his cage. Now he wants to eat me. Is that fair?"

Jai twitched his sharp ears. "I cannot judge what I cannot see," he said wisely. "Show me exactly how it happened. Tiger, please step back inside the cage, just as you were."

The tiger, who loved showing off, bounded back into the cage. "Like this!" he roared. "I was sitting here, and the Brahmin opened the door like this —"

CLANG! Quick as a wink, Jai pushed the cage door shut and dropped the latch.

"Now I can see it clearly," said Jai with a smile. "A tiger who forgets kindness belongs safely back in his cage, where no one gets hurt — and where kind travellers can bring him dinner from outside."

The tiger grumbled, but he was safe inside, and no one was harmed.

Dev bowed to the clever jackal. "Thank you, Jai. You saved me with wisdom, not with fighting."

And from that day on, Dev always carried sweet mangoes for the tiger, and the tiger, behind his strong bars, learned to say a very polite "thank you" for every single one.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness deserves thanks, not harm — and clever thinking can solve what strength cannot.
