The Lion-Makers

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, four friends set off down a dusty forest path to find adventure — and maybe a little treasure! Three of them were great learners — Padhaku the book-lover, Vidwan the word-lover, and Gyani the know-it-all. They knew all the magic words in the world. The fourth, Samajh, had studied only a few books — but he had something better: good sense.

Deep in the forest, where the leaves lay thick and dark, the friends found a pile of old bones — white and dry as sticks. "These are lion bones!" cried Padhaku. "Let's use our magic to bring the lion to life! Just think how famous we'll be!"

"Wait!" said Samajh. "Bringing a lion to life sounds dangerous. Let's think first." What do you think they should do?

"Think?" laughed Vidwan. "We have THREE hundred magic words! Thinking will have to wait its turn!" And the three nodded at each other, very proud, and didn't notice Samajh quietly climbing the nearest tall tree.

So Padhaku fit the bones together, piece by piece, till they stood like a real skeleton, and Vidwan covered them with skin and fur. Then Gyani closed his eyes and sang the last magic word — it hummed like bees in a hollow tree — and the lion's bones began to move.

"At least ONE of us should watch from somewhere safe!" called Samajh down from his tree.

The lion's eyes blinked open — big and golden in the evening light. He shook out his great shaggy mane, and it fluffed up around his face like a soft, warm crown. And he opened his big, big mouth and let out a long, rumbly roar — roooar! — that rustled every leaf.

"A real lion!" gasped the three friends. They forgot every single magic word they knew, and they ran — ran — ran deep into the forest!

From the top of his tree, Samajh watched them go. And the lion, after his big roar, just gave one long, sleepy yawn — and tiptoed far, far into the deep, quiet forest.

Soon the three friends came hurrying back to Samajh's tree, dusty and out of breath. "You were right, Samajh," they huffed. "All our learning was no good without your good sense!"

And Samajh smiled. "Books are wonderful," he said softly, "but sense tells you when to use them."

Then the four friends walked home together under soft evening stars.

Goodnight, Samajh… can you whisper goodnight to Samajh, too?… The end. 🌙

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Good sense is greater than book-learning — wisdom knows when to act, and when to stop.
