# Nasruddin and the Feast Coat

*A timeless Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One fine morning, a letter came for Nasruddin. It was an invitation! A wedding feast, in the next village.

"Oh, how lovely!" said Nasruddin. He set off down the dusty path, walking, walking, all the way to the next village.

He was wearing his old work clothes. They were dusty and patched, but oh, so comfy.

At the door of the feast, the host barely looked at him. "Sit over there," said the host, pointing him to a dark corner.

The servants brought him only a dry crust of bread. Nobody smiled. Nobody said, "Eat, eat!"

Nasruddin nibbled the crust. Then he stood up, bowed politely, and walked all the way home.

At home, he opened his big wooden chest. Inside lay his finest coat — a blue one, with shiny silver buttons.

He brushed it smooth. He put it on. Oh, he looked very fancy — his silver buttons shone!

Back he went, walking, walking, to the next village again.

This time, at the door, the host rushed out. "Welcome! Welcome!" he called out. "Sit here, my friend! Eat, eat!"

Plates of rice were piled before Nasruddin. Bowls of sweets. Steaming hot soup. Everyone smiled at him.

Nasruddin smiled back. Then, slowly, he took off his fine blue coat. He laid it on his lap.

He picked up a spoonful of rice. And he fed it to the coat! "Eat, my dear coat, eat!"

He tucked a sweet into the coat pocket. "Eat, my dear coat, eat!"

The guests stared. Their eyes grew big. "What are you doing?" they gasped.

Nasruddin grinned. "When I came in my old clothes, nobody fed ME," he said. "You gave me only a crust in the corner."

"Now I come back in my fine coat, and you feed my COAT. So my coat should eat!"

For a moment, nobody spoke. Then everyone burst out laughing! The host laughed loudest of all.

He hugged Nasruddin tight. "You are right, friend!" he said. "We should welcome the PERSON, not the clothes!"

They all shared the feast together happily. Nasruddin ate the rice himself this time — and the sweets too.

That night, Nasruddin walked home under a sky full of soft, twinkling stars. The air was cool and sweet.

His coat felt very, very full — from all that rice tucked in its pockets!

And Nasruddin felt very, very happy. And very, very sleepy.

The stars winked at him all the way home. "Sleep tight, Nasruddin," they seemed to say.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Welcome the person, not the clothes.*
