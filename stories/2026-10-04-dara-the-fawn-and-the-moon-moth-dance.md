# Dara the Fawn and the Moon-Moth Dance

At the edge of the whispering forest, in a meadow full of sweet clover, lived a small fawn named Dara. He had a soft chestnut-brown coat dotted with creamy spots, big round ears, and legs that loved to run — run, run, run! All day the meadow rang with his happy little hooves.

But one beautiful thing always slipped away from him. When the sun sank behind the hills and the first star blinked awake, the moon moths came out — big silver moths with wings like moonlit paper, floating over the meadow in slow, swirling circles. Each evening Dara whispered, "Oh, let me dance with you!"

And off he would dash, as fast as his little legs could carry him. But his hooves drummed the grass, the clover shook, and the moths lifted all at once, drifting away like smoke. Dara stamped his hoof. "Come back!" he cried. They did not.

The next evening, Dara tiptoed instead — one careful step at a time, so softly the clover barely bent. Almost close enough to see the silver patterns on their wings… then his tail gave a happy thump-swish, and away went the moths again. His ears drooped down to his toes.

Beside the pond, old Marsh the toad blinked slowly at the sky. "Why so long-faced, little runner?" she croaked. Dara told her everything. Marsh hummed thoughtfully. "The moon moths are shy dancers," she said. "They do not come to chasing feet. They come to quiet."

So the third evening, Dara lay very still in the grass — for a whole minute. Then a beetle buzzed past his ear, and he popped up — sproing! — quick as a jackrabbit. The moths scattered far away. "Ohhh," groaned Dara. "I can't be still. I just can't!"

Marsh's croak drifted over the water. "Breathe, little one. Breathe with the meadow." Dara sighed a long, sleepy sigh. He listened — the crickets' song, the pond's soft lap, the wind in the clover. He felt the cool grass under his chin. He stopped counting the minutes, and just… was.

And then — like snowflakes made of moonlight — the moon moths came. First one. Then another. Then a whole swirl of silver wings, circling over Dara in slow, shining rings. The most beautiful dance in the whole wide world. And Dara did not move a muscle. He just smiled.

He watched until his eyelids grew heavy, the moths weaving their silver rings all around him, the quiet wrapping him like a warm blanket. And he understood, in the way little ones understand, that some of the loveliest things do not need chasing — they come when you sit still and let them.

Sleep now, little Dara.

The end. 🌙
