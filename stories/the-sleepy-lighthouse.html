The Sleepy Lighthouse

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

On a rocky cliff by the sea stood a tall lighthouse. Her name was Luma, and every night she shone her bright beam across the waves, helping the boats find their way home.

Pela the little puffin lived in a nest nearby, and she loved watching Luma's light sweep the sea. But tonight something looked different. Luma's beam drooped low, moving very, very slowly.

Pela fluttered up to the lighthouse door. "Luma?" she called. "Are you feeling all right?"

Luma's big round light blinked sleepily. "I am so tired, little puffin," she sighed. "I have shone all night, every night, for a hundred years. My light wants to rest."

Pela's heart squeezed with pity. She looked out at the sea. Every boat was already home — the fishing boats tied up tight, the sailboats rocking gently at the dock. No one needed the bright beam tonight.

"You can rest, Luma," Pela said kindly. "All the boats are sleeping too."

"But what if someone needs me?" Luma worried.

"I will keep watch," Pela promised. "I will sit right here. If a boat comes, I will wake you."

Luma's light softened with relief, growing dimmer — from bright white to soft gold, a gentle nightlight for the waves.

"Shine soft, then sleep," Pela whispered.

Luma's big round eye closed halfway. The beam grew softer still, and the waves lapped quietly against the rocks. Watching her friend rest, a deep, warm calm spread through Pela.

"Shine soft, then sleep," Pela whispered again.

Luma's eye closed a little more. Her light was now a warm glow, like a candle, and the stars came out to keep the sea company. Pela tucked her head under her wing.

"Shine soft, then sleep," Pela whispered one last time.

Luma's eye closed all the way. Her glow was faint and cozy, and the whole cliff felt peaceful. Pela's own eyes grew heavy. She had kept her promise, and now she could rest too.

Sleep now, little lighthouse. Sleep now, little puffin. Even the brightest lights need to close their eyes sometimes.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Even the kindest helpers need their rest — and the kindest thing is to let them have it.
