# Whistle the Wind

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

The wind had a problem.

He could whoosh and he could howl.

But he could not whistle.

All the other winds in the sky could whistle.

Whee, went the north wind.

Whee, went the south wind.

Whee, went the east wind and the west wind.

Little Wind felt left out.

He felt sad.

He tried to whistle through the trees.

Whoosh.

He tried to whistle through the grass.

Whoosh.

He tried to whistle through the laundry on the line.

Flap, flap, flap.

No whistle.

"Why can't I whistle?" he asked the old oak tree.

"Maybe you whistle differently," said the oak.

Little Wind felt curious.

He blew softly through a hollow reed.

Toot.

He blew through the chimes on the porch.

Ting, ting, ting.

He blew across the top of an empty bottle.

Hoo.

Little Wind felt excited.

Those were sounds.

Were they whistles?

A little girl named Gia heard the sounds.

She clapped her hands.

"The wind is making music!" she said.

"Music, music, music," she sang.

That was three times she sang it.

She felt joyful.

Little Wind felt proud.

He was not whistling like the other winds.

He was making his own music.

He played the reeds like flutes.

Toot, toot, toot.

He played the chimes like bells.

Ting, ting, ting.

He played the bottle like a horn.

Hoo, hoo, hoo.

Gia danced to the wind's music.

She twirled and twirled.

Then the evening came.

Little Wind grew tired.

His music grew softer.

And softer.

And softer.

Ting.

Toot.

Hoo.

Gia yawned.

She waved at the sky.

"Goodnight, wind," she whispered.

"Goodnight, goodnight, goodnight."

That was three times she whispered it.

Little Wind curled up behind the hills.

Sleep now, little wind.

Dream of reeds and chimes and bottles.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: You don't have to sound like everyone else to make music.*
