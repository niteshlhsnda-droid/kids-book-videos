Why the Mosquitoes Buzz in People's Ears

A timeless world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the green, green jungle, where the river sang softly at dusk, there lived a little mosquito named Mizzy.

Mizzy loved stories. But most of all, she loved telling tall tales — stories that were not quite true.

One sunny morning, Mizzy zipped over to Iguana, who was warming his toes on a rock.

"Iguana! Iguana!" she buzzed. "I saw a farmer digging potatoes as big as elephants! As big as houses!"

Iguana wrinkled his nose. "La la la!" he said, and he pushed two little sticks into his ears. "I don't want to hear silly stories!" Then he marched off, grumpy and wiggly.

Old Python, curled sleepy on a low branch, saw Iguana marching by, looking grumpy. "Oh no," he thought. "Something must be wrong."

So Python slithered down and whispered to Rabbit, "Iguana is very grumpy today. Something must be wrong!"

Rabbit's nose twitched. Hop, hop, hop — she was off to tell Crow! "Crow! Crow! Iguana is grumpy and something is wrong!"

Crow flapped his wings and flew up to the tall mango tree. "CAW! CAW! Something is wrong! Something is wrong!" he shouted.

But up in the mango tree, Baby Owl was taking his morning nap.

"Caw! Caw!" went Crow.

Baby Owl jumped awake — bonk! — and gave his head a tiny bump on the branch.

Mother Owl swooped down at once. She hugged him close and sang, "Hush, my baby, hush, my sweet, a little bump won't spoil your sleep." And Baby Owl giggled, safe in her soft feathers.

Then Mother Owl said, "We must find out what started all this fuss." She hooted — "Whoooo? Whooo started this?"

Crow told about Rabbit, Rabbit told about Python, Python told about Iguana, and Iguana pulled the sticks out of his ears and pointed at little Mizzy.

"I only told a little story!" squeaked Mizzy, her wings trembling.

Then the King of the Jungle padded out from behind the trees. He was big, but his voice was soft as warm honey. "Little untrue stories grow bigger than you think," said the lion kindly. "From now on, tell only TRUE stories. Or buzz a soft sorry-song into people's ears."

Mizzy's eyes grew big and round. "I'm sorry! I'm sorry!" she buzzed.

And that is why, to this very day, mosquitoes buzz in people's ears. They are whispering, "Sorry! Sorry! My story wasn't true!"

All the animals smiled soft, sleepy smiles. Then the jungle hummed a sleepy song, the river sang softly, and Mizzy buzzed the softest lullaby of all.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Telling stories is fun, but only tell stories that are true — tall tales can grow bigger than you think.
