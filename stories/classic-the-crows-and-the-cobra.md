# The Crows and the Cobra

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High in the branches of a tall peepal tree lived an old crow named Kaga and his wife, Kagi.

They had built the softest nest of twigs and feathers, and in it lay three tiny speckled eggs. Every morning, Kaga and Kagi took turns sitting on the eggs, keeping them warm.

But at the foot of the tree lived a long black cobra, and the cobra had sharp eyes. Every day, it slid up to the nest and tried to frighten the crows away.

"Hssss!" went the cobra, swaying its head. "This tree is mine. Go away, or I will gobble your eggs!"

Kaga flapped his wings bravely. "You will not touch our eggs!" he cawed. But the cobra was long and strong, and the poor crows were small and old. Every day, they had to fly away and watch from a far branch while the cobra curled around their nest.

"This cannot go on," sighed Kagi one evening. "We are too old to fight him. But we are not too old to think!"

Kaga nodded slowly. "You are right, my dear. A clever head is stronger than a long tail."

The next morning, Kaga flew over the palace by the river. The queen was sitting in her garden, and her maids had laid her pearl necklace on a stone to dry in the sun.

Kaga had an idea. A big, clever idea.

He swooped down, picked up the pearl necklace in his strong beak, and flew — slowly, heavily — back toward the peepal tree. The queen's guards saw him and ran after him, shouting, "Stop! Thief! Stop!"

Kaga flew straight to the cobra's hole at the foot of the tree. And plop! He dropped the shining necklace right at the mouth of the hole.

The guards came running with sticks. They saw the necklace glittering at the hole — and they saw the long black cobra sliding out, hissing angrily.

"There is the thief's hiding place!" cried the captain of the guards. They chased the cobra with their sticks — far, far away, deep into the forest, where it could never find its way back.

Kaga and Kagi watched from their branch, and they laughed a happy crow-laugh. "Caw! Caw!"

That evening, the old crows sat on their eggs in peace. The peepal leaves whispered softly in the breeze, and no cobra hissed below.

"You see," said Kaga, tucking his head under his wing, "we did not need to be the strongest. We only needed to be the cleverest."

And the three speckled eggs rocked gently in the nest, warm and safe.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clever idea is stronger than sharp teeth — wit wins where strength cannot.*
