# How the Whale Got His Throat

*A playful Just So story by Rudyard Kipling, retold for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in the big blue sea, there lived a whale named Wilbur.

Wilbur was the biggest fish in the sea. He had a very big mouth, and a very big appetite. He ate the starfish, and he ate the crabs, and he ate the little fish, and he ate the big fish.

"Yum, yum, yum!" said Wilbur. "I will eat everything in the sea!"

But there was one little fish who was very, very clever. She was small and quick, and she always swam just out of Wilbur's way.

"Ha!" said the little fish. "You cannot catch me, Mr. Whale!"

Wilbur opened his mouth wide — WIDE! — and tried to gulp her down. But the little fish darted away, laughing.

"One day," grumbled Wilbur, "I will eat you too!"

One day, a kind sailor named Sam was sailing in his little boat. A great storm came, and — SPLASH! — Sam tumbled into the sea.

Wilbur saw him. "A sailor!" said Wilbur. "Yum, yum, yum!" And he opened his mouth wide — WIDE! — and swallowed Sam whole!

Inside Wilbur's tummy, it was dark and warm. Sam sat down and thought, "Well, this is a fine pickle! How will I ever get out?"

Then Sam had an idea. He began to dance! He jumped up and down. He stamped his feet. He wiggled and jiggled and tickled Wilbur's tummy from the inside!

"HEE-HEE-HEE!" laughed Wilbur. "Something is tickling me!"

Sam danced harder. He jumped higher. He tickled and tickled until Wilbur could not stop laughing.

"HA-HA-HA!" laughed Wilbur. "HIC! HIC! HIC!" And Wilbur got the hiccups!

"HIC!" went Wilbur. "HIC! HIC!" And with one great big hiccup — HIC-CUP! — out popped Sam, right onto the beach, safe and sound!

"Thank you, Mr. Whale!" said Sam, waving. "That was a very ticklish ride!"

But Wilbur's throat felt funny. All that laughing and hiccuping had made his big throat very, very small.

"Oh dear," said Wilbur. "Now I cannot swallow big fish anymore!"

The little fish swam up to him and smiled. "That is a good thing, Mr. Whale! Now you will eat only the tiniest fish, and all the big fish can swim safely."

Wilbur thought about this. Then he smiled a great big whale smile.

"You are right, little fish," said Wilbur. "I was greedy. From today, I will eat only the tiniest fish. And I will be a kind whale, not a greedy whale."

And so it was. From that day on, Wilbur the Whale swam happily through the sea, eating only the tiniest fish, and all the other fish swam safely around him.

And that is how the whale got his throat.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Even the biggest can learn to be gentle — a kind heart is better than a big appetite.*
