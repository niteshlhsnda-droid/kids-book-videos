The Parrot King

A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a tall, green forest, where the mango trees swayed and the river sang, there lived a flock of bright green parrots.

And their king was a kind, wise parrot named Kiku.

Kiku had soft green feathers, a bright red beak, and a golden ring around his neck.

Every morning, Kiku woke before the sun, and he flew to the top of the tallest mango tree.

"Good morning, my friends!" he called, in a clear, happy voice. "Let us fly together and find sweet food for everyone!"

The parrots flapped their wings and flew after him, green as leaves, happy as the morning breeze.

Kiku always tasted the food first, to make sure it was sweet and safe. Then he called, "Come, my friends! There is plenty for all!"

And the parrots swooped down, sharing the ripe mangoes and the sweet seeds, all together.

One soft morning, Kiku flew far over the forest, and he saw something wonderful.

Beyond the river, there stood a great mango grove, full of ripe, golden mangoes, more than the flock had ever seen.

Kiku flew back, quick as a green flash, and he called to his flock.

"My friends!" he cried. "I have found a grove full of golden mangoes! Follow me, and we will all eat well today!"

The parrots cheered and flapped their wings, and they flew after Kiku, over the river, to the mango grove.

Oh, what a feast they had! The mangoes were sweet as honey, soft as clouds.

But Kiku waited that day. He watched over his flock, making sure every little parrot had a mango.

"Take one for your baby," he said to a young mother parrot. "And one for you too."

The parrots ate and chatted and laughed, and Kiku smiled, warm and happy.

Then, as the sun climbed high, Kiku looked up at the sky and saw dark, grey clouds rolling in.

"A storm is coming, my friends!" he called. "The wind is rising! Let us fly home now, before the rain."

The parrots flapped their wings and flew after Kiku, swift and steady, over the river and back to their tall mango tree.

The wind blew, whoosh, whoosh, and the rain pattered, tip, tip, tip, on the green leaves.

But the parrots were safe and dry, tucked under the big, leafy branches, all together.

Kiku counted his flock, one by one, to make sure everyone was home.

"One, two, three," he counted, softly. "Everyone is here. Everyone is safe."

The little parrots snuggled close to their mothers. Everyone smiled, safe and warm.

"Thank you, King Kiku," said the young mother parrot. "You always look after us."

"That is what a king is for," said Kiku, softly. "To share the sweet food, and to keep everyone safe."

As the rain softened, the sun peeped out again, warm and gold, and a soft rainbow curved over the forest.

Then the parrots settled down for the night, tucked under the leaves, warm and close together. The moon rose, soft and silver, and Kiku closed his eyes, dreaming of golden mangoes and gentle rain.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A good king shares what he finds and keeps everyone safe.
