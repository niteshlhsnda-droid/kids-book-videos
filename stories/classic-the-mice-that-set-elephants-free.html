The Mice That Set Elephants Free

A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a corner of the great forest stood an old banyan tree. Under its roots lived hundreds of mice, in tiny mud houses no bigger than your fist. Their king was Chintu, a tiny mouse who was kind to everyone.

One day, the ground began to tremble — THUMP! THUMP! THUMP! A herd of elephants came marching through, on their way to the lake. Crunch went the tiny mouse houses. Crash went the tiny grain stores!

Chintu hurried up to the great elephant king. "O great king!" he squeaked, bowing low. "Please, PLEASE walk around our little city — not through it! Our homes are so tiny!"

The elephant king bent one great grey ear down to hear him better. "I'm sorry, little one," he rumbled. "From today, we'll take the long way round. No more squished homes!"

The mice cheered, "Hurray for the kind elephant king!"

Many days later, the elephants were caught in the hunters' strong rope nets! They pulled and pushed, but the ropes only grew tighter. "We're trapped!" trumpeted the elephant king.

But Chintu heard the trumpets. "Friends!" squeaked Chintu. "The elephant king was kind to us. Come on — I have an idea!" Can you guess what Chintu's idea was? … Nibble-nibble-nibble!

Under the round white moon, hundreds of mice scampered to the nets. Nibble-nibble-nibble, chew-chew-chew — all through the night!

By morning, every rope lay in soft little pieces on the grass, and the elephants stood free, stretching in the soft morning sun.

The elephant king picked Chintu up in his trunk and laid him gently on the soft grass under the banyan tree. Chintu curled up in the warm shade and yawned a tiny mouse yawn. "Thank you, little friend," rumbled the elephant king. "Today you showed me — even the smallest friend can be the biggest help."

And that night, elephants and mice slept side by side, the elephants' great soft breathing rocking the little mice to sleep.

Goodnight, Chintu. The end. 🌙

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: No one is too small to help — kindness always comes back.
