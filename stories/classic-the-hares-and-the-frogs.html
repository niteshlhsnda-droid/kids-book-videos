The Hares and the Frogs

A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a soft green meadow, behind the sleepy hills, lived a family of hares.

The hares were gentle and kind — but oh, they were such timid little things! A leaf going rustle-rustle made them jump. An owl going hoo-hoo in the dark made them shiver. And sometimes they were even scared of their own long shadows!

Can you be scared of your own shadow? The hares were!

Every morning, the littlest hare, Pip, tiptoed out of the burrow — their snug underground home. His long ears twitched this way and that, listening for scary sounds. "All clear," he would whisper. But by lunch, something had always made him jump — a bee going bzz-bzz, a twig going snap-snap, or just the wind tickling the grass.

"Why are we so afraid?" Pip sighed one evening. "The wind scares us. The dark scares us. Everything scares us!"

The oldest hare, Grandpa Hopper, gave him a warm, slow smile. "We are small, Pip. And small things feel big fears. It is nothing to be ashamed of."

But that evening, Pip had an idea. He thumped his big back foot — thump-thump! — and called a meeting under the moon.

"Listen, everyone!" said Pip, in his bravest voice. "Let us run away! Far, far away, where nothing can ever scare us. We will hide forever!"

"Forever and ever!" thumped the hares with their big back feet. Thump-thump-thump!

And off they went — hop-hop-hopping across the meadow, their long ears bouncing in the moonlight, their little tails bobbing like puffs of cloud.

Soon they came to a still, silver pond. At the edge of the pond, the frogs were sunbathing — dozing softly in the evening cool, their green backs still warm from the last of the sun.

But the hares' feet went THUMP-THUMP-THUMP! — so loud and so near!

The frogs woke with a jump. "Eeek!" they croaked. And plop-plop-PLIP! — one by one, they splashed into the water and hid under the lily leaves!

The hares stopped and stared.

"Did... did we do that?" whispered Pip.

The frogs peeped out from under the leaves, their round eyes wide with fright. They were trembling!

Pip's ears went up-up-up. Then a giggle bubbled up. "They're afraid of US!" he giggled. "Little, tiny, timid US!"

Grandpa Hopper smiled a slow, warm smile. "See, my little one? Everyone feels afraid sometimes — even of little us."

The hares looked at one another. Pip stood up a little taller. Then a little taller still. He wasn't running away anymore — not one little hop.

"You know what?" said Pip. "Maybe we don't need to run away after all. Tell me, little listener — have you ever felt afraid too?"

"Then come," said Grandpa Hopper softly. "Let us hop home — hop-hop-hop — and sleep soundly in our own warm burrow."

And that is just what they did.

The moon watched over the meadow. The pond went shhh. The frogs sang their soft night song — ribbit-ribbit — and the hares curled up together, warm and safe, and fell fast, fast asleep. They were not afraid at all.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: everyone feels afraid sometimes — courage grows when we see we're not alone in it.
