# Kavi the Kingfisher and the First Dive

At the edge of the Willow Pond, where the water lay smooth as glass, lived a young kingfisher named Kavi.

Kavi had the brightest feathers of any bird at the pond — turquoise wings that shone like the sky, and a chest the warm orange of sunset. But there was one thing Kavi could not do.

He could not dive.

Every morning, the grown-up kingfishers lined up on the tall reeds, folded their wings, and dropped like tiny blue arrows — *splash!* — into the pond. Up they came with silver fish wiggling in their beaks.

Kavi watched from his favorite reed. His tummy did a fluttery flip.

"You go, Kavi," called his sister, Zara. She tucked her wings and dove — *plip!* — perfect as a pebble skipping.

Kavi shuffled to the very tip of the reed. He looked down. The water looked very, very far away.

"Maybe tomorrow," he whispered, and hopped back.

That afternoon, a dragonfly named Dee landed beside him. Her wings were thin as moonlight, and she still wobbled a little when she flew.

"Your wings are very bright today," Dee said.

"They don't feel bright," Kavi sighed. "Everyone can dive but me."

Dee nodded slowly. "When I was small, I was afraid to fly over the water. I thought I would fall in and sink like a stone."

"What did you do?" asked Kavi.

"I flew one wing-beat over the edge," said Dee. "Just one. Then I came back. The next day, two."

Kavi thought about that all evening.

The next morning, he climbed onto the low stone by the pond — not the tall reed, just the low stone. He stretched his neck, closed his eyes, and dipped only the tip of his beak into the water.

*Blip.*

"Again!" cheered Dee, doing a loopy loop above him.

Kavi tried again — this time he dipped his whole beak in, right up to his eyes. *Blip-blip.* The water tickled.

Then, without quite deciding to, Kavi hopped. Just a tiny hop, off the low stone.

*SPLASH!*

He was in the pond! Water fountained everywhere. He popped up, shaking drops off his turquoise wings, laughing and laughing.

Dee landed on a floating leaf, laughing too. "You did it! You did your very own first dive!"

It wasn't a long dive, or a deep one. But it was Kavi's.

That evening, as the sun melted into the pond, Kavi practiced again and again — hop, splash, giggle, repeat. Zara cheered from the reeds. And Dee, who once feared the water, skimmed right beside him, low and brave.

When the stars came out, Kavi tucked himself into his nest in the willow roots. His feathers were still a little damp, and his heart felt big and warm.

Sleep now, little Kavi.

The end. 🌙
