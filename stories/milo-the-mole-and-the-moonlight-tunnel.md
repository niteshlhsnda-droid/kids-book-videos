# Milo the Mole and the Moonlight Tunnel

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Deep under a green hill, in a cozy tunnel lined with soft dry leaves, lived a small, soft-nosed mole named Milo.

Milo had lived all his life in the dark, twisty tunnels. He had never once seen the sky.

But his grandmother told him stories about it. She said, "Up above the ground, little one, there hangs a great silver lamp in the sky. They call it the moon."

"A silver lamp," Milo whispered. He thought about it every single night before he fell asleep. He dreamed of silver light spilling over silver hills, and of a lamp so big it could light up the whole world at once.

"Is it warm, Grandmother?" he asked one night.

"Warm as a blanket fresh from the sun," she said, and nuzzled him with her soft old nose.

One evening, Milo's nose began to twitch. His little paws began to itch. He wanted — oh, he wanted more than anything — to see that silver lamp for himself.

"Grandmother," he said, "I'm going up to see the moon. Just for a little while."

Grandmother smiled her soft old smile. "Be gentle with the earth, little one," she said. "And come back before the night grows old."

So he started to dig. Up, up, up he went — scratch, scratch, scratch.

Past the roots, like thick brown ropes. Past the sleeping beetles, curled up like tiny black pebbles. Past an old acorn, smooth as a marble, and a worm who waved one sleepy end and whispered, "Keep going, little digger."

Milo's heart went thump-thump-thump. He dug a little faster. The earth grew cooler. The earth grew looser. And then — POP!

Milo tumbled out of the tunnel and rolled onto the softest, coolest grass you ever felt. He blinked. He blinked again.

Above him — oh, above him! — hung a huge, round, silvery moon, glowing like the biggest lamp in the world.

Moonlight poured over Milo like warm milk. It tickled his soft nose. It made his dark fur shine silver. Milo gave the great silver lamp the tiniest wave with his little paw — and high above, the moon seemed to glow just a little brighter, as if it were waving back.

Little fireflies danced around him — blink, blink, blink — like tiny lanterns floating in the night air.

And somewhere in a dark tree, a nightingale began to sing — the softest lullaby Milo had ever heard. Tra-la-la, tra-la-lee…

Milo lay back in the cool grass and watched the moon. He watched and watched until his eyelids grew heavy. His paws stopped wiggling. His twitchy nose stopped twitching.

Then, with one last happy sigh, Milo slipped back down into his tunnel — down, down to his warm burrow lined with soft dry leaves.

He curled up in a snug little ball, and dreamed in silver — of moonlight and fireflies and a nightingale's song.

And the moon shone on, soft and kind, over the hill and over little sleeping Milo, all night long.

In the morning, Milo told his grandmother everything, and she smiled and said, "I told you, little one — the moon is the kindest lamp of all."

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A little gentle courage shows you beautiful things.*
