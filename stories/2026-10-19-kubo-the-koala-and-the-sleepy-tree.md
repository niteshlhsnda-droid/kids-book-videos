# Kubo the Koala and the Sleepy Tree

In a tall gum tree at the edge of a soft green meadow, there lived a little koala joey named Kubo.

Kubo had the roundest ears, the softest grey fur, and a tiny pink nose that twitched at interesting smells. Every evening, when the sky turned the color of warm honey, his friends came to play beneath his tree.

One evening, Willa the wombat came waddling up the hill. Kiki the kookaburra swooped down onto the lowest branch. And Bindi the bilby came hopping through the tall grass, her long ears bouncing.

"Hide-and-seek!" cried Kiki. "Moon-spot hide-and-seek! First one to find the moon's reflection wins!"

Kubo's friends cheered. But Kubo only yawned — a great, wide yawn that showed all his tiny teeth.

His eyelids felt heavy, like warm stones. His arms felt wobbly, like jelly.

Still, Kubo did not want to miss the game, so he climbed slowly down the trunk, one paw after another. His friends were already counting, their voices bright and quick.

Willa saw Kubo swaying where he stood. "Come sit on my moss cushion," she said softly, patting the soft green moss beside her. "Just rest your eyes for one little breath."

Kubo curled onto the moss. It smelled like rain. His eyes closed.

Just then Kiki let out a big kookaburra laugh — "Ha-ha-ha-ha!" — right beside his ear. Kubo startled awake. His ears drooped. A grumpy feeling bubbled up inside him, hot and prickly, like stepping on a burr.

"I wanted to play," Kubo mumbled, and his voice came out all wobbly.

Bindi hopped close and gently patted his paw. "You know what?" she whispered. "Let's play the quiet game. The tree game. We hide around the trunk, and you can hear us giggle from up there."

Willa nodded. "Rest your sleepy bones, Kubo. We'll be right here."

So Kubo climbed back up to his favorite fork in the branches — the one shaped just like a cradle — and curled into it. The moon poured silver light over his fur. Below him, he heard soft footsteps in the grass. He heard Willa's tiny snorts. He heard Kiki's muffled giggles. It sounded like a lullaby made just for him.

When Kubo opened his eyes, the stars were out, twinkling like spilled sugar. His friends were curled together at the foot of the tree, and Willa was holding out the tenderest gum leaf she had saved — the softest, sweetest one.

Kubo munched it slowly, feeling warm all the way down to his toes. Nobody had left. Nobody was cross. The game had waited for him.

Above the gum tree, the moon smiled her round silver smile.

Sleep now, little Kubo.

The end. 🌙
