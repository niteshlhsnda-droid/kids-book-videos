# Hazel the Hedgehog and the Lantern of Little Wonders

In a snug burrow under the oldest hazel bush in the meadow lived a small hedgehog named Hazel. She had russet-brown spines, a cream-soft tummy, and bright button eyes that loved to look at everything twice — once at what it was, and once at what it could be.

Every evening Hazel took her little lantern — a jam jar with a candle inside — and waddled out to find something wonderful before bedtime. The other animals said, "There's nothing wonderful out there, Hazel. It's just the same old meadow." Hazel only smiled.

Tonight the meadow was quiet and silver-blue. Hazel held up her lantern. "Let's see what we can see," she whispered.

First she found a dewdrop on a blade of grass. To anyone else it was just a drop of water. But Hazel looked twice. "A tiny moon," she breathed, "resting on a green hill." The dewdrop twinkled, as if it liked its new name.

Then she spotted a fallen leaf floating in a puddle. "A leaf-boat," Hazel decided, "sailing to the far side of the sea." She gave it a gentle push with her nose and watched it sail.

Down the path came Brin the mole, grumbling. "Same dirt. Same stones. Same boring dark," he muttered.

"Look twice, Brin," said Hazel, and she showed him the leaf-boat and the tiny moon. Brin blinked. "Well," he said slowly, "that puddle does look like a sea." His grumble softened into a small smile.

Just then — poof! A breeze swept past, and Hazel's candle went out. The meadow turned dark as a pocket.

"Oh no," whispered Brin. "Now there's nothing to see at all."

Hazel sat very still. She didn't need the lantern. She looked at the dark twice. "It's not dark," she said. "It's a great velvet blanket, pulled right up to the stars." Above them the stars were out — hundreds of them. "And every star is a lantern," Hazel went on, "lit for somebody's bedtime walk."

Brin stared up. "My stars are lanterns too?" he asked.

"Every single one," said Hazel.

So they walked home under the lantern-stars, and Hazel told Brin a story about a brave little mole who sailed the puddle-sea on a leaf-boat to bring the tiny moon home. Brin laughed all the way — a warm, rumbly laugh that sounded like a happy drum.

At the burrow, Hazel curled into her mossy bed. The candle was still out, but she didn't mind. The ordinary dark was full of lanterns now, and the meadow would be there again tomorrow, waiting to be looked at twice.

Sleep now, little Hazel.

The end. 🌙
