# Rosie the Raccoon and the Shiniest Button

In a hollow oak tree beside a quiet pond, there lived a little raccoon named Rosie.

Rosie had soft grey fur, a stripy tail, and a black mask across her eyes, as if she were always ready for a nighttime adventure. But most of all, Rosie loved shiny things.

Inside her hollow oak, on a shelf made of smooth bark, she kept her treasures: a silver thimble, a blue marble bright as the sky, and a round blue button, brightest of all.

Each night before bed, Rosie polished her blue button till it gleamed. "My shiniest treasure," she whispered, and tucked it safely away.

One windy evening, as leaves rustled and the pond rippled, Rosie heard a soft sound above.

It was a little sparrow named Saffy, crying softly.

"Oh, Rosie," sniffed Saffy, "the wind blew my nest door open, and I have lost my little blue button. Grandmother gave it to me, and it kept the cold wind out. Now my baby brother shivers, and I cannot find it anywhere."

Rosie's paws went very still. A little blue button. She knew exactly where a little blue button was: sitting on her shelf, polished and gleaming.

Rosie looked up at Saffy, and then she looked at her shelf. Her heart felt heavy as a stone.

"But it is mine," she thought. "I found it by the pond. I polished it. It is my shiniest thing."

The wind blew colder, and Saffy shivered, wrapping her wings around herself.

Rosie closed her eyes and remembered finding the button, glinting in the mud by the pond. She had been so happy. But now she understood something new: the button had a home, and a sparrow who needed it, and a baby brother who was cold.

Rosie climbed to her shelf, took the blue button down, and held it out to Saffy.

"Here," she said softly. "I found it by the pond. I think it has been waiting to come home to you."

Saffy's eyes went wide. "My button! Oh, Rosie, thank you!"

Saffy flew home and sewed the button on her nest door with spider silk. The door shut snug against the wind, and her baby brother stopped shivering and fell fast asleep.

Then Saffy came back and sang Rosie the sweetest thank-you song, floating over the pond like moonlight on water.

Rosie listened, and her chest felt warm — warmer than any shiny thing ever had.

That night, Rosie curled up in her hollow oak as the moon peeked through the leaves and the pond went quiet and still.

She did not polish anything tonight. Her heart was full, and her eyes were heavy with sleep.

Sleep now, little Rosie.

The end. 🌙
