# The Clever Crab

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a small blue pond, there lived a little crab named Chintu.

Chintu had eight busy legs and two little claws that went snap-snap-snap. He loved his pond. He loved the cool water, the soft mud, and his many fish friends who swam around him all day.

One hot morning, a tall grey crane named Kesar came to the pond. Kesar stood on one long leg and looked down at the little fish with a very sweet smile.

"Oh, little fish," said Kesar. "This pond is getting small and hot. But I know a big, cool pond over the hill. It is full of fresh water and soft green plants. Shall I carry you there, one by one?"

The little fish were so happy! "Yes, please!" they cried. "Take us to the big, cool pond!"

One by one, Kesar picked up a little fish in his long beak and flew away over the hill. And one by one, he came back alone.

"Where are our friends?" asked the little fish. "Why do they not come back to tell us about the big pond?"

"Oh, they are so happy there," said Kesar, smiling his sweet smile. "They never want to come back!"

Now Chintu the crab was listening from behind a rock. Chintu was small, but he was very clever. And something did not feel right.

"Why does Kesar always come back alone?" thought Chintu. "Why does no fish ever return to say hello?"

So Chintu scuttled up to Kesar and said, "Dear Kesar, please take me to the big pond too! I would love to see it."

Kesar looked at the little crab and smiled. "Of course," he said. "Climb onto my neck and hold on tight."

Chintu climbed up and held on tight with his little claws. Off they flew, over the hill and far away.

But when they landed, Chintu's heart went thump-thump-thump. There was no big, cool pond! There was only a dry old tree — and under it, a pile of little fish scales, shining sadly in the sun. No pond. No happy fish. Only scales.

"Oh no," whispered Chintu. "Kesar has been tricking us all along!"

Kesar opened his long beak wide. "And now, little crab," he said, "it is your turn!"

But Chintu was clever, and Chintu was brave. Quick as a flash, he gave Kesar's long neck a firm little pinch with his claw — not to hurt him badly, just enough to make him say "Ouch!"

"Ouch!" cried Kesar. He dropped Chintu — plop! — right into a deep, cool stream below.

Chintu splashed and swam, fast as he could, all the way back to his own little pond.

"Everyone, listen!" cried Chintu. "There is no big pond over the hill! Kesar was tricking us! Stay in our pond, where we are safe together!"

The little fish listened. They stayed close together, safe in their own pond.

And Kesar? He flew away, feeling very ashamed. He never came back to the little blue pond again.

That night, all the fish thanked Chintu. "You saved us with your clever head and your brave heart," they said.

And Chintu just smiled and snapped his little claws — snap-snap-snap — happy to be home.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A clear head and a brave heart can save the day — and looking carefully is better than believing sweet words.*
