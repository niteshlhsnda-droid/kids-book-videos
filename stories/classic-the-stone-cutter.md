# The Stone Cutter

*A timeless Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, at the foot of a great mountain, lived a stone cutter named Taro.

Every day, Taro chipped away at the rocks with his hammer — clink, clank, clink! — and carried the stones down to the village.

It was hard work, and Taro often wished his life could be different.

"Oh," he said one hot day, wiping his forehead, "how I wish I were a rich merchant, riding in a fine carriage with soft cushions!"

Just then, a kind mountain spirit appeared in a swirl of golden mist.

"Your wish is granted," said the spirit.

And poof! Taro was a rich merchant, riding in a fine carriage with soft cushions!

But the sun beat down, hotter and hotter, and Taro began to sweat under his fine clothes.

"Oh," he said, "how I wish I were the sun! Then I could shine on everyone!"

"Your wish is granted," said the spirit.

And poof! Taro was the sun, shining brightly over the whole land!

But then a big, dark cloud floated by and covered his light.

"Oh," said Taro, "how I wish I were a cloud! Then nothing could stop me!"

"Your wish is granted," said the spirit.

And poof! Taro was a cloud, floating high in the sky and pouring soft rain on the fields below.

But then the great mountain stood in his way, and no matter how hard he rained, the mountain would not move.

"Oh," said Taro, "how I wish I were the mountain! Then I would be the strongest of all!"

"Your wish is granted," said the spirit.

And poof! Taro was the great mountain, standing tall and strong against the sky.

But then he heard a tiny sound far below — clink, clank, clink! A little stone cutter was chipping away at his rocks, one chip at a time.

And Taro laughed a big, deep mountain laugh.

"Oh," he said, "how I wish I were a stone cutter again! He can shape even a mountain, one small chip at a time!"

"Your wish is granted," said the spirit, smiling.

And poof! Taro was himself again, standing at the foot of the mountain with his hammer in his hand.

He looked at the mountain, he looked at the sky, and he smiled the happiest smile of his life.

"Being me is quite wonderful after all," he said. And he went back to his work — clink, clank, clink! — happier than a merchant, a sun, a cloud, or a mountain.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: You are already enough — happiness is not somewhere else, it is right where you are.*
