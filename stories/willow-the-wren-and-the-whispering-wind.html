Willow the Wren and the Whispering Wind

A gentle original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green, leafy hedge at the bottom of a garden, there lived a little brown wren called Willow. She had a tiny beak, a perky tail that pointed up to the sky, and a very sweet singing voice. Willow loved her nest. It was round and soft and warm, lined with moss and feathers. She loved it most of all at bedtime.

But every night, just as Willow was settling down to sleep, she heard a sound outside. "Whoooosh," went the wind through the leaves. "Whisper, whisper, whisper."

Willow pulled her blanket of feathers tight around her. "What is that noise?" she peeped. "It sounds so big and so scary. I do not like it at all."

Night after night, Willow hid in her nest and listened to the whispering wind. She was too afraid to peep out. She was too afraid to sing. She just tucked her head under her wing and waited for morning.

One evening, the moon was big and round, and the stars twinkled like tiny fairy lights. The wind came again, soft and low. "Whoooosh. Whisper, whisper, whisper."

Willow took a deep breath. "Tonight," she said, "I am going to find out what that sound really is." She hopped to the edge of her nest. She fluttered her little wings. And then — hop, hop, hop — she followed the sound out into the hedge.

The wind brushed gently against her feathers. It was not cold or rough. It was soft, like a lullaby. Willow listened closely. The wind was moving through the leaves, making a quiet, sleepy song. "Whisper, whisper," sang the leaves. "Hush, hush," sang the wind. It was not scary at all. It was a beautiful lullaby, just for the night.

Willow smiled. She opened her beak and sang along, very softly at first. "Tweet, tweet, la, la, la." Then a little louder. "Tweet, tweet, la, la, la." Her sweet voice joined the wind's soft song, and together they floated through the hedge like a gentle dream.

All the baby birds in the hedge heard the song. The little robins closed their eyes. The baby sparrows tucked their heads under their wings. One by one, they all drifted off to sleep, safe and warm in their nests. "Hush, hush," sang the wind. "Tweet, tweet, la, la, la," sang Willow.

At last, Willow flew back to her own nest. She snuggled into the soft moss and feathers. The wind whispered its lullaby, and Willow sang one last sweet note. Then she closed her eyes, and she fell fast asleep, dreaming of songs that float like feathers through the night.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A scary sound can be a song once you listen.
