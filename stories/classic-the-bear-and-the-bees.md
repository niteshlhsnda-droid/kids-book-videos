# The Bear and the Bees

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

At the edge of a pine forest that smelled of warm pine and crackling needles lived a young bear named Bhola.

Bhola loved many things — soft moss, warm sunshine, and long afternoon naps under his favorite pine tree.

But most of all, Bhola loved honey — thick, golden, and slow, melting on his tongue.

That morning he had rolled in the clover. Then he chased a butterfly all the way to the stream. But by afternoon, all he could think about was something sweet.

A warm, waxy smell floated through the air — honey, rich and golden as melted butter. Sniff, sniff went Bhola's nose. Sniff, sniff, sniff!

"Honey!" he cried, his tummy rumbling. "I can smell honey!"

He followed the smell past the berry bushes and around the old fallen log. At last he came to a tall oak tree.

High up in its branches hung a beehive — round and golden, buzzing softly in the sunshine.

Bhola's mouth watered. "Mmm!" he whispered. "I can almost taste it — thick and golden, dripping down my chin."

He picked up a long stick from the ground. "I'll just poke it a little," he said. "Just a tiny taste."

Poke, poke, poke went the stick against the hive.

And out poured the bees — buzz, buzz, buzz!

They zipped and zoomed all around him, buzzing loudly to tell him to go away!

"Oh no! Oh no!" cried Bhola. "I'm sorry! I'm sorry!"

But the bees only buzzed louder, like a pressure cooker whistling on the stove.

So Bhola ran. He ran past the tall pines and through the flower meadow.

Buzz, buzz, buzz went the bees, right behind him. Their wings went whirr, whirr, whirr — loud and buzzy, right behind him.

Bhola's paws went patter, patter, patter over the soft forest floor.

Then Bhola saw the pond, cool and blue. Splash. Bhola slipped gently into the water.

The bees buzzed above the water for a little while… and then, one by one, they flew home.

Bhola climbed out and sat on the bank, dripping from his ears to his toes.

A sorry feeling sat heavy in his chest, like a wet stone, and his ears drooped right down to his shoulders.

"I poked their home," he said quietly. "That wasn't very kind."

So Bhola dragged his heavy, dripping feet back to the old oak tree, pond-water squelching between his toes.

He stood under the hive and called up in a voice as soft as moth wings: "Dear bees, I am sorry. I shouldn't have poked your home. May I please have just a little honey?"

The bees whispered together, their buzzing soft and thoughtful. Then the queen bee flew down, looking golden in the evening light.

She looked at Bhola's round, hopeful face, his ears still dripping with pond-water, and she smiled.

"Well," she said, "manners are sweet too."

And she gave him one small, golden honeycomb.

Bhola took one tiny lick. It was thick and warm and sweet, melting across his tongue like liquid sunshine.

That night, as the fireflies blinked over the pond, Bhola curled up in his den.

He had learned that saying sorry and asking kindly was even better than honey.

And he drifted off to sleep, smiling.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Never poke at someone's home — ask kindly, and kindness comes back.*
