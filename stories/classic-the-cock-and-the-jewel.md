# The Cock and the Jewel

*A gentle Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a sunny farmyard lived a proud rooster named Kuku.

Every morning, Kuku woke the whole farm with his loud, happy crow — "Cock-a-doodle-doo!"

Then he scratched in the dust with his feet — scratch, scratch, scratch — looking for his breakfast.

One morning, as he scratched, his claw hit something hard.

Clink!

Kuku looked down. There, in the dust, lay a beautiful jewel!

It was round and shiny, and it sparkled pink and blue in the morning sun.

"Oh!" said Kuku. "What a pretty thing! It shines like a little rainbow!"

All the hens came running. "A jewel! A jewel!" they clucked. "Kuku, you are rich!"

But Kuku tilted his head and looked at the jewel very carefully.

"It is pretty," he said. "But can I eat it? No. Can it fill my tummy? No. One fat grain of corn would make me much, much happier than this shiny stone."

So Kuku left the jewel where it was and went back to scratching — scratch, scratch, scratch — until he found three fat grains of corn.

"Now that," he said, munching happily, "is a real treasure!"

Just then, the farmer's little daughter, Meena, came into the yard.

She saw the jewel sparkling in the dust. "Oh! How beautiful!" she cried. "It will shine on my mother's birthday necklace!"

She picked it up, thanked Kuku with a pat, and ran home singing.

Kuku watched her go, and then he crowed his happiest crow of all — "Cock-a-doodle-doo!"

The hens asked, "Kuku, are you not sad? You gave away a jewel!"

Kuku laughed. "Why should I be sad? The jewel found the person who loves shiny things. And I found my corn, which is what I love. Everyone got their own treasure!"

And that is true, you know.

What is precious is not the same for everyone. For a rooster, a grain of corn beats a jewel. For a little girl, a jewel for her mother's necklace beats a grain of corn.

And the happiest treasure of all is the one that fits your own heart.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: What is precious depends on who you are — a grain of corn can beat a jewel.*
