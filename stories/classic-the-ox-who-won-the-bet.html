The Ox Who Won the Bet

A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little village lived a farmer named Bhola and his gentle ox Moti — a big, soft-eyed ox with a little brass bell at his neck that went tink-tink wherever he walked.

One day, Bhola made a bet with the oldest man in the village. "My Moti can pull a hundred carts, all tied together!" he said, puffing out his chest. "If he does it, you give me a hundred gold coins!"

The next morning, the whole village gathered. One hundred carts were tied in a long, long line down the dusty road. Longer than your whole street — can you picture it? Bhola climbed up. "Move, you silly old ox!" he shouted. "PULL!"

Moti's ears flopped. Such unkind words! He stood still as a rock and did not move — not one step. He didn't even flick his tail.

The villagers laughed. Bhola lost the bet, and his face turned hot and red.

That evening, Bhola sat beside Moti in the warm barn, where the hay smelled sweet. What do you think Bhola said to Moti? "I'm sorry, my friend," he said softly, stroking Moti's head. "I was rude to you. Will you try again tomorrow?"

Moti mooed gently and nuzzled Bhola's hand with his big, tickly nose. What do you think that little nuzzle meant?

The next morning, the carts were tied again. This time Bhola smiled and said, "Come on, my strong, handsome Moti! You can do it! Heave! Ho!"

And Moti pulled — heave... ho... heave... ho — slow and steady, till every wheel was rolling. The wheels went creak, creak, the ropes stretched tight — and all one hundred carts rolled forward, slow and steady. Everyone smiled, quiet and happy.

Bhola won the hundred gold coins — and hugged Moti. "You taught me, old friend — kind words moved a hundred carts today. And harsh words couldn't move even one."

Goodnight, Moti. The end. 🌙

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kind words work wonders — speak gently, and hard tasks grow light.
