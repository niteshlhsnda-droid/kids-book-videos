Rumpelstiltskin

A timeless Grimm's fairy tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, there lived a miller who loved to tell tall tales. One day, he went to the king and said, "Your Majesty, my daughter Nina can spin straw into gold!"

Now, this was not true at all — but the king's eyes lit up. "Bring her to me!" he cried.

So poor Nina was brought to the palace, where the king showed her a little room full of straw.

"Spin this straw into gold by morning," said the king, "or you will have to stay here and spin, spin, spin, day after day."

Nina looked at the mountain of straw, and tears filled her eyes. "I do not know how to spin gold," she whispered. "What shall I do?"

Just then — pop! — a funny little man appeared in the room. He had a long beard, a pointed hat, and shoes that curled up at the toes.

"Why are you crying, little miller's daughter?" he asked.

"Because I must spin this straw into gold, and I do not know how," said Nina.

"I can do it for you," said the little man, with a twinkle in his eye. "But what will you give me?"

Nina thought. "I will give you my necklace," she said.

"Done!" cried the little man. He sat down at the spinning wheel — whirr, whirr, whirr! — and by morning, all the straw had turned to shining gold!

The king was delighted. "Do it again!" he said, and he showed Nina a room even fuller of straw.

Again Nina cried. Again the little man popped up. "What will you give me this time?"

"I will give you my ring," said Nina.

"Done!" said the little man. Whirr, whirr, whirr! By morning, all the straw was gold again!

On the third night, the king showed Nina the biggest room of all, piled high with straw. "Spin this into gold," he said, "and I will let you go home."

Nina cried once more. The little man popped up again. "What will you give me this time, little miller's daughter?"

Nina thought hard. "I will give you my golden locket — my dearest treasure," she said. "But let us make a bargain: if I can guess your name in three days, I will keep my locket."

"Ha!" laughed the little man. "No one can guess my name! It is a deal!"

Whirr, whirr, whirr! By morning, all the straw was gold. The king kept his promise, and Nina was free to go home.

But Nina still had to guess the little man's name! So she sent her kindest messenger into the forest to listen and learn.

Deep in the forest, the messenger saw the little man dancing around a fire, singing a silly song:

"Today I bake, tomorrow I brew, Then my golden locket comes to me — tra-la! Little knows my royal lady That Rumpelstiltskin is my name!"

The messenger ran back as fast as his legs could carry him. "His name is Rumpelstiltskin!" he cried.

The next day, the little man came hopping up to Nina. "Well, little miller's daughter," he grinned, "have you guessed my name?"

"Is it… Rumpelstiltskin?" asked Nina.

The little man's eyes went wide. His pointed hat wobbled. "How did you know? HOW DID YOU KNOW?"

He stamped his foot so hard that the whole floor shook — boom! — and then, with a grumpy little hop, he jumped right out of the window and disappeared into the forest, never to be seen again.

Nina laughed with joy. She kept her golden locket, she went home to her father, and she never, ever had to spin straw into gold again.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A clever guess and a brave heart can untangle any trouble.
