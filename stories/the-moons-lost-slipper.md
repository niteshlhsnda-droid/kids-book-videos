# The Moon's Lost Slipper

*A cozy original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

High, high above the sleeping world, the Moon was dancing — her round white face glowing against the deep-blue night.

She twirled among the twinkling stars in her silver slippers, gliding across the soft night sky. It was her favorite night game — dancing until the stars twinkled their brightest.

But oh! On one especially swoopy turn — slip! One silver slipper flew right off her foot!

Down, down, down it tumbled, spinning like a shiny snowflake, until it landed with the softest *poof* in a moonlit meadow far below.

In the morning, a little mouse scurried through the wet morning grass and stopped. "Oh my whiskers!" she squeaked. There, sparkling in the sunlight, lay a slipper made of pure silver.

She pushed and pulled and wriggled her tiny toes in. Far too big! It was like a boat on her foot. She tried hopping, but the slipper just flopped off.

Then along came a hedgehog, rustling through the clover. "Let me try," he said, squeezing in one spiky foot. "Much too big!" he huffed, wobbling around in a circle. They both giggled.

Just then, a kind old owl fluttered down from her oak tree. She tilted her head and looked at the slipper with her big round eyes. "Hoo!" she said softly. "Can you guess whose sparkly slipper this is? ... It belongs to the Moon! She danced right over my tree last night, and I heard something go *slip!*"

"Oh — can we give it back to her?" whispered the mouse.

"Oh yes," said the owl. "But first — what do you think we could use to make it shine for her?"

So the three friends shined it together. The mouse rubbed the slipper with a soft dandelion puff. The hedgehog polished it with the gentlest feather he could find. And the owl blew one warm little breath on it, then rubbed it shiny with her wing.

Soon the slipper gleamed so bright that the mouse saw her own tiny reflection in its silver toe.

That night, the owl tucked the silver slipper under her wing and flew up, up, up into the starry sky. The Moon was back again, hopping on one foot, her one bare toe wiggling in the cold starlight.

"Oh, my slipper — thank you, little friends!" sang the Moon, slipping her foot back in. She twirled once, twice — and her silver shoe clicked softly as it landed home.

Then the Moon smiled down at the meadow. She gave a happy little shake, and a soft drift of silver moon-dust floated down. It settled over the grass and the clover, over the mouse's nest, the hedgehog's burrow, and the owl's tall oak tree.

The whole meadow began to glow — soft and silvery and warm.

And ever since that night, whenever the moon is full, that little meadow glows softly, like a little night-light. The mouse curls up in her nest of soft dried grass and clover. The hedgehog snuggles into his leaves. The owl tucks her head under her wing.

And they all drift off as the gentle silver light brushes over their fur and feathers, remembering the night they helped the Moon.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When you help others with a kind heart, you feel warm and happy too.*
