# The Parrot Who Saved the Forest

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a deep green forest, there lived a little parrot named Piko.

Piko was small, with bright green feathers and a tiny red beak. He lived in a cosy hole in a tall mango tree, and he loved his forest more than anything in the world.

He loved the tall trees. He loved the soft moss. He loved his friends — the deer, the rabbits, the monkeys, and the little brown bear.

Every morning, Piko flew from tree to tree, singing, "Good morning, forest! Good morning, friends!"

One hot, dry afternoon, something terrible happened.

A spark fell from the sky — crackle! — and landed on a dry leaf. The leaf caught fire. Then a twig caught fire. Then a bush caught fire.

"Fire! Fire!" cried the monkeys, swinging away through the trees.

The deer ran. The rabbits hopped. The little brown bear trotted away. All the animals ran to the river, where the water would keep them safe.

But Piko did not run. He flew to the river, dipped his little beak into the cool water, and flew back to the fire.

Drip, drip, drip. He let the drops fall on the flames.

Then he flew back to the river again. Drip, drip, drip.

"Little Piko," said the big grey elephant, "what are you doing? Your beak is so small. The fire is so big. You cannot put it out with drops of water!"

"I know," said Piko, "but this is my home. I must try."

So Piko flew back and forth, back and forth. River, fire. River, fire. His little wings grew tired. His little beak grew dry. But he did not stop.

High up in the clouds, the god of rain was watching.

"What a brave little heart," said the god of rain. "He is so small, and the fire is so big — yet he does not give up."

The god of rain felt his heart grow warm. And do you know what he did? He gathered the soft grey clouds together and let the rain fall — pitter, patter, pitter, patter — all over the forest.

The rain put out the fire. The smoke drifted away. The trees stood safe and green.

All the animals came back from the river. The deer, the rabbits, the monkeys, and the little brown bear gathered around Piko.

"Piko, you saved our forest!" they cheered.

Piko smiled and ruffled his green feathers. "I only carried drops of water," he said. "But a small heart that does not give up can do big things."

That evening, the forest smelled fresh and clean after the rain. The moon rose, round and soft. Piko curled up in his cosy hole in the mango tree, and all his friends slept safely in the forest he had saved.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A small heart that does not give up can do big things.*
