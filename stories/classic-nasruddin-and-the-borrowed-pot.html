Nasruddin and the Borrowed Pot

A funny Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Hello, little listener. Come and snuggle in close, because tonight I have a funny story for you about a clever man named Nasruddin. He lived a long, long time ago in a town with sunny streets. The bakeries there filled the air with the smell of warm, crusty bread. Everybody loved him because he was kind and fair, and he always made people laugh.

One sunny morning, Nasruddin had a wonderful idea. He was going to make a big, bubbling feast for his friends. There would be golden rice, warm bread, and sweet honey cakes. But oh dear — his own little cooking pot could barely hold soup for one!

So Nasruddin walked next door and knocked on his neighbor's door. "May I borrow your big cooking pot, please?" he asked with a smile. "I am making a feast for our friends, and I promise to bring it back."

The neighbor was a kind man too, and he said, "Of course! Take it, and enjoy your feast."

Nasruddin lifted the big pot onto his head — it sat there cool and smooth, wobbling a little like a hat — and walked home, humming all the way. All afternoon, the smells of golden rice and melted butter drifted out of his kitchen window like warm ribbons — can you sniff what Nasruddin is cooking? When the feast was over and the last crumb of honey cake was gone, Nasruddin washed the big pot until it shone like the moon.

But Nasruddin loved a playful trick. He found a tiny pot of his own — no bigger than an apple — and slipped it into the big pot. Then he carried it back to his neighbor.

"Thank you for the pot!" Nasruddin said. "And wait — your pot had a baby at my house!"

The neighbor peeked inside and found the tiny pot. His eyes grew round and wide. "A baby pot!" he laughed. "Ha! What a fine little baby!" He kept the little pot, grinning from ear to ear.

About a week later, Nasruddin knocked at his neighbor's house again. "May I borrow your big pot one more time?" he asked. "The little baby pot must miss its mother!"

The neighbor laughed and handed over the big pot. Now — what do YOU think Nasruddin will do this time? This time, Nasruddin cooked a quiet little supper — just the soft hiss of a simmering pot, thin ribbons of steam, and the smell of warm bread. But when he came back to return the pot, he wore the very serious face grown-ups wear when they are hiding a giggle.

"Oh, neighbor," Nasruddin said in a small, serious voice, "I have silly news. Your pot has fallen fast asleep — and it will not be waking up."

"Goodness," said the neighbor, blinking. "Now that is silly, Nasruddin. Pots cannot fall asleep forever!"

Nasruddin looked up, and a slow, twinkling smile spread across his face. "Dear neighbor," he said, "if a pot can have a baby… why can't it die?"

The neighbor opened his mouth, and closed it again. And then he began to laugh — a soft, sleepy laugh — until happy tears twinkled in his eyes. They laughed together, quiet and warm, because the trick had been fair and funny, and nobody was hurt — and of course, Nasruddin carried the big pot straight back to his neighbor's kitchen, where it belonged.

Then Nasruddin shared the leftovers of his supper with his neighbor. They sat together in the soft evening light, eating warm bread and telling happy stories, until the stars came out to watch.

So, little listener, if someone tells you something silly — like a pot having a baby — stop and think about it with your clever little brain. And always be fair to the people you love.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: If something sounds too good to be true, think twice — and always be fair.
