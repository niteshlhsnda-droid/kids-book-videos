Tenali Rama and the Parrot

A cheerful Tenali Rama tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In the grand palace of Vijayanagara, King Krishnadeva had a beautiful green parrot named Mithu.

Mithu had shiny feathers and a sharp beak — and the rudest squawk in the whole kingdom. "SQUAWK! SQUAWK!" he shrieked at everyone who passed. "SQUAWK!"

The king sighed. "My parrot greets my guests with squawks! Can no one teach him polite words?"

The royal teacher tried. "Say: good morning," he said sternly. "SQUAWK!" said Mithu.

The royal musician tried. He played a sweet flute tune. "SQUAWK!" said Mithu, even louder.

The courtiers shook their heads. "That bird will never learn," they whispered.

Then Tenali Rama stepped forward with a twinkle in his eye. "Give me seven days, Your Majesty — and a bowl of mango pieces."

For seven days, Tenali sat by Mithu's golden cage. Every time the parrot stayed quiet for a moment, Tenali popped a juicy mango piece through the bars and said softly, "Good morning, kind king."

Nibble, nibble went Mithu. "Good morning, kind king," said Tenali again.

On the first day, Mithu only squawked. On the third day, he tilted his head and listened. On the fifth day, he mumbled, "Guh... morn..."

And on the seventh morning, when King Krishnadeva walked past the cage —

"Good morning, kind king!" said Mithu, clear as a bell. Then he fluffed his feathers proudly and added, "Mango, please!"

The king laughed so hard his crown wobbled. "Tenali! You did what teachers and musicians could not!"

Tenali bowed. "Your Majesty, scolding teaches a bird to squawk louder. But kindness — and mango — teach a bird to sing."

From that day on, Mithu greeted everyone at the palace door each morning: "Good morning, kind king! Good morning, kind queen! Good morning, friends!"

And everyone who heard him started their day with a smile.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness and patience teach better than scolding ever can.
