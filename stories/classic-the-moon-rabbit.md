# The Moon Rabbit

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a soft green meadow under a wide silver sky, there lived a kind little rabbit named Milo.

He had the warmest heart of all.

Every morning, Milo hopped out of his burrow and called his three best friends. "Good morning! Time to play!"

Kiki the monkey swung down from her tree. Jack the jackal trotted over the hill. Ollie the otter splashed out of the stream.

All day they played in the soft grass and shared berries and carrots under the warm sun.

Every evening, when the sky turned pink and gold, they sat together on the grassy hill and watched the moon rise, big and round and bright.

One evening, just as the moon was rising, a tired traveller came slowly down the path.

He wore a long grey coat, and his tummy rumbled — rumble, rumble, rumble.

"Please," he said in a soft, tired voice, "may I rest here for a little while? I have walked a very long way, and I am so hungry and so cold."

"Of course!" cried Kiki, and she brought sweet yellow bananas from her tree. "Here you are, my friend. Eat, eat!"

Jack shared the ripe red berries he had saved in a leaf. "They are sweet as honey," he said.

Ollie brought the juiciest plums he could find. "Here," he said, "these are for you."

Then Milo hopped forward with his very sweetest carrots, the ones he had saved for his own supper. "Please take these," he said. "Stay with us. You do not have to be alone tonight."

The traveller smiled a slow, warm smile. He ate the bananas, the berries, the plums, and the carrots, and his eyes grew bright again.

But Milo did not stop there. He made the traveller a snug bed of leaves and grass, and sang him a soft, sleepy song, until the traveller's eyes grew heavy and happy.

"Why are you so kind to me?" asked the traveller. "You do not even know my name."

Milo twitched his little nose. "Because you needed a friend," he said simply. "And friends share what they have."

Then the traveller stood up tall — and oh! His grey coat turned to silver, and his tired face grew bright and round, like the moon itself.

"I am the Moon," she said, in a voice as soft as moonlight. "I came down to see who is kind in this meadow. And you, dear Milo, are the kindest of all."

Milo gasped, and his friends cheered.

"Dear Milo," said the Moon, "your kindness should shine for everyone to see. I will paint your picture upon my face, where all the children of the world can see it."

And with one gentle touch, she painted Milo's soft ears, his twitching nose, and his kind, bright eyes upon the moon.

From that night on, children who looked up at the full moon could see the little rabbit, smiling down at them. "Look!" they cried. "There is the Moon Rabbit!"

So tonight, look up at the moon. You might just see Milo smiling back at you.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A little kindness, freely shared, shines bright enough for the whole world to see.*
