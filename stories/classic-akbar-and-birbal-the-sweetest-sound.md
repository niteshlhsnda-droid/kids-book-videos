# Akbar and Birbal: The Sweetest Sound

*A timeless Akbar and Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

One golden evening, Emperor Akbar sat in his grand marble hall, chin in his hand, wondering.

"Tell me, my wise friends — and you, little listener," he said to the people around him, "what is the sweetest sound in the whole world?"

The court singer stepped forward. "Your Majesty, it is the veena — a beautiful instrument with many long strings," he said. "Its strings sing like silver rain."

Just then, a koel sang in the palace garden — koo-oo, koo-oo. "No, Your Majesty — just listen!" said the gardener, bowing low. "It is the koel's song."

Far away, the temple bells began to ring — dong… dong… dong. "Listen — the temple bells," said the priest with a smile, "calling everyone to prayer."

Akbar stroked his beard. "Each of you says something different. But which is truly the sweetest?"

Birbal, the cleverest of them all, only smiled. "Come with me at dusk, Your Majesty," he said, "and I will show you."

That evening, Birbal led Akbar quietly to a little courtyard behind the palace. The sky was turning pink, like strawberry milk.

In the courtyard sat a young mother on a low step. In her lap lay her tiny baby, wrapped in a soft yellow cloth. The mother was singing a lullaby — a gentle, rocking song, no louder than a whisper. Close your eyes — can you hear it?

And the baby giggled. A tiny, bubbling giggle — hee-hee-hee — like little bells made of sunshine.

Akbar stood very still. He listened until the song was done.

Then he turned to Birbal with a big, warm smile. "You are right, my friend," he said. "No veena, no koel, no temple bell is sweeter than a happy child's laughter."

And all the way back to the palace, the emperor hummed the mother's lullaby softly to himself, feeling peaceful.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The sweetest things in life are the simple ones — like a happy child's laughter.*
