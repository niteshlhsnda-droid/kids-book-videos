# How the Birds Picked a King

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, the birds of the forest had no king. "The lions have a king! The bees have a queen!" chirped the sparrows. "We must pick a king too!"

So all the birds gathered in the great banyan tree — green parrots and soft grey pigeons, chattery mynahs and blue-black magpies, sleepy owls and grand eagles. They flapped and fluttered till the big green leaves shook and rustled. Just imagine all those wings flapping and all those voices chattering at once!

"Let's make Ollie the Owl our king!" hooted a young owl from a low branch. "Just look at him — those big round eyes, slow and steady, like he's thinking the deepest thoughts!"

Ollie puffed up his feathers proudly and was just about to step forward — when a tiny yawn slipped out. It was almost his naptime, after all.

Just then an old crow named Kaga hopped up and cawed, "WAIT! Before you choose him, listen to me first!"

The birds settled down. High above, the morning sun warmed the leaves, and long leaf-shadows swayed softly on the branches.

"Friends," said Kaga, "Ollie looks wise — it's true. But tell me — when is Ollie awake? All night! And when do WE fly and play and find our food? All day! When danger comes in the daytime, our king will be fast asleep. And at night, when we are all tucked into our nests and dreaming, poor Ollie would be wide awake all alone in the dark — with nobody to play with. Do you want a king you can never play with?"

The birds looked at one another. The little sparrows nodded slowly, and the pigeons just looked down at the ground.

"Kaga's right, Kaga's right!" chattered the mynah, bobbing up and down. "Our king has to wake up when WE wake up — and be kind when we need a kind friend!"

So the birds thought and thought… and if YOU had been sitting in that banyan tree, which bird would YOU have picked? … At last, they chose Garuda, the great eagle, to glide soft and slow above the banyan tree in the gentle morning light.

And Ollie the Owl? He blinked his big, sleepy eyes slowly, fluffed up his feathers, and settled into his hollow tree for a long, cozy nap. He decided that being king was far too much trouble — he'd rather nap the whole day long!

And the birds learned something that day — to take their time and think, and not rush just because everyone else is cheering.

Goodnight, Ollie. Goodnight, little birds. The end. 🌙
