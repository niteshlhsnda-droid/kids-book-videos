The Star Coins

A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a little village, where the lanes smelled of warm bread, there lived a kind little girl named Mina.

Mina did not have much. Her house was small, her shoes were old, and her supper was often just a small end piece of bread. But her heart was as big as the whole sky, and her smile was warm as morning sunshine.

One evening, Mina's mother gave her a small loaf of bread and said, "Take this for your walk, my love. Come home before the stars come out."

So Mina tucked the bread under her arm and set off down the lane, humming a little song.

Soon she met a small boy sitting by the road. His tummy was rumbling, and his eyes were big and hungry.

"Please," said the boy, "may I have a little bread? I have not eaten all day."

Mina did not stop to think. She broke her loaf and gave him the bigger half. "Here you are," she said. "Eat slowly, and feel better."

The boy smiled, and Mina's heart felt warm and full.

A little further on, she met a small girl with no cap on her head. The evening wind was cool, and the girl's ears were cold and pink.

"Oh, your ears are chilly!" said Mina. She lifted her own soft cap and tucked it gently over the girl's ears. "There. Now you are cozy."

The girl giggled. "Thank you, Mina!"

Mina walked on, her hair dancing in the breeze. Then she saw two little children sitting close together under a tree, shivering in the cold. One had no shawl, and the other had only a thin little dress.

Mina wrapped her warm shawl around the first child. "Snug as a hug," she said.

Then she looked at the second child, who was shivering the most. Mina slipped off her warm dress — underneath she wore her soft little nightgown — and tucked the dress around the shivering child like a blanket. "There," she said softly. "Warm now."

The children smiled up at her, warm and safe.

But now Mina had given away her bread, her cap, her shawl, and her dress. She had nothing left but her little nightgown and her big kind heart. The evening grew cool, and she was a little bit cold. Still, she smiled. "I am glad they are warm," she said.

Just then, she looked up — and oh! The night sky began to twinkle and glow.

The stars blinked like sleepy eyes. One by one, they drifted down, soft as snowflakes, and landed all around her feet. And they were not only stars. They were golden star-coins, round and warm, tinkling like tiny bells as they fell.

Plink, plink, plink! Down they came, until the whole lane sparkled like a river of gold.

And the kindest wonder of all: as the star-coins fell, a new dress floated down to Mina, too — a dress woven out of soft starlight, silver and warm, with tiny stars stitched all along the hem. Mina slipped it on, and it fit her just right.

Mina gathered up the golden star-coins in her dress, like apples in an apron. She carried them home, and she shared them with everyone in the village, so no one ever had to be hungry or cold again.

That night, Mina lay in her bed and looked out of her window at the twinkling stars. The stars twinkled back, as if to say, "Well done, kind Mina."

And Mina fell asleep, warm and happy, under her blanket of stars.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When you give with a kind heart, kindness comes back to you like starlight.
