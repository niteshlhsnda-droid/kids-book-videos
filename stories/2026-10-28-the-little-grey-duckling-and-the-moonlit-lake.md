# The Little Grey Duckling and the Moonlit Lake

In a sunny farmyard, beside a pond that sparkled like a mirror, Mother Duck sat upon her nest. She had five soft eggs, and she waited and waited, keeping them warm beneath her feathers.

One by one, pop, pop, pop, the eggs cracked open. Out tumbled four fluffy yellow ducklings, peeping and wobbling on their tiny orange feet. Then the last egg cracked — and out came a duckling who was big, and grey, and fluffy all over.

The yellow ducklings stared. "Why are you so big?" peeped one. "Why are you so grey?" peeped another. Even the old hen clucked, "What a funny-looking duckling!" The little grey duckling hung his head. He did not feel funny. He felt sad.

So when the moon rose and the farmyard grew quiet, the little grey duckling slipped away down the grassy bank and followed the winding stream, all by himself, to find a place where he belonged.

Beside a quiet lily pond, a friendly frog sang a low, sleepy song. "Come and rest on my lily pad," said the frog. "There is room for one more." The duckling rested, and for the first time that day, he smiled.

In the morning, a family of wild ducks came swimming by. "Swim with us!" they quacked. "You swim beautifully!" And the little grey duckling swam and splashed and dived, and he felt almost like one of the family.

But soon the leaves turned gold and red, and a cold wind blew across the pond. The wild ducks flew away to warmer lands, and the little grey duckling was left alone. The pond froze, and he shivered in the tall reeds, dreaming of the sun.

Then, one soft spring morning, the ice melted into sparkling water. The duckling stretched his wings — and oh! They felt strong and wide. He flapped them, and up he rose, higher and higher, over the reeds and over the trees.

Below him lay a great moonlit lake, and upon it glided the most beautiful white swans he had ever seen. Trembling, he swam toward them. "Please don't chase me away," he whispered.

But the swans bent their long necks and welcomed him. "Come and swim with us!" they sang. The little grey duckling looked down into the clear water — and there, looking back at him, was a beautiful white swan. It was him! He had grown into a swan, in his own good time.

That night, the swans settled beside him on the moonlit lake, and the water rocked them gently, like a cradle.

Sleep now, little grey duckling.
