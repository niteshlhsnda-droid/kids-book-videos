The Hungry Jackal and the Drums

A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Jhanjhar was a little jackal with soft, sandy fur and the hungriest tummy in the whole forest.

All evening long, he trotted along the forest paths, sniffing under the bushes and poking his nose around the rocks.

"Nothing to eat. Nothing to eat," he sighed. "My tummy is singing its empty song — grrroooomm."

Then the sun dipped low and melted into gold. The forest grew soft and shadowy, and the evening breeze began to rustle through the leaves.

And just then — BOOM-BOOM-BOOM!

Jhanjhar froze mid-step — his ears shot straight up, and his tail puffed out big and fluffy.

BOOM-BOOM-BOOM! The sound rolled through the trees again, deep and slow.

"Oh no," whispered Jhanjhar, his voice all wobbly. "That must be a giant monster, thumping this way to gobble me up!"

He ducked behind the nearest bush and peeked out with one round eye.

Nothing came. Only the leaves, swaying back and forth in the wind. Only a little bird, settling into its nest.

But then — BOOM-BOOM-BOOM!

"I am NOT going to just stand here and be gobbled," said Jhanjhar. So he puffed up his courage like a balloon and crept forward.

Tip-toe. Tip-toe. One soft paw after another, past the tall wavy grass.

BOOM-BOOM-BOOM!

Tip-toe. Tip-toe. Around the big grey rocks, his tummy pressed low to the ground.

BOOM-BOOM-BOOM! Louder now. Closer now.

And there, under the biggest tree in the forest, leaning against its wide old trunk — were two big war drums.

Someone had left them there long ago. And the evening wind was bumping and nudging them, so their loose skins went BOOM... BOOM... BOOM — all by themselves!

Jhanjhar blinked. He blinked again.

He tiptoed right up to the first drum and sniffed it. Sniff-sniff.

It was hollow. Empty inside. And quite, quite harmless.

He sniffed the second drum. Sniff-sniff. Just the same — hollow and empty and harmless.

"Ha!" laughed Jhanjhar — a big, bouncy laugh. "It was only the wind, playing the drums like music! There was never any monster at all — just silly me, scared of the wind!"

He felt so light and giggly that he gave one drum a gentle tap with his paw. Tap-tap. It went BOOM-BOOM back at him, and that made him laugh all over again.

Then Jhanjhar curled himself into a cozy little ball right beside the quiet drums, in the very last warm sunbeam of the day.

His tummy was still singing its empty song... but somehow, he did not mind one bit.

The wind hummed softly through the leaves. The drums were silent now. And Jhanjhar drifted off to sleep, dreaming of a breakfast as big as the moon.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Loud noises are not always scary things — look before you fear.
