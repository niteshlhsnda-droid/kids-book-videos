# The Golden Peacock

*A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a green forest full of mango trees, there lived a peacock named Rio.

Rio was not an ordinary peacock. His feathers shone like the morning sun — gold and green and blue, all at once. When he opened his tail, it looked like a hundred little suns had come out to play.

Every morning, as the sun peeped over the hills, Rio stood on a smooth grey rock and sang a sweet song.

"May all my friends be safe," he sang. "May all my friends be happy. May the forest be kind to everyone today."

The little birds loved his song. The deer loved his song. Even the sleepy old tortoise opened one eye and smiled.

But far away, in a grand palace, lived King Kabir. One day, the king heard about the golden peacock.

"A golden peacock!" said the king. "I want him in my garden. Send my best hunter to catch him!"

So the hunter Hari packed his bag and walked into the forest. He was very good at catching animals. He had a net, and he had a plan.

"I will hide behind this big tree," said Hari. "And when the peacock comes to sing, I will catch him!"

The next morning, Rio flew to his smooth grey rock. He shook out his golden feathers. And he began to sing.

"May all my friends be safe," sang Rio. "May all my friends be happy. May the forest be kind to everyone today."

Hari stood behind the tree, holding his net. But as he listened, his hands grew still.

"What a beautiful song," whispered Hari. "He is not singing for himself. He is singing for all his friends."

Rio sang again, softer and sweeter. The morning light fell on his golden tail like warm honey.

Hari looked at his net. Then he looked at the peacock. And his heart felt very full and very soft.

"I cannot catch a singer like this," said Hari. "His song is a gift to the whole forest."

So Hari put down his net. He walked back to the palace and told the king everything.

"Your Majesty," said Hari, "the golden peacock sings a song of kindness every morning. If we take him away, the forest will lose its song. Let him stay free, and his song will be a gift to us all."

King Kabir thought for a long, long time. Then he smiled.

"You are right," said the king. "Let the golden peacock sing in his forest. And from today, no hunter shall catch any bird in that forest. It will be a safe forest, for everyone."

Hari hurried back to tell Rio the good news. Rio was so happy that he opened his tail wide — gold and green and blue, all at once — and sang his sweetest song of all.

"May all my friends be safe," sang Rio. "May all my friends be happy. May the forest be kind to everyone today."

And from that day on, every morning, the forest woke up to Rio's song. And everyone — the birds, the deer, the tortoise, and even the king in his palace — smiled, and felt safe, and felt loved.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A kind heart and a gentle song can keep us safer than any cage.*
