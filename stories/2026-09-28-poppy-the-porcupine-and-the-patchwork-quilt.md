# Poppy the Porcupine and the Patchwork Quilt

At the foot of a mossy hill, in a snug den lined with soft ferns, lived a young porcupine named Poppy. Her quills were warm chestnut, and she had the roundest nose in the forest.

Poppy owned a patchwork quilt sewn from a hundred scraps of cloth — blue squares, yellow squares, one with tiny stars, and one soft red square that was her favorite. But the quilt was old, its edges frayed, some patches worn thin as leaves.

"It's so shabby," Poppy sighed, pulling the quilt around her shoulders. "I wish I had a brand-new quilt, all in one piece, with no patches at all."

The next morning she set out to find one. The badger's big striped blanket dragged on the ground. The rabbit warren's smooth green quilt made her quills itch. And the crows' shiny quilt crinkled and crackled all night long.

Poppy trudged home, grumpier than ever. "Nothing fits. Nothing is right."

That evening, a cold wind came whistling down the hill. It found a thin, worn patch in Poppy's quilt and slipped right through. Poppy shivered and pulled the quilt tighter — and heard a tiny tap at her door.

It was a wren, no bigger than a pinecone. "I heard you shivering," she chirped, laying a bundle of the softest down feathers on the floor.

Then an old field mouse arrived with a square of warm red cloth. "For the thin patch," he squeaked. "Every quilt needs mending now and then."

Poppy fetched her grandmother's sewing basket and stitched the red cloth over the worn patch, tucking the soft feathers inside. As she sewed, she looked at the quilt properly for the first time in ages.

The blue square — her grandmother had cut it from her own winter shawl. The yellow one — the wren's mother had given it years ago. The square with tiny stars — the field mouse had traded three acorns for it. Every patch was a gift. Every patch was someone saying, "I love you, little Poppy."

Her eyes felt warm and prickly. "I have been grumbling about the most loved quilt in the forest," she whispered.

She wrapped herself in the mended quilt. The wind whistled outside, but inside the den it was warm as fresh bread. The wren nestled into the feathers, the field mouse curled up on the red square, and Poppy held them both close, her heart fuller than her tummy after berry pie.

"Thank you," she said softly, to the quilt, to her friends, to the whole kind night. "Thank you for every single patch."

The wind sang over the hill, but Poppy did not hear it. She was already drifting off, wrapped in a hundred little loves.

Sleep now, little Poppy.

The end. 🌙
