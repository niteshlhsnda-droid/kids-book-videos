Birbal and the Two Beggars

A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Outside the grand palace in Delhi sat two beggars. They sat side by side every day, with two little bowls in front of them.

One beggar was called Raju. Every morning, Raju would smile and say, "God will provide."

The other beggar was called Kalu. Every morning, Kalu would say, "The King will provide."

One day, King Akbar was walking past the palace gate with his clever minister Birbal, and he heard them both.

"God will provide," said Raju.

"The King will provide," said Kalu.

The king chuckled. "Birbal, let us see who is right. Let us give them a little test."

Birbal smiled. "A very good idea, Your Majesty. But let us make it a kind test."

So the king asked the royal cook to bake a big, golden loaf of bread. And inside the soft, warm loaf, the cook hid ten shiny gold coins.

The next morning, King Akbar walked up to Kalu, the beggar who always said, "The King will provide."

"Here is a gift for you," said the king, and he placed the warm loaf in Kalu's hands.

Kalu beamed. "Thank you, Your Majesty! The King provides, just as I always said!"

But as Kalu held the loaf, he frowned a little. "This bread feels heavy," he thought. "I would much rather have a nice hot meal instead."

So Kalu turned to his friend Raju and said, "Raju, would you like this loaf? I will sell it to you for two small coins, so I can buy a nice hot meal instead."

Raju was hungry. "Two coins for a whole loaf? Yes, please!" he said, and he handed Kalu the coins and took the bread.

Kalu hurried off happily to buy his hot meal. Raju sat down and broke the loaf open to eat it.

And what did he find inside? Ten shiny gold coins, sparkling in the sunlight!

"Oh!" cried Raju, his eyes wide with wonder. "Look at this! God has provided after all, in the most surprising way!"

When King Akbar heard what had happened, he laughed and clapped his hands. "Birbal, your test worked beautifully," he said.

Birbal smiled. "You see, Your Majesty, goodness finds its way to a trusting heart."

The king called both beggars to the palace. He gave Kalu a warm meal and a small pouch of coins too, saying kindly, "The King provides as well — but sometimes in his own time."

Raju used his gold to open a tiny tea stall, where he gave a free cup of tea to anyone who was hungry. And every evening, both beggars sat together under the big banyan tree, sharing stories and warm bread, happy and safe as the stars came out.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Goodness and trust are rewarded in unexpected ways.
