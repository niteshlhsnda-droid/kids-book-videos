The Moon Shares Its Light

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

The moon loved its light. It kept every beam for itself, shining bright and proud all alone. Being the brightest thing in the sky was all the happiness it needed.

But down below, the night animals stumbled in the dark. Tilda the turtle bumped into a rock. Bodhi the bunny tripped over a root. The dark was too deep for their eyes, and they were getting frustrated.

"Excuse me, Moon," called Tilda politely. "Could you share a little light? We cannot see our way."

The moon hesitated. "But it is MY light," it said. "If I share it, I will have less."

Just then, a little firefly floated up, its glow no bigger than a spark. "I share my light every night," it said. "And I never run out. Sharing makes it grow."

The moon had never thought of that. Curious, it broke off one small beam — just a sliver of silver — and gave it to Tilda the turtle.

"Share a little light, and it shines twice as bright," sang the firefly.

Tilda glowed softly, and now she could see the path. She gave a happy, relieved sigh. The moon watched closely — and it did not feel dimmer at all!

So the moon broke off another beam and gave it to Bodhi the bunny. Bodhi's ears glowed pink, and he hopped home safely. "Share a little light, and it shines twice as bright," sang the firefly again.

The moon felt wonderful — sharing was fun! It gave a beam to the sleepy owl, and another to the tiny mice. Soon the whole meadow glowed gently. "Share a little light, and it shines twice as bright," sang the firefly one last time.

The moon looked down. The meadow was beautiful, every animal with its own little glow. And the moon? It shone brighter than ever. The firefly was right — sharing had made its light grow.

The animals looked up. "Thank you, Moon!" they called. The moon felt warm inside. It had never felt this happy alone.

One by one, the animals fell asleep in the gentle glow. The moon watched over them proudly. It was no longer just bright. It was kind.

Sleep now, little moon. You shared your light beautifully. Rest knowing the meadow dreams in your gentle glow.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When you share what you have, little one, it shines even brighter.
