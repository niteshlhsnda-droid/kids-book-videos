The Lad and the North Wind

A timeless Norwegian folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once, in a little wooden house at the edge of a snowy forest — where woodsmoke curled from the chimney and the snow squeaked underfoot — lived a poor boy named Arjun and his mother.

One winter morning, Arjun carried a small sack of flour to make bread. But as he crossed the hill — whoooosh! — the North Wind came roaring down and blew every bit of the flour away.

Arjun was not angry. He simply walked up the hill, cupped his hands around his mouth, and called out, "Dear North Wind, please give back our flour. We have so little."

The North Wind's roar softened into a long, chilly sigh. He felt a little sorry.

"I ate your flour, and I cannot give it back," he rumbled. "But take this little stone hand-mill instead — small enough to carry in both hands, with a tiny wooden handle to turn. Say, 'Grind, little mill,' and name what you want — and it will grind it for you. When you have enough, say, 'Hush, little mill,' and it will stop."

Arjun thanked the Wind and hurried home. "Grind, little mill, grind us bread!" he said — and out came warm, golden bread! His mother laughed with joy.

The next day, Arjun had to travel to the next village. On the way, he stopped at an inn to rest. The innkeeper was a greedy man with hungry eyes. While Arjun slept, the innkeeper crept in and stole the little mill.

When Arjun woke, the mill was gone. But he only smiled a small, clever smile. Can you guess what Arjun knew that the innkeeper didn't?

That very night, the greedy innkeeper rubbed his hands and whispered, "Grind, little mill, grind me salt! I'll sell it at the market and get rich!"

Out poured the salt — a hill of salt, then a mountain of salt, then a whole white mountain of salt! The innkeeper shouted, "Stop! Stop!" But he did not know the words to stop it.

Soon salt was piled to the windows. The innkeeper waded through it, sneezing and coughing, and begged everyone for help. But nobody knew what to say.

Just then, Arjun knocked on the door. "Oh my," he said politely. "Hush, little mill."

The mill fell silent at once.

The innkeeper's face went white with fright — and then pink with shame. "Take it back, take it back!" he cried. "I am glad to be rid of it!"

Arjun picked up his little mill and walked home through the soft snow. From that day on, he and his mother never went hungry — and the innkeeper never stole anything again. For kindness is always rewarded — and greed gently comes undone.
