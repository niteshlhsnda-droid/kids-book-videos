# The Vain Jackdaw

*A timeless Aesop's fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green valley lived a little jackdaw named Jacko. He was a clever bird, with shiny black feathers and bright, curious eyes.

But Jacko had one big wish. Every morning, he watched the peacocks in the meadow open their great tails — shimmering blue and green and gold, like a hundred little suns.

"Oh," sighed Jacko, "if only I had feathers like that! Then everyone would look at me, and no one would look at the peacocks."

One day, Jacko found a pile of peacock feathers lying in the grass, where the peacocks had been tidying their feathers. His eyes grew round with delight.

"What a lucky day!" he chirped. He picked up the long feathers one by one and tucked them into his own black wings and tail. Blue here, green there, gold all around.

Then Jacko strutted into the meadow, holding his head very high. "Look at me!" he cawed. "Am I not the most beautiful bird you have ever seen?"

The peacocks turned and stared. Then the oldest peacock stepped forward. "Those are OUR feathers," she said sternly. And one by one, the peacocks plucked their feathers back — pluck, pluck, pluck! — until poor Jacko stood there in only his own plain black feathers, feeling very small.

With drooping wings, Jacko flew back to the jackdaw tree. But when he landed, the other jackdaws looked at him coldly.

"You tried to be a peacock," said his friend Jini. "You were ashamed of being a jackdaw — one of us."

Jacko hung his head. "I am sorry," he whispered. "My own black feathers were never ugly. I only thought they were."

Jini hopped closer and nudged him with her beak. "Silly Jacko. Black feathers shine like midnight silk when the sun touches them. Come, let us fly together."

So Jacko flew with his friends, and as the evening sun turned his black wings to deep, shining blue-black, he laughed a happy jackdaw laugh.

And from that day on, he never wished to be anyone but himself.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Borrowed feathers never fit — your own true self is the most beautiful thing to be.*
