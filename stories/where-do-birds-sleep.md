# Where Do Birds Sleep?

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

At bedtime, little Fari had a question.

"Mama, where do birds sleep?" she asked.

She felt curious.

"Let's find out tomorrow," said Mama.

The next evening, they went for a walk.

The sun was going down.

"Listen," said Mama.

They heard chirps and tweets in the big banyan tree.

"Up there," whispered Mama.

Fari looked up.

She saw birds settling onto branches.

They fluffed their feathers.

They tucked their heads under their wings.

"They sleep in the tree," said Fari.

She felt surprised.

"Some do," said Mama.

They walked further.

They passed a little birdhouse on a pole.

A tiny bird peeked out of the round door.

"Hello in there," giggled Fari.

She felt delighted.

"Some sleep in birdhouses," said Mama.

They walked to the pond.

Ducks floated on the water.

Their eyes were closed.

"They sleep on the water?" asked Fari.

She felt amazed.

"They nap with one eye open," said Mama.

"Clever ducks."

Fari counted the sleepy ducks.

One, two, three.

Three sleepy ducks.

Then they heard a soft hoot.

Hoo, hoo.

An owl opened its big eyes in the old neem tree.

"Is the owl waking up?" asked Fari.

"Owls sleep in the day," said Mama.

"And wake up at night."

Fari felt thoughtful.

Birds slept in trees.

Birds slept in birdhouses.

Birds slept on ponds.

"Wherever they feel safe," said Mama.

"Just like you feel safe in your bed."

Fari felt cozy inside.

That night, Fari tucked her toy bird into its nest.

"Sleep, sleep, sleep," she whispered.

That was three times she whispered it.

She felt sleepy too.

Outside, the birds in the banyan tree were quiet.

The ducks drifted on the dark pond.

The owl hooted softly.

Hoo.

Sleep now, little bird-watcher.

Dream of feathery nests in quiet trees.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Everyone sleeps best where they feel safe.*
