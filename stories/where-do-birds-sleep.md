# Where Do Birds Sleep?

*An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

At bedtime, little Fari had a question. "Mama, where do birds sleep?" she asked, her eyes bright with curiosity.

"Let's find out tomorrow," Mama said.

The next evening they went for a walk as the sun was going down. "Listen," said Mama. Chirps and tweets rustled through the big banyan tree. "Up there," Mama whispered. Fari looked up to see birds settling onto the branches — fluffing their feathers, tucking their heads under their wings. "They sleep in the tree," Fari said, surprised. "Some do," said Mama.

They walked further and passed a little birdhouse on a pole, where a tiny bird peeked out of the round door. "Hello in there," Fari giggled, delighted. "Some sleep in birdhouses," said Mama.

Then they reached the pond, where ducks floated with their eyes closed. "They sleep on the water?" asked Fari, amazed. "They nap with one eye open," said Mama. "Clever ducks." Fari counted the sleepy ducks — one, two, three. Three sleepy ducks.

Just then they heard a soft hoot — hoo, hoo — and an owl opened its big eyes in the old neem tree. "Is the owl waking up?" asked Fari. "Owls sleep in the day," said Mama, "and wake up at night."

Fari thought it all over. Birds slept in trees, birds slept in birdhouses, birds slept on ponds. "Wherever they feel safe," said Mama, "just like you feel safe in your bed." Fari felt cozy all the way inside.

That night she tucked her toy bird into its nest. "Sleep, sleep, sleep," she whispered — three times she whispered it. She felt sleepy too.

Outside, the birds in the banyan tree were quiet. The ducks drifted on the dark pond. The owl hooted softly. Hoo.

Sleep now, little bird-watcher. Dream of feathery nests in quiet trees.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Everyone sleeps best wherever they feel safe.*
