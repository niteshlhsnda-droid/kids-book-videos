# The Two-Headed Bird

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a big green forest, there lived a very special bird.

His name was Bharunda. He had one soft, feathery body — and two heads!

One head was called Bholu. The other head was called Chintu.

Bholu and Chintu shared everything — one pair of wings, one little nest in a tall mango tree, and one tummy.

Every morning, the two heads woke up together and said, "Good morning!" to the sun.

"Good morning, Bholu!" said Chintu.

"Good morning, Chintu!" said Bholu.

Then they flew off together to look for breakfast.

One sunny morning, Bholu spotted something shiny in the mango tree. It was a golden mango — the sweetest, juiciest mango in the whole forest!

"Oh, look!" cried Bholu. "A golden mango! All for me!"

And before Chintu could say a word, Bholu gobbled it all up. Slurp, slurp, slurp!

Chintu watched with sad eyes. "Bholu," he said quietly, "we share one tummy. That mango went into OUR tummy. Why didn't you share it with me?"

Bholu just laughed. "I found it first!"

Chintu felt a little ache in his heart. It was not a tummy-ache. It was a lonely ache, the kind you get when someone forgets to share.

The next morning, Chintu spotted something shiny under a bush. It was a big purple berry. It looked sweet and juicy.

Bholu's eyes went wide. "Oh, a berry! Let us share it!"

But Chintu was still feeling hurt. "No," he said. "You did not share your mango with me. So I will not share my berry with you."

And he gobbled the purple berry all up. Slurp, slurp, slurp!

But oh no. That purple berry was not a good berry at all. It was a bitter, prickly berry.

A moment later, both heads felt a terrible tummy-ache. Their shared tummy rumbled and grumbled. "Grrr! Grr!"

"Ouch!" cried Bholu.

"Ouch!" cried Chintu.

The two heads looked at each other. They were both in pain — because they shared one tummy, and what hurt one head hurt them both.

Just then, the wise old owl flew down from the mango tree. She brought cool water in a leaf cup and soft, green leaves to soothe their tummy.

"Little bird," hooted the owl softly, "you have one body and one tummy. When one head is greedy, both heads feel the hurt. When one head is kind, both heads feel the joy."

Bholu looked at Chintu. "I am sorry," he said. "I should have shared my mango."

Chintu looked at Bholu. "And I am sorry too," he said. "I should not have eaten that berry just to get even."

From that day on, Bholu and Chintu shared everything. When they found a mango, they took turns having the first bite. When they found a berry, they sniffed it together first, to check that it was good.

And do you know what? The mangoes tasted sweeter when they were shared. The mornings felt happier when they were shared too.

That night, the two heads tucked themselves into their nest, side by side, and whispered together, "Good night, moon. Good night, stars. Good night, forest."

And they fell asleep with full, happy tummies and even happier hearts.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: When we share, everyone feels the joy — and when we don't, everyone feels the hurt.*
