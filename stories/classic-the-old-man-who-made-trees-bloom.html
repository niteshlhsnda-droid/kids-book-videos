The Old Man Who Made the Trees Bloom

A timeless Japanese folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in a little village in Japan, lived a kind old man named Grandpa Hana. He loved trees more than anything in the world. Every morning he walked among the cherry trees, patting their trunks and whispering, "Grow tall, grow strong, and bloom beautifully."

Grandpa Hana had a little white dog named Shiro, who followed him everywhere, wagging his fluffy tail.

One winter's day, Shiro began digging under a bare cherry tree — dig, dig, dig! — and out popped a little wooden box, full of shining gold coins!

"Goodness me!" laughed Grandpa Hana. "This must be a treasure for someone kind."

He took the box to the village and shared every coin with his neighbours. Everyone had warm rice and new sandals that winter, and everyone thanked Grandpa Hana and little Shiro.

But next door lived a grumpy neighbour named Mr. Grumble, who was never happy with what he had. "If that old man's dog can find gold," he muttered, "my dog can find even more!"

So Mr. Grumble borrowed Shiro and took him to his own garden. "Dig, dog, dig!" he ordered. But Shiro only sniffed the ground and wagged his tail. No gold appeared at all.

Mr. Grumble grew crosser and crosser — and then he did a very unkind thing. He was so cross that he shouted and stamped, and poor Shiro ran all the way home, trembling, to Grandpa Hana.

Grandpa Hana hugged Shiro tight. "Never mind, little friend," he said softly. "You are safe now."

Many winters passed, and Shiro grew old and grey, with a slow, happy wag. One cold night, he curled up by the warm fireplace, closed his eyes, and fell into a long, peaceful sleep — the kind of sleep from which old, tired friends do not wake up.

Grandpa Hana was very sad, but he kept Shiro close in his heart. That night, he dreamed of Shiro, wagging his fluffy tail, who seemed to say, "Do not be sad, dear master. Take my ashes, and sprinkle them on the cherry trees."

In the morning, Grandpa Hana carried a little pot of ashes to the cherry trees. "Grow tall, grow strong, and bloom beautifully," he whispered — and he sprinkled the ashes over the branches.

And oh, what a wonder! In the very next moment, every bare branch burst into bloom! Pink blossoms opened all at once, soft as clouds, sweet as spring rain. The whole village came running to see, and everyone laughed and clapped in delight.

The king himself heard about the wonderful blossoms, and he invited Grandpa Hana to his palace. There, Grandpa Hana sprinkled a pinch of ashes on the palace cherry tree — and it bloomed more beautifully than any tree in the land!

The king smiled and gave Grandpa Hana a fine silk robe and a basket of rice cakes. But Grandpa Hana smiled right back and said, "The best gift is seeing everyone happy under the blossom trees."

Mr. Grumble watched all this and felt very small inside. He walked up to Grandpa Hana and said, very quietly, "I am sorry I was unkind to Shiro. May I help you plant a new cherry tree?"

"Of course," said Grandpa Hana with a warm smile. "Kind hands make the best gardeners."

And together they planted a young cherry tree in the village square. Every spring after that, it bloomed pink and lovely — a reminder that kindness, not greed, makes beautiful things grow.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness makes beautiful things bloom; greed only makes grumbles grow.
