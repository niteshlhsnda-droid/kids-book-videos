The Seven Ravens

A timeless Grimm tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there lived a mother and father who had seven sons and one little daughter named Sofia.

The seven brothers were strong and noisy and full of fun. They loved to race each other to the top of the hill, splash in the stream, and build towers out of sticks.

Little Sofia loved her brothers more than anything in the world. She would clap her hands and laugh when they played, and they would carry her on their shoulders like a little queen.

But one day, something very sad happened.

The brothers had gone to fetch water from the well, and they were taking far too long. Their father grew impatient. "Those lazy boys!" he cried in anger. "I wish they would all turn into ravens and fly away!"

No sooner had he spoken the angry words than — flap, flap, flap! — seven big black ravens rose up from the well and flew away over the trees, cawing sadly.

The father was horrified. "Oh, what have I said?" he cried. "My poor boys!"

Sofia was not frightened. She was brave, and she loved her brothers. "I will bring them home," she said. "I will walk to the end of the world if I must."

So Sofia packed a little bundle: a loaf of bread, a bottle of water, and a warm shawl. Then she set off, all alone, down the long road.

She walked and walked until she came to the Sun. "Dear Sun," she said, "have you seen seven ravens?"

The Sun was very hot and bright. "I am too hot to help you, little girl," said the Sun. "But go on, and you will find the Moon."

So Sofia walked on until she came to the Moon. "Dear Moon," she said, "have you seen seven ravens?"

The Moon was cold and pale. "I am too cold to help you," said the Moon. "But go on, and you will find the Morning Star."

So Sofia walked on, through the night, until she met the Morning Star, twinkling gently in the sky.

"Dear Morning Star," said Sofia, "have you seen my seven brothers, who are now ravens?"

The Morning Star twinkled kindly. "Yes," she said. "They live on the Glass Mountain, far away. Here is a little golden key. It will open the door of the mountain. And here is a loaf of bread and a cup of water, for your journey."

Sofia thanked the Morning Star and walked on until she came to the Glass Mountain. It shone and sparkled in the sunlight, smooth as a mirror.

Sofia took the little golden key, fitted it into the tiny golden door — click! — and the door swung open.

Inside, she found seven little cups and seven little plates, all laid out for supper. And perched on seven little chairs were seven black ravens.

Sofia's heart leaped. She took a sip from each little cup and a nibble from each little plate, and as she did, the spell began to melt away like snow in spring.

One by one, the ravens fluttered and changed — feathers fell away, wings turned into arms — until there stood her seven brothers, laughing and cheering!

"Sofia! You found us!" they cried, and they hugged her tight.

Then Sofia led her brothers all the way home. Their father ran out to meet them, tears of joy in his eyes. "Forgive my angry words," he said, hugging all eight of his children at once.

And from that day on, the family never let an angry word fly out of the door again. They lived happily together, and Sofia's brave heart was the story everyone told at bedtime.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Love and courage can undo any spell — and angry words should never be spoken in haste.
