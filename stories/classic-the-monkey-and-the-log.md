# The Monkey and the Log

*A timeless Panchatantra tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a green forest beside a little village, a band of monkeys played among the trees all day long.

One morning, the monkeys found a big wooden log lying near the village carpenter's hut. A sharp wedge of wood was stuck in a crack in the log, holding the crack wide open.

"What is this?" chattered a curious young monkey named Minku. "It looks like a wonderful toy!"

The older monkeys warned him, "Leave it alone, Minku. That log belongs to the carpenter, and the wedge is doing an important job."

But Minku was very curious. He climbed onto the log, sat right in the middle of the crack, and gave the wedge a mighty tug.

"Look at me!" he laughed. "I am so strong!"

Pop! The wedge came flying out — and the crack in the log snapped shut, right around Minku's tail!

"Oh no! Oh no!" squeaked Minku. "My tail is stuck! Help! Help!"

The monkeys rushed over. They pulled gently. They pushed softly. They wiggled the log this way and that. But the log held Minku's tail fast.

"Do not worry, Minku," said Grandmother Monkey, the wisest monkey in the band. "Panicking never helped anyone. Let us think, slowly and together."

Grandmother Monkey looked at the log very carefully. "The wedge held the crack open," she said. "If we put the wedge back, the crack will open again, and your tail will be free."

So the strongest monkeys lifted the wedge together — heave, ho, heave, ho — and slid it gently back into the crack.

Creak! The crack opened wide. Minku hopped free, tail and all, and did a happy little dance.

"Thank you! Thank you!" laughed Minku, hugging Grandmother Monkey. "I will never meddle with things that are not mine again — especially not wedges!"

From that day on, whenever Minku felt curious about something strange, he stopped and asked, "Is this mine to touch? Is it safe?"

And whenever a young monkey forgot, all the monkeys would grin and say, "Remember Minku and the log!"

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Curiosity is wonderful, but it is wise to ask before touching things that are not yours.*
