The Wolf and the Crane

A timeless Aesop fable for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

One evening in the forest, a big grey wolf named Wilbur sat down to a fine supper. He had found a tasty bone, and he was crunching it happily.

Crunch, crunch, crunch.

But all of a sudden — gulp! — a sharp piece of the bone stuck in Wilbur's throat.

"Oh! Oh!" coughed Wilbur. "It is stuck! It hurts! Whatever shall I do?"

Just then, a tall, graceful crane named Cara flew down to the riverbank. She had long legs, soft white feathers, and a very long beak.

"Dear Cara," said Wilbur in a hoarse, sore voice, "please help me. There is a bone stuck in my throat, and your long beak could reach it. I will be so grateful."

Cara looked at Wilbur's great big teeth. She felt a little nervous. But she could see that Wilbur was truly in trouble, and a kind heart is braver than fear.

"Very well," said Cara. "Hold very still, and keep your mouth wide open."

Wilbur opened his mouth wide. Cara stretched her long neck in, in, in — and with her long beak, she plucked the bone out as neatly as you please.

"There!" said Cara, dropping the bone on the grass. "All better."

Wilbur swallowed. He took a deep breath. The hurt was gone.

"Thank you," he said, in a small, gruff voice. Then he turned to walk away.

"Wait," said Cara. "You said you would be grateful. Is that all?"

Wilbur stopped. His ears went pink. He had been so happy that the hurt was gone that he had forgotten his manners entirely.

"You are right," said Wilbur. "I did promise. And a promise is a promise."

So Wilbur trotted off into the forest, and soon he came back carrying a big, silvery fish, fresh from the stream.

"For you," said Wilbur, laying the fish gently at Cara's feet. "Thank you for helping me, even when you felt nervous. You were brave and kind, and I will not forget it."

Cara smiled a wide, happy smile. "A thank-you like that is the best reward of all," she said.

And from that day on, whenever Wilbur saw Cara by the river, he waved his big paw and called, "Hello, my brave friend!" And Cara always waved her long wing back.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: When someone helps you, say thank you properly — a kind thank-you is the best reward of all.
