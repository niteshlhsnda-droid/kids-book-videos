# The Hare in the Moon

*A timeless Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long ago, in a quiet meadow full of soft grass and sweet clover, there lived a kind little hare named Chandu. Every evening, Chandu nibbled clover and watched the moon rise, round and silver, over the hills.

One evening, as the stars began to twinkle, a tired traveller came walking down the meadow path. His feet were dusty, his bag was empty, and his tummy rumbled like a little drum.

"Good evening, little hare," said the traveller with a weary smile. "I have walked all day, and I have not a single crumb left to eat."

Chandu's heart felt warm with kindness. He hopped to his favourite clover patch and gathered the sweetest, softest leaves he could find.

"Please share my supper," said Chandu. "There is plenty for us both."

The traveller sat down on the grass and ate the clover leaves. "How kind you are!" he said. "You gave me your very best supper, and you asked for nothing at all."

Chandu twitched his long ears happily. "Sharing makes supper taste twice as good," he said.

The traveller smiled a mysterious smile, and as he stood up to go, his dusty cloak shimmered like starlight. For this was no ordinary traveller — it was the Moon himself, walking the earth in disguise to find the kindest heart in the meadow!

"Little hare," said the Moon, "your kindness deserves to be remembered forever."

That night, when the Moon rose over the hills, all the animals gasped. There, painted softly among the Moon's silver light, was the shape of a little hare — long ears, round eyes, and a happy smile.

"Look!" squeaked the field mice. "Chandu is in the Moon!"

And even now, when you look up at the full moon on a clear night, you can see the little hare sitting there, smiling down at the meadow where he once shared his supper.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: The smallest act of kindness can shine for a very long time.*
