The Rabbit in the Moon

A gentle world folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long ago, in a forest where fireflies danced like tiny lanterns, four good friends lived together.

There was Miko the monkey, who could pick the ripest mangoes. There was Otto the otter, who could catch the silvery fish. There was Jax the jackal, who knew where the sweetest berries grew.

And there was Ruby the rabbit — the smallest of them all, with fur as soft as dandelion fluff.

One cold evening, a tired old traveler knocked at their hollow-tree home. His cloak was thin, and his tummy rumbled like a little drum.

"Welcome, welcome!" said the four friends. "Please share our supper!"

Miko swung up a tree and brought down golden mangoes. Otto dived into the stream and brought up shiny fish. Jax trotted off and returned with a basket of berries.

But Ruby had nothing to give. She had only grass for supper, and the old traveler could not eat grass.

Ruby thought and thought. Then her face lit up like the moon.

"I may be small," she said, "but I have a warm burrow and the softest songs. Tonight I will sing you to sleep, and you shall rest in my cozy burrow, safe and warm."

And she sang the sweetest lullaby, in her tiny rabbit voice, until the old traveler's eyes grew heavy and he smiled in his sleep.

When the traveler woke, his cloak had turned to silver moonlight — for he was the Moon Spirit himself!

"Dear Ruby," he said, "your friends gave me food, but you gave me your kindness and your song. That is the greatest gift of all."

And with a wave of his silver hand, he painted Ruby's shape — her round head, her long ears — upon the face of the moon.

That is why, on clear nights, if you look up at the moon, you can still see the little rabbit there, singing her soft lullaby to all the children of the world.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: The kindest gifts are not things — they are love, comfort, and a song from the heart.
