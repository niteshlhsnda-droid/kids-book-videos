Ella Is Kind to Someone New

An original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Ella was a young elephant calf who lived by the big waterhole, where she had many friends. One morning, a new family arrived — and with them a small foal named Zip, standing alone at the edge. He looked shy. He did not know anyone.

Ella felt curious and walked over slowly. "Hello! I am Ella," she said.

Zip looked up. "Hello," he said quietly. "I am Zip. We just moved here."

Ella could see he felt nervous, and she remembered her own first day. She wanted to be kind. Mama Elephant always said, "Kindness is a gift we all can give."

"Kindness is a gift we all can give," Ella said to herself. "Kindness is a gift we all can give," she said again. "Kindness is a gift we all can give," she said one more time.

Ella smiled at Zip. "Want to see the best splashing spot?" she asked. Zip's eyes lit up. "Yes, please!"

She showed him around — the shallow end for splashing, the shady tree for naps, the sweet grass patch — and introduced him to her friends. "This is Zip," she said proudly. They all said hello and played water games together. Splash, splish, splash! Zip laughed and laughed. He did not feel new anymore. He felt welcome.

At sunset, Zip's mama came over. "Thank you, Ella," she said. "You made our first day wonderful." Ella felt warm inside — kindness felt like sunshine.

That night, Ella lay beside her mama thinking about Zip's smile. She had been kind, and she felt happy. The stars reflected in the waterhole as she closed her eyes. She was kind. She was loved. Goodnight, Ella.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: A little kindness can make a brand-new friend feel right at home.
