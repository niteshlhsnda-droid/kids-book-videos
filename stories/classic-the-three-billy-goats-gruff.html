The Three Billy Goats Gruff

A beloved Norse folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, there were three billy goats who lived on a hillside.

There was the littlest billy goat, whose voice went, "Trip, trap, trip, trap," ever so softly.

There was the middle-sized billy goat, whose voice went, "Trip, trap, trip, trap," a little louder.

And there was the great big billy goat, whose voice went, "TRIP, TRAP, TRIP, TRAP!" very loud indeed.

All three goats were named Gruff, and all three loved sweet green grass more than anything in the world.

One sunny morning, the littlest goat looked across the stream and saw the most beautiful meadow he had ever seen. The grass there was tall and green and sweet, and yellow flowers nodded in the breeze.

"Oh, I must go to that meadow!" said the littlest goat. "But first I must cross the bridge over the stream."

So he trotted down to the bridge and began to cross. "Trip, trap, trip, trap," went his little hooves on the wooden planks.

Suddenly, a deep, grumbly voice roared from under the bridge. "Who is that trip-trapping over my bridge?"

The littlest goat peeped over the edge. Under the bridge sat a troll — big and grey and grumpy, with a rumbling tummy.

"I am only the littlest billy goat," said the little goat in a tiny voice. "I am going to the meadow to eat sweet grass."

"Oh no, you are not!" growled the troll. "I am going to gobble you up!"

"Please don't gobble me," said the littlest goat. "I am very small, and I would make a very small supper. Wait for my brother — he is much bigger, and he is coming soon."

The troll's tummy rumbled. "Hmm," he said. "Very well. You may pass. But I shall wait for your brother."

So the littlest goat trip-trapped across the bridge and began nibbling the sweet grass.

Soon the middle-sized goat came to the bridge. "Trip, trap, trip, trap," went his hooves.

"Who is that trip-trapping over my bridge?" roared the troll.

"I am the middle-sized billy goat," said the goat. "I am going to the meadow to eat sweet grass."

"Oh no, you are not!" growled the troll. "I am going to gobble you up!"

"Please don't gobble me," said the middle-sized goat. "Wait for my big brother — he is much, much bigger, and he is coming soon."

"Hmm," said the troll, and his tummy rumbled louder. "Very well. You may pass."

So the middle-sized goat trip-trapped across the bridge and joined his brother in the meadow.

Then came the great big billy goat. "TRIP, TRAP, TRIP, TRAP!" went his great big hooves, and the whole bridge shook.

"WHO IS THAT TRIP-TRAPPING OVER MY BRIDGE?" roared the troll, jumping up.

"I am the great big billy goat!" said the big goat in his great big voice. "And I have something to say to you, Mr Troll. You sound very hungry — and very lonely, too."

The troll blinked. Nobody had ever said such a kind thing to him before.

"You see," said the big goat gently, "there is plenty of sweet grass in the meadow for everyone — even for a troll. Will you come and share it with us?"

The troll's grumpy face softened. "Share? With me?" he whispered. "Nobody has ever shared with me before."

So the great big goat led the troll across the bridge and into the meadow. The three goats showed him the sweetest grass, and the troll munched happily beside them.

From that day on, the troll was no longer grumpy. He kept the bridge tidy, he waved to everyone who crossed it, and every evening he shared supper with the three billy goats Gruff in the sunny meadow.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Sharing turns a grump into a friend — there is always enough sweet grass for everyone.
