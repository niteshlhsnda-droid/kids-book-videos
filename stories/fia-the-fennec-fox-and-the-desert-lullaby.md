# Fia the Fennec Fox and the Desert Lullaby

*A gentle original bedtime tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a wide, sandy desert, where the dunes rolled like golden waves, there lived a little fennec fox named Fia.

Fia had the biggest, softest ears in the whole desert. She could hear a beetle tiptoeing, a seed sighing, and a star twinkling far, far away.

But at bedtime, Fia had one problem: she could not fall asleep. The desert wind would howl around her burrow — WHOOO! — and Fia's big ears heard every single howl.

"Oh, wind," Fia whispered one night, pulling her sandy blanket up to her chin. "Please do not howl so loudly. I am trying to sleep."

But the wind only howled louder. WHOOO!

Fia sat up. "If the wind will not be quiet," she said, "then perhaps I can sing it a lullaby."

So Fia took a deep breath and began to sing, soft and sweet:

"Hush, little wind, hush and slow,
Rock the dunes to sleep, soft and low."

To her surprise, the wind slowed down to listen. It swirled gently around her burrow, like it was dancing.

Fia sang on, a little louder, a little braver:

"Sand so gold and sky so deep,
All the desert dreams in sleep."

One by one, the desert animals heard her song. A jerboa stopped hopping and yawned. A sleepy lizard curled up under a stone. Even the moon leaned a little closer to listen.

The wind, who had never heard a lullaby before, whispered happily and settled down among the dunes, as quiet as a sleeping cat.

"Thank you, Fia," whispered the wind. "No one has ever sung me to sleep before."

Fia smiled, tucked her big ears under her blanket, and closed her eyes. And as the stars twinkled above the quiet desert, Fia sang one last sleepy line, just for herself:

"Hush, little Fia, hush and slow…
It is time for dreams to grow."

And she drifted off to sleep, while the whole desert dreamed along with her.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A gentle song can calm even the wildest wind — and yourself too.*
