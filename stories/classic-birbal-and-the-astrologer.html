Birbal and the Astrologer

A timeless Akbar-Birbal tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Long, long ago, in India, there lived a great king named Akbar.

One day, a new man came to the palace. He wore a long robe covered in stars and moons, and a tall, pointy hat. He bowed low before the king.

"Your Majesty," he said, "I am the greatest astrologer in the whole world! I can see the future! I can tell you what will happen tomorrow, next week, and next year!"

The king's eyes went wide. "Really? Then tell me — will next week be a happy week or a gloomy week?"

The astrologer closed his eyes and waved his hands in the air. "Oh, Your Majesty," he said in a deep, spooky voice, "next week will be a very, very gloomy week. Dark clouds will come. Bad things will happen. Be very, very afraid!"

Everyone in the court gasped. The ministers looked worried. The guards shuffled their feet. Even the king tugged at his beard.

But Birbal, the cleverest man in the kingdom, just smiled.

"Oh, wise astrologer," said Birbal, "if you can see the future, you must be able to answer my little questions."

"Of course," said the astrologer, puffing out his chest. "Ask me anything!"

"First," said Birbal, "what will I dream about tonight?"

The astrologer blinked. "Well… that is… I cannot say."

"Second," said Birbal, "which crow will caw first tomorrow morning — the one on the left tree or the one on the right tree?"

The astrologer scratched his head. "Well… that is… I cannot say."

"Third," said Birbal, "will it rain on Tuesday?"

The astrologer looked at his starry robe. He looked at his pointy hat. "Well… that is… I cannot say."

Birbal smiled. "Then how can you tell us about a whole gloomy week, when you cannot tell us about one little dream, one little crow, or one little rain cloud?"

The whole court burst out laughing! The king laughed so hard that his crown wobbled on his head!

The astrologer turned red as a tomato. "You are right," he said quietly. "I was only guessing. Nobody can truly see the future."

King Akbar smiled kindly. "Then let us not worry about tomorrow," he said. "Let us be kind today, and tomorrow will take care of itself."

And do you know what? The next week was not gloomy at all. The sun shone. The birds sang. And the astrologer stayed in the palace — not as a fortune-teller, but as the court's storyteller, telling wonderful stories every evening.

And Birbal? He went home that night and dreamed about sweet mangoes and happy friends — and when he woke up, he smiled, because dreams are the nicest kind of future.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: No one can see tomorrow — kind deeds today make a happy tomorrow.
