# Clover the Chipmunk and the Quiet Leaf Blanket

In a tall oak tree at the edge of Willow Wood lived a small chipmunk named Clover. She had soft russet fur, a cream stripe down her back, and cheeks that puffed up like round acorns when she smiled. Every autumn, she loved one job best: gathering leaves for winter.

This autumn, Clover had a plan. She would gather the softest, warmest leaves in the wood and press them into a thick leaf blanket for her hollow, so cold nights would feel cosy. She took her woven basket and set off while morning light turned the leaves to gold.

First she found a crimson maple leaf, soft as velvet. Then a golden birch leaf that rustled like paper. Beside the brook she found a broad oak leaf, still warm from the sun. "This will be the middle," she whispered, smoothing it flat.

On her way home through the fern hollow, Clover heard a tiny shiver. Under a fallen log sat Squeak, a baby field mouse, curled up tight, his whiskers trembling. His nest had blown apart in last night's wind, and he had nothing soft to sleep on.

Clover did not stop to think. She lifted the softest crimson leaf from her basket and laid it over Squeak like a little blanket. Then she added the golden leaf, and tucked the oak leaf around his feet. His shivering slowed, and he gave a sleepy squeak of thanks. Clover smiled and slipped away quietly.

The next morning, the wind came back, wild and whistling. It tore through the oak tree and scattered Clover's remaining leaves all over the wood. She chased one, then another, but they tumbled over the brook and into the brambles. When the wind settled, her basket was almost empty, and her blanket was gone.

Clover sat on a mossy root and looked at her empty basket. Her cheeks drooped, and she hugged her knees close. She had given her best leaves away, and now she had almost nothing left for cold nights. Perhaps, she thought, she should have kept them after all.

Just then, she heard a rustle, and then another. Squeak came trotting along the path, dragging a soft crimson leaf. Behind him came Juniper, a young squirrel, with a golden leaf, and Briar, a little hedgehog, rolling a broad oak leaf. One by one, they laid them at Clover's feet, until the pile grew soft and high.

Together, they pressed the leaves into a thick blanket, layer upon layer, in Clover's hollow. When night fell, Clover curled beneath it, and Squeak tucked into his own leaf nest close by. Stars blinked through the oak branches, the wind sang softly outside, and inside, all was warm.

Sleep now, little Clover.

The end. 🌙
