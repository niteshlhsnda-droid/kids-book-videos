The Golden Bangle

A timeless Hitopadesha tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a forest by a muddy lake — where the air smelled of wet earth and the frogs went croak-croak at dusk — there lived Bagha the Tiger.

Bagha was old now — his whiskers had turned grey, his teeth weren't sharp anymore, and his paws were too tired to run after his dinner.

"I know!" thought Bagha. "I will trick the travelers into coming close to me — that's much easier than chasing them!" He held up a golden bangle, round and gold, winking and sparkling in the sun. Can you see it in your mind? And he called out in his sweetest voice, "Come, come! Take this golden bangle! It's yours, my dear! I am old and gentle now — I don't chase or pounce anymore!"

Along came Hari, a boy with a little cloth bundle over his shoulder, humming a walking song on his way to his grandmother's house. His grandmother had told him: "Never trust sweet words alone, my boy. Watch what they DO, not just what they SAY."

Hari saw the golden bangle glittering, and his feet crept one step closer. Then he stopped. Hmm — if YOU were Hari, what would you do?

He looked at Bagha more closely — and behind that sweet smile, he spotted long, sharp teeth… and under those soft-looking paws? Can you guess what was hiding? Claws!

"Thank you, Mr. Tiger, but no, thank you!" said Hari, stepping BACK. "I can see your teeth — and your claws! You're not gentle at all!"

And he hurried home to his grandmother's house, safe and sound.

Bagha was left sitting all alone in the mud, holding up his golden bangle to nobody at all. For a tiger may talk sweetly — but those sharp claws told Hari the truth.

That evening, Hari hugged his grandmother. "You saved me today, Grandma!"

And his grandmother hugged him tighter. "I only reminded you, my boy. Your own wise eyes did the rest."

Goodnight, little one. The end. 🌙
