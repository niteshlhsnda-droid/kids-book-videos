# Faye the Little Frog and the Firefly Dance

Down by the pond, where the reeds whispered and the water wore the moon like a silver button, there lived a little green frog named Faye. She had long springy legs and a smile that crinkled at the corners, and she loved her pond more than anywhere in the world.

Every evening, as the sky turned the color of warm plum jam, the fireflies on the far bank woke up and began their dance. They twirled and twinkled over the tall grass, spelling golden shapes in the dark, and Faye watched from her lily pad, her chin resting on her knees.

"Oh, how I wish I could dance with them," she whispered one evening. But the pond was wide, and between Faye's lily pad and the far bank lay five big lily pads, floating like green stepping stones.

Faye took a deep breath, wiggled her toes, and hopped — one, two — but on the third hop her foot slipped, and splash! Down she went into the cool water, with a plop that made the little minnows giggle.

She climbed back onto her pad, shaking the water from her ears. "Again," she said, very quietly. Just then a dragonfly with wings like thin blue glass fluttered down beside her. "Little hops," she said kindly, "and soft landings. Bend your knees, and land like a falling leaf."

Faye tried, just as the dragonfly had shown her. One, two, three, four — and there she was, halfway across the pond! But the next pad was far away, and her legs were tired, so she sat down to rest a little while.

On that pad, an old turtle was sunning his shell in the moonlight. "Rest a little," he said in his slow, warm voice. "Even the river stops to rest in the quiet pools." So Faye sat beside him and watched the fireflies blink, blink, blink, until her legs felt strong again.

Then Faye stood up tall, bent her knees, and hopped — hop! — and landed, soft as a falling leaf, on the grass of the far bank. The fireflies turned all at once, like a sky full of tiny lanterns. "Faye!" they twinkled. "You came!"

They made a space for her in the very middle of their dance, and Faye hopped and twirled and spun until her green toes tingled. She did not dance the highest, and she did not dance the fastest — but she danced, and the whole meadow twinkled with her.

When the moon climbed high and the dance slowed, the smallest firefly settled on Faye's nose and glowed, warm as a night-light. "Same time tomorrow?" it asked. "Same time tomorrow," said Faye, sleepy and happy. And all the way home, hop by hop across the lily pads, the fireflies lit her way.

Sleep now, little Faye.

The end. 🌙
