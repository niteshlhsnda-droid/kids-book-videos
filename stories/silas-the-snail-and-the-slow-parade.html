Silas the Snail and the Slow Parade

A sweet original bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

In a green garden, under a big oak tree, there lived a little snail named Silas.

Silas was slow. Very, very slow. He carried his round, brown shell on his back, and he moved at a snail's pace — one slow step, then another slow step, then another.

One sunny morning, the garden animals decided to have a parade! The rabbit hopped. The robin sang. The butterfly fluttered. The ladybird marched.

"Come and join the parade, Silas!" cried the ladybird.

"Oh, I would love to," said Silas. "But I am so slow. The parade will be over before I take my first step!"

"Then we will walk with you," said the ladybird. "A parade is no fun without all our friends."

So the ladybird walked beside Silas. The bee buzzed beside Silas. The caterpillar crawled beside Silas. And the rabbit — who usually hopped very fast — hopped very, very slowly beside Silas.

The parade began. It was the slowest parade the garden had ever seen!

One slow step. Two slow steps. Three slow steps. The animals sang a slow, happy song — "La-a-a, la-a-a, la-a-a" — and the flowers swayed along. The caterpillar sang a slow song about the sun and the rain.

They passed the red roses. They passed the yellow sunflowers. They passed the little blue pond, where the water went, "Lap, lap, lap."

"Look!" said the bee. "When we go slowly, we can see everything! Look at the dewdrops! Look at the butterflies! Look at the tiny mushrooms!"

The ladybird pointed out a tiny mushroom with white spots. The bee showed them a dewdrop shining like a diamond.

"Going slowly is wonderful!" said Silas. "I can see everything!"

And it was true. Going slowly, they saw all the small, beautiful things they usually rushed past.

At last, just as the sun was going down, the slow parade reached the big oak tree. The sky was pink and gold. The fireflies were beginning to blink.

"We did it!" cried the ladybird. "We reached the oak tree!"

"Hooray!" cried the bee. "Hooray for Silas! Hooray for the slowest parade ever!"

Silas smiled. His shell felt light. His heart felt warm. "Thank you for waiting for me," he said. "This was the best parade in the whole world."

That night, all the animals lay down under the big oak tree. Silas curled up in his shell, cosy and safe. He counted the fireflies blinking in the grass — one, two, three, four, five — until his eyes grew heavy.

And under the twinkling stars, they all fell fast asleep.

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Slow is not the same as late — and the best parades wait for everyone.
