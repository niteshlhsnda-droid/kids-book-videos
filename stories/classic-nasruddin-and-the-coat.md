# Nasruddin and the Coat

*A gentle Mullah Nasruddin tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

Long, long ago, in a little town with white walls and narrow lanes, there lived a wise and funny man named Nasruddin.

Nasruddin loved good food, good friends, and good jokes — in that order. He wore simple clothes, a plain shirt and a soft old cap, because he liked to be comfortable.

One day, a rich neighbour named Rahim sent Nasruddin an invitation.

"Dear Nasruddin," said the letter, "please come to my house tonight for a grand feast! There will be rice and kebabs and sweet, sticky halwa!"

Nasruddin was delighted. He put on his simple shirt and his soft old cap, and he walked happily to Rahim's big house.

But when he reached the door, the servant looked at his plain clothes and frowned.

"This is a grand feast," said the servant. "You cannot come in dressed like that. Go away!"

Nasruddin was surprised. "But I am invited!" he said.

The servant shook his head and closed the door.

Nasruddin walked home, thinking. "Hmm," he said to himself. "They did not want me. They wanted fine clothes."

His wife Amina was waiting at home. "What happened?" she asked.

"They turned me away because of my old shirt," said Nasruddin. "So let us give them what they want!"

Nasruddin opened his wooden chest and took out his very best coat. It was long and rich and embroidered with gold thread. He put it on, smoothed his moustache, and walked back to Rahim's house.

This time, the servant opened the door wide and bowed low. "Welcome, welcome, honoured guest! Please come in! Please sit at the best seat!"

Inside, the room was full of delicious food. Platters of rice, bowls of kebabs, and a big dish of sweet, sticky halwa.

Rahim himself came to greet Nasruddin. "My dear friend! You look splendid! Please, eat! Eat!"

Nasruddin smiled. He picked up his sleeve — the long, rich, gold-embroidered sleeve of his best coat — and dipped it into the bowl of halwa.

"Eat, my coat, eat!" said Nasruddin, in a loud, cheerful voice. "Eat all you want!"

Everyone stared. "Nasruddin!" cried Rahim. "What are you doing? Why are you feeding your coat?"

Nasruddin looked up with a twinkle in his eye. "Because, my friend, it was my coat you invited — not me. When I came in my plain shirt, your servant sent me away. When I came in this fine coat, you welcomed me to the best seat. So the coat must be the honoured guest. Let it eat!"

The room went very quiet. Then Rahim's face turned red — not with anger, but with shame.

"Oh, Nasruddin," said Rahim. "You are right. I am so sorry. I welcomed your coat, and I forgot to welcome you. Please forgive me."

Nasruddin smiled and wiped the halwa off his sleeve. "I forgive you," he said. "But remember — a person's clothes are not the person."

Rahim laughed, and everyone laughed with him. He served Nasruddin himself, with his own hands, and they ate the feast together, talking and laughing late into the night.

And from that day on, whenever someone came to Rahim's house, he welcomed them with a smile — whether they wore a fine coat or a plain old shirt.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Judge people by their hearts, not by their clothes.*
