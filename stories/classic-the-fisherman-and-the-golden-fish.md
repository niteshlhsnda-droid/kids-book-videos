# The Fisherman and the Golden Fish

*A beloved Russian folktale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

By the edge of a wide blue sea lived an old fisherman and his wife in a tiny, crooked little hut.

Every morning the old fisherman rowed out and threw his net into the sparkling water. Every evening he came home with only a few small fish — but he was content, and hummed as he mended his net.

One morning, his net felt heavy — very heavy! He pulled and pulled, and up came a golden fish, all shimmering gold — and, wonder of wonders, it could speak!

"Kind fisherman," said the golden fish in a small, silvery voice, "please let me go. Put me back in the sea, and I will grant you anything your heart wishes."

The old fisherman's eyes grew round. But he smiled and slipped it gently back into the waves.

"Swim free, little one," he said. "I ask for nothing."

He rowed home and told his wife everything.

His wife clapped her hands. "Oh! Then go back at once! Our trough — the big wooden tub — is cracked and broken — ask the fish for a new trough!"

He sighed, but he loved his wife, so he rowed back out to the calm blue sea.

"Golden fish," he called softly, "my wife would like a new trough."

"It is done," said the golden fish, and slipped away.

He rowed home — and there stood a fine new trough!

But the next morning his wife said, "A new trough is not enough. Ask the fish for a little cottage — with a warm stove!"

He rowed back out. "Golden fish," he called over the gray, whispering water, "my wife would like a little cottage."

"It is done," said the golden fish.

And home he rowed — to a cozy little cottage with a warm, glowing stove!

But the next morning his wife stamped her foot. "I want to be a grand lady, with fine dresses!"

He rowed back out, his heart heavy. "Golden fish," he whispered over the restless gray waves, "my wife wishes to be a grand lady."

"It is done," said the golden fish.

And home he rowed — and his wife stood in fine dresses, as grand as grand can be!

But the next morning she cried, "A grand lady is not enough! I want to be a queen, with a golden crown!"

The poor fisherman nearly wept. But he rowed back out into the dark, rocking waves.

"Golden fish," he said, hanging his head, "my wife wishes to be a queen."

"It is done," said the golden fish quietly.

And home he rowed — and there was a palace, and his wife wore a golden crown!

But the next morning — oh! — his wife's eyes glittered like cold stars. "I want to rule the whole sea!" she cried. "And the golden fish shall be my servant!"

The old fisherman was ashamed to his very toes. "Please, wife," he begged, "enough is enough."

But she would not listen. So with a heavy heart, he rowed back out one last time.

Now the sea had risen up, dark and rolling — and then, gently, gently, a soft storm breathed across the water... and passed. The waves sighed themselves to sleep, and the sea lay still and silver under the moon.

He rowed slowly home.

And there — where the palace had stood — was their tiny, crooked little hut. And before it stood the old, cracked, broken trough.

His wife stood in the doorway. She looked at the hut, and the trough — and smiled a small, soft smile.

"Enough is enough, my dear," she said.

He took her hand, and they sat together on the step of their little hut, under the quiet silver moon, listening to the soft hush-hush of the sea.

And they were content — just as before.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: Wanting more and more spoils the happiness we already have.*
