The Pigeon and the Ant

A gentle Jataka tale for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.

Once upon a time, beside a bright blue river, there lived a kind grey pigeon named Pip.

Pip lived in a tall tree with soft green leaves, and every morning he sang a sweet song.

"Coo, coo! Good morning, river! Coo, coo! Good morning, sun!"

One sunny morning, a tiny ant named Annie was walking along the riverbank.

Annie was carrying a crumb of bread, almost as big as herself, back to her little home.

She was so busy watching her crumb that she did not see the smooth, slippery stone.

Oh no! Annie slipped — plop! — and fell right into the cool, fast river!

"Help! Help!" cried Annie, in her tiny, tiny voice. "I cannot swim!"

The river carried her round and round, faster and faster.

Pip heard her tiny cry from his tree.

"Oh, that poor little ant!" said Pip. "I must help her, quickly!"

Pip flew down and picked up a big, green leaf in his beak.

He flew over the river and dropped the leaf gently into the water, right beside Annie.

"Climb on, little ant!" called Pip. "Climb on, quickly!"

Annie grabbed the leaf with her six tiny legs and pulled herself up, up, up.

Phew! She was safe at last, sitting on her little green boat.

The leaf floated gently to the riverbank, and Annie stepped onto the dry, warm sand.

"Thank you, kind pigeon!" said Annie, with a happy wiggle. "You saved my life!"

"You are most welcome, little friend," said Pip with a smile. "Kindness is the best gift of all."

Annie waved her tiny feelers. "One day, I will help you too," she promised.

Pip laughed a soft, kind laugh. "You are so tiny," he said. "But I will remember your promise."

Many days later, Pip was sitting in his tree, singing his morning song.

"Coo, coo! Good morning, river! Coo, coo! Good morning, sun!"

But down below, behind a bush, a hunter named Hari was hiding very quietly.

He had a big net, and he watched Pip hungrily.

"Ah, a fine fat pigeon," whispered Hari. "I will catch him for my dinner!"

He raised his net, slowly, slowly, and tiptoed closer and closer.

Just then, Annie the ant was walking by on her way home.

She saw the hunter, and she saw Pip, and she knew just what to do.

Annie hurried up, as fast as her six little legs could go, and climbed onto Hari's foot.

Then she gave his toe one tiny nip — nip!

"Ouch! Ouch!" cried Hari, hopping up and down on one foot.

"Something bit my toe! Ouch, ouch, ouch!"

Hari dropped his net and hopped away down the path, holding his sore toe.

Pip heard the noise and looked down. "Oh, thank you, Annie!" he cooed happily.

"You saved my life, just as I saved yours!"

Annie smiled up at her friend. "A friend in need is a friend indeed," she said.

And from that day on, Pip and Annie were the very best of friends.

Every morning, they met by the river, and Pip sang his song while Annie danced on the warm sand.

"Coo, coo!" sang Pip. "Thank you, little friend!"

💛 Tonight's gentle lesson: Kindness always finds its way back to you.
