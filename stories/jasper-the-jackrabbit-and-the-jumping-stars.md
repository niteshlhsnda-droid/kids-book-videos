# Jasper the Jackrabbit and the Jumping Stars

*A brand-new bedtime story for ages 4–7. Reading time: about 4 minutes.*

---

In a wide, golden meadow, there lived a young jackrabbit named Jasper.

Jasper had very long ears and very strong legs. He could jump higher than any other rabbit in the whole meadow. Boing! Boing! Boing!

But Jasper had one little problem. At night, when all the other rabbits curled up to sleep, Jasper could not fall asleep. His legs wanted to jump, and his ears wanted to listen, and his eyes wanted to stay wide open.

"I wish I could sleep like everyone else," sighed Jasper.

One night, as Jasper sat in the grass, he saw something wonderful. A little star came twinkling down from the sky — twinkle, twinkle — and landed softly on a dandelion.

"Hello, little star," whispered Jasper.

"Hello, Jasper," said the star in a tiny, twinkly voice. "Why are you still awake?"

"I cannot fall asleep," said Jasper. "My legs want to jump!"

The little star giggled. "Then jump with me!" it said. "Jump once for every star in the sky, and when you have jumped them all, you will be sleepy."

So Jasper jumped — boing! — one jump for the first star. Boing! — one jump for the second star. Boing! Boing! Boing!

The little star bounced beside him, twinkling with laughter.

Jasper jumped for the big stars and the little stars. He jumped for the bright stars and the shy stars that hid behind the clouds. He jumped for the stars over the meadow, and the stars over the river, and the stars over the faraway hills. He jumped until his legs felt soft and floaty, like clouds themselves.

"One more jump," said the little star, "for the sleepiest star of all."

Jasper took a deep breath and jumped — boing! — the highest jump of the whole night. And when he landed, he landed softly, softly, right in a cosy hollow in the grass.

The little star settled down beside him, glowing warm and golden.

"Now close your eyes," whispered the star. "And think of all the stars you jumped for. They are all tucked up in the sky now, just like you."

Jasper closed his eyes. He thought of the bright stars and the shy stars. He thought of his jumps — boing, boing, boing — growing slower and softer in his mind.

And before he knew it, Jasper was fast asleep, dreaming of jumping from star to star across the whole night sky.

In the morning, all the other rabbits woke up and stretched.

"Did you sleep well, Jasper?" they asked.

Jasper smiled a big, sleepy smile. "I slept wonderfully," he said. "I jumped with a star."

And every night after that, whenever Jasper could not sleep, he jumped once for every star — and the stars always helped him drift off to dreamland.

The end. 🌙

---

*Moral: A little gentle play before bed can help even the bounciest legs fall asleep.*
